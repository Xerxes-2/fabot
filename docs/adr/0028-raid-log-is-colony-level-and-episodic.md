# The Raid log is colony-level and episodic, because the per-creep log prunes the dead

> **Amended by #216 R4** on where the quiet gap comes from, and on nothing it decides. The fifty ticks is `Tuning.QuietGap` on the [[colony view]] (ADR 0052 decision 5), read by `foldRaids` rather than handed to it, because it is a judgement about how long an absence has to run before it is a departure — a colony's choice — where the ring's depth beside it is a Memory-leaf budget and stays the caller's. The number, the poke-and-heal argument for it and every rule it gates are unchanged.

> **Amended by #201** on the room set, on the field this ADR left out, and on what `Damage` may be read over. **An episode opens on the first tick a room the colony works and can see holds a hostile**, not a spawn room: ADR 0033's Reach and Flee read `Snapshot.Hostiles`, and swept over the spawn rooms a raider in an [[outpost]] was not merely unhandled by them but *absent* — a live raid in W13S28 at t161,9xx killed every creep the colony had in the room while the reach gate held nothing back and no episode recorded it. So the window, `LastSeen` and the roster are every worked room's. **The room name on `HostileInfo` is load-bearing**, against the Consequence below that called it "a field no decision reads": ADR 0041 gave it the closest approach, #138 files a Threat's Reach under it, and the two colony reflexes now read the home room out of the list by it. **`Damage` alone stays one room's.** The Keep and its ramparts stand at home (ADR 0034) and nothing else is measured, so the hits are summed over the ticks a hostile stood *there* — an episode held open from an outpost would otherwise charge this room's rampart decay, three hits a tick apiece, as what a raid that never touched it cost. What is not amended: the ring is still one colony-wide twenty, an episode still names no room, and the quiet gap, the losses rule and "a record to be read, never a signal sent" are untouched.

> **Revised by #80**: "issue #80's hole, open and unchanged" no longer holds. The wire spelling of every closed vocabulary now lives in Core beside its union, and `Core.Tests` enumerates each union by reflection and round-trips every case through its name — so a case added to `allBodyParts`'s union, or to any of the four Verdict vocabularies, without its wire name is a red test rather than a silent decode miss. This ADR's reading of the successor chain stands (it is why the literal stayed), and so does the blast-radius bound: what changed is that the Transition log's `load` now degrades creep by creep the way `loadRaids` already degraded episode by episode.

Player giaco probed `W12S28` from t68824 to t69040 — one squad of five, the same creeps re-entering over and over across ~220 ticks, standing at (38-41, 47-49) where the single tower does its minimum 150 damage a tick (#66). The colony survived that raid by geometry, and it left no trace anywhere in the bot: `Decide.fs` reads `Snapshot.Hostiles` in the two reflexes and nowhere else, and the [[transition log]] is keyed by creep with a fold that keeps only creeps still alive (`Set.intersect living`), so the one event a raid record exists for — a creep killed — is precisely the event that fold erases on the tick it happens. #66's report had to be reconstructed by hand from the room-history HTTP API after the fact.

We decided **hostile presence is a second observe channel, colony-level and episodic** (#73): a pure fold in Core, a thin App shell writing a sibling leaf under `Memory.fabot.observe`, and a one-shot CLI read — ADR 0009's shape unchanged, applied to a record that has no creep to key it on.

- **An episode, not a tick series.** An episode opens on the first tick a spawn room holds any hostile, stays open while hostiles keep appearing, and closes after fifty quiet ticks. Fifty is derived, not picked: it has to outlast the poke-and-heal cycle #66 recorded — in for a tick or two at the tower's minimum damage, back out to heal, over and over — and it is about the round trip a squad retreating off-room makes before it can be back, so a shorter absence is the same squad still working the room and a longer one is a decision to leave. Under it #66 is one episode of 217 ticks rather than forty of five. Being wrong is cheap both ways: too short splits one raid into rows a reader can still see are contiguous, too long merges two raids two and a half real minutes apart, which are one raid by any operational reading. The ring keeps twenty episodes — one ring for the colony, not one per creep, so twenty windows of #66's size are around 10KB against the 2MB Memory, and twenty raids is more history than stays actionable.
- **What an episode records.** The window (the tick it opened, and the last tick a hostile actually stood in a spawn room); the **roster** — one row per distinct hostile id, with its owner and its body part counts, taken from the first sighting and unioned over the window, so a squad reads as five rows however often it steps back in and a row records what entered the room rather than what the tower left of it; the **closest approach** — the smallest range over the whole episode between any hostile and anything of ours, with the tile the hostile stood on and the tick it was reached; and our **losses** inside the window, by name and tick. Closest approach is the number that matters: the same squad at range 29 is a non-event and at range 5 is a loss.
- **A loss is a creep gone while a hostile stood there, and never one whose own clock ran out.** The record answers what a raid cost, so the two ways a name leaves `Game.creeps` have to be told apart, and the Snapshot already carries the discriminator: `TicksToLive` says before the fact which creep is going to be gone next tick whatever the raiders do, so a creep on its last tick leaves the baseline the difference is read against. The reading also lags the death by a tick — a name is missing the tick *after* its creep died — so a loss is stamped at the tick it was last seen alive and is recorded only when a hostile stood there on that tick. That keeps the window honest in both directions: every Loss falls between `Opened` and the last sighting, the poke-and-heal kill read on the first quiet tick is still the raid's, and a name that vanishes deeper into the fifty-tick gap is ordinary attrition the raid is not charged with. At the shipped gap, with a handful of creeps on 1,500-tick lives, charging the whole gap would have padded a quarter of all episodes with a death the raid never caused.
- **The projection grows an owner and nothing else.** `HostileInfo` gains the hostile's username. That is ADR 0007's own rule applied rather than broken — the field list grows the tick a reader for it exists, and attribution is that reader. Hostiles stay **out** of the [[spatial projection]] exactly where ADR 0014 left them: they block no tiles, price no paths, gate no Tasks, and nothing in the Task pipeline reads `Snapshot.Hostiles` when this lands.
- **A record to be read, never a signal sent.** No reaction of any kind: no fleeing, no Task release, no rank change, no spawn response, no ramparts, no alerting. Those are #66's open questions and they stay open; this is what makes "wait and see" a strategy rather than a hope.

## Considered Options

- **Extend the Transition log instead** — rejected structurally: its fold prunes a creep's whole timeline the tick it dies, so it cannot record a death however it is capped, and a raid is a colony fact with no creep to key it on.
- **A per-tick series of sightings rather than episodes** — rejected: 220 ticks of unchanged rows across five creeps is exactly the crowding ADR 0009 rejected full per-tick history for, and the questions a reader actually asks — was there a raid, who, how close did it get, what did it cost — are an episode's shape, not a tick's.
- **Record damage in hits** — deferred: `CreepInfo` would have to carry hits, a Snapshot field no decision reads, which is the growth rule this ADR is invoking, pointed the other way. Losses are the proxy until a decision needs the finer number — most likely whichever way #66's first question lands.
- **Tower accounting: shots fired, energy spent** — rejected: the fire reflex speaks no Verdict (ADR 0014) and this record does not give it one.
- **A Verdict channel for the raid** — rejected: a Verdict is attribution for a decision, and there is no decision here. ADR 0018's verbose-only rule for manufactured evidence does not bind a record nobody decided from, which is also why the Raid log is always on.
- **Alerting, email, `notify()`** — rejected: pushing turns a record into a signal, and nothing in the colony yet knows what to do with a signal.
- **Derive `allBodyParts` from a compiler-checked successor chain** — tried and rejected: it does not check what it looks like it checks. The compiler checks a `nextBodyPart : BodyPart -> BodyPart option` for *exhaustiveness*, never for *reachability* from the chain's head, so a part added to the union with the minimal arm the compiler demands — `| NewPart -> None` — compiles clean and never enters the list. It also buys a hazard the literal cannot have: `List.unfold` is eager, so a chain accidentally made cyclic hangs at module initialisation and the bundle never loads. The literal stays, issue #80's hole stays open and stays named, and what this change closes instead is the blast radius (below).
- **Cap the losses and the roster inside an episode too** — deferred: an episode nothing ever closes — a hostile the room cannot kill, so no quiet gap ever elapses — is always the ring's tail, so `trim` can never reach it, and its rows grow without a bound. Left as is because the loss rule above removes the ordinary driver (attrition padding an open episode) and what remains is one row per creep the raiders actually kill, in a colony that is losing creeps steadily to a raid it cannot end. A per-episode cap is a number that would need its own justification, and a silently truncated cost is worse than a long one.

## Consequences

- `HostileInfo` gains `Owner`, `ICreep` gains an `owner` binding, and the one hostile fixture in DecideTests fills it. No reflex reads it; the roster is its only reader.
- The owned set the closest approach measures against is the owned set as the Snapshot can approximate it, not the whole of it: the Refillables (`FIND_MY_STRUCTURES` — spawn, extensions, tower) and the controller (`my`), plus our creeps. Roads and containers cannot be owned in the engine; the Storage can, and is out of it, because ADR 0022 puts it on the cluster's first pick — a tile or two from the spawn, inside the ring the Refillables already cover — so it moves no measured minimum. Ramparts (#66) would, and this derivation is the thing that would then have to change.
- `Snapshot.Hostiles` sweeps every spawn room while the projection covers only the first (ADR 0005's single-colony assumption), so a hostile standing in a second spawn room would be measured against the first room's tiles. Deliberately not fixed: a room name on `HostileInfo` is a field no decision reads, and there is one spawn.
- The Chebyshev `range` moves to `Types.fs` beside `Pos` as the one definition; `Atlas.fs` and `Decide.fs` drop their byte-identical private copies, and the fold in `Observe.fs` measures with the same function the reflexes do.
- The roster puts body parts on the wire, and the decode's reverse table is derived from `allBodyParts`, a hand-written literal that can fall behind the union — issue #80's hole, open and unchanged. What is bounded is the damage: `loadRaids` decodes episode by episode, so an unreadable row costs that row and not the ring, and `Snapshot.bodyPartOf` already resolves an engine part string the list lacks to `Tough`, so the bot's own writes cannot emit a name its own decoder lacks. The same per-episode decode is what lets a hand-edit through the Memory HTTP API, or a rollback across a wire-shape change, cost one episode rather than the history.
- The Raid log's leaf is written every tick, whether or not the fold changed anything. That is a re-encode of the ring on quiet ticks, bought deliberately: the leaf's presence is the signal that this bundle is live, which is what lets `observe.mjs raids` distinguish "no channel" from "no raids" instead of answering an operator on a stale deploy with a confident false negative.
- The ObserveTests surface: four lists through the pure fold — `raid fold: episodes` (a quiet tick that records nothing, opening and extension, a re-entry inside the gap, a return after it, the ring cap, an empty log folding like a fresh boot), `raid fold: roster` (one row per id with owner and part counts, and the first sighting winning over a later chewed-up one), `raid fold: closest approach` (the minimum with its tile and tick, a tie keeping the tick it was first reached, an owned creep counting as much as an owned structure, and `None` when the projection places nothing of ours) and `raid fold: losses` (stamped at the last tick alive, the kill read on the first quiet tick, nothing charged for a name gone deeper into the gap, nothing for a creep whose own clock ran out, nothing on the seam between two raids, and old age in peacetime recorded nowhere). The App half — a discarded subtree reading empty, the `creeps` and `verbose` siblings surviving a raid write — has no test project to live in and is held by construction, the same way `Snapshot.fs`'s projections are; that is a stated gap in the evidence, not coverage.
- `observe.mjs raids` joins the read commands, newest episode first, with `--json` like the rest — printing the episodes alone, since `living` is the fold's own baseline and not part of the record. `Memory.fabot.observe` now carries three leaves, and each writer assigns only its own.
