# The clustered horizon moves to RCL6: the cluster steps one ring out, and one room's trunk moves by a tile

ADR 0039 moved the horizon to RCL5 and closed on a question it deliberately left for this level: it "inherits a north band that is now spoken for", and whether one level of lookahead survives "a room that has run out of cluster room" is "a decision for the level that meets it". W12S28 reaches RCL6 tomorrow, so this is that level.

The move is not optional in the way a horizon usually is, for the reason ADR 0039 already stated and this ADR only re-states because it is the whole urgency: the clustered kinds are **sized** at the horizon and only **filtered** at the current level, so a room standing at RCL6 under a horizon of 5 computes an extension gap of `30 − 30 built = 0`, asks for none of the ten the engine just unlocked, and holds its bank at 1,800 instead of 2,300. Left alone the horizon is not a conservative choice at RCL6; it is a room that stops growing without saying why. And ADR 0011's bargain — today's trunks route around tomorrow's structures — is only bought if the constant moves *before* the level lands: the whole cost of a horizon is paid early or not at all.

We re-derived it ADR 0039's way, on the committed captures, planning the same room at horizon 5 and at horizon 6 and diffing the plan. **W12S28 from `12,40`, the tile the live spawn stands on:** the trunks do not move — both plans pave the same 25 tiles, not a comparable length but the same set; the Storage stays on `11,39` and both towers on `11,41` and `13,39` — RCL6 allows no third tower, so the entire delta of this move is ten extension slots; all four Link footings are served in both, on the same tiles (`10,42`, `15,39`, `8,39`, `11,40`), with no shortfall; and the ten further extensions land on `17,35 17,37 17,43 17,45 8,34 10,34 12,34 14,34 16,34 16,46`.

That answers ADR 0039's question. The north band (rows 35–37) is spoken for, so **the cluster does not squeeze into it — it steps one ring out**: north onto row 34 (`8,34` through `16,34`), east onto column 17 (`17,35` through `17,45`), and one tile south-east onto `16,46`. It treads on no [[working ground]]: not one of the ten is a [[seat]] or an Upgrade Work Area tile, and it needs no new rule to keep it off — ADR 0022's exclusion is what pushes the ordering outward in the first place. The room has the tiles, and the answer to "does one level of lookahead survive a room that has run out of cluster room" is that this room has not run out.

**W13S28 from `16,12`,** the child's own Spawn2, is where the re-derivation is less clean and the honest record is the point: `13,7` is a trunk tile at horizon 5 and an extension pick at horizon 6, so the router routes around it, and the road plan goes from **30 paved tiles to 29** — shorter, because the detour runs over tiles another route had already paved. The Storage (`15,11`), both towers (`15,13`, `17,11`) and all four footings are unmoved, no trunk is dropped, and the ten land on `20,12 20,14 11,7 11,9 11,11 11,13 11,15 11,17 13,7 13,17`, again none of them on working ground. So "the trunks do not move" is W12S28's result and not a general one; what generalises is that a wider reservation costs the router a detour and, here, not even a longer one.

The price of the ten tiles is paid somewhere, and we went looking for it rather than reading it off the sweep's stride. Planned from **every** plain spawn tile of all four captures — 5,114 of them, at horizon 5 and at horizon 6 — the move regresses exactly **three**, all in W12S27 (`31,1`, `32,2`, `33,1`): the wider cluster closes the corridor out of that pocket, `src-0`'s trunk to the spawn cannot be routed, and it is recorded as dropped (#107). That is #105's mechanism, not a new one — the clustered ordering is kept off the [[working ground]] and off nothing else, so it is free to take the tiles a spawn drives out through — and the ten new tiles simply reach into a corridor the thirty stopped short of. No room gains an unserved footing anywhere, W12S28 regresses at none of its 1,666 tiles, and neither live spawn (`12,40` and `16,12`) is among the three. `32,2` joins `6,18` in `RoomInvariantTests`' sealed-doorstep pins, so the sweep now sees one of them; the other two are the same tile in a different corner and wait on #105 with it. Everything else in the four-room sweep passes unchanged.

## Considered Options

- **Leave the horizon at 5 and raise it when RCL6 actually lands** — rejected for ADR 0039's reason, unchanged and now with a second year of evidence behind it: raising the horizon the tick the level arrives is exactly the moment the reservation can no longer route today's trunks around tomorrow's structures.
- **A horizon of `current level + 1`, computed rather than declared** — rejected; ADR 0039 rejected it against ADR 0027's determinism invariant and nothing here reopens that argument. Cited, not re-argued.
- **Horizon to RCL8, ending the revisits** — rejected; ADR 0011's original reason and ADR 0039's restatement of it both stand. Cited, not re-argued.
- **Modelling the terminal RCL6 unlocks** — rejected here, and deliberately not as a horizon question. RCL6 adds exactly two things: the ten extensions, and a second [[storage]]-class structure, the terminal. This repo models no terminal, no extractor and no lab; each is an ADR-shaped new capability with its own Task semantics, not a row in an allowance table, and bolting one onto the commit that moves a constant is how a horizon move becomes a feature. Ask the user for each on its own ticket.

## Consequences

- **The plan changes once, on the commit that moves the constant**, and it changes by ten reserved tiles. For a living room this costs no demolition — a standing structure is a target, its tile is out of the ordering, and nothing already built moves — but ADR 0027's invariant steps once here, and it steps because a human moved a constant rather than because the Layout churned. That is the same step ADR 0039 recorded, in the same shape.
- **W13S28's road plan loses `13,7` to an extension**, which is ADR 0011's stated precedence — reservations win every overlap — arriving one tile at a time rather than as a rule change. It is a plan-time divergence only, and it stays one: a standing road is a target, so its tile is out of the clustered ordering entirely, and had `13,7` been paved before this commit the reservation would simply have fallen to the next tile with the trunk keeping its pavement. The room is at RCL2 and places no road site at all below `Tuning.BootstrapLevel` (#209), so nothing is paved there and nothing is demolished. The order the two arrive in is what decides which of them holds the tile, and both orders are consistent plans — which is the honest reading of ADR 0027's invariant here: it holds across the build-out of *one* plan, and this commit is the human moving the constant that it explicitly permits.
- **A room short of candidate tiles pays for the ten at its current level**, exactly as ADR 0039's second tower made it pay for one. The clustered window is ten tiles wider before the trunks are routed, so in a cramped room a tile that would have been an extension today is held for one a level away, and the router has ten more tiles to dodge. W12S28 and W13S28 both have the spare tiles; #83's remote candidates are not W12S28.
- **The [[link footing]]s are untouched and stay unfilled.** ADR 0038's RCL6 revisit says to re-run the quota arithmetic, and this ticket does not: no link is built here and no footing moves. What the revisit needs is a number the colony can now produce, and the ticket that opens it should be sent to the reading that exists rather than to the one this ADR first wrote down: `npm run observe -- quotas` prints no `buffer` **row** — its rows are the cast's, `worker` through `upgrader` — and carries `buffer` instead as a **sink** on each source container's `haul:` line, beside its round trip in ticks. That is the number: a container priced at the buffer is priced at the dearest sink it can reach (ADR 0049), so a controller link is worth the difference between the summed demand as printed and the same sum with the buffer leg gone, read against the `haulers before the ferry` the line divides out to. Anything finer than that — the buffer's own throughput as a row of its own — is a number `observe` does not print yet, and producing it is part of that issue. It is its own issue either way, opened after the room stands at RCL6.
- **The next revisit is RCL7**, which adds ten more extensions, a second spawn and a third tower. The second spawn is the first thing a horizon has ever reserved that the Layout has no rule for — the plan is oriented on *the* spawn tile — so that revisit is a design question and not a constant, and it should be met with the same re-derivation this ADR ran rather than with a number.
