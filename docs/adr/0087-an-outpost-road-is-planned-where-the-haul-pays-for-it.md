# An outpost road is planned where the haul pays for it

> **Status:** accepted

> **Proposed 2026-10-06** for #548. It amends ADR 0042 ("the outpost gets a container and nothing else", and #266's lift of every outpost site) and ADR 0011 ("sites place all at once"); both carry an "amended by 0087" banner. It does not fold #342: every road standing in an outpost is still repaired.

The Layout paved the home alone: trunks from each source to the spawns and the controller (ADR 0011). Every outpost road the colony has was laid by the user's hand: W18S25's 24 tiles, W17S26's 8, W12S27's 5. W13S26 and W12S25, declared for W12S26 on 2026-10-06, stood with none. An unpaved outpost costs the [[hauler unit]] its body: `[Carry, Carry, Move]` is cast for full speed loaded on a road, and on plain it walks loaded at half speed.

## Decision

1. **Every outpost source of the colony with a container gets one road**, from the home's drop point to a tile beside that container. The drop point is the Storage's tile (standing, pending, or the plan's Storage pick, which is reserved from level 0) or the oldest spawn beside it, whichever the line leaves more cheaply. A Storage boxed in by extensions would otherwise leave no road at all. The road is part of the Layout's plan (`planLayout`), so it rides the census-keyed plan memo (ADR 0017). It is planned again only when a structure, a site, a room's hold or a stage moves, never on a quiet tick. A replan re-floods only a room whose priced grid or seeds moved: each room's flood is kept under a key that spells both (`RoadFloodTable`), so a census move at home leaves an outpost room's flood as it was wherever the line still crosses into it at the same tiles and cost.

2. **It runs along the route the haulers take.** The route is the cheapest of the chains `Atlas.routesFor` gives a `Bold` walker. Those chains never enter a rival's room (#444) or a Source Keeper's room. They are not the `Wary` chains a hauler walks, because the rooms a raid was seen in (`AvoidRooms`) are not in the plan's signature. A road that moved with every raid sighting would orphan its own tiles. One flood is chained across each chain's Seams (`Atlas.roadAlong`), so the road is the cheapest path over the whole chain, not a room-by-room greedy one. It is priced as a trunk is (plain 2, swamp `TrunkSwampWeight`), around the home's reservation. A tile that already carries a road is priced at 1: a standing road (the user's count), our pending road site, a home trunk, or a road an earlier outpost's plan took this tick. So a hand road is followed wherever it is near the cheapest line, and two outposts share the stretch they have in common. The discount falls only on tiles the line already takes, so its own roads being built never move it.

3. **It is planned only where it pays, per outpost.** A road tile saves a loaded hauler the ticks between its ground and a road (`Gait.ticks`): one on plain and nine on swamp for the `[C, C, M]` block. A hauler tick is worth the block's cost over its life per unit carried, 150 / (1,500 × 100) = 0.001 energy per unit. So a tile saves `F × Δticks / 1000` energy a tick, where `F` is the source's rate as the reserver row holds it (#515's reading, `reservableRateOf`): 10 in a room the row can reserve while the bank can buy a reserver, whatever the reservation reads this tick, and the room's own rate everywhere else. A lapse the reserver restores in a few hundred ticks must not flip a verdict that weighs fifty thousand. Against that the tile costs its upkeep. Decay is 100 hits each time the road's decay timer runs out (1,000 ticks), 500 on swamp, at 1 energy per 100 hits repaired: 0.001 and 0.005 energy a tick. Wear runs the same timer down faster. The engine's move processor does `road.nextDecayTime -= ROAD_WEAROUT * body.length`, so each step takes one timer tick per body part (checked in `@screeps/engine` `processor/intents/movement.js` and `roads/tick.js`). The haul crosses a tile twice per trip, loaded and back, at `F / capacity` trips a tick. For `[C, C, M]` blocks the parts and the load scale together, so the wear is `2F × 3 / 100 = 0.06F` timer ticks a tick whatever the hauler's size: 1.6 times the bare decay at `F` = 10, 1.3 times at `F` = 5. An unbuilt tile also costs its build (300 plain, 1,500 swamp). The road pays when, over `Tuning.RoadPayback` ticks, the saving over its tiles in the rooms we work covers their upkeep plus the build of the unbuilt ones. A standing tile costs nothing to build, so a hand road weighs in its route's favour. The miner's and the reserver's walks are left out, which errs toward not paving.
   At 50,000 ticks (about two days of the season's clock), a reserved source pays for plain ground: a plain tile repays its 300 in 35,700 ticks at `F` = 10, and a swamp tile its 1,500 in 18,300. An unreserved source pays only where swamp carries it: 81,100 ticks for a plain tile at `F` = 5, 39,000 for a swamp tile. Counting wear moves those numbers by 7–10% and changes no verdict. Every live outpost is reserved (W13S26, W12S25, W17S26, W18S25), so all of them pay on plain and swamp alike.
   **The payback gates new sites only.** A road that stands, hand-laid or ours, is kept and repaired by the ordinary repair rules whatever the verdict. An `unpaid` outpost gets no new sites, and nothing else changes for it.

4. **Sites go down a few at a time.** The pending road sites on the planned outpost roads are capped at `Tuning.OutpostRoadSites`, three, placed from home outward, so the next goes down only as one completes. This is pacing, which ADR 0011 rejected for the home. It needs no memory here: the cap is read off the census like any gap. There are two reasons. First, the engine allows a player 100 construction sites across every room, shared by every colony: six colonies at ten apiece held 60 of them, and a level-up's extensions would meet `ERR_FULL`. So no outpost road site goes down while the player holds `Tuning.OutpostRoadSiteCeiling` (70) sites or more, counted off `Game.constructionSites` (`World.Sites`); which side of that line the count stands on is in the census signature, so crossing it replans. Second, one worker at a time crosses for them (decision 8), and dozens of sites would stand for thousands of ticks. The stage gate of the home's roads (#209, `Independent`) holds here too, and so does the home's build phase (#526): no new outpost road site goes down while an extension, Storage or tower site stands at home or is placed this tick.

5. **Only rooms the colony works are paved.** These are the home and the colony's own outpost rooms, while it sees them and nobody else holds their controller: the engine refuses a site under another's reservation (#334), so a held room's stretch is planned and counted as `skipped`, like a crossed room's. A room the colony only crosses keeps no sites and no hits (ADR 0058: `transiting` takes every id out), so a road site there could neither be built nor seen pending, and would be asked for again on every replan. Its stretch of the road is planned and counted (`skipped`), never placed. An outpost the colony cannot see this tick is not judged at all (`dark`), since its rate is unknown.

6. **Hand roads are always kept.** Every road standing in an outpost room keeps its Repair, on the planned line or off it. The user laid W18S25's and W17S26's roads by hand, and where they go is the user's decision, not the plan's. #342 (off-plan roads staying hungry) stays open and is not folded here. An undeclared or stood-down outpost needs no rule: it leaves the scan set or becomes a transit room, and neither carries hits.

7. **The layout record names each outpost road's state** (`observe layout`): `planned`, `unpaid`, `unrouted` (no chain, or no path along one), `no-container`, or `dark`. Each counts the path's tiles, and how many of them stand, are pending, or are skipped.

8. **An outpost road site takes no builders' lift.** #266 lifted every outpost site into the builders' queue, the user's hand-laid roads included. Now only the non-road sites stand in it (the container first). The container is the income switch; a road only makes the haul cheaper. A road site in an outpost room, hand-laid or ours, is a surplus Build with a rung of its own. While the home builds (#526: an extension, Storage or tower site stands there) it steps one rung under its tier (`OneRungDown`), under every home Build, the Storage #526 holds on the tier included. Past the build phase the colony's one lead road site (the nearest its crossing, in the builders' queue order) is one rung over the tier, above the controller, and takes one body: on the tier alone it loses every travel-cost comparison to the Upgrade a loaded worker stands in the Work Area of, haulers carry no Work, and it would never be built. There it stands level with a home site and the walk decides. Every other outpost road site stays on the tier, where travel decides, so a body already out there builds the next and the roads never take the row the controller and the repairs are owed (#157; a low home like W17S25 must keep levelling). The road's home stretch is a home site and ranks as one, as a trunk's does.

## Considered options

- **Pave every outpost unconditionally.** It is simplest, and it is what the user's hand did. Rejected because the per-outpost rule costs one sum, and it is what stops a five-a-tick plain room from buying a road it never repays within the season.
- **Per-tile paving (swamp only, where plain does not pay).** Rejected. A gap-toothed road makes the line the haulers walk depend on which tiles happened to pay. The saving is additive per tile, but a road that is paved where it is cheapest and walked where it is not is not a road anybody can read.
- **Lift the transit narrowing for a road's rooms.** It would let a two-hop outpost's middle room be paved. Rejected for now: it reopens what ADR 0058 keeps out of a crossed room (sites, hits, the census signature), and that is the world layer's ground (ADR 0086), not the Layout's.
- **Stop repairing roads off the plan in a judged outpost** (the outpost half of #342). Rejected: it would let the user's hand roads decay because the router chose another line.

## Consequences

- W13S26 and W12S25 get their roads three sites at a time, once W12S26's own extensions, Storage and towers stand, one worker crossing for them at a time. W18S25's and W17S26's hand roads are followed and kept, and only the gaps between them and the drop point are placed.
- A raid seen in a transit room moves no road (decision 2).
- A two-crossing outpost whose middle room the colony only crosses gets its road in the home and in its own room, with a gap in between, until the transit narrowing is lifted for it.
- The memo carries the road records and not the planned tiles: nothing downstream reads them.
