# The reservation is sized at the allowance ceiling and the level sizes only the placement: one ordering, two windows

> **Status:** accepted

ADR 0063 derived the clustered horizon from the controller level, `controller.Level + 1`, and it was right about what #341 broke: a room standing at its horizon computes a gap of zero and asks for none of what the engine unlocked. What it did not separate is that `Layout.fs` truncates the one clustered ordering **twice** — once as `clustered`/`reserved`, the window the trunk router dodges, and once as `clusterPicks`, the window the tower and extension tiles are drawn from — and it sized both at `Tuning.horizonOf`. The cluster needed the level. The **road reservation never did**, and the road reservation is where the entire cost of the derivation landed: 589 paved tiles abandoned across the sweep's level-ups, ten source-container picks moved out from under standing containers, and upkeep for ever on every orphan (#342).

**We decided the reservation is sized at the level `allowanceOf` stops growing and the horizon sizes the placement alone.** The reservation is then a function of the terrain and the census and of no level at all, so a bare room's road plan is identical at every level *by construction* — which is ADR 0027's determinism invariant, the one ADR 0039 raised against a derived horizon and ADR 0063 priced rather than met.

This is a successor to ADR 0063 and not an amendment to it. ADR 0063's decision sentence stands for the cluster and is retired for the roads, which is a different decision from the one it made; an accepted ADR is not rewritten (`docs/agents/orchestration.md` § ADRs during the run).

## What this replaces, clause by clause

- **ADR 0063, Considered Options**: *"Separate the road reservation from the clustered sizing — size the reservation the trunks route around at the level `allowanceOf` stops growing, and leave the *placement* derived — measured and deferred to #344, not rejected."* **Taken**, on the measurement re-run here.
- **ADR 0063, Consequences, the determinism bullet**: *"ADR 0027's determinism invariant holds for the clustered kinds and is broken for the roads, which is the price of this decision and not an oversight in it… For the **roads** it does not survive: the reservation the router dodges is now a function of the level, so a level-up re-routes a trunk that is already paved and orphans the pavement it leaves behind, 589 tiles over the sweep's 684 level-ups and up to 60 in one room in one tick… What this ADR decides is that the trade is worth taking, not that the cost is zero."* **Replaced.** The reservation is not a function of the level, the invariant survives for the roads too, and the cost is zero — measured, not argued. ADR 0063 carries the banner for this bullet.
- **ADR 0063, Consequences, the abandoned-road bullet**: *"An abandoned road is not written off once; it draws upkeep for ever… That is a standing cost on top of the 176,700 energy the table above prices."* **The standing cost is gone with the churn that created it.** The gap in the Repair rule is **not** — nothing here demolishes an orphan, `Facts.hungryStructures` still walks every standing structure with no reference to the road plan, and a colony that already paved under ADR 0063 keeps whatever it orphaned. #342 stays open on its own merits, and this ADR narrows only what feeds it.
- **ADR 0063, Consequences, the road-gate bullet**: *"The road gate's own test loses its level-invariance pin… That is now false — not because the gate paces, which it still does not, but because the plan it filters is a function of the level."* **Restored.** `the road gap is all the level withheld, and it drops whole` pins W13S28's RCL3 road set against RCL4's again, beside the "drops whole" pin ADR 0063 added, which stays.
- **ADR 0063, Consequences, the #105 bullet**: *"#105's sealed doorstep at W12S27 `32,2` becomes level-dependent… It seals from RCL5 up, and a test of its own says so at every level from 5 to 8… What the derivation bought is that a room pays for it at the level it reaches rather than at RCL1."* **Reverted**, and this is the one measured regression of this decision. The tile is sealed at every level again — RCL3 to RCL8 — so it is back in the sweep's `SealedDoorsteps` beside `6,18`, which is where ADR 0055 had it. What that costs is nothing a colony pays: no colony stands on `32,2`, and what the seal replaces is a corridor the room used to pave for 95 tiles at RCL4 and walk away from on the tick it reached RCL5. The mechanism is #105's, but **#105 is closed** (2026-09-08) and so is #107, the recording half it was split into during triage: triage measured the doorstep exclusion, found it moves the live colony's Storage and five hand-built fixtures and amends ADR 0011 and ADR 0022, and judged it a decision rather than a fix. So the seal is **accepted behaviour** with the loss recorded (`UnroutedTrunks`, `SealedDoorsteps`), not a defect waiting on a ticket, and ADR 0063's own *"#105 is still open"* has stopped reading true along with the clause it sat in.
- **ADR 0039, Considered Options**: *"A horizon of `current level + 1`, computed rather than declared — rejected: it makes the plan a function of the level, and ADR 0027's invariant — `reserved ∪ standing sites` unchanged as the Layout is built out — is what makes the plan deterministic across ticks. A horizon that moves on its own breaks it once per level instead of once, deliberately, in a commit."* ADR 0063 replaced this by answering it for the cluster and conceding it for the roads. **Met** here: the objection was about the reservation, and the reservation no longer moves on its own. ADR 0039 was right about the roads and wrong about the cluster, and both halves are now settled rather than one of them traded.
- **ADR 0027**, whose invariant is the subject of all of the above: **restored for the roads**, unchanged for the cluster. The reserved window still carries one spare tile per Link footing, and the footings are still settled after the trunks.
- **ADR 0022's ordering is untouched**, and this is what keeps the change small: there is **one** ordering, one sort and one flood. What the two windows truncate is the same list, so the reservation is always a prefix of the ordering that contains the placement's prefix — `allowanceOf` never decreases and the ceiling is where it stops.

**The clustered placement moves in 5 of the 1,026 cases**, one tile each, and it moves because the Link footings and the controller container are chosen off the road plan: a moved road plan moves a footing, and a footing outranks the tower and the extensions in the one ordering. Four of the five are `W13S28 36,42` at RCL3 through RCL6, where the ceiling rule answers `34,40` at **every** level and the shipped rule answers `33,41`, `32,44`, `31,41` and `41,45` in turn — so where the two disagree it is the ceiling rule that is stable and the shipped rule that walks. The fifth is `W15S28 24,24` at RCL4, `21,27` for `20,22`.

## Where the ceiling comes from

`Layout.allowanceCeiling`, and it is **derived off `allowanceOf`'s own table**: the smallest level at which every clustered kind already answers what its catch-all row answers, found by asking each kind for a level nothing can exceed and then climbing from zero. It is 8 today. **It is not written as `8` anywhere**, because a level named in a constant is exactly what went stale three times running and cost ADR 0063 its Considered Options section — a literal here would be #341's own mistake with a different number. The day the engine adds a level, the table moves and the ceiling climbs to wherever it stopped.

**What the climb asks of the table**, stated because the climb cannot check it and a check that fails at module load is a bot that never boots — it is a top-level `const` in the emitted JS and runs once, before anything: *every clustered kind has a wildcard row of its own*, which is what makes the climb terminate (the table's trailing `| _ -> 0` satisfies the weaker "every row ends in a wildcard" vacuously, and an unbounded row such as `Extension, l -> l * 10` makes the climb diverge rather than answer); and *every clustered kind's row is non-decreasing in the level*, which is what makes the ceiling an actual ceiling and so what makes the reservation never narrower than the placement. Both premises are recorded on `allowanceCeiling` itself. Also recorded there: `terminal` asks for `Int32.MaxValue` directly and not an expression over it, because a future `when` guard doing arithmetic on the level is the one place .NET's wrapping int and Fable's JS number would answer differently and the suite and the bot would disagree about the ceiling.

**`Tuning.HorizonLookahead` keeps exactly one meaning** and gains nothing. It is how far above its own level the **cluster** is sized, which is what it has always been; the ceiling is not a second lookahead, is not a tunable, and is not in `Tuning` at all. ADR 0052 decision 5 draws that line — every number this bot *chose* rather than read off the server — and the ceiling is read off the engine's own `CONTROLLER_STRUCTURES` table, which makes it the opposite of a choice.

**The plan memo needs no second key, and this was checked rather than assumed.** `Decide.censusSignature` signs the home controller's level and has since ADR 0017. Under this shape the road half stops depending on it while the cluster half still does. That retires one of the level's three readings and leaves the other two, so the key stays **exactly** what the plan reads and not a superset of it: every level-up still moves the plan, through the placement horizon and through the allowance gate. The level is read for two other reasons anyway (the placement's allowance gate and the bank Capacity the hauler quota reads), and `a level-up invalidates the memo` still finds the two levels planning differently, now on the cluster alone.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the road reservation at the old absolute constant 6 and derive only the cluster** — dead, not merely rejected. At RCL7 and RCL8 the widened cluster reaches **past** a horizon-6 reservation and lands on tiles the plan paved in the same tick: measured over the same 1,026 cases with the reservation pinned at 6 and the placement left derived, **164 clustered tiles collide with the road plan across 118 of them**, every one at RCL7 or RCL8 and none below. That is precisely the conflict the reservation exists to prevent. Both the shipped rule and the ceiling rule collide in **zero** cases of the same 1,026. It also still churns 102 tiles on the built-out sweep, because a horizon-6 reservation *narrows* as a room builds out past RCL6.
- **Leave ADR 0063 as shipped and pay the 589** — rejected, which is what filing #344 already decided, but it is worth naming what makes it easy: the ceiling rule is not a trade. It abandons less road (589 → 0), paves fewer tiles (−21 to −42 per level across the sweep), moves fewer container picks (10 → 0) and moves the clustered placement in 5 cases of 1,026. The only column it loses is `32,2`.
- **Demolish the orphans instead** — rejected as a different ticket and a worse fix. A rule that demolishes a road the plan stopped wanting is #342's territory, it costs a tick's Intent and the energy already spent, and it repairs a symptom of a plan that moves rather than the plan. Nothing here demolishes anything.
- **Clamp the reservation somewhere below the ceiling — an RCL2 room need not reserve for sixty extensions** — rejected, and it is ADR 0063's own "Horizon to RCL8" bullet read one window over, so the answer has to be different from that one's. There it was rejected because it made the *placement* reach six levels past the room. Here it is the *reservation* alone, which places nothing: what a wide reservation costs is that the router dodges more tiles, and the measurement above is what that cost turned out to be — shorter trunks, because the room routes once for the shape it will end up with instead of re-routing at every level. A young room's reservation is the widest it will ever be, and that is the point: it is the shape the room is growing into.
- **A second `Tuning` field for the ceiling** — rejected on ADR 0052 decision 5. See above; a number read off the engine's table is not a number this bot chose.

## Consequences

- **`LevelUpAbandons` inverts from a recorded loss into an invariant.** `a level-up abandons paved road, and one pick of the container it moves (#341)` becomes `a level-up abandons no paved road, and moves no container's pick (#344)`, and its sweep-wide **ratchet** — "no more than 37 churning cases", "no more than 152 tiles" — becomes zero, stated as zero. That inversion is the change's acceptance criterion and not a side effect of it: a bound of "no more than 152" is green on a change that churns 151, and by construction the number is now none. The two per-room pins stay, restated: W13S28 from `36,42` paves the 92 from the start and RCL6 → RCL7 is free; W15S28 from `18,30` abandons nothing where ADR 0063 priced four tiles.
- **`32,2` returns to `SealedDoorsteps` and the #105 magnitude pin is restated.** `the doorstep 32,2 seals only once the room's own level widens the reservation (#105)` becomes `the doorstep 32,2 is sealed at every level, and no level-up pays for it (#105)`. The numbers it carried — 95 paved at RCL4, 35 at RCL5, 60 orphaned — are gone rather than moved, because the 95 is never laid: the room plans the sealed 35 at every level from 3 to 8, and the RCL4 → RCL5 level-up that orphaned 60 tiles orphans none. The pin stays because the loss stays, which is a different reason: #105 and #107 are both closed and the seal is accepted behaviour, so the pin records what the colony lives with rather than what it is waiting on.
- **The suite's whole-room sweep now exercises the widest reservation there is at every case.** ADR 0063's sweep note said the widest window this bot opens was reached only by the per-room ladders, because the sweep plans at RCL4 and RCL4's horizon is 5. That is still true of the **placement**. The **reservation** every swept case routes its trunks around is now sixty extensions and six towers at RCL4 exactly as at RCL8 — so the sweep's 171 spawns test the widest reservation on real terrain, which they never did before, at no extra plan per case.
- **`sealedPocketColony` moved one tile, and it was a level away from silence already.** The #77 regression fixture seals a pocket source's container pick by spending all eight of its neighbours: five wall and source, two standing extensions, and the trunk road out. Its exit was **orthogonal** — `22,30`, five tiles from the spawn and on the spawn's own checkerboard colour — so a reservation wide enough to reach range 5 claims it, the trunk cannot route, the source gets no container pick, no footing target is constructed and the shortfall the fixture exists to record disappears by having nothing to fall short of. Measured on the shipped rule: the old fixture serves its four targets and records its one shortfall at RCL3, RCL4 and RCL5, and from **RCL6** up drops both of `src-b`'s trunks and records nothing. So ADR 0063 had already made this fixture vacuous two levels above where anything instantiates it. It was not broken *in the suite* — every use is `sealedPocketColony 4`, and at 4 the shipped rule still gave the right answer — it was one `sealedPocketColony 6` away from silence, which is the weaker claim and the true one. The trunk now leaves by the **diagonal** `22,29`, which is on the other colour and which no reservation at any level can take, and the two standing extensions moved to `22,30` and `22,31`. The seal is the same eight tiles in the same proportions, and it now holds under both rules at every level from 3 to 8.
- **A room's pocket corridor is guarded by parity and by nothing else, which is #105 restated in general.** The working-ground exclusion keeps the cluster off Seats and off the Upgrade Work Area; it does not keep it off the one tile a Seat is reached *through*. Under a reservation sized at the ceiling that exposure is at its maximum from RCL1 rather than growing into it — which is better, because a colony now meets it before it has paved anything, and it is the whole of what `32,2` and the pocket fixture are saying. Nothing here fixes it. #105 owned it, measured a doorstep exclusion, priced it at the live colony's Storage plus five hand-built fixtures plus an amendment to ADR 0011 and ADR 0022, and closed without taking it — so this is a standing exposure with no ticket behind it, and a rule that wants to close it starts from that measurement rather than from a fresh one.
- **No `Tuning` field, no `Atlas` query, no second ordering and no second flood.** The diff in `Layout.fs` is one derived value, two `gapAt` calls and one widened `List.truncate`. The plan's cost per tick is unchanged but for a slightly larger reserved set for the router to test membership against — the set is built once per plan and joined once per room (#216 R3), and the profile shows no hotspot entering or leaving.
- **The switch itself orphans six paved tiles, once, at W15S28 — and that is the whole deploy-tick bill.** The invariant above is that no *future* level-up orphans road; the transition *into* it is a separate question and silence would read as 0 → 0, which is not what the colony sees. Priced against the live server rather than the harness, each colony planned from its own live spawn at its own live level with its own standing census (45/40/30 extensions, two towers and a Storage apiece): **W12S28** at RCL7 from `12,40` and **W13S28** at RCL6 from `16,12` plan byte-identically under both rules — nothing orphaned, nothing new. **W15S28** at RCL5 from `18,30` plans 57 tiles under both, but six of them move one tile: `10,26 11,27 12,28 21,23 21,25 22,24` are walked away from and `10,27 11,28 12,29 22,23 22,25 23,24` are asked for. Two parallel one-tile shifts, **1,800 energy to rebuild** plus the six orphans' standing upkeep (#342), against a cumulative-to-RCL8 pavement that drops from 64 tiles to 57 — recovered inside one level-up. Worth recording beside it: the live rooms stand **109, 116 and 84** roads against plans of 25, 29 and 57, so 84, 87 and 27 standing roads already match neither rule's plan. That gap is not this decision's and is #342's and #345's; this decision adds six tiles to it and removes every future addition.
- **The guard is widened past the sweep's one transition, because the sweep's one transition is not the rule.** `LevelUpAbandons` plans RCL4 → RCL5 and only that, and one transition is what ADR 0036's budget allows — the honest lever there is fewer plans per case, not more levels. But the tower allowance moves at RCL5, RCL7 and RCL8 and the extension allowance at every level, so a reservation re-coupled to the level in the **tower** half alone churns at RCL6 → 7 and nowhere the sweep looks: measured at 32 cases, 89 orphaned tiles and one source container's pick moved, with the whole suite green. Two more per-room level-ups close it at a cost of four plans total rather than by doubling the sweep — `W15S28` from `18,12` and `W12S27` from `18,30`, both RCL6 → RCL7, both asserting the road set *and* the container picks. Those are the two worst cases that mutation produces, and they are now red under it. The pick half of the invariant is pinned at three spawns across two transitions and is deliberately not swept: a sweep-wide pick gate costs a second `decide` per case with the roads standing, which is the plan budget ADR 0036 draws the line at.
- **The reservation's *width* is guarded separately from its level-blindness, and the guard's resolution is four slots.** Level-blindness is what the level-up pins catch; a reservation that is level-blind and simply too *narrow* slips past all of them, because nothing moves. What catches that is a collision — a tower or an extension planted on a tile the same plan paves, the failure that made the constant-6 option dead rather than merely worse. Measured over a nine-mutation battery on the reservation's two `gapAt` calls: the full revert and both half-reverts red 5, 5 and 1 test; a reservation one level below the ceiling reds 3; one *four* tower slots below the ceiling reds the new RCL8 collision pin. One mutant survives and is reported rather than hidden: a tower reservation of three instead of six passes everything, because the reservation carries `sources + 2` spare tiles for the Link footings (ADR 0027) and that slack is at least three on every capture — so a three-slot narrowing is absorbed before it can collide. It is not an equivalent mutant (it lengthens the sweep's RCL4 pavement by five tiles) and it is not caught; closing it would need an assertion on the reserved set itself, which the Layout does not record. Sizing the reservation **above** the ceiling is genuinely equivalent — `allowanceOf` is flat there — and reds nothing, correctly.
- **The profile gate proves little here and says so.** Seven scenarios, before and after, no decision line moved and no hotspot entering or leaving. That is the expected result and it is not evidence: `profile.mjs` stocks every Storage at 200,000 and `furnitureFor(rcl)` furnishes each room's full allowance, so **no scenario stands an under-built room or a thin Storage** (#343), and none of them levels a room up at all. A change whose entire subject is what a level-up does to a road plan has no scenario that can see it. What sees it is the capture sweep, which is where the numbers above come from.
