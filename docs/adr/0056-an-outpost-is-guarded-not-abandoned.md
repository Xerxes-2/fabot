# An outpost is guarded and not abandoned: a melee guard cast on contact, and a stand-down clocked to the invader's own life

> **Amended by #272** on decision 1's count rule, and on nothing else it decides. Written as "2 when the raid's healing per tick is at least **the standing guards'** damage per tick", the number was not monotone in the raid: one guard standing said 2 and the second one arriving said 1, so the escalation cancelled itself — the reinforcement the row had just bought was `CapacityFull`-evicted on arrival, onto a [[flee]] whose safe set is that same room, where it stood for its whole life adding the damage that held the count at 1. A stable state, not an oscillation, and 750 energy for a body that never issues an `AttackCreep`, on exactly the two-healer raid this decision buys the second guard for. **So the count reads the raid and never our own answer to it**: healing stays `Engine.healPower × HEAL parts` over that room's hostiles, and the damage it is measured against is **one `guardPattern` block** — `Engine.attackPower × ATTACK + Engine.rangedAttackPower × RANGED_ATTACK` over `guardPattern.Block`, never over the bodies standing there. Never a living body, for the reason ADR 0042 stopped reading the living Anchor's (#208): a quota priced off a body that stands moves when that body dies. And one *block* rather than the whole body this bank would cast, because that body grows with the bank while the row's `Living` counts the body that is standing — a survivor cast at a smaller bank would then veto its own reinforcement, and this decision's own two-healer case would be out of reach at every bank above 1,300. Nothing about the guards already sent enters the number, so the row hires the second guard and the Task's cap admits it, and neither can retract while the raid is unchanged. The escalation therefore arrives on the tick the raid is **seen** rather than an oven after the first body landed: a two-healer raid is 1,500 energy of body bought at once, which is the direction decision 4's two-cast bound and the [[stand-down]] behind it are there to hold. **The rule now prices the raid's healing against what a guard *is*, not against what our guards are *doing*** — which is also what makes it survive #270, a disarmed guard no longer dragging the count down because the count never counted it. The worked example below is unchanged and now holds at every bank: 90 against an unboosted `smallHealer`'s 60 stays at 1, and a second healer's 120 buys the second body, at 800 and at the live 1,800 alike — the count is monotone in the raid and constant in the bank. The cap of 2, the per-outpost sum, the no-decay clause and everything decisions 2–5 decide are untouched.

ADR 0043 decided an [[outpost]] withdraws, said in as many words that it "decides no fighting", and named the reason: nothing in the Task pipeline read `Snapshot.Hostiles` at all, and "a bot whose creeps do not flee at home has no business opening a front fifty tiles away". ADR 0033 closed that first clause — creeps flee — so the deferral has run out, and `docs/research/remote-invader-defence.md` is the reading for it. Three of its findings decide this ADR between them.

**Nine raids in ten are one melee invader that walks into range 1 by itself.** `createRaid` leaves `count = 1` unless `Math.random() > 0.9`; the first creep of any raid outside a sector centre is `smallMelee`, `[TOUGH,TOUGH,MOVE,MOVE,MOVE,MOVE,RANGED_ATTACK,WORK,ATTACK,MOVE]` — 1,000 hits, 40 damage at range 1 — and because it carries an ATTACK part `findAttack.js` never routes it into `flee.js`: it chases the closest hostile creep by path. **A stationary defender wins by standing still**, and the interposition this ADR was asked to compute is the engine's own rule and not a tile set of ours.

**Melee is 4.6× the damage per energy of ranged**, counting the MOVE that carries the part: ATTACK 30 damage for 130 energy is 0.231 dmg/e against RANGED_ATTACK's 0.050, and HEAL is 0.040 hp/e. Two independent pairs of mature bots converged on the same two bodies at this budget — Overmind and bonzAI on `[TOUGH, ATTACK×3, MOVE×5, HEAL]` at 750 energy, Winsley and TooAngel on `[RANGED_ATTACK, MOVE, MOVE, HEAL]` at 500 — and the ranged one loses on arithmetic: 1,500 energy of it deals 30 damage a tick and **cannot out-damage even an unboosted `smallHealer`'s 60**.

**And the guard is an order of magnitude cheaper than the withdrawal.** At W12S27 (+6.58 e/tick, a raid every ~10,000 ticks) ADR 0043's 2,500-tick fallback forgoes 16,450 energy against a 1,500-energy guard — **11×**. At W13S29 (+14.24 e/tick, every ~5,000) it forgoes 35,600 — half of everything the outpost earns between raids — against 750, **47×**. Reserving the exit neighbours to shut `checkExit` costs ~35,000 energy a cycle where it works at all, and it can never work for W11S28, W12S29 or W13S29, each of which borders a highway that has no controller to test.

We decided **a declared outpost holding an armed hostile is garrisoned rather than emptied**: one melee guard, cast when the hostile is seen and not before, and ADR 0043's gate kept behind it as the answer for the cases a guard does not win.

**1. The guard row.** A sixth [[body pattern]], `guard`, block `[Tough; Attack; Attack; Attack; Move; Move; Move; Move; Move; Heal]` — 750 energy, ten parts, 1,000 hits, 90 damage and 12 self-heal a tick, one Move per non-Move part so ADR 0003's parity holds with no exemption of the [[anchor]]'s kind. Sized by `wholeBlockBodyFor`, the rule the hauler and [[reserver]] rows already share: as many whole blocks as the bank buys, never below one, capped at five by `Engine.maxBodyParts`. `List.distinct` keeps the block's order, so a two-block body is `[T;T; A×6; M×10; H;H]` — TOUGH eats first and HEAL dies last, which is what the community's ordering is for. What the banks cast: **300** — 750 is unaffordable and the row **yields** (ADR 0050), so a colony this small has the [[stand-down]] and nothing else; **800** and **1,300** — one block, 750 e, 90/12/1,000; **1,800** — two blocks, 1,500 e, 180/24/2,000; **2,300** — three blocks, 2,250 e, 270/36/3,000. Its [[body class]] is a new one, **Fighter** (an Attack part), first in the ordered ladder, because a guard has no Work part and would otherwise fall into `Carrier` beside the haulers and be matched to their work. In the casting cascade it stands **second, behind the [[supply floor]] and in front of the reserver**: it is zero for the whole of a colony's ordinary life, and on the ticks it is not, every tick of delay is an [[anchor]] and a [[hauler unit]] dying in a room a reserver would walk into next.

Its quota is **0 normally; 1 per declared outpost in which a [[threat]] stands this tick; 2 when the raid's healing per tick is at least the standing guards' damage per tick**, capped at two and summed over the outposts. The arithmetic is the engine's, over the parts the projection already carries: healing is `Engine.healPower (12) × HEAL parts` over the hostiles in that room, damage is `Engine.attackPower (30) × ATTACK parts + Engine.rangedAttackPower (10) × RANGED_ATTACK parts` over our guards standing there. So one 750-e guard's 90 stands against an unboosted `smallHealer`'s 60 and the count stays at one; a second healer's 120 casts the second guard. `Engine.rangedHealPower (4)` is not read — a healer beside its own invader heals at 12, and pricing the raid at its cheapest is the wrong direction for a count that decides whether we fight at all.

The quota is a per-tick fact read off vision, and vision in a guarded outpost is the guard. It holds while an armed hostile is seen standing there and falls to 0 the tick the room is clear, and it **does not decay** in between: the cast is 1,500 ticks of body and a raid is 1,500 ticks, so one cast covers one raid by construction. A guard that outlives its raid is pooled no Guard, is applicable to nothing else (no Work, no Carry — every existing part gate already refuses it), and stands idle until it expires. That is accepted rather than recycled: it counts in the row's `Living`, so the next raid inside its life casts nothing and waits no ~30 ticks of oven, and the research prices a permanently standing guard at 3.5% of W13S29's income — an idle one costs strictly less.

**2. The Guard Task.** `Guard of roomName`, `guard:W12S27`, one per outpost holding a Threat — keyed on the **room** and not the hostile, which is ADR 0054's split applied where it was learnt: the Planner names a place, the [[emitter]] names the target at arrival. Keyed on the hostile, the 2% multi-creep raid would pool five Tasks and re-match the guard between them every time one moved.

- Its [[work area]] is the walkable range-1 ring of every Threat standing in that room. Range 1 and not two: 30 per ATTACK part is paid there and nothing is paid at range 2, and this body carries no RANGED_ATTACK by decision. Derived colony-level once a tick off the same `Threats` the [[reach]] is, filed by room (#138), and touching no [[atlas]] memo — ADR 0033's precedent for [[flee]]'s safe set, in the other direction.
- Its applicability is **a Fighter body**, and its [[capacity]] is `Fighter -> the room's quota (1 or 2)`, every other class **0**. One number, computed once in `planPool` and read as both the row's quota and the Task's cap, which is ADR 0052 decision 6: the Matcher recognises no Task kind and only counts holders against a cap the Planner set.
- Its [[priority]] is the **Safety** tier's, beside Flee, with no rung of its own — the two Tasks in that tier are disjoint by body class, so nothing ever asks how they order. Safety for Flee's own reason: no other work matters while a creep is being killed.
- The Emitter issues `AttackCreep` at the chosen Threat when it stands within range 1, `HealCreep` on the guard itself every tick (a separate intent kind, so the two do not compete), and **no movement of its own** — the mover walks it into the Work Area like any other Task, which is ADR 0033's whole argument for making a fight a Task instead of a reflex. The chosen Threat is **the one nearest a [[post]] of that room**, ties by id: that is "between the invader and the [[anchor]]" said in the repo's vocabulary, and it is the only place the Anchor enters the geometry. With no Post standing, the nearest Threat to the guard. Chat bubble ⚔️.

**3. A guard is exempt from Flee and from the Reach gate.** Two clauses, both read off the class rather than off the row's name. **Flee is inapplicable to a Fighter** — ADR 0033's [[work-heavy body]] clause restated for a different reason: not that it cannot run, but that a body with an ATTACK part does not run from its own target, which is the same part test `findAttack.js` splits on. **The Reach subtraction does not apply to a Task in the Safety tier.** ADR 0033 subtracts every Reach from every Work Area at applicability; a Guard's area is *made of* Reach tiles by construction, so unamended the gate empties it and the Task is inapplicable to everyone on every tick it exists. The exemption is written against the tier and not against the kind — Flee is already exempt in effect, its area being the safe set — so it generalises ADR 0033 by one clause rather than carving out this row. Everything else is untouched: [[hauler unit]]s still flee, Anchors still do not (ADR 0033 as amended), and the spawn hold still refuses to cast into a hot doorstep — which correctly means a raid **at home** casts no guard and is the Keep's business (ADR 0034), not this row's.

**4. ADR 0043's stand-down is re-clocked, and it stands behind the guard rather than beside it.** The deadline list gains a reading ahead of the fallback: **a hostile creep in a room with no owner is clocked to its own `ticksToLive`** — `now + the largest ticksToLive over the Threats in the room`. That number is ungated on hostile creeps in the engine (`creeps.js`: `ticksToLive: (o) => o.ageTime ? o.ageTime - runtimeData.time : undefined`) and bounded by `CREEP_LIFE_TIME` at 1,500, and it is precisely the "deadline read off the threat" ADR 0043 asked for and did not have for this case: an invader in an unowned room never suicides — `findAttack.js`'s suicide branch requires `roomController.user` — so it stands there for exactly that long. `TicksToLive` joins `HostileInfo` under ADR 0007's growth rule, on the tick a decision reads it. **`Tuning.StandDownFallback`'s 2,500 is kept and narrowed to invader cores and to a threat with no readable clock**, unchanged in every other respect including #136's floor under the reservation branch: a core spawns nothing, never leaves, and wants a dismantler.

And the load-bearing clause: **an outpost does not stand down while this colony has a guard standing or casting for it — the guard is why.** The two are answers to one fact and the colony must pick one of them, because a stand-down takes the room out of the [[spatial projection]], which would take the Guard, its Work Area and the guard's own tile with it and leave a body we have paid for standing in a room we cannot see. So on the creep branch the gate fires only where the row cannot answer: the bank cannot afford a block, or the episode has already cast **two** guards for this raid and is being asked for a third. That bound is the multi-creep case's exit — the guards die, the room stands down to the invader's own remaining life, and the colony has spent 1,500 energy rather than an unbounded stream of it.

**5. The [[raid log]] records the fight.** ADR 0028's outpost family gains a **garrison** list on the episode, one row per guard: the tick it was cast, its name, the hostile ids standing in the room on that tick, and an outcome — `HostileDied` when the roster row leaves the room with our guard still standing, `GuardDied` when the guard's name leaves `Game.creeps` while a hostile stood there (already a Loss under ADR 0028's rule; the garrison row is the link to it), `HostileExpired` when the hostile leaves on or after the tick its own `ticksToLive` named, and `Standing` while the episode is open. One field on an existing leaf, no new channel, printed by `observe.mjs outposts` beside the deadline it already names. It is also the record decision 1's count rule will be revised against, which is ADR 0028's own rule: measure before designing against it.

## Considered Options

- **Pre-spawn off the harvested-energy thresholds** (bonzAI's `InvaderGuru`, Overmind's `isInvasionLikely`: 65k/75k/90k with a 20,000-tick staleness guard) — rejected here and filed as its own question. It needs the server's `invaderHarvested` counter reconstructed client-side by sampling `source.energyCapacity - source.energy` on the tick `ticksToRegeneration === 1`, which is a memoised time series this colony keeps nothing like; and it buys ~30 ticks of oven against a threat that walks toward us for forty tiles.
- **The ranged "blinky" body**, `[RANGED_ATTACK, MOVE, MOVE, HEAL]` (Winsley, TooAngel) — rejected on arithmetic: at 1,500 energy it deals 30 damage a tick and cannot out-damage an unboosted `smallHealer`'s 60, so against the one raid shape that matters it is a permanent stalemate, and against the lone melee it wins in 50 ticks where the melee unit wins in 12.
- **Reserving the exit neighbours as the primary defence** — rejected: ~35,000 energy per raid cycle at W12S27 against 1,500 for a guard, 23× worse, and impossible for the three of five candidates that border a highway, whose neighbours have no controller for `checkExit` to test. Worth having as a *side effect* of declaring the candidates, never as the move.
- **Invader bait** — rejected as folklore: zero implementations across six mature bots. The nearest real mechanism points the other way, and weakly — `genInvaders` skips a room already holding a `user: '2'` creep, so a live invader postpones the next raid, but the goal was re-rolled at spawn time already.
- **Abandoning for the invader's TTL without fighting** (The International's `abandonRemote`, and ADR 0043 as it stands) — rejected as the *primary* answer and kept as the fallback: even re-clocked to 1,500 it forgoes 9,870 energy at W12S27 and 21,360 at W13S29, against 750 for the body that keeps the [[container]], the [[reservation]] and the Anchor where they are.
- **Ramparts or a tower in the outpost** — not an option at all, and confirmed in the engine rather than assumed: `CONTROLLER_STRUCTURES.rampart`, `.constructedWall` and `.tower` have no key `0`, and `checkControllerAvailability` reads `rcl = 0` for a controller that is merely reserved. **ADR 0034's answer for a threatened Post does not reach an outpost.** An outpost's Anchor has two options: die, or be defended by a creep.
- **A Guard keyed on the hostile id** — rejected above: correct for the 90% case, five Tasks and a re-match a tick for the 2%.
- **Computing an interposition tile set** — rejected: `findAttack.js` chases the closest hostile creep by path, so a guard standing on the invader's own ring *is* between it and everything behind it, by the engine's rule. A tile set of ours would be a second, weaker spelling of a fact we can read off primary source.

## Consequences

- **Four ADRs take a banner on the tickets that land the clauses amending them**: ADR 0033 (the Fighter's Flee exemption, the Safety tier's Reach exemption), ADR 0043 (the creep clock, the fallback's narrowing, and the gate standing behind the guard), ADR 0028 (the garrison rows) and ADR 0006 (a sixth row, and a fifth [[body class]] beside the four ADR 0052 decision 6 counts capacities over).
- **The count rule is a necessary condition and not a sufficient one, and the arithmetic says where it fails.** At a 1,300 bank one guard out-damages an unboosted healer — 90 against 60 — so the rule keeps the count at one, and the research's table says that fight is still lost: 30 net damage a tick, 34 ticks to the kill, 1,360 damage taken against 1,000 hits. The rule prices the raid's *healing* and not our *survival*. Accepted as decided, with the outcome recorded rather than predicted: decision 5's `GuardDied` rows are what a sharper rule — hits-to-kill compared both ways, Winsley's `score` — would be written against, and decision 4's two-cast bound is what stops the wrong answer costing more than one stand-down.
- **Boosts are invisible to the count, and half of the group raids carry one.** Each invader is boosted with probability 0.5 (T1 outside sector centres), so `heal→LO` doubles a healer to 120 hp/tick; the projection carries parts verbatim (ADR 0028) and no boost, so the rule under-counts that raid by exactly ×2 in roughly 3% of all raids. A `Boost` field on `HostileInfo` is ADR 0007's growth rule pointed at a decision that would read it, and it is filed rather than taken here: the stand-down behind the guard is what that case falls through to, and one field is cheaper to justify against real `GuardDied` rows than against this paragraph.
- **The 2% multi-creep raid is a bounded loss and not a new behaviour.** Two guards die, 1,500 energy is spent, the outpost stands down to the invader's remaining life, and the [[reservation]] absorbs it — `reserverBodyWithin` drives `ticksToEnd` to the 5,000 cap, and W12S27 measured 4,911 on 2026-09-07, so one hold of ≤1,500 ticks is inside the buffer where two consecutive 2,500-tick ones were not.
- **CPU is one more row in the cascade, one more Task kind in the pool, and one more colony-level derivation off `Threats`** — the range-1 ring of each Threat, one Chebyshev ring per hostile per tick, beside the Reach that is already built from the same list. It is unmeasurable on today's evidence and that is itself the finding: **no profile harness scenario carries a hostile**, so `Threats.Safe`, Flee and now the Guard are all paths `npm run profile` has never executed. A hostile in the `outpost` scenario is part of this work, not a follow-up.
- **The fixtures grow one hostile and one body.** DecideTests' outpost fixture gains a Threat with an ATTACK part standing in the outpost room, which is what pins the quota pairwise (none, one, and a healer's 120 against 90), the Work Area's ring, the capacity refusing every non-Fighter class, the Reach exemption (the Guard applicable where every other Task in that room is not), Flee's inapplicability to a Fighter beside its existing inapplicability to a work-heavy body, and the gate held open while a guard stands. The `bodyFor` pairwise table gains the five banks above. ObserveTests gain the garrison rows and their four outcomes.
- **#147 is decided by this ADR rather than merely touched by it.** Its fix — `threatened` reading the *target room's* own narrowed area less that room's Reach, where today it reads a cross-room area that is empty by construction — must be written **below the Safety tier**, or it lands as the bug this decision's exemption exists to prevent: a Guard is exactly "a Task whose whole Work Area lies inside a Reach", and it is meant to be.
- **#151 loses its worst case here and keeps its shape.** A guarded outpost keeps vision because the guard is standing in it, so the raid ticks are no longer the ticks the room blinks out; the anti-thrash keep it asks for is still owed to Build, Repair and Withdraw.
- **#165 is untouched and stays open.** `rivalHeld` is the clockless branch, a room another player holds; nothing here reads it and a guard is never the answer to a player's reserver.
- **This decision is for an invader creep and for nothing else.** An invader **core** is a structure, is not in the hostile list at all, derives no Reach, and pools no Guard — it keeps ADR 0043's clock and 2,500-tick fallback whole, and killing one is still its own ticket.

## Glossary additions

Proposed for `CONTEXT.md`, not written here:

- **Guard** — the sixth [[body pattern]] and the body it casts: `[Tough; Attack×3; Move×5; Heal]` repeated to the bank, cast when a [[threat]] is seen standing in a declared [[outpost]] and never before, and the one Task in the Safety tier that is not [[flee]]. _Avoid_: defender, bodyguard, soldier.
- **Fighter** — a fifth [[body class]] beside Heavy, Standing, Carrier and Light: a body with an ATTACK part, first in the ordered ladder. The one class Flee is inapplicable to for a reason other than slowness, and the only class a Guard's [[capacity]] answers for.
- **No entry for "garrison fight".** [[post]]'s entry already spends "garrison" on the [[anchor]] that stands on one, and a second sense of it in the same glossary would be the ambiguity the *Avoided terms* list exists to catch. What the fight needs is a name for the *record*, and that is the [[raid log]]'s new **garrison** field on an outpost episode — a list of rows, not a concept — so the existing entry gains a clause where a new term would have gone.
