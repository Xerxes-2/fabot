# Movement is an arbitrated intent, not an executor side effect

A creep that finished harvesting would upgrade the controller in place, squatting the mining Seat and blocking the next harvester — because standing tiles were represented nowhere: movement happened only as the Executor's `moveTo` fallback on `ERR_NOT_IN_RANGE`. We decided that no movement is ever issued outside a pure per-room Resolver: every creep's Move Intent (candidate standing tiles derived from its Task's Work Area, plus a priority reusing the task rank) is collected each tick and arbitrated once — priority descending, most-constrained first, swap when contested — following screeps-cartographer's semantics. The essential rule this buys: a creep with slack in its Work Area yields to a creep without.

## Considered Options

- **Seat reservation ledger** (The International style): assignment reserves concrete tiles, other tasks avoid them. Rejected as not root-cause — it models one contention (mining seats) instead of yielding in general; Seat *counting* survives as the Harvest capacity bound.
- **Push-on-block inside movement** (Overmind style): keep per-creep `moveTo`, shove blockers ad hoc. Rejected on TooAngel's evidence that pairwise push rules accrete badly.
- **Tick-end arbitration** (chosen): movement becomes a first-class Intent, same shape as the rest of the decision pipeline.

## Consequences

- Pathfinding moves into Core as a pure Dijkstra over the Snapshot's terrain projection — swamp cost is real on our common paths, so terrain is three-state (wall/swamp/plain, cost 1/5) from day one. We accept owning pathfinding in exchange for a fully testable decision chain; the engine's `PathFinder` is not used.
- `creep.moveTo` is forbidden everywhere; the Executor only replays resolved single-step moves (a creep may act and move in the same tick).
- Deferred, deliberately: container/static mining, construction-site eviction, path caching.
- **A principled global matching is not the push-on-block this decision rejected** (#216 R2b, #219). The Resolver's core is now a weighted augmenting search over the room's whole matching — the room starts as the identity, each intent in turn is searched for a chain of displacements ending on a free tile, and the chain is taken only when its net priority is positive, with a dead end dropped whole. What this decision rejected under "push-on-block inside movement" was Overmind's shape and TooAngel's evidence against it: ad hoc *pairwise* shove rules, accreting one special case at a time inside per-creep `moveTo`. This is the opposite arrangement and the stronger version of the one chosen here — one rule for the whole room, evaluated as a matching, still inside the pure Resolver and still returning Intents — and it subsumes the special cases the settle it replaced had grown: the swap needs none, and "most-constrained first" is what the search does by construction. What it adds that the settle could not promise is an **injective** answer: the settle's last resort put a creep back on its own tile after somebody else had claimed it, which is two creeps judged onto one tile and a `MoveCreep` the engine deletes in silence.
- **A creep with slack yields, and a creep with nowhere to go steps aside** (#219). The essential rule this decision bought is now the score's arithmetic: a creep pushed off a tile it merely stands on costs the chain nothing, and one pushed off the tile it asked for costs its rank's weight. Beside it, a Move Intent's candidates are a head and a tail rather than a single tile, so a traveller whose step is held by a body that cannot move sidesteps around it instead of standing there — the deadlock eight creeps sat in for ten minutes in W13S28's north corridor.
- **A body with no Task does not park on the working ground** (#241). The essential rule this decision bought is about a creep with slack yielding to one without, and it is silent on where a creep with no work at all should wait — so the answer was "wherever it happened to stop", which in W13S28's north pocket was the [[buffer]]'s own refill tiles. Two idle upgraders held two of the five for 190 ticks, and the hauler carrying the energy that would have given them work never got past the pocket's mouth: the bodies waiting on the store were standing where its feed had to stand. So an idle body's Move Intent heads the first step off ADR 0022's working ground — the Seats and the Upgrade Work Area, the tiles the colony works *from* — with its own tile in the tail behind that step, which leaves it parked where it stands when there is nowhere off the ground to go, when everywhere off it lies inside this tick's Reach (ADR 0033 — a [[work-heavy body]] has no Flee, and a step into an attacker is one nothing walks back), or when the way off is held by a body no intent in the fold can move: a fatigued one or another colony's, which is what actually stops an intent here and never the occupant's rank (ADR 0008, #220). The tail behind the head is ordered off the ground first for the reason the head exists at all: `arbitrate` re-houses a displaced body on the first free tile of its list, so a body shoved aside off the ground and put back onto it steps off again next tick, and two idle bodies trade the one tile off the ground for ever — the swap this ticket forbids, reintroduced by its own fix. It is the same judgement ADR 0022 makes about a tower or an extension, made about a body: what an idle one costs there is the tile.
