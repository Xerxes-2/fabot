# A row that cannot be afforded yields the tick, and a colony that cannot refill an extension hires the body that can

ADR 0006 made spawning a cascade of [[body pattern]] rows, each arriving with its own quota, and ADR 0021 fixed when a row is bought: **a body is sized to the bank's capacity and cast the tick the bank holds its cost.** Two ADRs later the head of that cascade became the [[reserver]] (ADR 0042), whose floor body costs 650 and whose live body costs 1,300. Nothing ever said what happens when the head row's price is more than the bank holds, and the implementation answered by accident: the cascade was an index into a list of gaps, so an unaffordable head row left every idle spawn asking for the head row again and **the whole tick cast nothing**.

On 2026-09-06 the live colony spent 1,235 ticks in that state (#203). An [[outpost]] raider the bot could not see (#201) killed its reservers, so the reserver row's gap opened and stayed open; the row wanted 1,300 and the bank held 361 against a capacity of 1,800; the cascade asked for a reserver, could not buy one, and stopped. Meanwhile the only two bodies left alive were [[anchor]]s — `6W/1C/1M`, a [[standing body]] and a [[work-heavy body]] at once, so [[refill]] and [[withdraw]] are both shut to them (ADR 0046, ADR 0016) — and 246,818 energy stood in the [[storage]] that no living body could reach. The bank did not creep upward either: the engine regenerates a spawn only while its room holds under 300, so 361 was a **fixed point**, not a slope.

**Two rules, and neither is sufficient alone.**

**1. A row that cannot be afforded yields the tick to the rows below it.** The cascade is asked in order — [[supply floor]], reserver, [[anchor]], hauler, [[upgrader]], [[worker unit]] — and a spawn casts the first body its bank can pay for; the row it filled is the one that leaves the tick's list. Priority is the order the rows are asked in and never a veto the head row holds over the rows behind it. Nothing is skipped permanently: the list is rebuilt from the quotas every tick, so the reserver the extensions could not pay for this tick is the head again the tick they can.

**2. A colony holding no body that can put energy into an extension casts one hauler in front of every row, sized from `bank.Available`.** This is ADR 0006's disaster fallback — *an empty colony can never refill extensions, so a capacity-sized body would wait forever; spawn a minimal worker unit from whatever is banked right now* — carried to the state that fallback cannot see. That fallback is gated on the colony holding **no creep at all**; #203's colony held two, and neither could refill anything. The argument is the fallback's own argument word for word: every row prices at `bank.Capacity`, extensions are filled by creeps, and a colony with nothing alive that can fill one is a colony for which every row is priced at a number the bank will never reach again.

**The gate is `Refill`'s conjunction beside `Withdraw`'s, and emphatically not "has a Carry part".** A body counts if it holds a Carry part, is not a [[standing body]] (ADR 0046) and is not a [[work-heavy body]] (ADR 0016) — the two gates read together, because a body that can deliver but never draw cannot reach the store the energy is standing in. `Refill`'s third conjunct, `Energy > 0`, is deliberately not read: that is a state a hauler passes through twice a trip and not a fact about its body. This is the whole load-bearing half of the rule. The Anchor that made #203 has one Carry part and one Move part; a floor gated on "no body with a Carry that can move" is a floor two Anchors hold down for ever, and the incident reproduces itself unchanged with the rule in place.

**And it is priced at `Available`, which no other row is.** The floor exists precisely because capacity pricing is unreachable, so pricing the floor at capacity would buy the 1,800-energy body that is what the colony cannot pay for. The hauler row's own sizing rule floors at one `[Carry;Carry;Move]` block, so 150 banked is enough; the ordinary affordability check still refuses to cast what is not there. The only other place in the module that prices from `Available` is ADR 0006's fallback, for this same reason.

## Considered Options

- **Rule 1 alone (the ticket's first half).** Rejected: it does not close the deadlock. At 361 the cascade falls through to an Anchor gap of zero and then to three rows priced at 1,800, 1,750 and 1,800, and still casts nothing. Falling through fixes "the head row blocks the rows behind it"; it cannot fix "every row behind it is priced at a bank that will never fill".
- **Rule 2 alone.** Rejected: it closes the deadlock but leaves the head row able to stop a tick for every other reason — an RCL step, a slipped reservation asking for a body a full bank cannot buy — with the rows below it standing idle and affordable.
- **Gate the floor on "no body with a Carry part".** Rejected: it is the ticket's own wording and it is wrong. The Anchor row's `6W/1C/1M` answers it, so on #203's own fleet the floor never arms.
- **Reserve energy for the hauler row — an extension floor the other rows may not spend below.** Rejected as a **non-goal**, not as a bad idea. Liveness does not need it: the floor arms on the tick the last refilling body dies, whatever the other rows did with the bank. What is left over is the *jitter* — a colony running on one minimal hauler while a 1,300 reserver is bought every few hundred ticks — and the root of that jitter is #201, an outpost raider that was invisible to Reach, Flee and the raid log alike and harvested reservers one after another. A reservation rule in `planSpawns` would mask that, and it is fixed at its own seam.
- **Let the floor fire only when the bank is short of its capacity.** Rejected: it re-opens the cheapest failure the incident showed. A colony with no carrier and a full bank would spend 1,300 on the reserver first and hire the carrier out of the remainder on the next tick, which is exactly the losing race the live colony ran — the manual hauler filled the bank to 1,900 and two reserver casts took 2,600 back out of it inside 64 ticks. The body a bank depends on is bought before the bodies that depend on the bank. **This is the one instruction of #203 the implementation overrides**: the ticket's amended first criterion asks for this reading in so many words — *`bank 361 1800`, two `6W/1C/1M`, nothing else → cast `[C;C;M]×2`; pairwise, change the bank to `1800 1800` → the ordinary cascade*. The first half is built and pinned; the pairwise is not, because at `1800/1800` that fleet still holds nothing that can refill an extension and the floor is armed by that absence alone. The pairwise the code does keep is the *fleet's* — one living hauler at the same full bank, and the head of the cascade is the reserver's again. Both readings are pinned in `supplyFloorTests`, so the override is a test and not only a paragraph.
- **Make the floor a quota (a hauler row minimum of one).** Rejected: it is not a fact about the ground the way every other row's quota is, and as a standing minimum it would hire a hauler into colonies that have no haul. It is a *floor*, armed by the absence of a capability and disarmed by one living body.

## Consequences

- **ADR 0006's law is untouched.** "A row arrives with its quota, never just a shape" still holds; this ADR adds a second sentence beside it — *a row that cannot be afforded yields the tick to the rows below it* — and one row that is a floor rather than a quota. ADR 0021's "sized to capacity, cast when the bank holds the cost" stands for every row but this one, which is sized from `Available` and states so.
- **ADR 0021's cast timing gains a stated failure mode.** Sizing every row at capacity means that when only one row is cheap enough for a broken bank, the colony's last energy goes to that row. At the live RCL5 bank the five rows price at 1,300 / **700** / 1,800 / 1,750 / 1,800, so the one row a half-empty bank can buy is the Anchor's — the one body that can neither Refill nor Withdraw. #203's colony spent its last 1,400 energy on two of them.
- **The reserver row's "cannot buy a reservation" guard loses its stated reason and keeps the rule.** `reserverClaimsOf` refuses the quota where `richestCapacity` cannot buy one block, and its comment justified that by the cascade stalling for ever behind an unfillable head row — which is the sentence rule 1 deletes. The guard stays, on the reason that survives: the row's quota is a colony fact and a colony whose bank can never be built up to 650 does not hold a reservation. It is not narrowed to `bank.Available`: that reading would make a quota — an addend of the [[workforce target]] and of its amortization — oscillate with the very spending it governs, dropping the target by one per [[outpost]] on the tick after each cast, and `richestCapacity` is one fold with four readers that must not disagree (ADR 0049, #161).
- **The floor is visible in the fleet, not in a log.** A colony that has just been rescued by it stands with one minimal hauler and whatever it had before; the row name in the creep's name says "hauler" like any other, since the floor casts from the hauler row and not from a row of its own.
- **The glossary gains `### Supply floor`**, beside `### Disaster fallback` whose argument it carries, and the [[workforce target]] and [[body pattern]] entries link it: it is the one term this decision adds to the project's vocabulary. ADR 0021 carries a banner for the sizing exception, as ADR 0006 does for the cascade's second sentence.
- **Test fixtures whose whole fleet is Anchors now arm the floor**, because such a colony genuinely cannot refill an extension — it is #203 one cast away. Several `DecideTests` fixtures were of that shape and gain one generalist so that they go on pinning the row they were written for (#203).
