# The logistics layer: containers, Posts, the hauler row, and an income-based workforce

> **Revised by ADR 0049** on the hauler quota's *granularity* and nothing else: "per source container, round-trip ticks × 10 ÷ carry capacity, rounded up" is now summed over every source [[container]] in the colony and rounded **once**, because one ceiling per container bought a whole body for each container's leftover fraction — eight haulers against a demand of 4.9 on the live colony. The distance-weighted share this ADR built is unchanged and so is the direction of the rounding; what makes the summed integer safe is #161's Withdraw capacity, which admits drawers at each container by that container's own stock, so "one hauler per container" is no longer what keeps the far source shipped — the cap is a capacity and not an order, and ADR 0049 says which is which. The flat quota this ADR rejected stays rejected.

> **Revised by ADR 0046** on one Considered Option: "a separate miner row beside the Anchor" was rejected because the Post had already generalized the seat, so "heavy-WORK body pinned to a high-value tile by its own travel cost" described both footings. That generalization does not reach the *controller* container this ADR builds. The tile beside the buffer is no Post and never becomes one — what pins a body there is one Task's Work Area — so the [[upgrader]] row is a second row after all: `11W/1C/11M` at RCL5 against the worker row's `9W/9C/9M`, standing where this ADR put the buffer for it. The Post's generalization stands everywhere it was made, over the *source* container and the Dual Seat.

> **Revised by ADR 0037**: the worker row's quota is rounded up, as this ADR's hauler quota always was — a floor drops a whole worker body's Work, and that granularity grows with RCL.

> **Revised by ADR 0022**: Seats are working ground and no longer reservable by the clustered ordering, so the source-container pick never has to dodge a reservation. **Revised by ADR 0023**: the Storage joins Withdraw's sources one tier below the source containers, pooled only while some other sink is hungry.

> **Revised by ADR 0020**: a Post is not merely the tile an Anchor is worth casting for — for a Work-heavy body it is the only tile Harvest's Work Area offers at a source that has one, and a creep acts only from inside its Work Area. Travel-cost pinning is now backed by geometry: nothing else on the source is a place such a body may stand or dig.

> **Revised by ADR 0016**: "travel cost arbitrating dig-vs-collect" holds only for bodies that are not Work-heavy — Withdraw is inapplicable to a body with more Work than Move, so travel-cost pinning no longer carries the whole weight of walking an Anchor to its Post. The layer itself stands unchanged.

Every worker walked its own energy from source to controller — half a loaded body's life spent on the road, WORK parts commuting instead of working. Live observation of W12S28 (RCL2, six identical `2W4C3M` workers) showed the seat-count workforce saturated and most creeps cycling harvest→walk→upgrade→walk; it also showed the Anchor row dead in any room without a Dual Seat, which this room is. We decided to build the logistics layer whole, since its parts are each other's preconditions: **containers enter the Layout** (a source container per source, on the seat nearest that source's trunk; a controller container on a work-area tile adjacent to a trunk — the buffer that lets upgraders work standing still); **the Anchor generalizes from Dual Seats to Posts** — a Post is a Dual Seat *or* the seat under a source container, one Anchor per Post, the body unchanged (the single Carry is kept on both footings so one sizing rule serves the row; on a container Post, harvest overflow falls into the container by engine rule); **one new Task, Withdraw** (take energy from a stocked container; feeding-tier beside Harvest, travel cost arbitrating dig-vs-collect), while the outflow reuses Refill with the controller container as one more surplus-tier target (ADR 0010's target layering); **a hauler row** joins the pattern table — [Carry;Carry;Move] at *road* parity, since a hauler's whole life is the trunk, with its quota the throughput arithmetic: per source container, round-trip ticks to the spawn (the canonical sink the trunks radiate from) × 10 e/tick ÷ carry capacity, rounded up; and **the workforce target trades its seat base for an income base** — Anchors = Posts, haulers = the throughput quota, workers = unallocated income ÷ one worker body's Work drain — because a source with a manned Post retires its other seats: one heavy body drains 10 e/tick alone, and counting seats after that is hiring for jobs that no longer exist. The layer switches on by presence, not by mode: Layout drops container sites, workers build them, the containers' appearance in the projection creates the Tasks, and generational turnover casts the new rows through the ordinary workforce gap. Season mechanics stay out of scope, but Withdraw/Refill are shaped over stores, not hard-coded to energy's name, so a future season resource is a new Task, not a new pattern.

## Considered Options

- **Source containers only, no controller buffer** — rejected: the throughput gain this layer exists for (Q2 of the design session: upgrade throughput) is realized at the controller end; without the buffer, upgraders still walk and only the harvest half improves.
- **A separate miner row beside the Anchor** — rejected: "heavy-WORK body pinned to a high-value tile by its own travel cost" already describes both footings; two near-identical rows would each need a quota rule and a reader would have to learn why they differ. The Post generalizes the seat, not the body.
- **A compound haul Task (withdraw+deliver)** — rejected, again (ADR 0006 rejected the compound for the Anchor): a filled hauler loses Withdraw applicability and rematches to Refill — the emergent alternation the pool already runs on, at zero new concepts.
- **Hauler at plain parity ([Carry;Move])** — rejected: a 50% capacity tax paid for off-road scenarios the hauler's own existence precondition (trunks) says shouldn't occur.
- **One hauler per container (flat quota)** — rejected: systematically under-ships the far source; the failure mode (container overflow, energy evaporating on the ground) is quiet. The arithmetic reuses the Atlas's travel-cost machinery the Matcher already runs on.
- **Patch the seat-based target (a Posted source contributes 1)** — rejected: the worker count would keep no causal link to actual demand — it would merely happen to be a plausible number. The income base gives every pattern row its own colony fact, per ADR 0006's rule that a row arrives with its quota or not at all.
- **Active retirement of generalists during the transition** — rejected: creeps live 1,500 ticks; the fleet re-shapes itself within a generation and a half through ordinary deaths, and a retirement state machine is persistent state this architecture spends heavily to avoid.

## Consequences

- The colony's economy becomes legible as three quotas hanging off three colony facts: Posts, container round-trips, surplus income. Tuning is arithmetic, not fiddling.
- The Anchor row comes alive in rooms without Dual Seats — W12S28 gains two Posts the tick its source containers stand.
- The interregnum (containers built, new rows not yet cast) is served by generalists at reduced efficiency, by design; nobody coordinates the transition.
- The hauler quota's canonical sink (spawn) is deliberately approximate: capacity planning, not routing. If containers visibly overflow, the anchor point is the first thing to revisit.
- Fatigue parity now has two named regimes — plain parity for the worker row, road parity for the hauler row — and ADR 0003's invariant narrows again; a reader must find the row's own declaration.
- Withdraw at feeding tier means an empty worker near a stocked controller container tops up in one tile instead of walking to a source — the buffer feeds both directions, which is the point.
- The retired seats mean the workforce shrinks per-source as Posts are manned while total throughput rises; dashboards (task observation CLI) will show fewer, more differentiated creeps.
- (Amended, #47.) "Harvest overflow falls into the container by engine rule" needs the applicability gate to know it: a full creep standing on a **built** source container (that source's own Seat — the container Post's footing) keeps Harvest, since the container underfoot is effectively the creep's capacity. Everywhere else — a bare Seat, a container *site*, a container off the source's Seats — the ordinary full-store rule stands. The widening is deliberately body-blind (ADR 0006): a full *worker* parked on the tile would also keep Harvest and camp as a de facto miner; travel-cost pinning and the workforce quotas, not the gate, are what make the tile an Anchor's home. A container on a Dual Seat gives a full creep both Harvest and Upgrade at cost 0 and matcher tier order decides — no special case.
