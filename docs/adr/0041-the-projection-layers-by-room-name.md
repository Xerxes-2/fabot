# The projection layers by room name; the Seam is a query and the flood stays in one room

ADR 0005 ended with a debt it named precisely: *"Deferred, deliberately: honest pricing of targets outside the projected room."* #83 is the ticket that spends it. This colony's economy is capped at two sources and twenty energy a tick, its Upgrade throughput now runs at 16.91 against a sustainable 17.47 (#99's re-measurement after #108), and the only way past that ceiling before a second owned room is to mine the neighbours. That means the decision layer has to price a source fifty tiles away in another room, and every geometry answer in the bot is single-room by construction.

We decided **the [[spatial projection]] stays one projection and gains a layer keyed by room name**. `SpatialInfo`'s four `Pos`-keyed or `Pos`-valued containers — `Terrain`, `Obstacles`, `Roads`, and the position tables — sit inside a `Map<roomName, _>`; the three id-keyed containers (`TargetKinds`, `Hits`, `Stores`) are untouched, because an object id is already unique across the world. The [[outpost]]'s Tasks join the **same** Task pool and the Matcher ranks them in the **same** order as the home room's. ADR 0004's totality carries the whole weight of a room we can only sometimes see: geometry the projection cannot place is unpriceable, never counts against a Task and never blocks an action — which is exactly what an [[outpost]] without vision is, entry by entry.

Three boundaries keep the change from spreading, and each is a decision in its own right.

**The [[seam]] is an Atlas query, not a tile in the projection.** `Snapshot.fs` builds the projection over `1..48` and drops the border rows (ADR 0036). That trim is correct and this decision makes it more correct: an exit tile is **not a standing tile** — the engine moves a creep that ends its tick on one into the neighbouring room. Admitting exit tiles as ordinary walkable ground would let the Matcher choose one as a [[seat]] or a standing candidate and have the creep teleported out from under its Task. So a Seam is a third kind of geometry beside the [[seat]] and the [[post]] — the tile that joins two rooms, never one to stand on — and it is answered from ADR 0031's per-room-name terrain memo, which holds the engine's full fifty-by-fifty grid, rather than from `SpatialInfo`.

**The flood stays in one room.** A cross-room [[walk]] is a minimum over the Seam band: `walk(creep → target) = min over seams s of [ walk_here(creep → s) + 1 + walk_there(s' → target) ]`, each leg a single-room flood on the existing memo. The far leg floods **from the target**, so one memo entry serves every creep in the colony rather than one per creep, which makes this cheaper than the multi-room alternative and not merely smaller. #81 measured `Atlas.castWalkTicks` at ~1.2 ms/tick against a 21.2 ms baseline with eight creeps and one room; #83's fleet is roughly sixteen creeps over three rooms, and the CPU limit on this season's shard is a flat 100 that does not grow with GCL. The Seam bands are 36 tiles north (W12S27) and 19 tiles west (W13S28) — a minimum over 36 additions, not 36 floods. ADR 0029's memo key `(tile, fatigue factor, pricing)` is unchanged, because no flood ever leaves its room.

**The set of [[outpost]]s is a constant, not a discovery.** A source's position needs vision; vision needs a creep there; a creep goes there because a Task exists; the Task exists because the source is in the projection. Every "the first creep to walk in writes it down" scheme has to answer what sends the first creep, and answering it means inventing scouting — a whole subsystem, persistent room intel, and staleness discounting, for a colony that has exactly two candidate neighbours already captured as fixtures under `tests/Core.Tests/rooms/` (ADR 0036). So the outpost room names and their sources are declared in code and moved by a human in a commit, precisely as ADR 0039 moves the Layout's horizon; vision is used only for what actually changes — reservation remaining, container and road hits, stores, hostiles — and those follow ADR 0004's per-entry absence when there is none.

## Considered Options

- **A second abstraction layer for remote state, leaving the projection alone.** Rejected: ADR 0005's three reasons for refusing two projections all hold here and get worse. The outpost's Harvest, Withdraw, Refill and Repair go into the same pool as the home room's, so the Matcher would have to ask two geometry sources and rank across them — the exact thing ADR 0004 spent an entire decision to eliminate.
- **One multi-room `PathFinder`-style flood gated by an allowed-room set** (Overmind, bonzAI, The International, screeps-cartographer — the community mainstream). Rejected on cost, not on correctness: it doubles the tiles per flood *and* multiplies the floods, pushing the tick from ~21 ms toward the 100 ms cap, and it buys optimality we do not need when every outpost is a direct neighbour with a wide exit. TooAngel's bot has run for years with every `PathFinder.search` pinned to `maxRooms: 1`, stitching per-room legs onto a route skeleton, which is the proof that this is a live option and not a compromise. It is recorded here because it is what the next reader will expect to find.
- **Give `Pos` a room field.** Rejected: every `Pos` in the colony would grow a field that is the same string in every home-room comparison, and structural comparison on `Pos` is already the projection's measured hot spot (ADR 0031). The outer `Map` puts the room dimension exactly where the room dimension is.
- **Admit the border rows into the projection with a movement penalty** (Quorum's exit-square weighting). Rejected: a large cost says "prefer not to", and the Matcher with no better candidate will still pick one and lose the creep to the neighbouring room. What is needed is a prohibition, and the prohibition already exists as ADR 0036's trim.
- **Discover outposts by scouting, with per-room-name intel in Memory** (every mature bot does this). Rejected for *this* colony, and the rejection is about scale: they run eleven outposts across many owned rooms and cannot enumerate them by hand; we run two, chosen by a human in an ADR, with the terrain already committed as fixtures.

## Consequences

- **The colony cannot grow a third outpost on its own.** That is the price of the constant, and it is a real one: adding one means a new ticket, a room-evaluation concept and a scouting behaviour. Nobody needs it yet — GCL stands at 517,525 of 1,000,000, so a second *owned* room arrives before a third outpost would.
- **Arbitrated movement (ADR 0001, ADR 0008) and the [[occupancy surcharge]] stay single-room, unchanged.** The Seam is where geometry and arbitration part company, and they part on the same line: screeps-cartographer, the most mature multi-room movement library in the community, likewise decomposes `reconcileTraffic` strictly per room and accepts that two creeps aiming at the same border tile from opposite sides are never checked against each other.
- **ADR 0031's terrain memo needs no change at all.** `Game.map.getRoomTerrain` works for any room in the world with no vision and never goes stale, so the terrain layer's marginal cost across rooms is zero — which ADR 0031's own Consequences already anticipated in as many words.
- **CPU is measured, not budgeted.** `npm run profile` gains an outpost scenario and the observe channel gains a per-tick CPU line. An explicit budget of the kind Winsley's bot keeps exists to *size a territory*, and this colony's territory is a constant — a governor with nothing to govern is a row arriving without its quota (ADR 0006). The condition to revisit is numeric rather than a feeling: **a mean tick above 50 ms, or any single tick above 80.** Guarding `Game.cpu.bucket` by skipping whole ticks is deliberately not decided here: it collides with the safe-mode reflex (ADR 0007, ADR 0015), which must be able to fire on the very tick a guard would skip, and that trade belongs with the defence work.
- The glossary gains [[outpost]] and [[seam]]; [[spatial projection]], [[atlas]] and [[walk]] each stop describing a single room.
- **The rejected "`Pos` with a room field" came back for the values that cross a boundary, and only those** (ADR 0052 decision 2, #216 R3). The rejection above is about the *grid*, and it stands there untouched: `Terrain`, `Obstacles`, `Roads`, the flood arrays, every Seat, Reach and Work-Area table are still keyed by a bare `Pos`, and the room is still chosen once by the outer `Map`. The tiles *handed between* those tables do carry a room, and comparing them costs a string: the four scenarios measure 1–4% more on a census tick and up to 4% on `pair`'s quiet one (#216 R3's landing entry in ADR 0052 records the numbers), which is the price the rejection above weighed — paid at a boundary crossed per creep per candidate rather than at every node of a grid, and two orders of magnitude under this ADR's own revisit trigger. What the rejection did not cover is a tile *handed between functions*, and that is where the cost it was weighed against turned out to be paid instead: this ADR patched each such site by carrying a room name **beside** the tile — `HostileInfo.RoomName` against `Pos`, `MoveIntent.Room` against its candidates, `Outpost.RoomName` against its sources, the `…In atlas room` query pairs — and each of those is two facts that can disagree, kept in step by whoever remembers. A phantom [[post]] (#191), a [[trunk]] drawn to another room's spawn, a raider at range 0 from home (#204) and a [[reach]] dug out of the wrong room's ground (#138) are all the same disagreement. `RoomPos` is that pairing written as one value, so the paragraphs above that read "a `Pos` carries no room" now describe `Pos` alone and are answered by the type at every boundary; the ADR's Consequences on movement, on the Seam and on the price crossing while standing and acting do not are unchanged, and are now stated as rules rather than as consequences of a set that could not say which room it held.
- **`RoomPos.range` is an option, and that is the load-bearing half** (#204). Two rooms' coordinate systems are not one metric space, so there is no distance across a border to answer with — every reader that answered one answered it by accident and answered "range 0". A reader measuring across rooms now has to say what it means by it.
