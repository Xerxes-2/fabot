# The upgrader row: a body that stands beside the buffer, hired out of surplus

> **Amended by #216 R5** on the question this ADR's Consequences left to the tracker — which body a **two-row** store divides by (#196): both, one per [[body class]]. The [[buffer]]'s Withdraw carries the generalists' 450-energy divisor for the Light and Carrier classes and the standing row's own fifty for the Standing one, counted against separate shares with no total over them — `Heavy` falls in neither share and needs none, because ADR 0016's gate refuses a [[work-heavy body]] a Withdraw outright, so the row hired to live at that store no longer competes with the generalists for a number derived from the generalists. The delivery gates this ADR decided are untouched.

> **Amended by #216 R4** on the quota's **divisor** in point 3 and on nothing else: `upgraderQuota = floor(surplus / (upgraderWork × upgradeDrainPerWork × CREEP_LIFE_TIME + bodyCost))`, where the divisor was the lifetime drink alone (#200). A row's quota is what the colony can pay for and what it pays for is the mouth *and* the body: at the RCL5 bank a surplus of 33,100 divided by a 16,500 drink hires two, and two of those bodies cost 36,400 to run and replace — the worker row's income term goes to zero and the colony has promised 3,300 more over a lifetime than its rocks bring in, about 2.2 energy a tick out of the [[storage]]. It is #195's double sale one order of magnitude smaller, in the band `[q·drain, q·(drain + bodyCost))`, a fortieth of a lifetime's drink wide. The rounding's direction, the gate and the cascade are unchanged, and the standing body's ratio is now `Tuning.StandingCarryPerWork` (ADR 0052 decision 5) rather than a module constant.

> **Amended by #206**: a standing body fetches from the buffer alone. Its Withdraw is applicable only to the controller's container and Pickup not at all — a pile and the Storage are intakes, but not at its feet, and the walk to either is the commute this ADR refuses. Live, an `11W/1C/11M` upgrader crossed a Seam for fifty energy the tick its buffer ran dry; dry, it now waits for the haulers (`NoneApplicable`). Build, Repair and Refill stay gated as written; Upgrade and Harvest stay open.

> **Amended by #195** on the quota's rounding in point 3 below and on nothing else: `upgraderQuota = floor(surplus / (upgraderWork × upgradeDrainPerWork))` where point 3 wrote `ceil`, and nothing else in that expression moves. Two rows are hired out of one surplus and only one of them may round up — ADR 0037 licenses an oversell bounded by *one body's* lifetime drain, and two roundings against the same number sell that bound twice — so the rounding stays with the smaller body and the remainder this row leaves passes to the worker row, whose own division still rounds up. The gate (a built controller container, and a bank whose own cast is a standing body), the worker row's floor and the casting cascade are unchanged. The argument is the last two Consequences bullets.

> **Amended by #205** on one exception to the standing body's [[build]] gate in point 2 below, and on nothing else: a [[container]] **construction site** standing on the creep's own [[post]], under its own feet, is applicable to it whatever its shape. The gate's argument is a walk — "one tick spent delivering for every tick of the walk out and the walk back, with the Work idle at both ends" — and there is no walk here. The body digs the source beside it and spends the fifty it dug into the progress it is standing on, twelve a tick, which is how an [[outpost]]'s container was raised before this rule and ADR 0045 closed that door between them. Everything the gate was written for stands: a delivery this body would have to *travel* to is still forbidden, in the [[home room]] and across a [[seam]] alike, and the [[upgrader]] row beside its buffer is untouched. The exception reads the tile and never the row (ADR 0006), so the [[worker unit]] standing on the same site takes the same Build it always took.

ADR 0012 put a container at the controller so that "upgraders work standing still", and then the body never followed: the [[worker unit]] is the only row that has ever spent the surplus, and at RCL5's 1,800 bank it is `9W/9C/9M` — eighteen of its twenty-seven parts paying for a commute, in exchange for nine Work. #99 measured that row spending most of its life upgrading, and since ADR 0042 it also crosses a [[seam]] to build and repair an [[outpost]]'s [[container]]. So the row cannot simply be made dense: a body with one Carry that walks fifty tiles delivers fifty energy. It needs a **second row**, and the reason ADR 0012 gave for refusing one — "a separate miner row beside the Anchor" was rejected because the [[post]] had already generalized the seat, so "heavy-WORK body pinned to a high-value tile by its own travel cost" described both footings — does not reach this case. The tile beside the buffer is not a Post and never becomes one; what pins a body there is the [[work area]] of one Task, and "a heavy body pinned to a Post" is exactly the description that does *not* fit an upgrader.

**The decision.**

1. **A new row, `upgrader`**, arriving with a sizing rule of its own (ADR 0006): one Carry, and every part slot the rest of the capacity affords spent on Work/Move **pairs** — `W = M = floor((capacity − 50) / 150)`, never below one pair. At 1,800 that is `11W/1C/11M` for 1,700 energy: eleven Work where the generalist buys nine, twenty-two per cent more upgrade out of the same bank. The pairing is not decoration — it holds the row at `Work = Move`, inside ADR 0016's gate, so the row may [[withdraw]] from the buffer it stands beside (ADR 0019 asks it for the Work part it obviously has). The single Carry is one draw's worth of store, which is all a body that never walks anywhere needs.

2. **The standing body.** A body carrying fewer than one Carry per four Work — `Carry × 4 < Work` — is a **standing body**, and [[build]], [[repair]] and [[refill]] are **inapplicable** to one. Each of the three is a delivery, and a delivery of fifty energy by a body holding eleven Work is a commute: one tick spent delivering for every tick of the walk out and the walk back, with the Work idle at both ends. Every other Task is left exactly as its own gates already had it — Upgrade, Withdraw and Harvest, which are the working life this row was shaped for, and [[pickup]] too, which stays open because a pile is an intake and not a delivery. "Untouched" is not "applicable": Withdraw stays shut to a [[work-heavy body]] by ADR 0016's own gate, so of the two standing bodies below only the upgrader draws. The rule reads parts and never a row name (ADR 0006), so it catches the [[anchor]]'s `6W/1C/1M` as well, where it merely writes down what a rank-0 Harvest at a Post was already doing wherever a Harvest is in the pool at all; and it misses the [[hauler unit]] by arithmetic rather than by exception, `Carry × 4 < 0` being false at every size. The [[worker unit]] is outside it by its own [[fatigue parity]], which buys one Carry per Work where this line is one per four.

3. **A quota out of surplus.** `upgraderQuota = ceil(surplus / (upgraderWork × upgradeDrainPerWork))`, **non-zero only while a built controller container stands in the room**: the buffer is this row's working ground — the [[work area]] of one Task, and never a Post, as the background above says — so a room with no buffer standing hires none and the worker row commutes as it always did. That is the only gate stated here: the container plan places the buffer under no level gate of its own, so "no buffer yet" is a fact about the room and not about RCL, and the bank floor below is #187's to settle. The worker row's income term becomes the surplus the upgraders have not eaten, with a **floor**: two while anything is in the Build or Repair pool, one otherwise. The casting cascade gains one step: [[reserver]] → Anchor → hauler → **upgrader** → worker.

4. **Unchanged**: ADR 0016's gate, ADR 0019, the Matcher's key and its tiers. An upgrader and a worker compete for Upgrade on [[travel cost]] like any two bodies, and the one standing beside the buffer wins.

## Considered Options

- **② Make the worker row denser instead** — one row, sized `11W/1C/11M`, no new concept. Rejected on the premise this ADR is written under: the worker row crosses a Seam to build an outpost's container and to repair the road it walks (ADR 0042, #157), and a one-Carry body arriving there delivers fifty energy per fifty-tile trip. The commuting body and the standing body want opposite shapes, which is what makes them two rows rather than one number.
- **③ Exempt a controller-container body from ADR 0016 and cast `14W/1C/7M`** — 55% more Work for the same energy, by dropping the Move parts a body that stands still does not use. **Deferred, not rejected.** ADR 0016 says in as many words "revisit if a Dual-Seat room shows the loss", and the honest order is to measure ① before spending that ADR: option ③ is worth three Work per body over ①, and it costs the exemption plus a slower walk out of the spawn for a body that must still reach the buffer once.
- **Widen the Matcher instead of the applicability gate** — a tier or a travel-cost penalty that makes a delivery unattractive to a dense body rather than forbidden. Rejected for the reason ADR 0045 records against weighted exit tiles: a price says "prefer not to", and the delivery a standing body must not take is usually the one *closest* to it — the site under its feet at the buffer. A prohibition is what is needed, and `applicable` is where this codebase keeps prohibitions.
- **A named role on the creep** — "upgraders don't build". Rejected by ADR 0006 outright: what a creep can do is read off what it is made of, and a rule keyed on the row name in a creep's name would be wrong for every body the colony inherits, resizes or meets.
- **Gate on the row rather than on the ratio** — `pattern.Name = "upgrader"`. The same rejection, one step subtler: `patternOf` would then have to be right before `applicable` could be, and the Anchor's `6W/1C/1M` — a body whose whole life this rule describes — would fall outside it.

## Consequences

- **Upgrade throughput rises about 22% at RCL5** for the same spawn energy, and the colony runs one to two fewer creeps a tick (CPU, and spawn time that stops being spent on Carry parts).
- **A new part predicate joins the vocabulary** — the standing body — beside the [[work-heavy body]] ADR 0016 introduced. Two comparative gates now read a body's shape rather than its parts' presence, and the glossary carries both.
- **The pattern table grows a row and `bodyFor` a branch**, which #155's table-walking gate requires to come with a sizing rule; and `workforceTarget` grows an amortization term.
- **The row lands ahead of its quota, once.** ADR 0006's law is that a row arrives with the colony fact that says when it is cast, and this one arrives across two tickets: #186 lands the row, the sizing rule, the applicability gate and this file, and #187 lands the quota, the worker row's floor, and the cascade. Between them `planSpawns` computes no upgrader gap, so the colony casts none — the table's promise that every body it casts is a row here holds throughout; it is the converse that is briefly untrue. `patternOf` reads the row from the moment it is declared, so a body of the shape this rule describes is priced off its own row rather than the generalist's (ADR 0026).
- **The row is its own standing body only from an 800 bank**, because the sizing rule is one Carry against `floor((capacity − 50) / 150)` Work and the predicate wants four Work per Carry: five pairs is where the two meet. Below it — `3W/1C/3M` at the RCL2 bank of 550 — the row's own cast is *not* a standing body, keeps all three deliveries and is read back to the generalist. That is the ratio doing what it was written to do rather than a hole in it: three Work against a fifty-energy load is not yet a commute. It is #187's to decide whether the quota hires under that bank at all, and this ADR states the floor rather than assuming a level gate the container plan does not have.
- **The Anchor row's behaviour does not move**, and that is a claim about the ranks and not about the gate: Harvest is feeding-tier and its Post is where travel cost already pinned the body, so the gate only decides what an Anchor does in a pool with no Harvest in it at all. That pool is reachable — a full Anchor whose Post has no standing container to catch its overflow — and there the gate is not free: the body takes a pooled Upgrade at any distance in place of a nearer Refill it used to take on the feeding tier, and where no Upgrade is pooled at all it holds no Task that tick rather than repairing the road at its feet. Accepted here, and pinned by a test rather than left to be discovered: an Anchor that cannot dig is not a body the colony should be routing work through.
- **The buffer's Withdraw cap is still divided by the [[worker unit]]'s load.** `taskCapacities` sizes a store's concurrent drawers off the row that draws from it, and until #187 hires an upgrader that row is still the generalist. The tick the quota lands, a 900-energy buffer admits two drawers where the row that actually drinks from it carries fifty, so the divisor is #187's to carry over with the quota.
- **Nothing refills the buffer that also upgrades from it.** The buffer's own Refill is one tier below every surplus Task (ADR 0012) and is now inapplicable to the row that drinks from it, which is the shape ADR 0012 asked for out loud — "the buffer is filled by bodies with no surplus work of their own", the hauler row's whole life.
- **ADR 0012's rejection of "a separate miner row beside the Anchor" is partly overridden**, and the banner on that file says so. The generalization it rested on — that a Post describes both footings — is what does not reach the buffer: a body beside the buffer is pinned by a Work Area, not by a Post.
- **(Amended, #187.) The bank floor this ADR leaves open is the row's own cast: the quota hires nothing at a bank where that cast is not a standing body.** No level gate — the container plan places the buffer under none, so "a buffer stands" is still the whole of the *room's* half of the condition — but a bank floor there must be, and it is not a number. The row is counted by `patternOf`, off the parts (ADR 0006), so under the 800 bank, where the sizing rule buys `3W/1C/3M` and the ratio reads it back to the generalist, a hired row would be a row the colony cannot count: `upgraderGap` stays at the full quota every tick and the cascade casts a fresh `3W/1C/3M` "upgrader" forever, ahead of the whole-fleet deficit that is the only thing gating the generalist row. That is a spawn loop and not a cheap mistake, and it is reachable — RCL1 and RCL2 after a respawn, and routinely once a second colony starts under RCL3 (#192). So the gate is written as the predicate over the cast (`isStandingCast (bodyFor upgraderPattern capacity)`) rather than as the number 800: it is then kept true *by* the sizing rule and the ratio instead of against them, and it says the one thing that has to hold — a row hires only where it can count what it hired. The band loses nothing the row exists for: three Work against a fifty-energy load is not yet a commute, so the generalist row buys the same body at the same price out of the same surplus there.
- **(Amended, #187.) The worker row's floor sits on that row's whole share of the target, not on its income term alone.** Both addends — the unposted [[seat]] crews and the income mouths — hire the same body from the same row, so a colony already running three seat crews has three bodies that may build, and a floor read off the income term alone hires a fourth against a job that does not exist. The floor is what the *decision* above says it is (two with a Build or Repair in the pool, one otherwise); where it is applied is this amendment, and it is what keeps a colony whose sources are all unposted hiring exactly the seat crews it always did.
- **(Amended, #187.) The worker row is at that floor whenever a buffer stands, and its income term is dead there.** The upgrader quota rounds up, so it is hired for at least the whole surplus and the remainder the worker row divides is always at or under zero — the row's amortization term, which this ADR asks `workforceTarget` to grow, therefore cannot move the target at today's rounding either. Both are written down all the same, because the sum is the rule; a quota with a cap or a ceiling on it (option ③ above, or a bank too poor for the row) leaves a remainder for them to bite into.
- **(Amended, #187.) The buffer's Withdraw cap was *not* carried over to the upgrader's load**, and the bullet above is superseded rather than satisfied. The premise it rests on — that the row drawing from the buffer is now the upgrader — is only half true after the quota lands: the worker row's floor keeps one or two generalists in the colony and ADR 0019 admits them to the buffer exactly as before, so the store has two rows of drawers with a 24× difference in load. Divided by the upgrader's fifty, a 900-energy buffer admits eighteen, which is no cap at all and re-opens for the generalists the pile-on #161 put the cap there to stop. Which body a two-row store divides by is a decision this ticket does not carry and it goes back to the tracker; `taskCapacities` keeps the generalist's load meanwhile, with the reading written beside it.
- **(Amended, #195.) The quota rounds *down*, and the remainder goes to the worker row, whose own division still rounds up.** The decision above wrote `ceil` on the strength of ADR 0037, and it took ADR 0037's precedent one row too far: what that ADR licenses is an oversell bounded by **one body's** lifetime drain, paid out of stock rather than out of income, and two rows dividing the same surplus and both rounding up sell that bound twice. Which of the two keeps the rounding is settled by the size of the body it oversells, because the body *is* the bound — and ADR 0037 argued its case on the 1–6 Work generalists of the day, while this row's cast at the live RCL5 bank is eleven Work. At that bank a surplus of 26,800 over a 16,500 drink is one and a half standing bodies: rounded up it hired two, promising 33,000 — twenty-two a tick against twenty of income, the difference drawn out of the [[storage]] every tick of both lives, which is the redundancy the user reported as an overcrowded colony. Rounded down it hires the one whole body the surplus pays for and hands the remaining 8,600 to the worker row, where ADR 0037's own rounding turns it into a nine-Work body — the bound that ADR argued for, and the body it argued about.
- **(Amended, #195.) The two amendments above about the worker row are superseded on their arithmetic and kept on their rule.** The income term is no longer dead beside a standing buffer: the quota rounding down leaves a real remainder, so the worker row is hired out of it and is at its **floor** only where that remainder does not reach one body — which is now a reachable colony rather than every colony, since the standing row's own replacement cost is charged against the remainder first (two standing bodies cost 3,400 of body on top of the 33,000 their Work drinks). The floor itself is unchanged, and so is where it is applied: the worker row's whole share of the target, seat crews included. The amortization term this ADR asks `workforceTarget` to grow stops being redundant for the same reason — a remainder is exactly what it was written to bite into.
