# The reactor is a declared errand, a keeper lair is terrain, and the programme is W15S28's

ADR 0057 was written on 2026-09-07 against `docs/research/thorium-reactor.md` at tick 202,041. Its **rules** are still the authority and were re-verified against `mod-season5` (`da59118`) on 2026-09-13; its **world** is gone. `docs/research/thorium-season-plan.md` is that re-read, at ticks 396,515–396,757, and it lists every number of its predecessor that has moved. Three things moved far enough to need a decision rather than a correction:

- **The room the programme runs from.** ADR 0057 assumes W12S28, which is **six** crossings from the reactor and outside `Tuning.MaxHops`. Its decision 4 answered that with a hand-written `Tuning.ReactorRoute` and set the test of its own workaround: *"landing #243 should delete `Tuning.ReactorRoute` and change no rule."* ADR 0058 and ADR 0059 landed the derived chain, so the constant need never be written — but only from a home that can reach the room in three.
- **The vocabulary.** The reactor's room has no controller, `Outpost.Controller` is mandatory and says in its own doc comment why, and `Atlas.routes` is only ever asked about rooms in the projection. A room we cannot declare is a room nothing walks to. `docs/research/third-colony.md` §4 put it plainly: *"'3 hop' 意味着的是'现有的 Seam-chain 定价模型能覆盖这段路'，不是'declare 一下就能送钍'。"*
- **The threat model on the way there.** ADR 0057 decision 5 says a route room *"carries terrain and our own creeps and no hostiles at all, so there is no Reach out there to be inside of."* #286 (`87374e2`) deliberately made that false — a seen transit room carries its hostiles, because they are *"the hostiles ADR 0033's Reach and Flee owe an answer about"* — and the chain to the reactor crosses W15S26, a Source Keeper room with four lairs and live keepers.

Two of the three were put to a human on 2026-09-13 and answered on the tickets (#300, #301); the third follows from the first two and from the measurements. ADR 0057 is accepted and is not edited; this successor records the new rules and every clause of ADR 0057 and ADR 0058 it replaces.

## The decision, in four parts

### 1. A controller-less remote goal is its own declaration kind: the **errand**

**Decision (user, 2026-09-13, #300):** not an optional `Controller` on `Outpost`. An `Errand` is a second declaration kind beside it — a room name and the one object in that room we must be able to name before we have ever seen it.

Making `Controller` optional is the smallest diff and the worst shape, and the reason is that every reader of an outpost asks a question that presumes a controller: the reservation (ADR 0042), the [[stand-down]] and its rival latch (ADR 0043, #165), the reserver row's per-declared-outpost quota, ADR 0056's guard. Each would have to learn to skip, and each is a place the skip can be forgotten. It would also put a room we mine nothing in on the list of rooms we mine, which is what `Outpost`'s own type is for. The narrowing this needs already exists in a weaker form: `ColonyView.transiting` (#286) knows how to carry a room as ground-and-no-work.

An errand is therefore **more than a [[transit room]] and less than an [[outpost]]**:

```
Errand = { RoomName: string; Target: string * RoomPos }
```

and it is declared **inside the colony that runs it**, beside `Colony.Outposts`, for ADR 0047's reason: an outpost is one home room's business and a room's name in that list is what makes it that colony's. The errand in force names W15S25 and the reactor's engine id and tile — the ids are the **engine's own**, as every declaration in this repo is, because a declaration written in readable names matches nothing on a live server and does it in silence (ADR 0041).

The three questions #300 says the decision has to settle, each answered and each with its reason:

**Terrain only, or the object as well? The object as well, and only the object the declaration names.** The errand room enters the [[spatial projection]] carrying terrain, a border ring, and the declared target laid under whatever vision answers — which is exactly the shape ADR 0041 gave an outpost's sources and controller, for exactly the same reason: absence read onto the declaration as well is what left ADR 0042's chain with no first step, because no Task could name the room and so nothing ever walked there to get the vision. A courier has to hold `Deliver of reactorId` before any body of ours has stood in W15S25. What does **not** wait on the declaration is everything that changes — the reactor's `store.T`, its `owner`, its `continuousWork` — which is read off the projection where there is vision and is absent entry by entry where there is none (ADR 0004). That per-entry absence is precisely why ADR 0057 decision 5's last clause survives untouched: **the re-claimer standing there is the colony's only vision of the room**, and the `reactor` leaf reads stale rather than zero when the relay gaps.

Two narrowings follow and are the whole of "less than an outpost". `#286`'s `transiting` filter does **not** reach an errand room — a declared room's vision is work, and the one target it names is the work — but nothing else in that room is: no furniture is laid there (`furnitureOf` reads a declaration that names none), no source or controller of it is ever pooled however much vision we pay for, and no row hires for it except the two the errand's own Tasks belong to. The failure #286 found live in W14S28 — a reserver hired against a controller no declaration names, an Anchor on a rock nobody declared, a container site built to 2,604/5,000 — is the failure this narrowing exists to prevent one room further out, where the room has no controller to hire against but does have three sources and an owner-less extractor.

**Every colony's `worldRooms`, or only the declaring one? Only the declaring one.** `Colony.roomsProjected` unions the errand room and `RoomName.transitBetween home errandRoom` into the colony's own scan set exactly as it unions an outpost's, and `World.worldRooms` picks the room up because a standing colony declares it — the same path, one declaration kind wider. W12S28 and W13S28 declare no errand and project nothing of it. That is not an economy, it is the rule: the reactor is five and six crossings from those two homes, outside `Tuning.MaxHops`, so a price into it from either is `None` and a room in their projection would be one every rule answers nothing about. That is #243's silent failure with a bigger body standing beside the spawn.

**And when `Atlas.routes` answers `[]`? Refuse it loudly, exactly as `Outpost` does.** `Errand.routable` beside `Outpost.routable`, asked in `World.scanOf` on the same tick and against the same `linked`; a refused errand leaves the scan set, so nothing is projected, pooled or hired for it; and the refusal is **named** on the colony's [[layout record]] beside the refused outposts, with the kind said, because "W15S25" appearing under a heading that reads *"declared outposts that do not border this home"* would be a second silent failure wearing the first one's clothes. A test over the live `Colony.declared` is red on a human's slip before it is deployed. Carrying an unreachable errand is strictly worse than carrying an unreachable outpost: an outpost with no chain wastes a reserver, and an errand with no chain wastes the whole programme, the errand's entire content being a walk.

What an errand is **not**, said once so no reader has to infer it: it is not a [[claim]] (ADR 0047 — that Task spends a GCL level and needs a takeable controller, and this room has none); it is not an [[outpost]] (ADR 0042 — nothing to reserve, no source we work); ADR 0056's guard does not reach it (a Guard is cast on a Threat standing in a declared *outpost*, and decision 2 below is the whole answer for the one class of hostile on this chain); ADR 0043's stand-down has nothing to withhold from it, no row hiring per errand on a per-tick fact; and ADR 0042's reserver quota counts declared outposts and is not widened.

### 2. A keeper lair is terrain, masked at a margin derived from the Reach

**Decision (user, 2026-09-13, #301):** option 1 — mask the declared keeper centres out of the walkable layer at `Tuning.KeeperMargin`. Not an exemption of the two new rows from [[flee]] (which contradicts ADR 0033 directly, and exempts a *row* where the honest subject is a *situation*), and not a narrowing of Flee's applicability by what a body can do about a threat (the most honest option and the one that touches the threat model for every body in the colony).

**The centres are the rocks and not only the lairs.** A keeper is pinned by `keepers/pretick.js` to within range 1 of its assigned **source or mineral**, not of its lair, and it attacks only at melee range 1 and ranged range 3. So the declared centres of a Source Keeper room are its **lairs, its sources and its mineral** — the three kinds of rock a keeper is ever found beside — which is what `thorium-reactor.md` §3 measured safe and what ADR 0057 decision 4 already wrote. #301's decision comment says "lair centres"; masking the lairs alone would leave the sources unmasked and the decision's own measurement does not cover that, so this ADR takes the wider reading the engine rule requires. W15S26's four lairs are at (35,11), (6,17), (5,36) and (42,39); its sources and mineral are read off the API once, like every other declared fact.

**The margin is derived from ADR 0033's Reach and not from the keeper's weapon, and it is 6.** ADR 0057 wrote 5 and 5 is the right number for **survival**: a keeper's longest reach is ranged 3, and a tile 6 from its rock is at least 5 from the keeper, which is out of range. It is the wrong number for the thing this decision exists to buy, because ADR 0033's Reach is `weapon + Tuning.ReachMargin` — 3 + 2 = **5** for a RANGED_ATTACK body, the two tiles bought for the hostile's next step and our own tick of lag. A tile at 5 from a keeper is *inside* that Reach, so at a margin of 5 a courier crossing W15S26 is a creep inside a Reach, Flee is applicable to it, and the whole decision buys nothing. The margin is therefore

```
KeeperMargin = keeper pin (1) + ranged weapon (3) + Tuning.ReachMargin (2) = 6
```

so that a walkable tile is at least 7 from every rock, at least 6 from every keeper, and **outside every keeper's Reach by construction**. Written as a derivation rather than as a digit, because the digit is only correct while `ReachMargin` is 2 and a human who moves one and not the other would re-open this silently.

**What that buys is that no rule has to learn about keepers.** With the mask at 6, every tile a keeper's Reach covers is a masked tile, so in a Source Keeper room we merely cross, the Reach and the walkable ground are disjoint: no Task's [[work area]] is subtracted to empty, no creep of ours ever stands inside a Reach, and **Flee is inapplicable out there by construction rather than by exemption**. ADR 0033 is untouched — no row exempted, no tier carved out, no part test invented. That is the whole of what this option wins over #301's options 2 and 3, and it is why it is worth the thing it costs.

**What it costs is that it edits a fact about the world before any rule sees it, which is what ADR 0004 usually refuses.** The [[spatial projection]] is meant to be raw data and every policy is meant to live above it. The exception is argued on the properties of this one class and not on convenience:

- **It is stationary.** The lairs, the sources and the mineral are fixed for the life of the server, and the keeper is pinned to one of them. There is no state to go stale.
- **It is known before the room is ever seen.** The centres are read off the API once and declared, exactly as an outpost's sources are, so the mask does not wait on vision and never blinks. ADR 0057's rejected option stands: deriving the margin from vision needs a creep in the room, which needs a Task, which needs the room in the projection.
- **It is geometry and not a reaction.** A [[reach]] is derived each tick from seen Threats, appears and vanishes with them, is a colony-level fact and is deliberately never a change to the map (ADR 0033). The keeper mask is the opposite on every one of those axes, and that difference **is** the test for the class: a hostile the map can answer before the tick begins is answered by the map; a hostile that has to be reacted to is ADR 0033's.
- **It costs zero extra steps on the measured route.** From W15S28's Thorium seat the route hugs the east edge of W15S26, 8+ tiles clear of the lair at (35,11), and the 5-tile margin costs nothing. *Unverified:* whether **6** also costs zero over the whole crossing was not measured — only 5 was, and only against one lair. It is an acceptance criterion on the ticket, not a claim here.

**The hostile list is untouched; the ground is.** This is the precise sense in which it **overrides #286's reasoning for this one class of hostile.** #286 keeps a seen transit room's hostiles because they are *"the hostiles ADR 0033's Reach and Flee owe an answer about"* — and that stays true of every hostile but this one. A keeper still enters `World.factsOf`'s `Hostiles` off `FIND_HOSTILE_CREEPS`, still derives a Reach, is still recorded by the [[raid log]] like any other. Nothing is filtered out of any list. What changes is that no walkable tile of ours ever lies in that Reach, so the answer Flee owes is already given by the ground. #286's rule is unchanged; what is overridden is the sentence that a *seen* keeper is therefore a threat the rows must be taught to handle.

**The failure mode, stated rather than discovered.** If a keeper ever left range 1 of its rock, the mask would be silent about it, a courier would walk into it and would not flee — geometry chosen over reaction has exactly that shape of hole. The margin is derived from the Reach rather than from the damage precisely so that reaching this hole takes a change in the engine rather than a bad tick.

**Two consequences of masking *ground*.** A mask near a room's border can take exit tiles out of a [[seam]] band, so a chain that existed over raw terrain may not exist over masked terrain — which is answered, loudly, by decision 1's third rule and by `Errand.routable` asking `linked` over the same masked layer every price will use. And a masked room is masked for **every** reader that projects it, which is why the centres are declared as a fact keyed by **room name** rather than hung off the errand: two colonies that ever project W15S26 must mask it identically, and a mask that lived on a declaration would let them disagree.

### 3. The programme is re-homed to W15S28

ADR 0057 assumes W12S28 and every constant in its decision 4 is that home's. W12S28 is **six** crossings from W15S25 and W13S28 is five; both are outside `Tuning.MaxHops = 3`, and raising the budget to six is the thing ADR 0041's *"a minimum over thirty-odd additions rather than thirty-odd floods"* exists to hold down. **W15S28 is three**, by W15S27 and the Source Keeper room W15S26, and it was sited for exactly that: `third-colony.md` §4 chose it over nearer rooms on this criterion, and the three seams on the chain measure 20, 20 and 20 tiles with `transitBetween`'s rectangle being that straight line, so no detour is needed and none would be found.

**Measured, over live terrain by Dijkstra on 2026-09-13:** from W15S28's Thorium seat at (29,12) to a tile adjacent to the reactor is **154 steps and three room transitions**, against **224–267 steps and six** from W12S28. All nine tiles around the reactor are plain. W15S28 is **RCL5** today (227k of 1,215k, ~987k to go) and needs RCL6 for its own extractor: ~20,000 ticks, about sixteen real hours at 50 e/tick, and it is the only item on the critical path — which is not tight against the ~1,474,000 ticks decision 4 counts.

Four things re-derive off the shorter walk, and they are re-derived here rather than inherited:

- **The energy, re-attributed.** ADR 0057 costs the programme at *"≈ 220,000 energy for 44,000 Thorium, about a quarter of what W12S28 grosses over the same 44,000 ticks."* **W12S28's Storage is 0 today** — it held 258k on 2026-09-07 — while W13S28 holds 469,537 and W15S28 168,885. The programme is bought out of the bank of the colony that runs it, and the fraction-of-gross comparison has to be re-taken against **W15S28's** income before it is quoted again. *Unverified:* W15S28's gross per 1,000 ticks was not measured for this ADR.
- **The re-claimer gets cheaper, and the courier does not.** The courier is 1,500 energy per 999 T whatever the walk is, its body being a constant: **1.50 e/T**, unchanged. The re-claimer's 2.17 e/T was derived from a 275-tick walk leaving ~325 useful ticks of a 600-tick `CREEP_CLAIM_LIFE_TIME`; at 154 steps it stands ~446, which is **1.46 e/T**.
- **Both cadences were derived off the 267-step walk and neither survives it.** `Tuning.ReclaimerInterval` (300) was "25 ticks before the incumbent expires" read off 600 − 275 = 325; off 600 − 154 = 446 the same rule gives ~420. `Tuning.DeliveryInterval` (800) was read off a 999-unit buffer against a 275-tick trip and has more slack at 154. ADR 0057's own Consequences named these as *"the fields most likely to be wrong, being derived from a 267-step walk measured once"*; they are now wrong for a reason we know, and each arrives on its ticket naming the [[stage]] and the walk it was re-derived at, with its own pairwise test.
- **"A courier makes one trip" was rejected on arithmetic that no longer holds.** ADR 0057: *"1,500 ticks less ~40 to reach the Storage less 825 for the loaded leg leaves ~635, which is most of the way home and not a second load."* At 154 steps the loaded leg costs 154 × 3 = **462** ticks of life at `p = 2` and the empty leg 154, so a courier has roughly 1,500 − 40 − 462 − 154 = **~844** left after one delivery, which is a second loaded leg and change. The rejection's arithmetic is retired. Whether to take the second trip is **not decided here** — it halves the energy per Thorium against a body spending 616 more ticks inside a Source Keeper crossing — and it is #264's to settle against these numbers.

**And the cost of re-homing, stated rather than buried: 44,000 of the 66,000 is banked in a colony that cannot deliver it.** W12S28 and W13S28 mine and bank their own 22,000 apiece into their own Storages — ADR 0057 decision 3, per colony, landed in `b2ce82b`, `5c6ecb7` and `92f3c80` — and those Storages are six and five crossings from the reactor, so neither colony can hold this errand. Re-homing does not make their ore deliverable; it makes it *somebody else's* ore. **The programme as re-homed delivers W15S28's 22,000 and no more**: 22,000 unbroken is **88,894**, not the 318,894 the stock is worth, and a reader who quotes the larger number is quoting a stock we cannot yet move.

What is missing is a **rule and not a route**, and that distinction is the whole of the ticket. The distances are inside the budget: W13S28 → W15S28 is **two** crossings and W15S28 is already declared as W13S28's outpost and its candidate colony, so that pair is priced today; W12S28 → W15S28 is **three**, inside `Tuning.MaxHops` on the names. What no rule in this colony does is haul into **another colony's** Storage: ADR 0057 decision 3's pair is the mineral container's [[withdraw]] and the *home* [[storage]]'s [[refill]], one colony's [[trunk]], and ADR 0049 sums the round trip into that colony's own hauler quota. A cross-colony Thorium leg is a decision about ADR 0047's boundary — whose quota buys the body, whose bank pays for it, and what happens when the receiving colony stands down — and it is not taken here. *Inferred, not measured:* the round trip is two to three hops of hauling and will be expensive; nothing in this ADR prices it.

The alternative is the **terminal**, and it is newly available for a reason ADR 0057 could not see: `terminal-restriction.js` nulls a `send` only where the target terminal has a *different* `user`, so our room to our room is permitted, and the cost is `ceil(amount · (1 − e^(−range/30)))` energy — about **65 energy per 1,000 T** at range 2 (W13S28 → W15S28) and **96** at range 3 (W12S28 → W15S28), against 1,500 energy for a courier's 999. ADR 0057 rejected the terminal on a gate that is gone: it wrote *"needs GCL 3 against our GCL 2"*, and GCL is **9,836,911** with three rooms owned. What gates it now is that **we hold no terminal in any room** and `src/` contains the word nowhere. *Unverified in this repo:* the level `CONTROLLER_STRUCTURES.terminal` unlocks at is written nowhere in the tree, and the ticket's first act is to read it off the engine rather than trust this paragraph. **This ADR scopes the first delivery to W15S28's own deposit and files the other 44,000 as its own decision** — a cross-colony haul or a terminal pair — and designs neither.

**`claimReactor` is the first act and not the last.** W15S25 (44,6) is owned by **`Odiodin`** (rank 3, 192,305), its store empty and `launchTime` null, so every Thorium delivered there today scores for him and the courier's own rule — pour while the reactor is ours or unowned — correctly makes it wait forever. ADR 0057 rejected claiming before the supply chain exists on the ground that *"what a claim buys is a flag on a map telling four scoring rivals which sector centre is contested."* **The flag is planted, and by somebody else.** So the re-claimer row opens on its own, ahead of the extractor and ahead of the banked trips, the tick W15S28 can afford 650 energy and a chain reaches the room; ADR 0057 decision 7's order — reclaimer at extractor + paved road + 2,997 T banked, courier one step later — is replaced by **the claim first, the ore second**, and the courier's opening condition is unchanged behind it. What the claim costs is 650 energy and one walk. What it buys is that the delivery has a sink at all, and a tick of vision on a leaf that today can only read stale.

**A steal does not break the streak, so theft is an income leak and not a penalty.** `claimReactor` does `bulk.update(target, {user: object.user})` and nothing else — no cooldown, no ownership precondition, `launchTime` untouched (re-verified in `src/creep.claimReactor.js`, 2026-09-13); only an **empty store** clears `launchTime`. ADR 0057 already said the streak survives the exchange; what this ADR adds is the size of the loss, which is **5 score a tick for the ticks between the theft and our next act** — one while the relay stands — against **11,106** for a break. Odiodin's W14S22 is three rooms from the centre, so he is in claimer range permanently and this is a standing claim war rather than cheap insurance. The re-claimer's cadence is still the entire defence: the reactor has no `hits`, cannot be attacked or dismantled, and cannot be ramparted in a room with no controller.

**And the stock is 66,000, not 44,000.** Three owned deposits, every one density 3 and 22,000, every one on a **wall** tile at the mouth of a wall: W12S28 (26,5), W13S28 (42,30), W15S28 (29,12). W12S28 and W13S28 have been RCL6 with the extractor kind unlocked for days; W15S28 needs RCL6 for its own. 66,000 unbroken scores **318,894** against today's leader at 293,347; 44,000 scores 208,894.

### 4. The premise is re-derived rather than inherited

ADR 0057's premise — **never let the store run dry, and do not chase one unbroken streak** — is the user's decision on #251 and is not reopened. Its stated **robustness argument** is: *"Three breaks still score 175,576 against a whole board's leader at 74,768, so the programme is robust by two and a half times."* Today's leader is **CrAzYDubC at 293,347** (live at tick 396,671; 75 registered, 15 scoring; the doc's leader giaco is rank 6 at 82,305). 175,576 against 293,347 is not robust by two and a half times; it is not a lead at all. **That argument does not hold and must not be repeated.**

The premise survives, by a different and stronger derivation:

- **The clock is not the constraint and it is not close.** The season ends 2026-11-01; the tick rate measured twice independently on 2026-09-13 is 0.348 ticks/s (2.87 s/tick), agreeing with the server's own 2,852.6 ms rolling mean, so ~**1,474,000 ticks** remain. 66,000 Thorium is 66,000 reactor-ticks — **2.19 real days** of reactor work, 22× less than the time available. The rate would have to fall to 64 s/tick for the clock to bind. *Unverified:* the hour of the Nov 1 shutdown; 00:00 UTC is assumed and a later hour only adds slack.
- **So ore acquisition dominates streak discipline by an order of magnitude.** A break costs exactly **11,106** wherever it falls (the next 9,999 ticks earn 38,889 where they would have earned 49,995). **One more 22,000-deposit room is worth 110,000** — ten breaks. ADR 0057's *"a break is a 5% tax, not a failure, and a programme that treats it as a failure buys perfection with the fragility that causes it"* was right and should be **leaned on harder, not less**: an hour spent on streak-perfection that could have been spent on a fourth deposit is an hour spent an order of magnitude wrong.
- **Three corollaries the re-derivation carries.** Do **not** run two reactors: 66,000 split scores 307,788 against 318,894, and parallelism costs exactly one break per extra reactor because the log bonus is superlinear. Do **not** plan offensive theft: the store caps at 1,000, so one uncontested steal nets ≤5,000, and of 100 sector centres 20 are owned and **exactly one is running** anywhere on the board. And the one threshold that would make the clock worth thinking about again is **110,000 T total**, which crosses 99,999 continuous ticks into the 6/tick band (548,895) — still only 3.65 real days of reactor work.

## What this replaces in ADR 0057, clause by clause

ADR 0057 is accepted and is not edited. Per `docs/agents/orchestration.md`, the banner lands on the ticket that implements the amending clause; this is the successor and not the implementation, so no banner is written here.

**Decision 4 — the declared route — is replaced in its entirety by ADR 0058, ADR 0059 and decisions 1–3 above.** Named clause by clause:

- *"`Tuning.ReactorRoute` is a list of room names from the colony's home to the reactor's room … chosen by a human in a commit"* — **retired, unwritten.** ADR 0057 set the test — *"landing #243 should delete `Tuning.ReactorRoute` and change no rule"* — and it is met by the constant never existing: the chain is `Atlas.routes`' answer, three crossings, chosen by the walker's price (ADR 0059).
- *"Beside each intermediate room a human also writes its keeper centres"* — **the declaredness survives, its home does not.** There is no route list to hang them off; they are declared as a fact keyed by room name and read wherever that room is projected (decision 2).
- *"each masking the tiles within `Tuning.KeeperMargin` (5) of it"* — **the number is replaced by a derivation and is 6** (decision 2). Five is right for damage and wrong for ADR 0033's Reach.
- *"A route room enters the spatial projection as a bare terrain layer and nothing else … targets, stores, creeps and hostiles stay absent"* — **replaced.** A room on the chain is a [[transit room]] (ADR 0058 decision 2) and carries terrain and a border ring; the **errand room** is declared and carries its one named target besides; and the clause's "no hostiles at all" is false since #286 and is answered by decision 2, not by absence.
- *"The mover walks the route hop by hop … the body's current room is looked up in the route, its successor is the next entry"* — **replaced** by the chain's own mover: the price and the step come out of one minimisation and the creep is aimed at the near side of the crossing the price was won at (ADR 0041, ADR 0059 decision 2). The one exception is ADR 0059's: `Atlas.stepTowardRoom`, the vision-grace mover, still walks the compass chain (#297).
- *"Its Task is `Deliver of reactorId`, and it is a new kind for the route and not for the resource … nothing in the pool can price it"* — **the reason is retired.** From W15S28 the reactor is inside the budget and `pricedAcross` answers a number, so `Deliver` is priced like every other cross-room Task. Whether it still earns a kind of its own under ADR 0054's test, or is a `Refill of reactorId * Thorium` with an applicability rule about ownership, is **#264's to settle** against the clause that carried it being gone.
- *"A courier in a route room is applicable to `Deliver` and to nothing else, and it is placeable. This is #164's defect stated as a rule"* — **replaced by a general rule that already landed.** ADR 0058's Consequences: a transit room is projected, so a creep standing in one is adopted, placed and priced. The special clause is unnecessary. The courier's applicability stays narrow for a different reason that survives — a body carries one resource at a time (ADR 0057 decision 3) — and not because an unpriced Task any body could hold is one every body would win.
- *"The `courier` row's body is a constant: `[20 Carry; 10 Move]`, 1,500 energy"*, *"`Tuning.ReactorLoad` (999)"* and the decade-cliff argument behind it, *"a reactor another player holds is not a sink"*, the range-1 Transfer, the Emitter's amount-carrying Withdraw — **all survive unchanged.**
- *"one cast per `Tuning.DeliveryInterval` (800 ticks)"* — **re-derived on its ticket** off a 154-step walk (decision 3).

**Decision 5 — the re-claimer — keeps its conclusion and loses its reason.**

- *"**Neither flees.** … a route room carries terrain and our own creeps and no hostiles at all, so there is no Reach out there to be inside of and no safe set to walk to. That is a deliberate hole."* — **replaced.** There are hostiles out there, they do derive a Reach, and #286 made that deliberate. Neither body flees all the same, and it is no longer a hole: decision 2 masks the ground so that no walkable tile of ours lies in a keeper's Reach, and Flee is inapplicable out there by construction.
- *"one cast per `Tuning.ReclaimerInterval` (300 ticks) … Three hundred and not 325"* — **re-derived**: 600 − 154 ≈ 446 useful ticks, so ~420 with the same 25-tick overlap.

## What this widens in ADR 0058

Nothing of ADR 0058 is contradicted; two clauses are read wider and it is worth saying which.

- **`Outpost.roomsProjected` is the union point and it gains a second declaration kind.** Decision 1's errand room and `RoomName.transitBetween home errandRoom` enter one colony's scan set by the same call that already unions an outpost's chain. The rule ADR 0058 stated — which rooms are projected is answered off the **names**, because the route needs their terrain and their terrain needs them projected — is unchanged and is what makes an errand declarable at all.
- **Decision 2's transit-room promise is about the pool and not about vision**, which is #286's correction and not this ADR's. What decision 1 adds beside it is a declared room that is neither a transit room nor an outpost, and a narrowing that says exactly how much of its vision is work: the one target the declaration names, and nothing else in the room however much we see.

## Considered Options

- **`Outpost.Controller` made optional** (#300 option 1). Rejected by the user and for the reason above: every reader of an outpost presumes a controller, each would learn to skip, and a room we mine nothing in would sit on the list of rooms we mine.
- **No declaration at all — a `Tuning` constant naming the reactor room, projected as a special case** (#300 option 3). Rejected: it is #263's hand-written route in a smaller hat, and it rots the same way — a constant read by one rule, invisible to `ColonyView.Refused`, and untestable against the live declaration.
- **Exempting the courier and the re-claimer from Flee** (#301 option 2). Rejected: it contradicts ADR 0033 directly, and exempting a *row* rather than a *situation* is the rule that later gets copied to a row that should have fled.
- **Narrowing Flee's applicability by what the body can do about the threat** (#301 option 3) — a body with no Attack and no Heal, in a room with no Reach it can leave cheaply. Rejected as the answer *here*, and it is the most honest of the three: it touches the threat model for every body in the colony, and it would be bought for a case the map answers for free. Worth reopening the day a hostile on a chain is something other than a keeper.
- **Keeping the programme on W12S28 by raising `Tuning.MaxHops` to six.** Rejected on ADR 0041's own cost argument, which ADR 0058 and ADR 0059 both lean on: the hop budget bounds k chained floods and k band joins per priced pair, and doubling it doubles the ceiling for every pair in the colony to buy one room's walk. W15S28 exists because this was foreseen.
- **Two reactors, one per colony.** Rejected on the engine's arithmetic: 66,000 split scores 307,788 against 318,894, the log bonus being superlinear, so parallelism costs exactly one break per extra reactor. Nothing in the rules forbids it — olek_PL owns four — and it is still wrong.
- **Delivering W12S28's and W13S28's 44,000 by hauling it into W15S28's Storage.** Not rejected, not taken. The walk is priceable already — two crossings from W13S28, three from W12S28 — so what is missing is a rule about hauling into another colony's Storage (ADR 0047's boundary: whose quota, whose bank, and what a stand-down does to it), and it competes with a terminal pair at ~65 energy per 1,000 T. Filed as its own decision by decision 3.
- **Masking the lairs alone rather than every rock.** Rejected against the engine: keepers are pinned to their **source or mineral**, so a mask around lairs alone leaves the sources unmasked and the safe-crossing measurement does not cover it.

## Glossary additions

Proposed for `CONTEXT.md`, not written here.

- **Errand** — a room a [[colony]] declares because it must walk a body there and act on **one** named object in it, and for no other reason: a room name and that object's engine id and tile, and nothing else (ADR 0060). More than a [[transit room]], which carries terrain and a border ring and nothing pooled; less than an [[outpost]], which is a room we mine and whose controller we reserve. It is what a room with **no controller at all** — a sector centre — can be declared as, an `Outpost` requiring one by its type. Declared inside the colony that runs it and projected for that colony alone, because it is the only one whose walk to it is inside `Tuning.MaxHops`; refused loudly and named on the [[layout record]] when no chain reaches it, exactly as an outpost with no chain is. Everything about the object that changes — its store, its owner — is read off vision and absent entry by entry where there is none (ADR 0004), which is why the body standing there is the colony's only eye on it. _Avoid_: goal, mission, remote target.
- **Keeper margin** — the tiles masked out of a Source Keeper room's walkable ground around every rock a keeper is pinned to — its lairs, its sources and its mineral — declared by room name and read wherever that room is projected (ADR 0060). `Tuning.KeeperMargin` is **derived and not chosen**: the keeper's pin range (1), plus its longest weapon range (3), plus `Tuning.ReachMargin` (2) — six — so that every walkable tile left is outside every keeper's [[reach]] and [[flee]] is inapplicable out there by construction rather than by exemption. The one place this colony edits a fact about the world before a rule reads it, and the exception is argued on the class: a keeper lair is stationary, is known before the room is ever seen, and is geometry rather than a reaction. _Avoid_: keeper radius, SK exclusion.
- **Retire the "Route" entry** proposed by ADR 0057 — a human-declared list of room names from a home to a goal no [[seam]] join can price. It was a workaround with an expiry date and the date has passed: the chain is derived (ADR 0058) and chosen by the walker's price (ADR 0059), and the keeper centres it carried are now a fact about a room.
