# Expiring creeps: the workforce and a Task's capacity are counted at arrival

The workforce deficit is `target − living creeps`, and the row gaps count living bodies, so a replacement is cast only once its predecessor is dead — `ticksToLive` was never projected. For the Anchor row that is the most expensive wait in the colony: 24 ticks to cast an 8-part body plus 60–100 ticks of walking (ADR 0025's arithmetic) leaves each Post unmanned for roughly a hundred ticks of every 1500-tick life, and the Post cap (ADR 0024) would then keep a replacement cast early from being matched at all — the dying garrison holds the one slot until it dies, so an early-cast Anchor would idle at the spawn `none-free` and walk only after the death it was cast to pre-empt.

We decided **a creep is counted at [[arrival]] — its replacement's, and its own**:

- The projection carries each living creep's **ticks to live** (a creep still spawning is already outside the projection).
- A creep's **[[lead]]** is the time its replacement needs to stand where it stands: the replacement body's cast time (3 ticks per part, the row's body at the bank's capacity) plus that body's travel from the spawn to the creep's current tile. Priced by the Atlas for the replacement's fatigue factor, not the incumbent's — a fresh Anchor is empty and slow, a hauler on a trunk is fast; no row needs its own rule (ADR 0006). Travel cost cannot serve it: that query is keyed on a creep name and a body that has not been cast has none, so the Atlas gains a one-way walk priced for a body. It is traffic-blind, as the hauler quota's round trip is — the walk does not begin until the body is cast, and the tile it ends on is the one the creep being replaced is standing on, so the occupancy surcharge (ADR 0008) would add its own step to every lead for a crowd of one that will be dead. The walk starts on the tiles *beside* the spawner, not on the spawner's own tile: the engine places a finished creep on a free neighbour and it pays no step to get there, so charging that step would buy a lead ticks the replacement never walks — and, since the goal is the incumbent's tile, would cast the successor early enough that the incumbent still counts against the Post it is being replaced on. Several spawns resolve as the hauler quota resolves them, at the cheapest — the optimistic bound on when a replacement could stand there, and the convention the row's quota is already read at; which spawn actually casts is the spawn fold's own business. Geometry the Atlas cannot price leads nobody (ADR 0004): an unplaced creep, an unplaced or unreachable spawn, a spawner with no free neighbour, a colony with no spawn — each leads 0, and a lead of 0 leaves every living creep counted.
- A creep whose ticks to live are at or under its lead is **[[expiring]]**: it counts neither toward the workforce nor toward its row's gap, so its replacement is cast now. It is *not* released — anti-thrash keeps it working to the last tick, and that has to be told to the cap as well: the release fold walks creep names in order, so the successor can be judged first, take the slot the incumbent's arrival-priced death frees, and leave the incumbent reading its own Post as full. An expiring creep is never released over capacity, so the fold's name order cannot decide which of the pair keeps the tile.
- **Capacity is counted at arrival**: when a candidate is judged against a Task's cap, a holder that will be dead before the candidate arrives (ticks to live < the candidate's travel ticks) does not count. The replacement leaves the spawn while its predecessor still stands; if it arrives early it waits on the adjacent tile and steps in when the tile frees. The window is read from **both** ends — a holder still walking when the candidate is dead (its own travel ticks > the candidate's ticks to live) does not count either — because two creeps hold the same standing room against each other only while both are standing on it, and a one-ended window is not symmetric: it would let a candidate a whole cast-and-walk away discount a garrison nowhere near its own lead, and the fold's name order would then decide which of the two keeps the tile. The travel cost is read one gate earlier than it is spent to price this, in the judgement and in the release fold alike; the order of the gates is deliberately unchanged, and a candidate the Atlas cannot price has no arrival to count holders at, so every holder counts against it and it reports the rejection it always did.
- Deliberately deferred: the mirror rule for the expiring creep's *own* fresh matches (a creep should not be sent to a Task it cannot reach alive). No observed symptom yet — a kept creep usually finishes its life on the Task it holds — so it waits for one.

## Considered Options

- **A per-row lead constant** — rejected: a number nobody can derive, and a row-specific rule where a body-derived one exists.
- **Early retirement: release the expiring creep's Task so the cap frees** — rejected: it throws away the last lead-ticks of a garrison's output, and needs a new release reason for a situation arrival-priced capacity handles with none.
- **Count creeps still in the spawner toward the workforce** — rejected as orthogonal: the deficit already stops double-casting through the spawn's own `IsSpawning`, and a spawning creep has no position for a lead to price.
- **Price the lead from the spawn to the creep's Work Area rather than to its tile** — rejected: it needs the creep's Task, which the spawn step runs before; the tile the creep stands on is where its work is for every row that matters (a garrison never leaves its Post), and a hauler's error is a few ticks either way.
- **Decide expiry in the Snapshot as a boolean** — rejected: expiry reads the replacement body and the Atlas, neither of which the Snapshot has; the projection carries the fact (`ticksToLive`), the decision layer derives the judgement (ADR 0013's rule for source stock).

## Consequences

- Two Anchors are alive against one Post for the lead's duration every generation, by design; the quota, the gap and the cap all agree on why, so neither is released for it. ADR 0024's "two Anchors alive against one Post" consequence is now the normal succession, not only a race.
- The colony pays the overlap — one lead's worth of double amortisation per creep per life; the workforce arithmetic (ADR 0012) already spreads body cost over the lifetime, so the target does not move.
- `IdleReason.NoneFree` on a freshly cast Anchor should no longer appear during a succession; if it does, the lead is mispriced.
- The succession's whole margin is the [[occupancy surcharge]] on the incumbent's own tile: the lead is traffic-blind and the successor's arrival is not, and the goal tile is occupied by the creep being replaced, so the surcharge's flat 10 cost units make every arrival exactly 5 ticks longer than the blind walk that set the cast tick. Five ticks is the slack the successor is admitted with at birth, and it holds for the whole walk — the blind walk is a lower bound on the traffic-priced one, so the margin can only grow while the successor is slowed and returns to 5 when it is not. That is why the lead must not also charge the step out of the spawner: on unpaved plain an Anchor's first step costs more than 5 ticks, which spends the margin before the successor is born.
- The hauler and worker rows get the same rule and a short lead, so their replacements are cast about a body-length before the death rather than after — a small, uniform gain.
- Three more traffic-blind floods a tick, one per pattern row per spawn, and `planSpawns` prices leads before it knows it has a deficit — so a colony at full strength pays them too. Measured at roughly a sixth of the tick's decision cost. The floods' goals are known up front (the row's creep tiles), so a batched walk query could stop each flood when its last goal settles; that is a follow-up, not part of this decision.
- DecideTests: an expiring creep drops out of the count and its row's gap; capacity ignores a holder dead before arrival, still counts one that outlives the walk, and ignores one whose own walk outlasts the candidate's life; the lead is priced for the replacement's body and does not charge the step out of the spawner; kept assignments on an expiring creep are untouched under either order the release fold can reach the pair in. AtlasTests pin the walk itself: priced for the body given, started beside the spawner, discounted by a road, traffic-blind, and unpriceable where nothing connects.
- Sibling decision: ADR 0025 applies the same arrival principle to a source's restock; implement it first — this ADR reads the candidate's travel ticks at the same gate 0025 introduces.
