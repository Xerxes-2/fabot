# A walk crosses a chain of Seams, and no flood leaves its room

ADR 0041 prices a crossing over **one** Seam band — `walk = min over seams (near leg + 1 + far leg)`, each leg a single-room flood — and #243 closed by writing that boundary down as an invariant: an [[outpost]] a home does not border is refused before the scan set is built, loudly, because accepted it would be projected, pooled and hired for and worked by nobody. That ticket also said what would lift it: *"多跳 outpost 另开设计 …… 现在不做，等一跳的 5 个房用完再说"*. The five rooms are used up. `docs/research/remote-candidates.md` enumerated them — W11S28, W12S29, W13S27, W13S29, W14S28, seven sources between them and six of them single-source — and every remaining room worth taking is two or three hops out.

We decided **a cross-room walk is priced over a chain of Seams, and every leg of it is still a single-room flood**. The hop budget is `Tuning.MaxHops`, three, a constant a human moves in a commit exactly as the Layout's horizon is (ADR 0039).

## The decision, in three parts

**1. The route is derived, not declared.** `Atlas.route` answers the rooms a walk crosses, ends included: a breadth-first search over the name grid (`RoomName.routeBy`) in which `seams` — the same Seam band ADR 0041 already reads off two rooms' border rings — decides which of a room's four grid neighbours a creep can actually step into. Memoised per ordered pair, `None` included. `RoomName.adjacent`'s fixed north-east-south-west order breaks ties, so the chain is a function of the world and not of the search.

This is the room-level route ADR 0057's Consequences described and wrote a constant for instead: *"A proper multi-hop route model is a room-level graph … with ADR 0041's Seam join applied pairwise along the resulting list and the per-room flood untouched. Decision 4's declared route **is** that list, written by a human."* That ADR set the test of its own workaround — landing this should delete `Tuning.ReactorRoute` and change no rule — and since #263 has not landed, what this buys is that the constant need never be written.

**2. Which rooms are projected is answered off the names, and that is what cuts the circle.** The route needs the transit rooms' terrain; their terrain needs them projected; the projection is chosen before any of it is read. `RoomName.transitBetween` cuts it: the interior of the name-grid rectangle two rooms span is exactly the set of rooms a *shortest* chain could pass through — every room in it lies on some monotone path and every room outside it lies on none — and it is answerable off two names. `Outpost.roomsProjected` unions it in, so a **transit room** enters the projection carrying terrain and a border ring and nothing else: no furniture is laid in it (`furnitureOf` reads a declaration that names none), nothing is pooled there, and no row hires for it. For a one-hop outpost the set is empty and the projection is the one every tick before this ADR built.

The cost of cutting the circle that way is stated rather than hidden: a **detour** around a walled border lies outside the rectangle, is not projected, and is therefore not found. Such a declaration is refused loudly, which is #243's answer unchanged, and widening `transitBetween` is what would buy it.

**3. The far leg becomes a chain, and the join does not change at all.** `joinedAcross` already took its far side as a bare `far: Pos -> int`; what changed is what is handed to it. `chainedInto` floods into the Task's own ground in the last room of the chain — today's `floodPricedInto`, unmoved — and then carries that field back across each Seam in turn: for every crossing, the field's own side, the exit tile's price, and the entry onto the tile the crossing lands beside, seeded through the `floodFromAllSeeded` primitive that was already there for the purpose. `carriedAcross` is that one hop's arithmetic and `foldChain` walks a chain of them, both written once and read in **both directions**: the backward chain a Task's price is built on and the forward chain a lead's cast walk is built on (`castAlong`, formerly `castAcross`) differ in nothing but which end the field starts at (ADR 0030), so neither is passed a direction — a `Hop` names the room its Seam band leaves, the caller says which room its field is over, and every selector follows from those two. `chainedInto` hands the fold the hops reversed and the flood into the Task's ground; `castAlong` hands it the same hops in order and the flood out of the spawner.

So a three-hop price is three single-room floods laid end to end and one join, never a flood over three rooms: **ADR 0041's "no flood ever leaves its room" is as literally true after this ADR as before it**, which is the whole reason this shape was chosen over the two below. `pricedAcrossInto`, `haulRoundTripTicks` and `castWalkTicks` each take the chain; the join is handed the *next* room and the band into it, and a one-hop route makes the two the same room and the call the one it always was.

## Considered Options

- **A declared route per (home, goal), ADR 0057 decision 4 generalised.** Rejected. It is data a human maintains for a derivation the bot can run, and ADR 0057 itself named the day it should go. It also cannot answer the question a *price* asks — a creep standing in a transit room prices from where it stands, and a route declared from the home says nothing about that.
- **Recursive Seam join: join the near leg to the middle room, then join again.** Rejected. The middle leg would depend on where the creep stands, so it could not be shared colony-wide, and ADR 0041's cost argument — *"a minimum over thirty-odd additions rather than over thirty-odd floods"* — is exactly the thing that argument buys. Carrying the chain **backwards from the Task's ground** keeps the far side independent of the creep, which is why the memo still answers every creep in the colony with one entry.
- **One flood over the rooms laid side by side.** Rejected, and not narrowly: every grid in the Atlas is one room's 50×50 array indexed by `indexOf`, the Resolver arbitrates movement a room at a time, and the border ring is deliberately not ground. This is the boundary ADR 0041 drew, and nothing here asks to move it.
- **The route searched in the shell over `Game.map.describeExits`.** Rejected. The shell would own a derivation Core has to agree with tile for tile, and the two would be free to disagree about which chain is *the* chain. `seams` is already the passability fact and it lives in Core; the shell reads `Outpost.roomsProjected` for the rooms it must hold terrain for, and that is the only thing it needs to know.
- **No hop bound, only reachability.** Rejected. The arithmetic has no upper bound in it — only the CPU does — so the bound is written down where a human can see and move it.

## Consequences

- **A one-hop price is the same price, to the digit.** The chain-of-one case *is* `floodPricedInto` under the same memo key, so every price pin standing before this change is green and **unedited** — every Seam pin, every worked example, every cross-room walk in `AtlasCrossRoomTests`, whose diff is append-only. Two test files were edited and neither is a price: `ViewTests`' declaration invariant, whose fixture room moved from two hops out to four because two hops is now inside the budget, and `AtlasFixtures`, which gained chain fixtures. Nothing about today's colony moves.
- **The multi-hop arithmetic is pinned by worked examples, not by a property.** `AtlasCrossRoomTests`' "a walk over two borders is the same three terms, twice" counts sixty-seven tiles over three rooms — ten, forty-nine and eight — with both landings charged nothing, and reads directly against the one-hop worked example it extends by one room. Beside it a second at the budget's own length, three crossings and 116 tiles (ten, forty-nine, forty-nine and eight), because a fold is where an off-by-one lives and two hops cannot tell a fold that runs `n-1` times from one that runs twice. And beside those: a creep four tiles further back pays four ticks more and not a tile besides (the far side is the chain's, not the creep's), a creep standing *in* the transit room prices the hop it has left, and a chain the terrain breaks is `None` rather than a number with a gap in it.
- **The capability costs nothing until it is used, and a use costs about a third of a millisecond.** Measured on the `pair` scenario, same machine, same command, and against a **re-measured** `main` rather than against the numbers ADR 0052 recorded (3.89 / 7.83): the colony has grown since, and a baseline read off a document instead of off the machine would have credited this change with the difference. Against `main` re-measured at 4.63 ms quiet / 9.10 ms census, this change under the same declaration measures **4.73 quiet** — **+0.10 ms**, which these runs barely resolve: the same pair measured 4.53 / 9.19 before the scan set began asking for a route, and 4.53 against 4.63 ran the *other* way. What is not noise is where that work went and what it cost to make it small. The scan set is cut several times a tick by every reader of it, so a route search per declared outpost per cut is the one hot thing this ADR adds, and two shapes carry it: each room's border ring is resolved **once** per crossing rather than per tile (forty-eight room lookups a crossing, the first measurement's 5.01 ms and most of the difference), and `RoomName.routeBy` tries the goal before expanding a frontier, so the one-hop route every declaration in force actually asks for costs one `linked` call and not four. With a real two-hop outpost declared for the measurement (W14S29 off W13S28, so W13S29 and W14S28 are both projected as transit rooms) it measures **4.90 quiet**: **+0.37 ms** against this branch without it. That is the honest price of a two-hop outpost — two more grids laid a tick, the chained flood per far field, and the outpost's own furniture and pool — and it is not the price of the mechanism, which is nil.
- **What the measurement does not cover.** There is no three-hop declaration to measure, because there is no three-hop room this colony has decided to take; the number above is two hops, and a third adds one more grid and one more flood per far field. The scenario's world is also frozen — no body is cast for the new outpost — so the creeps a real declaration would hire, and the prices they would ask for, are outside it. A profile scenario that stands a multi-hop outpost properly is its own ticket.
- **A test world without a border ring models a world the shell cannot build.** Since the scan set reads the rings, a fixture that leaves `RoomFacts.Border` empty now refuses every outpost in it — which is how five `ViewTests` cases failed on the change and what they were really saying: two of them modelled an *unseen* outpost by removing the room from the world, and the shell never does that (it reads terrain for every declared room and drops only what vision pays for). Both now keep the room with its terrain and its ring, which is `World.factsOf`'s own blind branch. The fixtures got more faithful, not more permissive.
- **A transit room is projected, so a creep standing in one is adopted.** `Colony.creepColonies` files a creep under the colony whose projection holds the room it stands in; before this ADR a body mid-crossing stood in a room nobody projected, held no tile and could not be moved (#164's shape). Now it is placed and priced, which is what lets it walk the second hop — and it is a widening of adoption that no rule asked for explicitly.
- **#259 closes, and the two shapes of it turn out to be one question.** A bordering room whose shared edge is walled end to end has no band — but if the projection carries a chain around it, the route finds one and the room is priced after all; and a room inside the hop budget that *no* chain reaches is refused, loudly, on the same reading. Both are `Outpost.routable`, which asks `RoomName.routeBy` with a real `linked` rather than the name distance alone.

  What made that possible is that **the terrain is already there**: `World.ofGame` reads terrain and the border ring for every declared and transit room whether or not there is vision (ADR 0031, ADR 0041), so by the time the scan set is cut the rings of every room a shortest chain could cross are in hand. What was missing was only that the band test lived in the Atlas, which is built after the view. `Seam.bandBy` / `Seam.joinedBy` now hold that geometry once, and the two callers differ only in where they keep their terrain — `Atlas.seams` over the ring grids it lays per tick, `World.linked` over the border maps themselves. One definition, so the scan set and the price cannot disagree about which rooms are joined.

  The name-level test survives beside it as `Outpost.withinHopBudget`, because it is the one a *declaration* can be checked with before any terrain is read, and `ViewTests`' invariant over the live `Colony.declared` is exactly that check.
- **The declaration's rule moves from a border to a reachable chain, and the refusal stays.** `Outpost.routable` replaces `Outpost.neighbouring` in `World.scanOf` and in `Outpost.refused`; `ColonyView.Refused` names a room past the budget exactly as it named a non-neighbour, and `ViewTests`' invariant over the live `Colony.declared` is red on the same slip at the new altitude. What moved is where the line falls, never that there is one.
- **This ADR does not say a multi-hop outpost is worth declaring.** `docs/research/remote-candidates.md` measured every two-hop candidate and found none with better net income than a one-hop room, and nothing here contradicts that: `haulRoundTripTicks` will honestly return a bigger number and ADR 0042's arithmetic will size the row off it. Whether a room earns its declaration is a human's judgement at declaration time, as it was before. What this ADR changes is that the answer is now a number instead of `None`.
