# The Anchor row's Work is capped at source saturation plus one

ADR 0006 sized the Anchor row as "one Carry, one Move, and every part slot the remaining energy affords on Work", with the 50-part engine cap as its only ceiling. A source regenerates 3,000 energy every 300 ticks and a Work part digs 2 a tick, so five Work saturate a source: from a 600-energy bank up (RCL3's 800 already casts `7W1C1M`, RCL4's 1,300 would cast twelve) every further Work drains the source sooner and then idles until regeneration, buying nothing but spawn time and energy. We decided **the Anchor row spends on Work only up to source saturation plus one — six Work — and the ceiling is derived from the source output the hauler quota and the workforce income already read (`sourceOutputPerTick`), not written as a literal.** The one spare Work is deliberate slack: with exactly five, every tick a Post stands unmanned is energy lost at the next regeneration; with six the source drains in 250 ticks and the remaining 50 absorb an Anchor's death, recast and walk to its Post at no cost in output. Inseparably, **a spawn casts a body as soon as its bank holds that body's cost**, instead of waiting for a full bank: the capped Anchor's price stops tracking capacity, so "wait for full" would delay every Anchor replacement past RCL3 for nothing. For the worker and hauler rows, whose bodies are sized to capacity, the two rules coincide, so the cast timing is one sentence for every row.

## Considered Options

- **Exactly five Work (the arithmetic saturation point)** — rejected: a 100-energy, 3-tick saving per Anchor against a full regeneration cycle's worth of exposure to the ordinary Post gap; community static-miner builds settle on the sixth Work for the same reason.
- **Size by the Post's kind (a Dual Seat Anchor keeps the open-ended rule, a container-Post Anchor takes the cap)** — rejected: a spawn does not know which Post an Anchor will man — the creep chooses by matching after birth (the no-role axiom, ADR 0006) — so the only footings are two pattern rows, each with its own quota (ADR 0012 already refused a separate miner row), or a room-wide switch on Dual Seat presence. Neither is warranted by what a Dual Seat actually wants: under one Task a tick, a one-Carry body alternating Harvest and Upgrade moves roughly 50 ÷ (⌈50/2W⌉ + ⌈50/W⌉) energy a tick — 3.3 at five Work, 6.3 at ten — so its bottleneck is the single Carry, not the Work count. That is a separate decision about the Dual Seat body, owed its own issue; today's colony has no Dual Seat.
- **Keep waiting for a full bank** — rejected: at RCL4 an Anchor gap would sit a full extension-refill cycle longer than its body costs, for no benefit; the disaster fallback (spawn a minimal worker from whatever is banked when the colony is empty) is unchanged and unrelated.
- **A per-row cast-timing exception for the Anchor alone** — rejected: "cast when the bank holds the body's cost" reads the same for every row and changes nothing for rows whose bodies already price at capacity.

## Consequences

- The workforce target's Anchor amortization (ADR 0012) falls with the cap and the income-fed worker count rises accordingly; no further arithmetic changes.
- A future mineral or remote row wanting more Work is its own pattern row with its own quota (ADR 0006's rule), not a loosening of this cap; the ceiling is a fact about a source's regeneration, not about heavy bodies in general.
- The DecideTests surface: `bodyFor anchorPattern` at 12,900 now answers `6W1C1M`, not 48 Work, and a bank holding a capped Anchor's cost but not its capacity casts the Anchor.
- The Dual Seat Anchor's Carry-bound throughput is now a recorded, open question rather than an unexamined consequence of "many Work".
