# A colony is a home room, and `decide` runs once per colony

ADR 0041 says "this colony's territory is a constant", and the whole decision layer is written in the language of *one* [[home room]] and its [[outpost]]s: `Snapshot.build` takes the first spawn's room as home, and there is one [[layout]], one Upgrade, one set of quotas and one plan memo. After GCL2 there has to be a second owned room. Dropped into today's bundle, a claimed W13S28 becomes a "outpost with a spawn in it": its controller is upgraded by nobody, no Layout is planned for it, and its [[anchor]] and haulers go on carrying energy back across the [[seam]] to W12S28's [[storage]] (#182).

**1. The colony is the unit of projection.** `Colony.declared : Colony list`, each entry a `{ Home; Outposts }` a human writes — today `[ W12S28 → [W12S27; W13S28] ]`, and the day W13S28 stands on its own `[ W12S28 → [W12S27]; W13S28 → [] ]`. It replaces the bare `Outpost.declared`, which said the same thing only while there was one home to say it about. `Main.loop` builds a Snapshot and runs `decide` once per **living** colony — one whose home controller is ours *and* whose home holds one of our spawns — each with its own Atlas, Layout, quotas, Task pool and plan memo. Memory keeps `assignments`, `observe.creeps` and `observe.cpu` flat, keyed by creep name and by tick, where two colonies cannot collide; `observe.raids` and `observe.layout` split by home (`observe.colonies.<home>.…`), and `observe.mjs` takes `--colony <home>`. The [[CPU line]] records the whole loop.

**2. A creep belongs to the colony that cast it**, read off the spawn name its own name carries — with one exception, **adoption**: a creep standing in a room only another colony projects is that colony's for the tick. A colony's `Snapshot.Creeps` is the ones it cast, plus the ones it has adopted, less the ones another colony has adopted from it.

**3. Claim is a Task.** `Task.Claim controllerId`, pooled one per **candidate colony** — an entry of `Colony.declared` whose home this colony does not own yet — capacity 1, Feeding tier beside [[reserve]], applicable to a CLAIM part, `Intent.ClaimController` and a `claimController` binding. The hire is the [[reserver]] row's: a candidate colony adds **one** `[Claim; Move]` block to `reserverClaimsOf`, and its room leaves the reservation demands, because a controller carries exactly one of the three Tasks that act on one — ours is Upgraded, a neutral one is Reserved, a candidate colony's is Claimed. The tick the claim succeeds the room is ours, the Task is gone from the next pool, and by #181 the room is not reserved either. A candidate colony is **projected by its mother as an outpost** until it is independent — W13S28 is one today — and that is what puts its controller in the projection for the Claim to name.

**4. The nursery, and pioneers.** A colony that is claimed but has no spawn of its own is not independent: it goes on being projected as its mother's outpost, and — unlike an ordinary outpost, where only the [[container]] site is lifted onto the feeding tier (#157) — **every** construction site in its room enters the feeding tier's Build, so the spawn site a human places is actually built. The mother's [[worker unit]] quota rises by `pioneerCount` (3, a tunable) until the child is independent. Independence is the tick our spawn stands in the child's room: a human moves the constant to two entries, the child begins running its own `decide`, and the mother's creeps standing in that room are adopted by it under 2. While the child is below RCL3 the mother keeps the pioneer addend, and the child's Upgrade and Build stay visible to the mother's workers — the one cross-colony borrowing rule there is, written down as a bootstrap exception and closed at RCL3.

**5. What does not move.** Every rule of the Matcher, the Atlas, the Layout and the quotas. They were already written for "a home room and its outposts"; what changes is that there is more than one of them.

## Considered Options

- **One projection over every room, with the Layout keyed by room.** Rejected: the quotas, the Task pool and the Matcher are colony-level, and mixed together W13S28's haulers would carry its energy back to W12S28's Storage — the ranking has no way to prefer the near sink, because there would be one colony's worth of ranking.
- **Let the bot rewrite the constant on the tick it becomes independent.** Rejected: `Outpost.declared` has always been a human's constant, moved in a commit (ADR 0039's precedent), and independence is exactly the kind of event a person should see happen rather than discover afterwards.
- **No pioneers — let the new spawn bootstrap itself.** Rejected by the user: a 300-energy bank starting from scratch is an order of magnitude slower than three workers walked over from a room already at RCL5.
- **A pattern row of its own for the claimer.** Rejected: `[Claim; Move]` is the [[reserver]] row's own block, and a second row of the same parts would be one body under two names — `patternOf` reads a body back off its parts (ADR 0006), so the casting order, the row's gap and the amortization would all have to be taught a distinction that does not exist in the body.
- **Pool Reserve and Claim on the same controller and let the Matcher choose.** Rejected: nothing between the pool and the Matcher reads intent. Inside a tier only [[travel cost]] speaks and both Tasks stand on the same tile, so the colony would hold the reservation of the room it is trying to take, at random, for as long as the tie held.

## Consequences

Decisions 1 and 3 land together (#189); 4's first half arrives with #190 and 2 and the rest with #191–#192, each adding its own consequences here.

- **`Colony.declared` replaces `Outpost.declared`**, and every reader moves with it: `Snapshot.build` looks its own entry up by the room its first spawn stands in, and a home nobody declared works no outposts — the behaviour the empty declaration shipped with (#124), rather than a state nothing downstream has a rule for.
- **The declaration reaches Core through the Snapshot** (`Snapshot.ColonyHomes`), never off the constant, which is the rule the declared outpost furniture already travels under (ADR 0041): the Planner derives what it pools from what it is handed. Which of those homes is a *candidate* is Core's, read off `RoomControl` — the shell passes on what a human declared and judges none of it — so the pool empties itself the tick the claim lands, with no state kept.
- **A new Task, a new Intent and a new binding**, and the five tables a Task rides — id, applicability, action, glyph, tier — gain an arm apiece. `Task.Claim` shares its name with `BodyPart.Claim` and is declared later, so a body part is spelled `BodyPart.Claim` from here on; both names are the engine's own and neither was renamed to dodge the other.
- **A candidate colony hires one body and stops being reserved.** The room's sources are worth five a tick from the tick the [[reservation]] the colony was holding runs out until the claim lands, which is bounded by the claimer's cast and walk *while the account can pay for the claim*; from the tick it lands the room is owned and worth the held ten again (`heldRateOf` prices an owned room at the held rate), so the economics do not move across the change of hands. A colony declared before there is a GCL level to spare has no such bound — the claim is refused every tick, the room stays unowned and unreserved, and Core has no GCL fact to see it with (below).
- **The engine can refuse the claim and Core will not know.** `claimController` answers ERR_GCL_NOT_ENOUGH when the account has no GCL level to spare; that code is logged by the Executor and read by nothing. The room stays unowned, so the Task is pooled again next tick and the body walks back to it — which is what every other refused act already does. What is not like the others is that this refusal can repeat forever, and the room it is refused over has left the reservation demands: a declaration written before the level is there costs that room half its income until a human reads the log. Carrying `Game.gcl` into the Snapshot so the pool could decline a claim the account cannot pay for is its own ticket, not this one's.
- **The [[reserver]] row is now two jobs on one body**, and its quota list says which is which by its entries: a reservation's deficit in blocks, or a claim's single block. That is the *demand*; the cast is the row's largest outstanding demand as it has always been, because which controller a finished CLAIM body holds is the Matcher's and a body cast at one block could land on a slipping reservation instead — so a claimer hired beside one is over-bought at that reservation's body, and the claim's own entry only ever keeps the row's size down. Nothing else in the pipeline distinguishes them, and nothing else needs to.
- **A nursery is read off three facts and keeps no state.** A declared home (`Snapshot.ColonyHomes`), owned by us (`RoomControl`), with none of our spawns standing in it (`Snapshot.Spawns`) — `Decide.isNurseryRoom`. It begins on the tick `claimTargets` stops answering for that room, so the two rules hand over with nothing carried between them, and it ends on the tick a spawn stands, which is *before* the human moves the constant and not because of it. Ownership is something vision pays for (ADR 0004), and the vision is the mother's, so a declared home nobody projects is no nursery — the same silence the Claim pool gives one. The colony's own home is excluded by name: `decide` runs only for a living colony, one whose home holds a spawn, so a home with no spawn is not a tick the rule is asked about, and #157's "home Build is untouched and stays Surplus" stays a sentence without a condition on it.
- **Every site in the room is feeding-tier, and the builders' budget does not reach them.** #157 lifted one site — an outpost's container — and named its own reason, that a standing container is the switch on whether that room is in the economy. A nursery is that question one level deeper, and the site it turns on is one a *human* places by hand, so the rule is the room and not the kind: a kind list here would be a list of the kinds a human is allowed to want built. The concurrent-builder budget stays behind, on the argument it was written with — two is what a 5,000-progress container is worth against fifty energy a trip, and what the budget rations is how much of the home room's surplus work a *third of the income* is worth, which is a question about a room the colony merely mines. So the exclusion is by room like the tier, and takes the nursery's own container site with it; the sites it still covers keep the budget they had, because a nursery's site is standing rather than finished and stays in the divisor. What that costs is not bounded: while a site stands in a nursery every loaded Work-part body in the colony may cross for it and the mother's own surplus Build and Repair go unheld, which is the price this decision was chosen at. The `pioneerCount` bodies below are what the mother *hires* for the job and never a ceiling on who takes it — nothing in the Matcher hires a body to a Task by the row it was cast for. The body gate travels *with* the tier rather than with the container (`isFeedingSite`), for the reason #157 already had: on the feeding tier no travel cost is left to pin an Anchor at its Post, and a heavy body's cross-room work is a Post and never a fifty-tile delivery (ADR 0020). A nursery is not Post-less — it is still the mother's outpost, so the container rule places a container on its source and the tick that container stands the room has a Post and hires an Anchor to it (`Atlas.postsIn`); the gate is what keeps that Anchor on it.
- **The pioneers are an addend on the worker row and are charged to nothing.** `pioneerCount = 3`, added on top of the whole generalist row and outside its floor, so a colony sitting at that floor with a nursery to build hires three more rather than none. It is an addend on the *target* and reaches the spawn through the generalist row's gate like every other generalist, so a specialist row standing over its own quota holds these three down with the rest of the row — the half of the whole-fleet gate #154 kept, unchanged here. The worker row and no other, because the job is a Build and the other three rows cannot take one. No term of the surplus answers for them, which is this row's pre-existing shape and not a new omission — the worker row's own replacement cost has never been deducted (ADR 0042 names it) — so a nursery's price to the mother is bodies and not a second arithmetic. Flat and not one per nursery: a declaration is written one candidate at a time, and two at once would share the three between them, which is a state the human who wrote them can see and retune.
- **The addend is hired off the room and not off the pool.** The quota rises while the child is not independent, whether or not a site is standing in it, so the crowd is already across the [[seam]] when the human's spawn site appears rather than starting a fifty-tile walk on that tick. What the mother loses meanwhile is three generalists' worth of its own surplus work, which is the price decision 4 was already chosen at.

- **The loop runs the colonies, and the phases stay the tick's.** `Main.loop` reads `Game.spawns` once, takes the living colonies off it (`Colony.living`: a declared home that is ours and holds one of our spawns), and then runs the tick's four stages *across* them — every Snapshot, then every `decide`, then every write, then one `Executor.run` over the concatenated Intents. Not colony after colony: the [[CPU line]] splits the tick at those four boundaries (#170), and a loop nested the other way would report one colony's snapshot phase and charge the second one's to `decide`. So each column is the sum over the colonies, the total is the whole loop's, and a two-colony tick reads as one tick that cost more rather than as two half-ticks.
- **A spawn room no declaration names is a colony of its own, and only when the declaration answers with nothing at all.** Decision 1 reads "a declared home that is ours and holds a spawn", and taken alone that hands a bot standing in an undeclared room a tick with no `decide` in it: nothing cast, nothing harvested, nothing moved, and no [[verdict]] to say why. `Colony.outpostsOf` already refuses the same shape one level down — a home nobody declared works no outposts *rather than* entering a state nothing has a rule for (#124) — and this is that sentence one level up: a slip in a constant a human moves costs the colony its outposts, never its whole tick. The fallback is the room the **first** spawn stands in, with no outposts, which is exactly what the shell read before there were colonies to declare, so a world the declaration does not describe decides today what it decided yesterday; and it fires only when nothing declared is living, so it can never add a colony beside a declared one and a room a human left out of the constant stays out. A respawn is the state that reaches it by itself, and every harness stub is the world that is it (`npm run profile`).
- **A Snapshot is cut by three handed-in facts and decides none of them.** `Snapshot.build` takes the colony whose declaration it is, the rooms the [[stand-down]] gate withholds *from that colony*, and the creeps that colony holds. The home room is the declaration's now and no longer "the room the first spawn stands in" — that reading was the single-colony assumption, and it is the one sentence of the shell ADR 0041 left standing. Everything the record carries is that colony's: its own spawns, its home room's bank and refillables, its controller, the rooms it projects, and — under `RoomName` — the room its Layout and its [[census signature]] are written for.
- **Adoption is cut once, above every Snapshot.** `Colony.creepColonies` reads the whole table of projections at once and files every creep under one colony: the one whose spawn its name carries, or — when it stands in a room *only* another colony projects — that one. It has to run before any Snapshot is built, because no single colony's projection can see the table. A room the creep's own colony projects too settles nothing (a [[mother colony]] works its [[nursery]] and keeps its crews), a room two others project names no single adopter, and a room nobody projects — a stood-down outpost — adopts nobody, so the creep there is still its caster's and still has no tile (#164 is answered only for the rooms some colony projects). A creep no spawn name claims falls to the first **living** colony rather than being dropped: dropped, it would be in no colony's `Creeps`, hold no assignment and stand still for the rest of its life. Living and not merely declared, and a creep whose caster's colony is not living falls there by the same rule — a home that runs no `decide` is a home with no `Creeps` to be in, so filing a creep under it is the drop wearing a name. The layers' `CreepPositions` are cut by the same set as `Creeps`, so a colony's geometry and its fleet cannot disagree about who is standing where — a body another colony holds is not an occupant this one's Resolver arbitrates against.
- **A nursery's third fact moves from `Snapshot.Spawns` to the projection.** Decision 4 above reads "no spawn of ours standing in it" off that list, which was every spawn in the world while there was one Snapshot a tick. It is the colony's own spawns now — the ones it casts from, banks for and anchors its Layout on — and a list holding the child's spawn would have the mother casting bodies out of a room she does not run, priced against a bank she does not hold. So `Decide.isNurseryRoom` reads a spawn *structure* standing in the room out of the kind census (`spawnStandsIn`), which is where its other two facts already come from. The rule does not move: the nursery still ends the tick a spawn stands, before the human's edit and not because of it. What it gains is vision's condition, and that costs nothing — a room the colony cannot see has no `RoomControl` entry either, so it fails the ownership half first.
- **Three leaves stay flat and two split by home.** `assignments`, `observe.creeps` and `observe.cpu` are keyed by creep name and by tick, where two colonies cannot collide: a creep is one colony's business for the tick, so the colonies' assignment maps are disjoint and the loop writes their union — the union and not the last colony's answer, because each Matcher drops what it was handed for creeps outside its own Snapshot, and writing one map alone would release every other colony's fleet. `observe.raids` and `observe.layout` move under `observe.colonies.<home>.…`: both answer for the rooms one colony works, the [[raid log]]'s ring is what the stand-down gate reads back per colony, and one shared ring would let one colony's twenty home-room raids evict another's stand-down. The old flat leaves are not migrated — they read as absent, which is the empty log a colony's first tick already starts from, and the rings refill within one window (ADR 0028's own degradation).
- **`observe.mjs` takes `--colony <home>` on the three reads of those two leaves**, and defaults to the first key under `observe.colonies` rather than to a name of its own: the script cannot see `Colony.declared`, and the first key is declaration order because the loop writes in it. An absent subtree fails loudly the way an absent leaf always has — a bundle predating the split writes the old flat leaves and nothing there, and reading that as "no raids" is the confident false negative these commands exist to prevent.
- **Between the spawn standing and the human's edit, mother and child both run over the child's room.** Decision 1's rule is read off the world and fires the tick our spawn stands in the child's home; decision 4's constant is moved by a human, in a commit, some ticks later. Between the two the child is living while its mother still declares it an `Outpost`, and the room is in both projections: two Task pools hold its rocks, two Layouts plan it, the mother's `planOutpostContainers` and the child's plan both name sites in it, and its one-Seat Post can be filled by an Anchor of each — the mother's Matcher counts only her holders and the child's only its own. Adoption is deliberately inert there for exactly this reason: two colonies project the room, so it names no single adopter and the mother keeps her crews and goes on hauling that room's energy home. The window is not closed in code, because closing it means gating the child's `decide` on the human's commit, and decision 1 states the rule as a fact about the world (`Colony.living`) precisely so that a constant a human has not got to yet costs a colony its outposts and never its whole tick — the same sentence `outpostsOf` and the undeclared-spawn-room fallback are written under. What bounds it is the deploy the human is already making, and what it costs until then is a second Anchor that cannot stand and one room planned twice; nothing is written to Memory that the next tick cannot correct, because the two colonies' `assignments` are disjoint by membership and their raid and layout leaves are keyed apart by home. The mirror case is the [[nursery]] and needs no window: a child with no spawn is not living, so its room is the mother's alone.
- **The plan memo is per colony** (`Map<home, PlanMemo>`), because the [[census signature]] names one home room and one colony's plan recalled under another's census would place the wrong room's sites. Keyed rather than listed, so a colony that stops running leaves an entry nobody asks for instead of one another lookup can find.
- **The relation the outpost entry carried becomes a field the day that entry goes** (`Colony.Mother`). Until independence the mother–child relation is one declaration reading another's home, and it needs nothing else: the room is in the mother's `Outposts`, and that one sentence says who projects it, who mines it and who reserves it. The tick the human splits the declaration that entry is gone — and decision 4's borrowing rule still has the whole of RCL1 and RCL2 to run, over a room no list names any more. So the child carries the mother's name, and carries exactly what the outpost entry carried: which colony's workers may cross for this one's Upgrade and Build. It is spelled `Mother` and not `Parent` because CONTEXT.md's [[mother colony]] entry names "parent colony" as the synonym to avoid, and a field is where a vocabulary drifts first. A human's, like the rest of the declaration, and cleared by a human — leaving it standing past RCL3 costs nothing, because the rule that reads it asks the world for the child's level and stops on its own.
- **The window is closed by the scan set, and the level is read exactly once.** RCL3 is a fact about a room the mother does not own the way she owns home, and it decides whether she projects that room *at all* — so it cannot be read out of a Snapshot whose scan set is what it decides. The shell reads the declared homes' controller levels off the world (`Snapshot.controllerLevels`), Core turns them into the rooms each colony bootstraps (`Colony.bootstrapping`), and three readers take that one answer: the scan set (`Colony.roomsProjected`), the narrowed layer those rooms are projected under, and the sources the colony pools. That is `Outpost.worked`'s shape and its reason — three readings of one subtraction, never three gates free to disagree — and it is why `Decide` asks for no level of its own: the tick the child reaches RCL3 the room leaves the projection, and the Upgrade, the Build, the pioneers' addend and the ownership entry every rule reads leave with it, from one subtraction. `Decide.isBootstrapRoom` is then the same three facts `isNurseryRoom` reads with the fourth inverted — declared, ours, not home, and a spawn *standing* rather than absent — so the two are complements over one room and one tick apart, and neither can answer for a room the other does. The subtraction is what closes the window, so it closes it for the shape the subtraction is cut from: a child that has **left** its mother's outpost list. While a human still declares the child's room an `Outpost` the room is in the scan set through the outpost reading, which asks no level, and `isBootstrapRoom` answers true there at any RCL — the borrowing and the addend run on until the commit takes that entry out. That is the window this ADR already names between the spawn standing and the human's edit, where the mother is also still mining the room, planning it and hauling its energy home; the borrowing joins that list and is bounded by the same deploy, and it is not gated at the predicate because a level read there would drop the mother's fleet by three on the tick the nursery ended and hand it back on the tick the human committed, against the flat addend below.
- **The mother projects the child's room, and carries only what she works there.** A bootstrapped child's home is another colony's home and not an outpost of hers, so its layer is narrowed to the ground a body walks on and three kinds of target (`Snapshot.narrowToBootstrap`): the controller her workers upgrade, the sites they build, and the spawn — which is not a target of hers at all but the fact that says this room is a child and no longer a [[nursery]]. Its rocks, containers, stock, hits, piles and tombstones are the child's own business, and the projection is the one place that can say so: carried, they would pool a Harvest on the child's rock, hire an Anchor for the Post the child already garrisons, count its Seats and its output into her quotas beside the child's own, and put its Storage's energy on the mother's haul across the [[seam]] — which is the single-projection arrangement this ADR's Considered Options rejected, arriving through the back door. What is left is ADR 0004's per-entry absence, the shape a room with no vision already has, so every rule downstream answers correctly without a clause of its own; the geometry is kept whole, because it is what the [[pioneer]]s walk over. `Snapshot.Sources` is swept over the mined rooms rather than the seen ones for the same reason and as the same subtraction.
- **The addend spans both states and stays flat.** `pioneerCount` is hired while a nursery of hers stands *or* while a child of hers is bootstrapped, which is decision 4's own sentence in two halves: the crowd that raises the spawn is the crowd that builds the child's first Layout, and a colony that stood one down at independence would take three bodies out of the room on the tick the work doubled. One addend across both, so the fleet is the same either side of the tick a spawn stands; and flat over children as it is flat over nurseries, because a human declaring a second child can see the three being shared and retune the number.
- **The child's Upgrade is feeding-tier work in the mother's pool, for `pioneerCount` bodies, and the two pools price one target apart** (#213, revising the surplus reading this bullet first carried). Read as surplus beside the home Upgrade, travel cost — a Seam and fifty tiles against five — kept every pioneer at home, and the addend was three more home upgraders: live at t~170,4xx the mother ran five loaded workers, four on her own controller and none across. So the lift is the hire: the borrowed Upgrade sits on the feeding tier (`tierOf`, `isBorrowedUpgrade`), capped at `pioneerCount` holders (`taskCapacities`) — the same constant the worker row rose by, so a human retuning one retunes both — and a [[standing body]] never takes it (ADR 0046: it holds no commuting body, and this is a commute). The fourth loaded body and every one past it price the home Upgrade like any surplus body. ADR 0007's deadline rank still sits above the feeding tier, so the mother's own controller inside its deadline pulls a pioneer home. The child's own tick pools the same target as its own controller, where none of this fires. The child pools the very same Task in its own tick off its own `Snapshot.Controller`, and each Matcher counts only its own holders, exactly as the two pools over a nursery's room do. What that pairing forced is one narrowing: ADR 0007's downgrade escalation is read off `Snapshot.Controller` and now had a second Upgrade in reach, so `rank` lifts the colony's **own** controller alone — the mother's timer sending her whole loaded fleet across the Seam on the tick her own controller was closest to downgrading is the exact opposite of what that escalation is for, and the child escalates its own on its own timer.
- **What the mother's other rules make of that room, they make by the rules they already had.** A site of the child's is the surplus Build every site outside a nursery is, except a *container* site, which is #157's feeding-tier switch by room and kind — and a bootstrapped child's container is exactly such a switch, on the income that ends the window, so it lifts and rides the builders' budget with the mother's own outposts' sites. Hostiles there enter her Threats, so ADR 0033's Reach and Flee cover the pioneers she sent. The [[stand-down]] does **not** gate the room: ADR 0043 withdraws a colony from a room it *mines*, and no home room is ever withdrawn from — gating it here would have the mother abandon a child the child does not abandon itself.
- **Adoption goes inert again for as long as the mother projects the room**, which is what the borrowing needs: a room two colonies project names no single adopter (decision 2), so the pioneers she hired stay hers to price, match and move — matched to Tasks that are in *her* pool. The tick the window closes they are adopted by the child like any other creep standing in a room only it projects, and the mother stops hiring them the same tick. The window ADR 0047 already named between the spawn standing and the human's edit is the same arrangement one step earlier, and it is now the ordinary state of a child colony rather than a gap between two edits.
- **A bootstrapping colony's Layout places no roads** (#209, amending ADR 0011). `bootstrapLevel` is read a second time, by the Layout, as `roadLevel`: below it the road plan is computed whole and none of it is placed. It is the same question this decision already answered — a colony under RCL3 is still buying the economy the roads would serve — so the two are one constant rather than two numbers free to drift, and the child's road sites arrive on the tick the borrowing ends. What it repairs is a failure inside the window and not at its edge: W13S28 at RCL1 planned 64 road sites against 8 energy a tick, and because the mother's pioneers pool the child's Build beside the child's own workers, the crowd this decision hires to raise the room was pouring into pavement while the 200 progress that unlocks the child's first extensions never arrived. The Upgrade the mother borrows and the sites she builds sit in the same surplus tier, separated by [[travel cost]] alone, so the nearer work wins — and a ring of road sites around the spawn is always nearer than a controller across the room. What the child's Build pool holds instead is its [[container]]s, released below the gate because the tile clause they waited on reads the road *sites* and there are none (ADR 0040 as #209 narrows it): W13S28 at RCL1 goes from one container beside its whole trunk set to three containers and no roads — some 15,000 energy of Build where there were 25,000 — and two of those three are the source containers that make a [[post]] and hire the [[anchor]]s the whole gate is protecting (#205). Two more of them land on this decision's feeding tier in the mother's pool by the bullet above, and her builders' budget is spread over three sites rather than one — which is the crowd doing what this decision hired it for, on the sites that end the window, instead of on pavement that does not.
- **And no ramparts** (#214, amending ADR 0034). The same constant, read a third time as `rampartLevel`: below it the covering rule places nothing and the Repair pool reads no rampart floor, so a rampart the engine allowed at RCL2 decays away instead of holding the loaded crowd to a 100,000-hit line derived for a home with a tower. The young Keep is one spawn and the safe-mode reflex is what stands in front of it.
- **A bootstrapping room builds its bank before its controller** (2026-09-06, user: "pioneer 都在升级没人建 extension … 小 worker 也都在升级"). A site standing in a bootstrapping room — the child's own tick and the mother's alike (`isBootstrappingSite`, one predicate over both readings) — is feeding-tier, and the borrowed Upgrade drops back to surplus while such a site stands, so the child's workers and the pioneers build the extensions (300 → 550, the Anchor body doubled) and upgrade after. In the mother's pool each such site is capped at `pioneerCount`; in the child's own pool its sites are its own workers' to crowd. The nursery's lift hands over to this one the tick the spawn stands, with no surplus tick between.
- **The four level readers and the two predicates read one `Stage`** (ADR 0052 decision 3, landed). `Colony.stageOf` derives `Nursery | Bootstrapping | Independent` off three facts of the world — ours, a spawn of ours standing, the controller's level — the shell runs it once a tick over the declared homes and every living colony's home (`Snapshot.colonyStages`), and it rides to every colony in `Snapshot.Stages`. `bootstrapLevel` is read there and nowhere else: `roadLevel` and `rampartLevel` are gone and both gates ask whether the colony is `Independent`; `isNurseryRoom` is `Nursery` beside the projection fact that says the room is *this* colony's; `isBootstrappingRoom`'s home half is `Bootstrapping` where it read `Controller.Level` itself; and `Colony.bootstrapping` takes the stages where it took the levels, raising a child at either stage before independence — a spawnless child off its mother's outpost list is a nursery she must go on projecting. What deliberately did **not** move is the borrowing predicate's reach: `isBootstrapRoom` answers for a child at `Bootstrapping` *or* `Independent`, because the stage is the world's answer and the pre-split window above is closed by the scan set alone — a predicate reading `Bootstrapping` alone would drop the mother's fleet by three on a tick no human touched.
- **What that costs, in both directions** (#216 R1, the same landing). The stage is not the level rule spelled differently, and two shapes move. It reads **wider** below independence: a nursery is a nursery at any level, so a child that stood its own spawn, reached RCL3 and then lost that spawn is raised again where `level < bootstrapLevel` orphaned it — the room is claimed, casts nothing, and only its mother can put the spawn site back up, so she pays the nursery's price over a grown room (every site in it feeding-tier and uncapped, plus the pioneer addend) until one stands. And it reads **narrower** where ownership goes: an unowned room has no stage at all, where the level rule read a 0 off any seen controller and bootstrapped it. So a **candidate** colony that names a mother and sits in no outpost list of hers is no longer projected by her — the user story above keeps a child in that list until the day it stands its own spawn, which is where every claim so far was pooled from — and neither is a declared child that stops being ours, so nothing pools a Claim to take such a room back and a human's edit is what recovers it. Both are inert on today's declaration, where W13S28 is owned and holds its spawn.
- **The Snapshot is a [[colony view]] now, and Core cuts it** (#216 R2a, ADR 0052 decision 1). The shell reads the engine once into a `World` — every room a standing colony declares and every room `Game.rooms` answers with, each under its own name, with every creep of ours beside them — and `ColonyView.ofWorld` cuts one colony's share of it: a **pure function**, handed the declaration, that colony's shut set, the creep holders and the world, deciding none of the four. Every rule this ADR states about which rooms a colony works, which bodies are its own and what it carries of a child's is therefore in Core, under test against a two-colony world (`ViewTests`), where until now the only way to run it was to deploy. That is the *cut*, and it is decision 8's first half only: the **read** — the terrain, structure and ownership classification in `World.ofGame` — is still compiled by no test project, so #137's own one-line reproduction stays green and #137 stays open. `Snapshot.build`, `Snapshot.projectedRooms` and `Snapshot.colonyStages` are gone; `World.living`, `World.stages`, `World.roomsProjected` and `World.creepColonies` are the same rules read off the world.
- **The narrowed bootstrap layer becomes a named borrowing** (ADR 0052 decision 7). What the mother carries of a child's room is unchanged — the controller, the sites and the spawn, over the whole ground, with the stores, the hits and the rocks left to the child — but it is `ColonyView.Borrowed.Rooms` that says which rooms are narrowed and `ColonyView.borrowed` that narrows them, in Core beside the rule that decides the set, rather than `Snapshot.narrowToBootstrap` in the shell. The **cap** on the borrowing is still the worker row's `pioneerCount` and not a field here: how many bodies cross is a quota, and it moves with the tunables (ADR 0052 decisions 5 and 6).
- **The declaration reaches Core as `ColonyView.Declared`** and off no constant of its own, which is what `Snapshot.ColonyHomes` already did — what changed is that the list is cut from the same `Colony list` `ofWorld` derives the scan set, the stages and the borrowing from, so there is one reading of the declaration per tick instead of two that could disagree.
- **The colony's bank is its home room's one account** (`ColonyView.Bank`, ADR 0052 decision 1 and 4). The per-room `RoomEnergy` map is gone and with it `richestCapacity`, the fold over every projected room's capacity: every spawn a colony casts from stands in its home room, so the fold had one entry to fold and would have read a child's 300 the day a mother's projection held a richer room than her own. The casting cascade folds one bank instead of a map keyed by the spawn's room, and a spawn record naming another room now draws the colony's bank rather than a zero one.
- **Another colony's bodies are visible, and not yet arbitrated** (`ColonyView.Foreign`, #220). A view carries where the creeps it does *not* hold stand in the rooms it works, cut from the same world layer its own fleet is cut from. Nothing reads it yet: the Resolver still arbitrates one colony's creeps at a time, so a mother's pioneer standing on the child's Anchor tile is still an invisible occupant to the child. The movement pass that reads it is one per room over every creep of ours, which is R2b's.
