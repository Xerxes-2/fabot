# An outpost stands down until a tick it reads off the threat

An [[outpost]] is a room the colony works and cannot hold. Its invasion clock is twenty times tighter than the home room's — the season's 1,000,000-energy grace is written only for the room that holds a spawn, and every other room uses `INVADERS_ENERGY_GOAL = 100000`, so a reserved two-source outpost at 20 energy a tick trips its first invasion in about 5,000 ticks and then every 70,000 to 130,000. Worse, a [[reservation]] does not keep an invader core out: `expandStronghold` tests only `!controller.user`, so a level-0 core can land in a room we hold, `attackController` it, and drain the reservation by 3 a tick against a 2-CLAIM [[reserver]]'s 2. This is not hypothetical here. W15S24, four rooms away, is a level-4 stronghold; its expansion frontier already stands at W14S23, two rooms from W12S27; it expands every 2,500 ticks; and it carries an `EFFECT_COLLAPSE_TIMER` ending at tick 170,283, after which this sector's invasion switch turns off until another stronghold spawns.

We decided **an outpost is stood down until a tick, and the tick is read off the threat rather than chosen by us**. The gate is a condition on ADR 0041's outpost constant: while a [[stand-down]] is in force the room does not enter the [[spatial projection]], so no Task pools there, no quota counts it and no creep walks toward it — which is the whole of "withdraw" in an architecture that keeps no state and recomputes every tick. The stand-down is recorded in the [[raid log]]'s episodic channel (ADR 0028), which is already colony-level, already episodic, already lives in `Memory.fabot.observe`, and already exists to record the thing a per-creep timeline structurally cannot. An outpost's threat episode is that channel's second family, not a new one.

The expiry has to come from the threat itself, and this is the load-bearing part. A gate that closes on sight and reopens on *not seeing* oscillates: the creeps withdraw, vision goes with them, the room looks clear because nobody is looking, the creeps walk forty-seven tiles back, meet the same core, and withdraw again — forever, because an invader core has 100,000 hits, spawns nothing, and never leaves. So while we still have vision we read the threat's own deadline — the core's `EFFECT_COLLAPSE_TIMER` end, or the `endTime` of the reservation it has taken — and hold the gate to that tick. Re-entry is a clock running out, not a look. Where there is no readable deadline, the fallback is 2,500 ticks, the stronghold expansion period.

The other trigger is the community's one unanimous abandonment rule and needs no clock: **another player owns or reserves the room.** That is visible whenever we have vision and is not a threat episode; it is a room that stopped being ours to work.

**This ADR decides no fighting.** Killing a core is the correct answer to a core and it is not this colony's next move: #66 established that nothing in the Task pipeline reads `Snapshot.Hostiles` at all — a worker being shot keeps harvesting until it dies — and a bot whose creeps do not flee at home has no business opening a front fifty tiles away. A remote guard and a core-killing behaviour are their own ticket, ordered after #66's answer.

## Considered Options

- **No automatic withdrawal: record the threat and let a human move the constant** (consistent with ADR 0041's declared outposts, and with ADR 0039's precedent of a constant a human moves). Rejected on the operator's absence: the stronghold expands on a 2,500-tick cadence and this colony runs unattended for days. A core landing in an outpost would kill a reserver, a miner and every hauler that walks in after them, on repeat, until somebody looked.
- **A gate judged only under vision, defaulting to open when blind.** Rejected: that is the oscillation above, at a period of roughly 150 ticks, burning a creep a cycle for the life of the core.
- **A gate plus periodic scouting to check whether the room has cleared.** Rejected: it re-introduces the scouting subsystem ADR 0041 declined, to learn a fact the threat already told us when we could see it.
- **Withdraw when the outpost stops paying** — the intuitive economic rule. Rejected because no surviving implementation does it: across six mature bots the abandonment triggers are ownership, danger and reachability, never profitability. An invasion is a *scheduling* fact, not an abandonment one, because its timetable is computable from our own extraction.

## Consequences

- **This extends ADR 0028.** The raid log gains outpost episodes beside spawn-room raids, and one field those did not need: an expiry tick. Its "a record to be read, never a signal sent" clause is narrowed exactly once and deliberately — the stand-down gate is the one reader that acts on the channel. Everything else still reads the Snapshot.
- **The gate can be wrong in the safe direction only.** A stale stand-down costs an outpost's income until its clock runs out; the failure it prevents costs creeps on a loop. Where the deadline is unreadable the 2,500-tick fallback errs long.
- **Tick 170,283 is a date worth watching, not background noise.** W15S24's collapse turns off invasion for the whole W1xS2x sector until another stronghold spawns. It belongs in the observe channel because it changes the conclusion, and this is the ADR that says so.
- **A second outpost is a second independent gate.** W12S27 and W13S28 stand down separately; three sources do not share one switch.
- The glossary gains [[stand-down]]; [[raid log]] gains the outpost family.
