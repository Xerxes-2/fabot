# An outpost stands down until a tick it reads off the threat

> **Amended by #275** on how the recheck stride is measured, and on nothing else it or #165 decides. #165's stride was written as an exact multiple of `Tuning.RivalRecheck` off the tick the gate shut on, which is a gate that has to be evaluated on one tick in five thousand or not at all — and a tick's evaluation is not guaranteed: the loop can throw before the fold writes the log, the engine cuts a tick short when the bot is out of CPU and the bucket is empty, and a deploy lands in the middle of one. Every tick lost that way silently cost the outpost a whole further stride of income, and the latch #165 exists to make falsifiable went back to being unfalsifiable for another 5,000 ticks. So the latch carries **two** ticks rather than one (`RivalLatch`): the tick the gate shut on, which dates the withdrawal for an operator (#117's US-20) and which no later look restamps, and the tick the **last look** was taken on, which the stride runs from — `tick - lastLooked >= RivalRecheck`. A look is owed from the stride onwards instead of due on one tick, so the first tick the gate is evaluated on pays it; taking the look stamps `lastLooked` whether or not vision answered, because the gate re-admitted the room to the scan and being blind in it is the common case rather than a look that did not happen. A record written before this reads its one tick as both fields, so a latch a live bundle is holding keeps exactly the next look the old rule would have given it. Three corners the two-tick shape opens, each shut here rather than left to be found: an entry that will **not** read as two ticks costs that entry alone and never the leaf around it — the room leaves the gate, re-enters the scan and the next look with vision decides it again, which is ADR 0028's row-by-row degradation applied to the one leaf whose documented use is being hand-edited, while `observe.mjs` refuses to print such an entry rather than dating it from a number it had to invent; a `lastLooked` **ahead of** the clock is a look owed now and not a look absorbed for a whole stride, because no look is taken on a tick that has not happened, and the alternative hands back the unfalsifiable latch #165 bought; and rolling the code **back** across this wire shape leaves the old reader unable to read `{ since, lastLooked }` at all, so every latched room stays shut with nothing logged anywhere — the leaf itself is unharmed and rolling forward recovers fully, and under a rollback that has to stand the way out is the hand edit this ADR has named since #136. What is unchanged: the latch itself and what triggers it, the re-admission being to the scan and to nothing else, the stride's length, and every deadline of the clocked family beside it.

> **Amended by #257** with a fourth deadline: a raid of plain [[invader]] creeps, clocked off the longest life among them. This ADR read a clock off an invader **core** and, since #165, off a rival's hold, and a raid with no core in it offered none at all — so W13S29 stayed open through a two-creep raid, the reserver row went on hiring one body per declared [[outpost]] and walking it into the fight, and in three hundred ticks the room took two reservers and a guard while the invaders kept full health. An Invader standing in a room nobody owns never suicides (the engine's suicide branch wants a controller owner), so what it has left is exactly what it will spend: the clock is read, not chosen, and takes no floor and no fallback. **It is read only for a raid the guard row's cap cannot beat** (`Decide.guardBlocksBeat` at `Engine.guardCap`, the same arithmetic ADR 0056 sizes the crowd with) — standing a room down withdraws it from the scan set, so a raid that shut the room on sight would hide its own hostiles and buy no guard at all. A raid two guards beat is a fight; a raid two guards lose is a room to leave, for exactly as long as the raid has to live. Where ADR 0056 decision 6 wrote that bound as _two casts spent_, this reads it up front off the raid instead: the same question asked before the bodies are spent rather than after.

> **Amended by #165** on the clockless trigger, which it splits in two, and on nothing else it decides. This ADR wrote "another player owns or reserves the room" as one fact needing no clock. Owned and reserved are one fact to the economics beside it (ADR 0042) and two facts to a gate that has to say when the room comes back: a [[reservation]] decays at one a tick, caps at 5,000 and ends on a tick the engine is already counting down, where ownership is the one trigger the engine gives no end for at all. So **a rival's reservation is a clocked stand-down** — an episode of this ADR's own ring, running to `RoomControl.Reservation.TicksToEnd` read on the tick the look was taken, with the basis naming it, and taking no floor under it because the floor #136 added exists for a core that re-takes the hold it lets lapse and a player's claimer that stops coming re-takes nothing. **A rival's ownership stays the latch** `RivalHeld` remembers, with one narrowing: the gate re-admits a latched room to the **scan** for one tick in every `Tuning.RivalRecheck` (5,000), counted from the tick it shut on, and to nothing else — the room is in no projection, no pool and no quota on that tick either, so the withdrawal stands through the look. Without it the latch is unfalsifiable by construction: this gate's own effect is to stop scanning the room, so the tick with vision the ADR says would clear it can never arrive, and #136's own resolve pass had to tell an operator that a hand-edit of Memory was the only way back. What is unchanged: the three threat deadlines and their order, the sampling only under vision, the fallback's length, the remembering of a conclusion between the ticks with vision, and "re-entry is a clock running out, not a look" — a rival's reservation now re-enters on exactly that rule, and the recheck is a look that re-admits a room to nothing but the scan.

> **Amended by #216 R4** on where the fallback clock comes from, and on nothing it decides. The 2,500 ticks is `Tuning.StandDownFallback` on the [[colony view]] (ADR 0052 decision 5) — the one number of the three this gate _chose_ rather than read, which is exactly what makes it a tunable — and it is read twice in one rule, as the deadline a clockless threat gets and as the floor under a hold too short to believe (#136's amendment above), so moving it moves both together. The number, the order of the three deadlines and the sampling under vision are unchanged.

> **Amended by #136**, the ticket that built the gate and so made a short clock observable for the first time, on two points. **The reservation branch may never answer earlier than the 2,500-tick fallback.** A core outlives the hold it takes — it re-reserves the controller the tick the hold lapses — so the end of a reservation is never the end of the core, where a collapse timer's end is exactly that, which is why only this branch takes a floor. Read literally the branch answers three ticks for a core that has _just_ `attackController`'d a controller nobody reserved (`endTime = gameTime + 1` plus `INVADER_CORE_CONTROLLER_POWER × CONTROLLER_RESERVE`), and "no path gets 'immediately'" is this ADR's own condition. Below the fallback the hold is not a readable deadline at all, so the clock is the one the colony chose and the recorded basis says so — rather than a maximum taken over the tick with the reason left standing, which would name a reservation that ends somewhere else. The order of the three deadlines, the sampling only under vision and the fallback's own length are unchanged. **And the clockless trigger is remembered rather than re-read.** "That is visible whenever we have vision" is true and not sufficient, because this gate's own effect is to withdraw the creeps that pay for the vision: judged fresh each tick it would reopen the room the tick after it shut it. So the rooms last seen in another player's hands ride the Raid log's leaf beside the episodes (`RaidState.RivalHeld`), each against the tick that look was taken on, written on the ticks with vision and held through the ticks without. The tick is no clock — nothing is measured from it — only the date this ADR's other family gets from its `Opened`, without which the closing of a gate leaves no trace an income drop can be lined up against. Nothing the colony does can then produce the tick that takes a room back out, which is this ADR's own reading of that trigger: not a threat that passes, but a room that stopped being ours to work.

An [[outpost]] is a room the colony works and cannot hold. Its invasion clock is twenty times tighter than the home room's — the season's 1,000,000-energy grace is written only for the room that holds a spawn, and every other room uses `INVADERS_ENERGY_GOAL = 100000`, so a reserved two-source outpost at 20 energy a tick trips its first invasion in about 5,000 ticks and then every 70,000 to 130,000. Worse, a [[reservation]] does not keep an invader core out: `expandStronghold` tests only `!controller.user`, so a level-0 core can land in a room we hold, `attackController` it, and drain the reservation by 3 a tick against a 2-CLAIM [[reserver]]'s 2. This is not hypothetical here. W15S24, four rooms away, is a level-4 stronghold; its expansion frontier already stands at W14S23, two rooms from W12S27; it expands every 2,500 ticks; and it carries an `EFFECT_COLLAPSE_TIMER` ending at tick 170,283, after which this sector's invasion switch turns off until another stronghold spawns.

We decided **an outpost is stood down until a tick, and the tick is read off the threat rather than chosen by us**. The gate is a condition on ADR 0041's outpost constant: while a [[stand-down]] is in force the room does not enter the [[spatial projection]], so no Task pools there, no quota counts it and no creep walks toward it — which is the whole of "withdraw" in an architecture that keeps no state and recomputes every tick. The stand-down is recorded in the [[raid log]]'s episodic channel (ADR 0028), which is already colony-level, already episodic, already lives in `Memory.fabot.observe`, and already exists to record the thing a per-creep timeline structurally cannot. An outpost's threat episode is that channel's second family, not a new one.

The expiry has to come from the threat itself, and this is the load-bearing part. A gate that closes on sight and reopens on _not seeing_ oscillates: the creeps withdraw, vision goes with them, the room looks clear because nobody is looking, the creeps walk forty-seven tiles back, meet the same core, and withdraw again — forever, because an invader core has 100,000 hits, spawns nothing, and never leaves. So while we still have vision we read the threat's own deadline — the core's `EFFECT_COLLAPSE_TIMER` end, or the `endTime` of the reservation it has taken — and hold the gate to that tick. Re-entry is a clock running out, not a look. Where there is no readable deadline, the fallback is 2,500 ticks, the stronghold expansion period.

The other trigger is the community's one unanimous abandonment rule and needs no clock: **another player owns or reserves the room.** That is visible whenever we have vision and is not a threat episode; it is a room that stopped being ours to work.

**This ADR decides no fighting.** Killing a core is the correct answer to a core and it is not this colony's next move: #66 established that nothing in the Task pipeline reads `Snapshot.Hostiles` at all — a worker being shot keeps harvesting until it dies — and a bot whose creeps do not flee at home has no business opening a front fifty tiles away. A remote guard and a core-killing behaviour are their own ticket, ordered after #66's answer.

## Considered Options

- **No automatic withdrawal: record the threat and let a human move the constant** (consistent with ADR 0041's declared outposts, and with ADR 0039's precedent of a constant a human moves). Rejected on the operator's absence: the stronghold expands on a 2,500-tick cadence and this colony runs unattended for days. A core landing in an outpost would kill a reserver, a miner and every hauler that walks in after them, on repeat, until somebody looked.
- **A gate judged only under vision, defaulting to open when blind.** Rejected: that is the oscillation above, at a period of roughly 150 ticks, burning a creep a cycle for the life of the core.
- **A gate plus periodic scouting to check whether the room has cleared.** Rejected: it re-introduces the scouting subsystem ADR 0041 declined, to learn a fact the threat already told us when we could see it.
- **Withdraw when the outpost stops paying** — the intuitive economic rule. Rejected because no surviving implementation does it: across six mature bots the abandonment triggers are ownership, danger and reachability, never profitability. An invasion is a _scheduling_ fact, not an abandonment one, because its timetable is computable from our own extraction.

## Consequences

- **This extends ADR 0028.** The raid log gains outpost episodes beside spawn-room raids, and one field those did not need: an expiry tick. Its "a record to be read, never a signal sent" clause is narrowed exactly once and deliberately — the stand-down gate is the one reader that acts on the channel. Everything else still reads the Snapshot.
- **The gate can be wrong in the safe direction only.** A stale stand-down costs an outpost's income until its clock runs out; the failure it prevents costs creeps on a loop. Where the deadline is unreadable the 2,500-tick fallback errs long.
- **Tick 170,283 is a date worth watching, not background noise.** W15S24's collapse turns off invasion for the whole W1xS2x sector until another stronghold spawns. It belongs in the observe channel because it changes the conclusion, and this is the ADR that says so.
- **A second outpost is a second independent gate.** W12S27 and W13S28 stand down separately; three sources do not share one switch.
- The glossary gains [[stand-down]]; [[raid log]] gains the outpost family.
