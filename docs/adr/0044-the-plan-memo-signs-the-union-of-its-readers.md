# The plan memo signs the union of what its readers read, not the widest single reader

ADR 0017 admitted the hauler quota to the census memo on a **containment** argument, and wrote that argument into its Consequences as the admission test for everything after it: *"The hauler quota rides the same memo only because its input set is a subset of the Layout's; anything census-derived added later may join, anything reading creep state may not."* That sentence stopped holding at #149 and was overtaken a second time at #169. The memo is still **right** — an unchanged signature still proves every plan riding it unchanged, which is the whole of ADR 0017's claim — but the reason it is right changed from containment to union, and the admission test as written would now reject the two readers already on the memo. We record the succession: **`Decide.censusSignature` signs the union of what its readers read, and a census-derived plan is admitted by widening the signature to its own inputs.** ADR 0017's third Consequence is now read as *"anything census-derived may join, and the signature widens to its inputs"*; the clause it keeps unchanged is *"anything reading creep state may not"*.

**Where containment broke (#149).** `Decide.haulerQuota` folds **every projected room's** source [[container]]s — `Snapshot.Spatial.TargetKinds` resolved through `SpatialInfo.placementOf`, so each container is carried beside the room it stands in — and prices each one at that room's own `RoomControl` rate (ADR 0042). `planLayout` reads the home layer's census, the home controller's level and the home room's name, and opens no other room at all. Neither set contains the other in either direction: the quota reads rooms and rates the Layout never looks at, and the Layout reads the home room's census **whole** — every built kind and every site, because it places against all of them — where the quota reads one kind, the source container, out of every room. The controller level is not the disjoint half it looks like, and this ADR does not claim it is: the Layout reads it straight (`storageGap`, `towerGap`, `extensionGap`), and the quota reaches it through `RoomEnergy.Capacity`, which the engine derives from the standing spawn/extension census *and* the level, so the signature carries the level on both readers' behalf — the reason `censusSignature`'s own comment gives for signing it. The signature widened to the union in the same ticket — `standing` spans every projected room, `held` carries `{room}:{rate}` per projected room — which is why nothing broke; what did not widen was the sentence explaining why that was allowed.

**And a second time (#169).** ADR 0032 had already put the spawn-origin [[walk]]s a [[lead]] is priced on onto this memo. #169 gave that table a far leg across the [[seam]], keyed by `(spawner tile, fatigue factor, goal room)`, and the far leg floods the **goal** room's own step-weight grid — the memo's second cross-room reader. It is not the first to read an outpost's *terrain-and-obstacles* grid rather than its census entries: the hauler quota has done that since #149, because `Atlas.haulRoundTripTicks` prices a container's round trip out of `weightsOf atlas fromRoom` — the container's own room — which is why the standing census signs every structure **kind** outside home and not `Container` alone: a road paved along the haul lane, or an invader core standing on it, moves a price the memo holds. What #169 added is a second such reader, and it is the tick that forced the signature's remaining home-only half, `pending`, to widen per room as well: `Snapshot.projectVisible` closes a tile under an obstacle-kind construction site in whatever room the site stands in, so a site outside home moves a grid the memo holds an answer off, and a census stopping at the home layer would have been ADR 0017's own signature gap — a lead recalled through ground the successor cannot cross, for the life of the census.

## Considered Options

- **Narrow the hauler quota back to the home room and keep containment.** Rejected: that is #149 undone. An [[outpost]]'s container is precisely the haul the row exists to size — 138 to 168 ticks unpaved, two haulers a container (ADR 0042) — and a quota blind to it under-hires the colony by a third of its income.
- **One signature per reader, each signing only its own inputs.** Rejected: three strings over one census is three chances to miss the same input, and the memo's value is that one comparison decides one recompute — `PlanMemo` is a single record crossing the tick with the Layout, the quota and the walk table inside it. Over-invalidating is the cheap error here (a recompute), where a missed input is the expensive one (a stall until a global reset). It is also the option that grows: every plan admitted later adds a string.
- **Hash the whole projection, or carry a coarse "a census entry moved" flag.** Rejected on ADR 0017's own ground: the signature must be derived **mechanically from the same queries the plans read**, so a test can assert one case per input that the signature moves. A hash over everything signs fields no plan reads — creep positions, stores, ticks-to-live — and would throw the memo away every tick; a flag answers "is the plan stale?" with something other than the plan's inputs.
- **Per-room signatures with per-room invalidation.** Rejected as costing more than it saves at this size, and recorded because it is what the next reader will reach for if the census tick grows: the Layout is anchored at home, the quota folds every room into one number, and only the walk table has a key that already names a room. Sharing that granularity would mean a memo per key rather than a memo per plan — a different data structure, not a different signature.

## Consequences

- **The admission test changes shape.** The question a new census-derived plan must answer is no longer "is your input set inside the Layout's?" but "is every input of yours in the signature, and is none of it creep state?". A reader who arrives at ADR 0017 for the test, adds a plan that reads a second room, and finds its inputs already covered because the Layout's were — the shape of the mistake #149 came within one ticket of making — is stopped by the banner on ADR 0017 pointing here.
- **The blast radius is colony-wide, and that is the price.** Any structure kind appearing, decaying or being demolished in **any** projected room moves the signature, so the Layout, the hauler quota and both legs of the walk table recompute together, even when the change is in a room only one of them reads: a road decaying in W13S28 recomputes the home Layout. Measured after #177, a census tick costs 8.41 ms against 2.58 ms on a quiet one (`npm run profile -- 300 20 --scenario outpost --census-every 50 --level 5`, local — the level named in the command so the figure does not silently re-baseline when the profile's default moves with the colony, as the outpost container plan's own figures name it), which is inside ADR 0041's revisit trigger of a 50 ms mean or any single tick above 80 by a wide margin. The condition to revisit is that trigger and not this ratio.
- **Both halves of the census name the room** (`{kind}@{room}:{x},{y}`), because two rooms hold the same coordinates and `Container@16,44` in either would otherwise be one entry. That is what makes "the signature widens to its inputs" mechanical rather than a judgement: a reader's new input is signed by naming where it is, in the one format both halves already use.
- **The set of projected rooms is itself signed**, through the per-room `held` half, which is keyed on `Snapshot.Spatial.Rooms`. A room the [[stand-down]] gate withdraws (ADR 0043) therefore drops the memo instead of leaving a stale cross-room answer inside it — the guard #169 needed once the walk table began holding answers about rooms the colony may stop scanning.
- **ADR 0017's other Consequences stand unchanged**: no timed fallback refresh, the memo is heap-only and dies with a global reset, a signature gap remains the failure mode the per-input tests exist to catch, and nothing reading creep state may join. So does ADR 0032's narrowing, now read through the union rather than through containment.
- **The glossary already says union**: the [[census signature]] entry names it, and the [[lead]] entry names the walk table's far leg as the second cross-room reader. This ADR is where the *decision* to widen rather than to narrow is recorded, which is what the glossary entry can cite and ADR 0017 cannot.
