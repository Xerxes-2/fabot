# Work-heavy bodies harvest only from a Post

> **Revised by ADR 0045** on one Consequence: "the fallback to the full Seat set is what carries RCL1–2" is the **home room's** alone from #159 onward. It is a *bootstrap* rule, and an outpost bootstraps through a reserver and a light builder raising the container instead (ADR 0042, #157) — so an unposted source outside the home room offers a Work-heavy body no tile at all: its Work Area is empty and its Harvest inapplicable to one. The narrowing to a source's Posts where it has any, and the inseparable half that a creep may act only from inside its Work Area, stand unchanged in every room.

Live observation of W12S28 (RCL3, tick ~65600) caught an Anchor (7W/1C/1M) commuting between a source and the controller on a ~39-tick cycle, digging for about 4 of them: both sources sat full while seven Work parts idled on the road. It had landed on an ordinary [[seat]] rather than the source container's, so the engine caught none of its overflow (ADR 0012); one CARRY fills in 4 ticks, Harvest went `inapplicable`, Upgrade became the only candidate, and the body walked five tiles to spend 50 energy and five tiles back. The other Anchor, on the container tile, was stable — the difference was where each happened to land. Nothing in the system pulled a heavy body onto a [[post]]: `buildWorkArea` offered Harvest every Seat of the source, and a creep already inside its Work Area stands still. We decided **Harvest's Work Area, for a body with more Work parts than Move parts, is that source's Posts when the source has any** — and, inseparably, **a creep may act only from inside its Work Area**: `mayAct` becomes Work-Area membership instead of a bare range test. Narrowing alone does not fix the bug. An Anchor pays 14 fatigue per plain step against 2 paid off per tick, so it fills its CARRY four ticks before it takes its first step; only the gate keeps it empty for the walk, and an empty walker keeps Harvest applicable, so [[travel cost]] — the same mechanism that has pinned Anchors since ADR 0006 — walks it onto the Post and leaves it there. The two halves are one decision: the gate changes nothing on its own (today's Work Area *is* the passable tiles in range), and the narrowing is inert without it.

## Considered Options

- **A preferred tile inside the Work Area, ranked in the Resolver's Move Intents** — rejected: it puts a second, invisible geometry rule beside the Work Area and leaves travel cost blind to it, so the Matcher still cannot tell a good footing from a bad one. The Matcher, Emitter and Resolver must price the same ground.
- **Pool Harvest per Post rather than per source** — rejected: it moves Task identity, Task capacity and ADR 0013's stocked-source shape to fix a standing-position problem.
- **Exempt a full off-Post heavy body from Harvest's capacity gate** (it arrives full, and the container catches everything after) — rejected as the weaker half of the same idea: it keeps the wasted digs and needs a capacity special case, where the action gate needs none and makes the [[work area]] entry's own words true.
- **Widen `catchesOverflow` to the source's other Seats** — rejected in the issue and here: overflow is a fact about the container under the creep, not a policy.
- **Give the Anchor row a second CARRY** — rejected: it buys commuting range for a body whose whole point (ADR 0006, ADR 0012) is to not commute.
- **Narrow Upgrade the same way for heavy bodies** — rejected for now: with the gate in place the observed loop has no trigger left, and narrowing Upgrade would strand a heavy body whose source is drained (ADR 0013) and whose Dual Seat is taken. It earns its own issue if drained-source commuting shows up in the numbers.
- **Cap a posted source's Harvest at its Post count**, so light workers never dig where an Anchor works — rejected as a separate decision about *who* mines a posted source, with its own failure mode at exactly the RCL2–3 moment this bug bites. Harvest stays capped by the Seat count.

## Consequences

- `Atlas.workArea` stays a pure function of the Task — `posts` itself depends on it, through the controller's Upgrade area — and the body-aware `workAreaFor atlas creep task` layers above it. The dependency is acyclic by construction because only Harvest narrows. Creep-free readers (`dualSeats`, `posts`, the Layout's swamp-trunk query) keep calling the base; every reader that has a creep — travel cost, first step, `mayAct`, the Resolver's Move Intents — calls the body-aware one. The narrowed set is memoised per Task beside the base areas (ADR 0017's shape), so `posts` is derived at most once per source per tick.
- The Atlas learns which bodies are Work-heavy (`workHeavy`, ADR 0016's predicate: Work > Move), and the Decide-side copy of that arithmetic retires — one definition now serves the Withdraw gate, the Anchor census and the Work Area.
- `mayAct` gains the ADR 0004 fallback it needs: a creep standing on a tile the projection calls impassable — an obstacle-type construction site dropped under it, which the engine allows to stay — is judged by range as before, rather than silently refusing to work until it moves. Everywhere else the gate is unchanged for light bodies: the base area is exactly the passable tiles in range.
- A heavy body walking to its Post is matched, says ⛏, and emits no action for the length of the walk. No new [[verdict]] narrates that: the Resolver's movement Verdicts already do, tick by tick, and an always-on "still not there" would write to the [[transition log]] on ticks where nothing changed.
- A Post that is genuinely unreachable makes Harvest inapplicable to that heavy body, exactly as an unreachable Work Area does for every other Task; there is no retry with the full Seat set. A broken Layout should surface as an unmatched Anchor in the Verdicts, not as a silent commute.
- Contention over a Post needs nothing new: an occupied tile costs the [[occupancy surcharge]], not infinity, so Harvest stays applicable and the Resolver arbitrates by rank and swap. The quota is one Anchor per Post, so heavy-on-heavy contention is transient.
- Before the first container is built and with no Dual Seat, a source has no Post and nothing changes — the fallback to the full Seat set is what carries RCL1–2.
- Deployment heals the live colony without a death: a mis-seated full Anchor spends its load at the controller once more, and the next empty tick prices the Post and walks there for good.
