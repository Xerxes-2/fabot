# The clustered horizon is derived from the level, not declared: `controller.Level + 1`

ADR 0011 set the [[layout]]'s clustered horizon at RCL4 and said to revisit it when RCL4 was in sight. ADR 0022 revisited it there and held. ADR 0039 moved it to RCL5. ADR 0055 moved it to RCL6. Each of those moves was made **before** the room reached the level, for the reason ADR 0039 stated and ADR 0055 re-stated:

> the clustered kinds are *sized* at the horizon and only *filtered* at the current level, so a room standing at RCL6 under a horizon of 5 computes an extension gap of zero and plans none of the ten the engine just unlocked.

On 2026-09-14, at t427,931, W12S28 stood at **RCL7** under a horizon of **6**, with 40 extensions, 2 towers and **zero construction sites**, entitled to 50 and 3. `gapAt Extension 6` is `40 − 40 = 0`, the sized window was empty, and the placement below it truncated an empty list however much RCL7 entitled the room (#341). `allowanceOf` knew level 7 perfectly well. The horizon was what was stale.

That is the fourth time this number has been wrong and the third time it has been moved. `Tuning.HorizonLevel`'s own doc comment predicted the failure, one level lower, and the prediction did not stop it — because a doc comment cannot notice a level-up and a human has to. **We decided the horizon is derived and not declared: `controller.Level + 1`.** There is no fourth move, because there is nothing left to move.

## What this replaces, clause by clause

- **ADR 0039, Considered Options**: *"A horizon of `current level + 1`, computed rather than declared — rejected: it makes the plan a function of the level, and ADR 0027's invariant — `reserved ∪ standing sites` unchanged as the Layout is built out — is what makes the plan deterministic across ticks. A horizon that moves on its own breaks it once per level instead of once, deliberately, in a commit."* **Replaced.** The argument is answered below rather than overruled: the invariant it protects is about a plan being **built out**, and a built-out room's plan does not step when the level does. What steps is the plan of an *unbuilt* room, and that plan has no standing sites to be inconsistent with.
- **ADR 0055, Considered Options**: the same rejection, cited by 0055 rather than re-argued (*"ADR 0039 rejected it against ADR 0027's determinism invariant and nothing here reopens that argument. Cited, not re-argued."*). **Replaced with its parent.** ADR 0055 carries the amendment banner, because ADR 0055 is the standing authority the code read until this commit.
- **ADR 0055, Consequences**: *"The next revisit is RCL7… that revisit is a design question and not a constant."* **Answered.** There is no revisit: RCL7 arrived and the horizon arrived with it. The *second spawn* half of that sentence is untouched and still open — the Layout is oriented on **the** spawn tile and has no rule for a second one. RCL7 reserving for a second spawn is a thing this ADR deliberately does **not** do: the horizon reserves extension and tower slots, `allowanceOf` answers `0` for everything else, and a second spawn needs a rule before it needs a reservation.
- **ADR 0011, decision**: *"The horizon truncates at RCL4"* — already history through ADR 0039 and ADR 0055, and now history in a second sense: it truncates at no fixed level at all.
- **ADR 0036, Considered Options**: *"The reservation is level-blind by construction (the tower's and the extensions' slots are both sized at the horizon, not at today's level), so what the invariant actually pins is that the *placement* filter only ever adds."* **No longer true**, and ADR 0036's invariant is restated below rather than deleted. That ADR carries a banner too, because it owns the sweep the restatement lives in.

## The measurement

ADR 0011's #211 amendment set the bar: measure the re-plan on the committed captures before shipping it. Done here on all three, at each room's **live** level, comparing the shipped constant horizon of 6 against the derived `level + 1`. Two readings per room — the plan a **bare** room computes (which is the plan itself, stated whole) and the sites a room carrying its **live census** would place this tick.

| room | RCL | horizon 6 → | Storage | Tower | Extension | trunk (roads) | containers | sites this tick, live census |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **W12S28** | 7 | **8** | unmoved (1) | 2 → 3, `13,41` joins | 40 → 50; 4 tiles leave, 14 join | **25, byte-identical set** | unmoved (2) | **32 → 43** |
| W13S28 | 6 | 7 | unmoved (1) | unmoved (2) | 40 → 40; `17,13` leaves, `15,17` joins | **29, byte-identical set** | unmoved (2) | 36 → 36 |
| W15S28 | 5 | 6 | unmoved (1) | unmoved (2) | **identical set** | **57, byte-identical set** | unmoved (0) | 61 → 61 |

**W12S28, the level-8 jump, and the one room this change actually moves.** `allowanceOf` answers `Extension, _ -> 60` and `Tower, _ -> 6` for anything above 7, so an RCL7 room's horizon of 8 reserves for sixty extensions and six towers where it reserved for forty and two. That is the widest window this derivation opens anywhere, and it was measured first for that reason. The bare plan gains fourteen extension tiles — `5,37 9,33 11,33 13,33 15,33 17,33 18,34 18,36 18,38 18,42 18,44 18,46 19,33 19,35`, a ring further out again — six onto row 33, six onto column 18, and two the ring does not describe (`19,35` beside the column, and `5,37` away west, which is the ordering reaching back for a tile the cluster had passed) — none of them [[working ground]] — and loses four, `10,38 10,40 12,38 13,41`, which are not lost at all: they are the tiles the *fourth, fifth and sixth towers* reservations take ahead of the extensions in the one ordering, plus `13,41`, which becomes the third tower's actual site. Six tower slots reserved, three placed at RCL7. **The trunks do not move**: both plans pave the same 25 tiles, the same set and not a comparable length, and the Storage, the containers and all four [[link footing]]s are unmoved with them.

Against the **live census** — 40 extensions, 2 towers and the Storage standing, on the tiles the shipped horizon gave them — W12S28 goes from asking for **32 sites to 43**: the ten extensions and the third tower #341 reported missing, and nothing else. The extensions land on `5,37 9,33 11,33 13,33 15,33 17,33 18,44 18,46 19,33 19,35` and the tower on `18,34`.

**No already-built structure's tile leaves the plan, in any room.** Measured explicitly, against every kind the Layout places and not the clustered ones alone: with the live census standing, the number of tiles a standing structure occupies that the plan asks for something *else* on is **0 before and 0 after**, in all three rooms, and no [[rampart]] over a standing Keep structure is lost. The mechanism is the one ADR 0055 relied on and this measurement checks rather than repeats: a standing structure is a **target**, `Atlas.buildableTilesIn` keeps its tile out of the ordering entirely, and `gapAt` subtracts the census from the horizon's allowance, so a widened window widens at its **tail**.

**W13S28 and W15S28 pay nothing today.** W13S28 at RCL6 places the same 36 sites either way — it stands at its full RCL6 allowance and RCL7's extensions are filtered out — and its plan moves by exactly one tile: `17,13` is an extension under the old horizon and the third tower's reservation under the new, with `15,17` taking the fortieth extension's place. W15S28 at RCL5 has a horizon of 6 under both rules and is **byte-identical in every kind**.

**What a level-up costs, measured over every spawn of every capture.** The derivation moves the plan when the *room* moves, so the price is per level rather than per commit — and it is a real price, an order of magnitude larger than the two captures first measured suggested. The first reading here was W15S28 from RCL5 to RCL6 (footings identical, 57 paved tiles at both, four of them moving — `10,26 11,27 12,28 21,25` for `10,27 11,28 12,29 22,25`) and W13S28 planned from `6,6` from RCL3 to RCL4 (32 paved tiles become 29, the three dropped being `10,4 11,3 12,2`). Both are true and both are the small end.

Swept properly — all 171 (room, spawn) pairs of the five captures, each **built out** at level *L* with its cluster standing and then levelled to *L+1*, counting the road tiles the room paved at *L* that the *L+1* plan no longer wants:

| transition | level-ups that abandon road | tiles abandoned | energy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RCL3 → 4 | 48 | 129 | 38,700 |
| RCL4 → 5 | 37 | 152 | 45,600 |
| RCL5 → 6 | 50 | 102 | 30,600 |
| RCL6 → 7 | 58 | 206 | 61,800 |
| RCL7 → 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **total** | **193 of 684 (28%)** | **589** | **176,700** |

The same sweep under the absolute horizon of 6 abandons **zero** tiles at every transition, from every spawn. That is not a coincidence and is the point: a level-blind reservation makes a bare room's road plan identical at every level *by construction*, so no level-up could orphan a road. Every tile in the table above is bought by this change.

The worst case is the tile the sweep already watches. **W12S27 from `32,2`, RCL4 → RCL5: 95 paved tiles become 35 — 60 abandoned, some 18,000 energy — and `src-0`'s spawn trunk is dropped whole.** Under the old constant this spawn was sealed at *every* level and never paved the 95; derived, the room builds the corridor and then walks away from it on the tick it levels. That is #105's mechanism (below) priced, and it is pinned beside the #105 record rather than left in this prose. The largest case in a room the colony owns is **W13S28 from `36,42`, RCL6 → RCL7: 111 paved tiles become 92, 34 abandoned, some 10,200 energy** — and RCL6 → RCL7 is the transition W12S28 made on the day this ticket was filed. `36,42` is a **stride tile of the sweep and not a spawn any colony stands on**, which is the distinction the next paragraph turns on: the table above is what the rule costs over arbitrary spawn placement, and it is not what this colony will pay.

**A source container's pick moves too, on ten of the 684.** A source container is planned onto the [[seat]] nearest its trunk, so a trunk that re-routes can move the pick out from under a container that is already standing: a second 5,000-energy site, the [[anchor]]'s [[post]] moving with it, and an orphan at the old tile. `W12S28 6,30` and `12,30` and `W15S28 24,24` at RCL4 → 5, four W12S27 spawns at RCL3 → 4 and RCL6 → 7, and **`W13S28 36,42` at RCL6 → 7, dropping `22,15`**. The level-blind reservation could not do this from a level-up either.

**What the three live spawns pay is almost nothing.** Planned from the tile each colony actually occupies, at the transitions each has left:

| room | live spawn | paved | what a level-up abandons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W12S28 | `12,40` | 25 at every level | **0** at every transition |
| W13S28 | `16,12` (Spawn2, ADR 0055) | 30 to RCL4, 29 from RCL5 | **1** tile at RCL4 → 5, already behind it; **0** from RCL5 up, so **0** at the RCL6 → 7 it faces |
| W15S28 | `18,30` | 57 at every level | **4** at RCL5 → 6, **2** at RCL6 → 7 |

So the whole realised future cost of this rule to the colony as it stands is **six tiles, some 1,800 energy**, all of it W15S28's. The 589 is the honest general number and the six is the honest local one; neither replaces the other, and a room re-founded on a different spawn tile buys the general number. Note that W13S28's live tile is **not** in the sweep — it carries no `AlsoSweep` entry, unlike W12S28 and W15S28 — so every W13S28 figure in the sweep above is a stride tile's and not the child's.

## Where the number lives now

**`Tuning.HorizonLevel` is gone. `Tuning.HorizonLookahead` replaces it, and it is `1`.** The horizon itself is `Tuning.horizonOf tuning level`, a function beside the record and not a field in it — `Tuning.keeperMargin`'s form, for `keeperMargin`'s reason and one of its own.

ADR 0052 decision 5 draws the line `Tuning` is: every number this bot **chose** rather than read off the server. A horizon is two numbers glued together, and only one of them was ever this bot's. The **level** is the server's, read off `controller.Level`; the **lookahead** is ADR 0011's standing bargain, one level and not four, and that half stays a tunable with a pairwise test on it exactly as every other field has. What made the old field wrong was not that it was in `Tuning` but that it was the **sum** — a chosen number standing in for a read one, which is precisely the shape that goes stale.

A lookahead of `0` is meaningful and means no lookahead: the clustered kinds sized at the level they are filtered at, which is what the horizon exists to avoid. A **negative** lookahead is the stale absolute constant written relatively, and it reproduces #341 exactly — which is how #341 is pinned in the suite, on the room it was found on. Nothing but a human setting the field below zero can produce that failure again.

## Considered Options

- **Move the constant to 7 and revisit at RCL8** — rejected, and it is the option the ticket opened with. It works, for one level, and then W12S28 reaches RCL8 and a fourth ADR says the same thing a fourth time. Three moves is enough evidence: the failure is not the number, it is that a number naming a level the room will reach has a date on it and nothing carries the date.
- **Horizon to RCL8, ending the revisits by exhaustion** — rejected for ADR 0011's original reason, undiminished and now measured: an RCL7 room's horizon of 8 reserves sixty extensions and six towers, and a room at RCL2 under the same rule would reserve for structures six levels away and hand its router a sixty-tile obstacle to dodge. What makes that cheap for W12S28 today is that W12S28 *is* nearly there. `openRoom 3` — a cramped room — pays for it at RCL2, which is the next bullet's other half.
- **Keep an absolute constant but assert it against the live level in a test** — rejected. A test that says "the horizon must be above the highest level any of our rooms stands at" is a constant with an alarm bolted to it: it fails the build on the tick a human is least able to respond, and it still needs a human. Deriving it removes the human, which is the whole decision.
- **Clamp the horizon at 8** — rejected as unnecessary rather than wrong. `allowanceOf`'s catch-all answers 9 exactly what it answers 8, so an RCL8 room asks for a horizon of 9 and gets its own terminal allowance. A clamp would be a second place to be wrong about where the top is. Confirmed by measurement: RCL7 and RCL8 plan identically — roads, containers, trunks and footings — from all 171 swept spawns.
- **Clamp the horizon at 0** — *taken*, and it is the same catch-all read from the other end. `allowanceOf` answers a **negative** level the sixty extensions and six towers it answers RCL8, so an unclamped `level + lookahead` would hand the *youngest* room the *widest* window in the table the moment a human set the lookahead to −2 or lower — the exact opposite of what the field means. `max 0` costs nothing (the whole suite passes unchanged with or without it at every shipped and tested value) and `QuotaTuningTests` reads it at a lookahead that reaches past zero. The top and the bottom are not symmetric: at the top the catch-all is *right*, and at the bottom it is an accident of a wildcard.
- **A lookahead per kind** — rejected, and named because it is what the tower's jump invites: `Tower` goes 3 → 6 between RCL7 and RCL8, which is the largest single step in the table, and reserving six tower tiles in a room that may place three is the biggest thing this change does. One lookahead for the clustered ordering is ADR 0022's "one ordering rule eats every clustered structure" applied to the window as well as to the order. A kind that wants its own horizon wants its own ordering, and that is a different ADR.
- **Separate the road reservation from the clustered sizing** — size the reservation the trunks route around at the level `allowanceOf` stops growing, and leave the *placement* derived — **measured and deferred to #344, not rejected**. Measured after this ADR was written, it takes the road churn priced above to zero and paves fewer tiles doing it. It is a different decision from this one and needs a successor ADR rather than a bullet here, because it answers ADR 0039's determinism objection instead of pricing it.

## Consequences

- **A young room's reservation *narrows*, which no ticket asked for and is the change's other half.** Under the absolute constants an RCL2 room reserved for the RCL5 or RCL6 cluster — two towers and thirty or forty extensions — whatever level it stood at. Derived, an RCL2 room's horizon is 3, which allows **one** tower. Two `LayoutPlacementTests` cases move on this: `placement.RCL2 on open terrain places 5 extensions checkerboard, nearest first` gets `26,24` back at the head of its list and loses `25,23` off the end, because only one tower's pick now sits ahead of the extensions; and `placement.the controller's tile is never chosen` goes from **four** extensions to **five**, which is ADR 0039's recorded cramped-room consequence *reversed*. ADR 0039 wrote that "a room short of candidate tiles pays for the lookahead at its current level" and named that exact fixture as the room that does. It now pays for one level of lookahead and never for four.
- **ADR 0036's monotonicity invariant is restated, not dropped — and the first restatement of it was vacuous.** Its form was "the tiles reserved at RCL *n* are a subset of RCL *n+1*'s", pinned on the 2→4 pair across the sweep, and it rested on the reservation being level-blind. It is not level-blind any more, and two **bare** rooms at neighbouring levels genuinely do not nest — a bare RCL5 room reserves two tower tiles where a bare RCL4 room reserves one, so its extension picks start a tile later. The first replacement written here was *"a room built out at RCL2 and then levelled to RCL4 is never asked to put anything but a [[rampart]] on a tile one of those structures stands on"*, and it constrains nothing: it stays green with the horizon reverted to a constant and green again with the rampart exclusion removed, because `withBuilt` registers a standing tile as an obstacle, `buildableTilesIn` drops occupied tiles from the ordering and the router routes around obstacles — so across the whole sweep nothing is *ever* planned onto a standing structure, and the assertion is implied by machinery pinned in three other places.

  What the sweep asserts instead is the claim #341 actually broke, per kind and against `allowanceOf`'s own table rather than against a second plan: **a room built out at RCL4 and then levelled asks for exactly what RCL5 unlocks, less what stands** — twenty extensions and one tower standing, ten extensions and one tower asked for, from every spawn of every capture. Read against the engine's table because a horizon wrong in the same direction at *both* levels satisfies a plan-against-plan comparison perfectly; read as counts and not as tiles because ADR 0036 forbids an expected value taken off real terrain. It reds the moment the horizon falls below the room's own level, which is #341's shape, at every spawn in the sweep.
- **The road gate's own test loses its level-invariance pin, and the gate is unchanged.** `the road gap is all the level withheld, and it drops whole` used to pin "RCL4 places what RCL3 places" for W13S28. That is now false — not because the gate paces, which it still does not, but because the plan it filters is a function of the level. "Drops whole" is pinned directly instead: pave the set RCL3 asks for and the same room at the same level asks for no road at all. W12S28's own invariance from RCL3 to RCL5, two tests down, still holds unchanged.
- **#105's sealed doorstep at W12S27 `32,2` becomes level-dependent, and is re-pinned rather than lost.** ADR 0055 widened every room's reservation to RCL6's forty tiles whatever level it stood at, which closed that spawn's corridor out and put the tile in the sweep's `SealedDoorsteps` beside `6,18`. The sweep plans at RCL4, whose horizon is now 5, so the tile routes there. It seals from RCL5 up, and a test of its own says so at every level from 5 to 8, naming the spawn trunk as the loss exactly as `6,18` does. #105 is still open and still the same mechanism — the working-ground exclusion guards [[seat]]s and the Upgrade Work Area, and nothing guards the spawn's own doorstep. What the derivation bought is that a room pays for it at the level it reaches rather than at RCL1.
- **The plan memo was already keyed on the level, and that is now load-bearing twice.** `Decide.censusSignature` signs the home controller's level, and has since ADR 0017 for a reason ADR 0044 states — the placement's allowance gate, and the bank Capacity the hauler quota reads. The horizon makes the level decide **which tiles the plan holds** and not only which of them go up this tick, so a memo not keyed on it would hand a room that had just levelled yesterday's *reservation*: #341 arriving through the memo instead of through a constant. Checked, found already correct, and pinned — `a level-up invalidates the memo` now also asserts that the two levels plan differently, so the recompute it checks is not a formality.
- **ADR 0027's determinism invariant holds for the *clustered* kinds and is broken for the roads, which is the price of this decision and not an oversight in it.** ADR 0027 is about `reserved ∪ standing sites` staying put **as the Layout is built out**, and building out is exactly what does not move the level. For a structure the clustered ordering places, the invariant survives a level-up too: a standing structure is a target, `buildableTilesIn` keeps its tile out of the ordering and `gapAt` subtracts the census, so the window widens at its **tail** and no tile a structure stands on is ever re-planned — 0 violations across the sweep, against every kind. For the **roads** it does not survive: the reservation the router dodges is now a function of the level, so a level-up re-routes a trunk that is already paved and orphans the pavement it leaves behind, 589 tiles over the sweep's 684 level-ups and up to 60 in one room in one tick. ADR 0039's rejection named exactly this — *"a horizon that moves on its own breaks it once per level instead of once, deliberately, in a commit"* — and it was right about the roads and wrong about the cluster. What this ADR decides is that the trade is worth taking, not that the cost is zero.
- **An abandoned road is not written off once; it draws upkeep for ever.** `Facts.hungryStructures` walks every standing structure and has no reference to the road plan, so a road the plan stopped wanting stays in the [[repair]] pool and is repaired at the [[hungry line]] for as long as it stands. Nothing in this change demolishes one. That is a standing cost on top of the 176,700 energy the table above prices, it is an existing gap in the Repair rule rather than a new one, and it is #342.
- **Three ADRs cite the horizon as the example of a constant a human moves in a commit**, and their example is now retired: ADR 0058 on `Tuning.MaxHops`, ADR 0057 on `Tuning.ReactorRoute`, and the [[outpost]] glossary entry on the declared room list. Those numbers are unchanged and the *shape* they claim is still real — a human does move them in a commit — but the horizon is no longer an instance of it. They are dated records and stay as written; the glossary entry, which is not, says [[outpost]] rather than horizon now.
- **Not one of the seven profile scenarios moves a decision line**, which was not the expectation and is worth writing down rather than glossing. `stub`, the default, `outpost` and `outpost --raided` stand their home room at RCL5, whose horizon is 6 under both rules, so they were never going to move. **Three** change horizon and none of them shows it, for reasons that are the design working and one that is a gap in the harness:
  - `young` stands W13S28 at RCL1. The horizon goes 6 → 2, the *placement* filter allows no extension and no tower at all, and `Tuning.BootstrapLevel` places no road — so the narrower reservation has nothing to show through.
  - `pair` stands an RCL5 **mother** and an RCL2 **child** (`DEFAULT_LEVEL.pair = 2`, `MOTHER_LEVEL = 5`): it is the child's horizon that moves, 6 → 3, and the child's plan does move with it — on W13S28's own capture from Spawn2 at `16,12`, `16,10` leaves the extension set and `17,11` joins. The *counts* do not: RCL2 filters to five extensions, no tower and no road either way.
  - `reactor` stands W15S28 at RCL6, where the horizon goes 6 → 7. The reservation widens to fifty extensions and a third tower, the placement filter stays RCL6's, and the **number** of sites the room asks for is unchanged. The **tiles** are not: on the capture the same move takes `19,31` out of the extension set for `17,25`, and moves four road tiles — `10,26 11,27 12,28 21,25` for `10,27 11,28 12,29 22,25`, the identical detour this ADR prices two sections above as W15S28's RCL5 → RCL6 cost.

  So the profile gate is **not** evidence that this change moves nothing; it is evidence that it moves nothing the harness reports. The harness reports hired counts, standing counts, stationing and stage rows, and none of those is a tile. It is also structurally unable to reproduce #341's own situation: `profile.mjs` stocks every Storage at 200,000 and `furnitureFor(rcl)` always furnishes the level's full allowance, so no scenario can stand an empty Storage or an under-built room (#343). The ms/tick differences across all seven are sampler noise in both directions (`reactor` 3.51 → 3.28, `outpost --raided` 5.01 → 5.13) with no hotspot entering or leaving the table.

## Glossary additions

Proposed for `CONTEXT.md`, not written here. All three are edits to existing entries rather than new terms — the horizon has had an entry since ADR 0011 and what changes is what it is made of.

- **[[layout]]** — "The clustered horizon is RCL6" becomes **`controller.Level + 1`**, derived and not declared, so it arrives with the room rather than ahead of it, and the only number a human chose is `Tuning.HorizonLookahead`. The entry's "a room built out at one level asks for exactly what the next unlocks the tick it lands" stands; its "and moves nothing it stands on" is narrowed to the **clustered** kinds, because the roads it does move are the price recorded above.
- **[[tuning]]** — the list of fields it carries loses "the horizon level" and gains "the horizon *lookahead*", the chosen half of a number whose other half is read off the server.
- **[[outpost]]** — the declared room list's analogy "a constant a human moves in a commit, exactly as the Layout's horizon is" loses its example, because the horizon is no longer one. It takes `Tuning.MaxHops` instead (ADR 0058).
