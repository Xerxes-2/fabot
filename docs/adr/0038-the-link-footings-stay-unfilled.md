# The Link footings stay unfilled at RCL5: the hauler row is already at its floor, and a link cannot go below a floor

ADR 0022 reserved the Link footings from level 0 and ADR 0027 made them count, both refusing to say what a link is for: the order the footings fill in and what the links send where was RCL5's design. This ADR spends that deferral, and spends it on a refusal — **this colony builds no links at RCL5**. The footings stay reserved, because their tiles never come back and holding them costs nothing; nothing is placed on them.

The case for a link here looked strong and is not. W12S28's controller container lands on `7,39` and its east source container on `16,39`, nine tiles apart, which reads like the room's long haul. It is not the haul the colony pays for. **The hauler quota is measured to the spawn** (ADR 0012), and both source containers sit three and four tiles from it. At RCL4 the room banks 1,300 energy, which sizes a hauler at sixteen Carry — 800 capacity — against a round trip of roughly ten ticks and a source that yields ten energy a tick: `ceil(10 × 10 / 800)` is **one hauler per source container**, and it takes a round trip of eighty ticks before that number becomes two. RCL5 raises the bank to 1,800 and the threshold to a hundred and twenty ticks. The room runs two haulers, and no link changes that.

So the whole prize is **one hauler body**: 1,800 energy over a 1,500-tick life, about 1.2 energy a tick. The price is two links at 5,000 each, plus three percent of everything that ever flows through them — 0.3 energy a tick at a source's full output. Ten thousand energy up front to net nine tenths of an energy a tick pays itself back in eleven thousand ticks, and that is the generous reading.

Even that reading is too generous, because the one hauler is not actually available to save. The quota sums **one term per source container, each rounded up**, so every term's floor is one and the row's floor is the number of source containers. This room sits exactly on that floor. A link's only possible prize is a body, and a floor is what a body count cannot go under — which is why the answer does not turn on where the wire is pointed, and the two candidate destinations fail symmetrically.

**Pointed at the controller container**, the wire does retire a hauler's job: the energy arrives in the upgrade buffer and the upgraders draw it themselves, so that source's term can honestly go to zero. It is paid for elsewhere. A wire to the controller hard-splits the room's twenty energy a tick into ten and ten, and ADR 0019's Work-part gate makes the upgrade half **irreversible** — no hauler carries it back to the spawn. Rank is how this colony decides anything: ADR 0010 layers Refill by target, ADR 0023 puts the Storage under the upgrade buffer, ADR 0012 puts the buffer under the spawn, and every tick re-judges where energy is most wanted. Ten thousand energy and a permanent tax to lose that over half the room's income, in exchange for one creep, is the trade this ADR refuses.

**Pointed at the Storage**, none of that applies and the wire is strictly better behaved: the energy stays fungible, haulers still distribute it by rank, ADR 0019's gate is not in the way, and a store of a million never backs the pair up the way a two-thousand container would. It also lands the east source's output one tile from the spawn instead of four, and four from the upgrade buffer instead of nine. And it saves **nothing**, because the energy still has to be carried from the Storage to the sinks: that source's quota term does not vanish, it re-anchors, and `ceil(2 × 10 / 1200)` from a Storage one tile off the spawn is one hauler again. The floor is unmoved.

A related error is worth recording because it was nearly shipped. A first draft of this decision dropped a linked source container's quota term to zero as a destination-blind rule. It is not destination-blind: zero is only honest for a wire to the controller, and a wire to the Storage re-anchors the term instead. The rule that was almost written would have stopped the east source feeding the spawn.

## Considered Options

- **Source → controller at RCL5** (the shape this ADR was opened to specify) — rejected on the arithmetic above: one hauler saved, the tiering spent, ten thousand energy and a permanent 3% to do it.
- **Source → Storage instead of the controller** — the *right* destination and still the wrong decision, which is why it is recorded here rather than dismissed: it keeps the energy fungible, costs no tiering, cannot back up, and shortens both of the east source's legs. It saves no hauler, because the Storage leg the quota re-anchors on is one tile long and the term rounds up to one either way. **When this room ever clears the floor, this is the pair to build** — not the one this ADR was opened to specify.
- **Build the pair anyway, as a cheap experiment** — rejected: it is not cheap. Ten thousand energy at the surplus tier is ten thousand energy of Upgrade not done, and the Layout is stateless — there is no "try it for a while" that does not mean building and then demolishing.
- **Leave the question open for RCL5 to answer** (do nothing, again) — rejected: ADR 0022 and ADR 0027 both deferred it, #77's second question has been waiting on it, and the deferral was already costing decisions elsewhere. The answer is cheap to compute and it is better recorded than re-derived.

## Consequences

- The footings keep doing the one job ADR 0027 gave them: holding tiles an extension would otherwise take forever. They are a reservation against a decision this ADR declines to make *for this room*, not against a decision nobody will ever make.
- **#77's second question is answered: `11,43` is not demolished.** Reclaiming the west source container's footing would buy a tile for a link this colony is not going to build, at the cost of 3,000 energy and one of its twenty extensions.
- The hauler quota, the Refill tiering and the Withdraw tiers are **unchanged**. So is `BuiltKind.Link`: a link built by anything else is still projected, censused and read back into the footing's candidates (ADR 0027), which is what keeps the footing stable if one ever stands.
- Nothing here says links are a bad structure — only that this room is the wrong room, and that the destination the question arrived with was the wrong end. **The answer flips when the hauler row leaves its floor**, which is a condition and not a feeling: when a source container's round trip to the spawn exceeds the eighty-to-a-hundred-and-twenty ticks that put a second hauler on the row, or when throughput rises past what one hauler's capacity covers. #83's remote mining is the live candidate for both, and a remote room's sources are far from this spawn by construction. A room whose spawn and controller sit on opposite sides of its sources is the other shape to watch for.
- The RCL6 revisit inherits a question that is now cheap rather than open: re-run the quota arithmetic against that level's bank and that tick's haul, and the same rule answers it.
