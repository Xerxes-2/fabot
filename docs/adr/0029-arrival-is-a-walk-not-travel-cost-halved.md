# Arrival is a walk, not travel cost halved: a traffic-blind clock beside the ranking price

One number in the [[atlas]] has been doing two jobs that want opposite things. **[[Travel cost]]** is a *ranking price*: it breaks rank ties in the [[matcher]], so it wants sub-tick granularity — a road step must price below a plain step for every body, or the [[trunk]] is scenery (ADR 0010) — and it wants to see crowds, so a traveller detours around standing traffic (ADR 0008). **[[Arrival]]** is a *clock*: the [[restock]] gate (ADR 0025) and the capacity-at-arrival window (ADR 0026) both ask "what tick can this creep first act?". A clock wants a floor of one tick per tile, because no body crosses a tile faster than that, and it wants to be blind to today's traffic, because a bystander who moves on next tick is not part of the walk. `CONTEXT.md` welded the two together — *"Priced in half-ticks (ADR 0010); halved, it is the creep's [[arrival]]"* — and two defects fell straight out of the weld.

**The halving had no per-step floor.** `stepUnits` floors a step at one *unit*, and a unit is a half-tick, so a body with a Move surplus priced a road step at half a tick. An empty [[hauler unit]] generates no fatigue at all and priced *every* step at one unit on every terrain: eight road tiles read as four ticks. A [[worker unit]] eight road tiles from a drained source was judged able to cover a four-tick wait it needed eight ticks to reach.

**The clock read the crowd.** Arrival carried the [[occupancy surcharge]] — 10 units, five ticks once halved. In a one-tile lane, where the flood has nowhere to detour, a bystander inflated a candidate's arrival, the walk appeared to cover the wait, the creep was dispatched — and the next tick the bystander had moved on, the arrival shrank below the wait, and the creep was released `TooEarly`. That is the wobble ADR 0025 exists to remove, re-entering through the price rather than through the pool.

The two were cancelling each other. Over every living creep on the live `W12S28` snapshot against both sources, priced empty — the shape the restock gate judges — no arrival fell below the Chebyshev floor as priced today, and **nine of fourteen** did with the surcharge removed and the halving kept. Fixing the traffic blindness alone makes arrival *more* wrong, not less; the per-step floor is its prerequisite, and the two must land together.

We decided **arrival stops being travel cost halved and becomes its own query over its own flood**. A new term enters the glossary — the **walk**: a traffic-blind path priced in whole ticks, no step below one tick.

- The Atlas answers `walkTicks` for a creep and a Task beside `travelCost`, over the same weights, the same body-aware [[work area]] and the same totality contract (ADR 0004): a creep or target the [[spatial projection]] cannot place prices 0, and an unreachable or empty Work Area has no walk at all, which its readers take as "no arrival" and count from now.
- A step costs `max(1, ceil(units / 2))` on the existing `stepUnits` value. The nested rounding is exact — `ceil(ceil(w·F / M) / 2) = ceil(w·F / 2M)`, the fatigue the step generates over what the body pays off in a tick — so this is the physical time of the step and not an approximation of it. The floor belongs on the step, never on the total: a path of eight tiles is eight ticks whatever it is paved with.
- **Travel cost is not touched.** It keeps its half-tick units, its per-unit floor and its occupancy surcharge, and it keeps the only job it now has: ranking. Matching behaviour is unchanged by construction.
- The flood memo's key gains a third dimension. It was `(tile, fatigue factor)`; it is now `(tile, fatigue factor, pricing)`, one lazy entry per pricing per placed creep, forced at most once a tick. One memo serves both floods rather than a second map laid beside the first, so the pair cannot drift apart, and a tick that asks for only one of them pays for only one. The pricing dimension carries *both* of the pair's differences — granularity and traffic — because nothing wants one without the other: a clock that saw crowds would be the defect above, and a ranking price floored at a tick would stop discounting the trunk.
- `Decide` converts nothing. The restock gate and the capacity-at-arrival window read the walk directly; the local half-tick conversion retires, and no caller halves anything.

## Considered Options

- **A step-count floor on the whole path** (`max(halved total, steps)`) — rejected: it is the same number only when every step is cheap, and it lies in both directions on mixed ground. A path of one swamp tile and seven road tiles would price at 8 by the step count and 9 by the truth; the per-step floor is the physical quantity and costs no more to compute.
- **Floor `stepUnits` at two units and keep the halving** — rejected: `stepUnits` is travel cost's own price, and two units a step is exactly the road discount ADR 0010 bought. It would fix the clock by breaking the ranking.
- **Give the walk a second memo beside the existing one** — rejected as the suggestion it was: two maps keyed the same way is an invariant nobody enforces. Widening the key is the same change with the drift designed out. (Make the change easy, then make the easy change.)
- **Leave the surcharge in arrival and damp the wobble with slack** — rejected for the reason ADR 0025 already rejected slack: N would be a number nobody can derive, and it treats a symptom of a wrong price as a tuning problem.
- **Make travel cost itself traffic-blind and whole-ticked, one number again** — rejected: the Matcher would be blind to crowds, re-creating the pile-up the [[seat]] caps exist to prevent (ADR 0008 weighed exactly this), and rank ties would stop seeing the trunk.

## Restatements

This ADR invalidates clauses of four accepted decisions, and restates the derivation behind a fifth. Those documents are not edited; what no longer holds is restated here.

- **ADR 0010's consequence** — *"any absolute reading of a cost in a Verdict or test must halve it to get ticks"* — no longer holds. Halving a travel cost yields nothing anyone should read: it is neither a rank nor a clock. A cost in a Verdict or a test is a ranking price, and the number of ticks is the walk, which is asked for by name.
- **ADR 0008's rejected option** — *"Surcharge in `firstStep` only, keeping `travelCost` terrain-pure. Rejected: the two answers must come from one flood (ADR 0004's one Atlas, one pricing)"* — is reopened here, at a different seam and for a different reason. 0008 was right that `firstStep` and `travelCost` must agree, and they still do: both are the ranking price, off one flood. The split is not between two routers but between a router and a clock, and one Atlas answering two questions with two documented prices is what ADR 0004 asks for. "One pricing" was a claim about consistency, and a memo keyed on the pricing keeps it.
- **ADR 0025's Matcher clause** — *"travel ticks (travel cost is priced in half-ticks, ADR 0010) ≥ ticks to restock. Half-ticks convert to ticks by rounding up"* — is replaced by: the creep's **walk** ≥ ticks to restock. No slack, and the rule is self-correcting exactly as 0025 argued; only the number on the left changes, and it now cannot fall below the tiles the creep must cross. 0025's Emitter justification — *"the occupancy surcharge (ADR 0008) prices a walk high, so a creep can arrive a tick or two early"* — is void: the surcharge is no longer in the walk, and a walk that never prices below a tick a tile cannot land a creep early.
- **ADR 0012's round trip** — *"per source container, round-trip ticks to the spawn (the canonical sink the trunks radiate from) × 10 e/tick ÷ carry capacity, rounded up"* — keeps its arithmetic exactly. What is restated is the derivation of its input, which 0012 never wrote down: the round trip was two floods in cost units, summed and halved on the total, and it is now two walks summed with no trailing conversion. The quota divides an honest number; it does not divide differently.
- **ADR 0026's slack clause** — *"The succession's whole margin is the [[occupancy surcharge]] on the incumbent's own tile: the lead is traffic-blind and the successor's arrival is not... five ticks is the slack the successor is admitted with at birth"* — no longer holds, because the arrival is traffic-blind too. The margin is spent: the successor's walk now meets the incumbent's remaining life at equality, so a successor born on the boundary reads its Post as full for the tick before the window opens. The rest of 0026 stands unchanged — the window is still read from both ends, an [[expiring]] creep is still never released over capacity, and the fold's name order still decides nothing.

## Consequences

- The Emitter's drained-source guard is **redundant rather than necessary**. ADR 0025 justified it by the surcharge landing a creep early; with an honest walk, early arrival is structurally impossible. It stays, as defence in depth: the `-6` spam ADR 0013 removed is worth a gate that costs nothing, and a guard that can no longer fire is the cheapest kind to keep. Its stated reason is the part that changed.
- Dispatch moves in both directions, and neither is a regression. Where the missing floor dominated, a creep leaves *earlier* — the paved eight-tile approach now covers an eight-tick wait rather than a four-tick one. Where the surcharge dominated, it leaves *later*: on the captured snapshot `worker-83265 → harvest:17,40` goes from an arrival of 9 to 5, and `hauler-84608 → harvest:9,44` from 8 to 6. A creep that leaves later now is a creep that was leaving too early before.
- One more flood per placed creep a tick, at worst — laid lazily, so only the creeps a time-aware gate actually asks about pay for one, and the memo makes it once however many Tasks ask. The flood is not what the walk costs, though: pricing one for every (creep, Task) pair put the stub colony's tick from 7.5 ms to 10.0 ms, because the per-pair price is a lookup on the flood memo's structural key and a fold over the Work Area, paid twice instead of once. The Matcher therefore *binds* the walk and forces it only in the two gates that spend it — the capacity gate on a capped Task, and the arrival gate on a Harvest — which brings the same tick back to 7.9 ms. Most of the pool is Refills, Withdraws and surplus work that asks for neither.
- The succession loses its five ticks of slack (above). ADR 0026's failure signal, `IdleReason.NoneFree` on a freshly cast Anchor, now fires for one tick on the boundary rather than never; the remaining gap closes when the [[lead]] is priced as a walk too, which is the sibling decision below.
- AtlasTests gain the walk's floor as a property over a fixture — for any body and any reachable goal, the walk is at least the Chebyshev distance — beside worked examples for the road corridor, the swamp step and the Move-surplus body whose two numbers deliberately disagree. `atlas travelCost` and `atlas road pricing` pass untouched; a diff there would have been a defect in this work.
- DecideTests pin the gate at the tick the walk equals the wait and one tick either side of it, and pin #78's reproduction inverted: the same Snapshot with and without a bystander in the lane decides identically.
- Sibling decision, since landed: the [[lead]] and the [[hauler unit]] quota's round trip each derived a units-to-ticks conversion of their own, and both now read this walk, so one rule turns units into ticks for the whole colony. The cast walk keeps every rule of its own — it starts *beside* the spawner where the engine places a finished creep, it prices the body empty, it was already traffic-blind — and changed only the conversion. The round trip prices each leg as a walk and sums them; its trailing halve-and-round-up is gone, because each leg is already whole ticks. ADR 0012's quota arithmetic is untouched: only its input moved.
- At home the fleet does not move, and that is the expected outcome rather than a fix that failed to land. The live room's two source containers sit two and three paved steps from the spawn; their round trips go 3 → 4 and 5 → 6 ticks, and the quota's `ceil(ticks × 10 / carry capacity)` stays 1 apiece — the live room's 16-Carry hauler wants a second body only past a round trip of 80 ticks, and the 4-Carry body a 300 bank casts only past 20. The error corrected bites at remote-mining distances, not at this room. Farther geometry does resize: the stylized eight-step W12S28 fixture in DecideTests goes from one hauler a container to two, because its 20-tick round trip is honestly 24. The [[lead]] moves by a few ticks at most — cast time is 3 ticks a part, 60–72 for the live bodies, and swamps a walk of single digits: the fixtures here move a worker's lead 19 → 22 and a hauler's 22 → 26, and leave the Anchor's 28 where it was.
