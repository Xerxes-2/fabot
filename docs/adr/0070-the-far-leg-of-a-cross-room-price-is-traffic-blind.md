# The far leg of a cross-room price is traffic-blind

> **Proposed 2026-09-18** out of the CPU round on #370; the numbers below are that round's and the decision is the user's. Nothing in the tree implements it.

A cross-room [[travel cost]] is joined on the [[seam]] from two legs: the creep's own near flood, and a **far field** flooded into the Task's ground and carried back across the chain (ADR 0041, ADR 0058). Since #358 the far field is held across ticks under a key that names, beside the chain and the body, the **standing crowd of every room the chain crosses** — because ADR 0008's [[occupancy surcharge]] is priced into it, and a field that priced a crowd is stale the tick the crowd moves. That key is what makes it the one flood in the bot that is rebuilt on a schedule nobody chose: an outpost's [[anchor]] stands still, a hauler stops beside its container to draw, a second one arrives, the first leaves — and each of those is a new key, so the whole far field is flooded again, and the whole chain behind it. The live probe (#370, 40 ticks at t539,03x) counted about **five such re-floods a tick, 9,464 heap pops — a third of every flood the tick runs**, while the traffic-blind far fields under `Walk` missed their memo on none. Every other cut this round was bit-identical; this one is not, which is why it is an ADR and not a commit.

We propose that **the far leg prices no traffic**: `farFieldAlong` floods every pricing over empty ground, exactly as `Walk` and `Baseline` already do, so every far field is a function of the census alone and rides the plan memo for as long as the census stands (ADR 0032). The near leg keeps the surcharge untouched: the creep's own room is where it is walking *now*, its own flood prices the crowd it will meet in the next few ticks, and its first step is read off that flood. The join changes in one term and the mover in none.

What the surcharge was doing on the far leg, stated precisely, is what the decision gives up. ADR 0008 priced the crowd for two readers: the mover, so a traveller detours around standing bodies within five ticks of its route, and the Matcher, as *"a mild pressure toward less crowded targets"*. Across a border the mover never read the far leg — `stepAcross` aims at the exit tile the join won and the body's next room is priced by its own flood once it stands there — so the far surcharge served the second reader only, and served it for a crowd the body would reach a room and many ticks later, by which time the crowd is somebody else's. The pressure it exerted was on the **ranking** of cross-room candidates against each other and against in-room ones, and it is small by construction: the surcharge is one swamp step (`occupancyPenalty = Engine.swampWeight`), a Work Area is a ring of several tiles, and the far field's minimum lands on a free ring tile whenever one exists. Where it would have mattered — the far room's only entry tile blocked by a standing body — the body arrives, its own near flood prices that tile, and it detours or waits exactly as ADR 0008 says; what it loses is the chance to have been ranked onto a different Task a room earlier. That is a ranking price becoming blind to a crowd it cannot act on, not a mover walking into one.

Two things follow that are worth more than the flood count. The three far-field tables collapse to one: `TravelCost` and `Baseline` differ in traffic and nothing else (ADR 0030), so under this decision they are the same field and the same key, and `FarFieldMemo`'s `LastTick`/`ThisTick` carry — the one-tick lifetime invented to bound a table that grew with the crowd — retires with the occupancy sign. And the second half of #171's goal-side direction opens: a far field that is the census's can be carried into the creep's own room under `TravelCost` too, as #171's `Walk` reading already is, once the mover's exit choice is settled — which this ADR does not decide.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the surcharge and key the far field on the crowd's *reachable* part only** — rejected: which standing tiles a field's paths cross is the flood's own answer, so the key cannot be known before the flood is run, and a key that must be computed by the work it guards guards nothing.
- **Keep the surcharge and refresh the far field on a cadence** (every N ticks, or when the crowd has moved more than K tiles) — rejected on ADR 0041's own ground: a price that is sometimes this tick's and sometimes last week's is a number nobody can reason about, and the ranking would flap on the refresh schedule instead of on the crowd.
- **Drop the surcharge from the near leg too, making `TravelCost` traffic-blind** — rejected: ADR 0008 weighed exactly this and the pile-ups the [[seat]] caps exist to prevent came back; the near leg is the half the mover reads and the half whose crowd is real when the step is taken.
- **Leave it, and buy the ms elsewhere** — the honest default. What it costs is the third of flood work above, growing with every outpost a colony works and every body that stops in one; the harness cannot see it (its bodies never move), so it will be paid live and measured only there.

## Consequences

- A cross-room candidate's ranking price no longer moves when a body stands still in a room along its chain. `MatcherVerdictTests` pins the tie-break tiers, not the far-leg surcharge; the test that would go red is one that stands a creep on a far room's ring tile and expects the price to rise, and none is known — the review round would say.
- `FarFieldMemo` becomes one table (`PerCensus`) and `occupancySign` retires; `Decide.decideUnarbitrated`'s carry of `TrafficFarFields` between ticks retires with it, which also closes half of #372.
- ADR 0008's Matcher clause — *"the surcharge flows into travel cost, so the Matcher also sees crowded approaches as dearer"* — is restated: it sees them in the creep's own room. ADR 0008 is not edited.
- Expected on the harness: nothing — the frozen world never moves a crowd. Expected live: the far re-floods vanish, roughly 9,000 pops of ~22,000 a tick after #171; the per-colony `decide` line is the reading, and it has to be taken with the intent count beside it, because the fleet's activity moved that line by more than any change did this week.
