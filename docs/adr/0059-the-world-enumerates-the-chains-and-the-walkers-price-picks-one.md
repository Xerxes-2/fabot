# The world enumerates the chains; the walker's price picks the one it walks

ADR 0058 decision 1 ends with a sentence that was true of every declaration in force on the day it was written and is false today:

> `RoomName.adjacent`'s fixed north-east-south-west order breaks ties, so the chain is a function of the world and not of the search.

It was true because it was untested: every outpost this colony declared at that moment was one hop from its home, and a one-hop pair answers exactly one chain. The tick a two-hop declaration landed, the sentence started deciding things. `docs/research/multihop-outposts.md` §3.2 measured what it decided — the compass order sends every south-west and north-west target round the *north* corner, because north is offered before west, and on the rooms this colony actually works that costs **+39, +40, +49 ticks a leg, up to +91%** against the cheapest chain of the same length. The ADR promised determinism and delivered determinism; what the reader heard was *the* chain, and there is no such thing.

#288 (`102312ea`) landed the correction: `RoomName.routesBy` answers **every** chain of the fewest crossings, and the cheapest is chosen where the price lives. `src/Core/Types/Geometry.fs` now quotes ADR 0058's sentence in order to contradict it, and `AtlasCrossRoomTests` pins the contradiction. Per `docs/agents/orchestration.md` § ADRs during the run, an accepted ADR is never rewritten even when the run proves its stated reason false: the code comment that repeated it is corrected, the test that pins the real invariant is added, and the successor is filed. ADR 0058 stands exactly as written. **This is the successor**, and it names every clause of its parent it replaces.

We decided **the enumeration of chains is a function of the world, and the chain a body walks is a function of the world and of that body's price**.

## The decision, in four parts

**1. The enumeration is the world's, and it is total.** `Atlas.routes` (`RoomName.routesBy` under it) answers **every** chain of the fewest possible crossings between an ordered pair of rooms, ends included, breadth-first over the name grid with `linked` — the Seam band ADR 0041 reads off two rooms' border rings — deciding which of a room's four grid neighbours a creep can step into. Memoised per **ordered** pair, the empty answer included, so `home → outpost` and `outpost → home` are two entries and are free to hold different chains. The shape of the answer changed with the count: **`[]` is where `None` was**, and it means the same thing it always meant — no chain inside `Tuning.MaxHops`, so every price into that room is `None` and by ADR 0004's totality the geometry counts against no Task.

Breadth-first still decides how *long* a chain may be, and that part of ADR 0058 is untouched: every chain offered crosses the fewest borders any chain could, a layer that reaches the goal is the last layer there is, and a room reached in an earlier layer is never extended into again. What breadth-first no longer does is pick one.

**2. The chain walked is the world's *and the walker's*.** `joinedAlong` prices every chain `routes` hands it and keeps the smallest `(sum, exit)` pair — the same minimum `joinedAcross` already takes over the crossings inside one band, taken once more over the chains, and for the same reason: the price is exactly the thing being chosen on. The reader that makes the choice is the one holding a **body, a pricing and a fatigue factor** — `pricedAcross` for a Task, `haulRoundTripTicks` for a quota, `castWalkTicks` for a lead — because those three are what make two chains of equal length different walks, and nothing above them has all three.

The load-bearing consequence is that one minimisation still answers both questions: the price and the exit come out of the same fold, so **the mover crosses at the border the price was won at** (ADR 0041's Seam rule as #142 landed it; ADR 0030's principle that a second derivation of one answer is a different answer and not the same answer from elsewhere). Two *pricings* of one journey may now win on different chains and that is ADR 0029's licence rather than a crack in it: `Walk` is traffic-blind and `TravelCost` carries the occupancy surcharge, so a packed corridor can move the mover to the other corner while the clock that plans against an empty world keeps the first. The disagreement lasts as long as the crowd does, and the alternative — letting this tick's standing creeps pick the chain a fleet is sized on — is the one ADR 0049 forbids.

**3. `adjacent`'s fixed order survives as the offer order and as the final tie-break, and as nothing else.** North, east, south, west, unchanged, in two places. It is the order `routesBy` *offers* its chains in, so a search whose remaining ties fell to a heap's whim cannot offer them differently on two ticks that read the same world. And it is the last tie-break inside `min (sum, exit)`: two chains that tie on the price fall to the lowest exit tile exactly as two crossings within one band do, and a chain that merely ties changes nothing the mover then walks. **Determinism is preserved and is no longer what picks the chain.** That distinction is the whole of this ADR: ADR 0058's sentence conflated the two, and the price of the conflation is measured in §3.2 of the research above.

**4. A round trip is the minimum over chains of the *sum of its legs*, and never the sum of two independent minima.** `haulRoundTripTicks` prices the loaded leg and the empty leg **on one chain** and minimises over chains at the end. The two legs are two *pricings* of one walk (ADR 0029), so the chain they share is the one thing about them that is not a body's business: a hauler that went out round one corner and came back round the other is not a journey anybody makes. Minimised per leg, the two minima sum **below every chain's own real trip** — 113 against a cheapest realisable 114 on the near fixture, and **147 against 182 on an amplified L, 19% under the best trip any chain realises**. This is the query ADR 0049 sums into the hauler quota, so the wrong shape here sizes a fleet for a haul nobody makes; it is #288's own opening complaint with its sign flipped.

## What this replaces in ADR 0058, clause by clause

ADR 0058 is accepted and is not edited. What follows is the list a reader needs to read it correctly today.

- **Decision 1, the last sentence** — *"`RoomName.adjacent`'s fixed order breaks ties, so the chain is a function of the world and not of the search."* Replaced by decision 1 and decision 3 above. The enumeration is the world's; the chain is the world's and the walker's price's; the fixed order is the offer order and the final tie-break. The clause was never wrong about determinism and was wrong about what determinism bought.
- **Decision 1, the query's name and answer shape** — *"`Atlas.route` answers the rooms a walk crosses, ends included … Memoised per ordered pair, `None` included."* Replaced: the priced readers take `Atlas.routes`, a `string list list`, memoised per ordered pair with `[]` included. `Atlas.route` / `RoomName.routeBy` survive as *the first chain*, which is what a reader with no price to choose with takes — `Outpost.routable`, which asks only whether a chain exists, and `Atlas.stepTowardRoom`, which is the stated exception below.
- **Decision 3's closing sentence** — *"`pricedAcrossInto`, `haulRoundTripTicks` and `castWalkTicks` each take the chain."* Replaced: each takes **every** chain and keeps the cheapest by its own price, and `haulRoundTripTicks` takes the cheapest over the whole trip rather than per leg (decision 4).
- **The Consequences' first bullet** — *"A one-hop price is the same price, to the digit."* **Untouched and re-confirmed.** A one-hop pair answers exactly one chain, so the fold runs once and the call is the one it always was, to the digit and to the flood. Every price pin standing before ADR 0058 and before #288 is green and unedited.
- **The Consequences' CPU bullet and its "+0.37 ms"** — untouched, and it does not mean what a reader coming from #288's close comment will assume it means. See the Consequences below; the two figures share their digits by coincidence.
- **Decision 2, `transitBetween`, and the detour cost it states** — untouched. Which rooms are projected is still answered off the names, the rectangle is still the interior of the name grid the two rooms span, and a detour around a walled border still lies outside it and is still not found. The research above re-tested that cost against every candidate in this box and found it never triggers here, on a parity argument — a chain's length has the parity of the name-grid Manhattan distance, so a detour is at least two hops longer than the shortest, which is either over budget or does not arise. It remains true and remains the price of cutting the circle.
- **Decision 3's "no flood ever leaves its room"** — untouched, and it is what makes this affordable. A chain is single-room floods laid end to end, k times over instead of once.
- **ADR 0041's cost argument that ADR 0058 was defending** — *"a minimum over thirty-odd additions rather than over thirty-odd floods"* — still holds, and holds for the reason ADR 0058 gave: the far leg is carried **backwards from the Task's ground**, so it does not depend on where the creep stands and one memo entry answers every creep in the colony. What this ADR multiplies is stated honestly in the Consequences and is bounded by three.

## The stated exception

**`Atlas.stepTowardRoom` still walks the compass's chain, and that is #297.** It is the mover of the vision grace (#151) — the creep whose Task left the pool with its target room's vision — and it takes `Atlas.route`, the first chain, because **a dark room prices nothing** and there is no far leg to rank the rest with. Before #288 the two agreed by construction, there being one chain. Now, for a room reachable two ways, the priced mover and the grace mover can walk a creep out of **opposite borders of its own room**, and which one it obeys flips with the target room's vision. `AtlasCrossRoomTests` pins the divergence on the `bothCorners` fixture, so closing it turns a test red rather than passing unnoticed.

This is the one place ADR 0030's principle — the mover goes where the price went, because a second derivation is a second answer — does not hold in this colony, and it is named here rather than left to be discovered. It is not decided here either: what a mover with no price is allowed to price is a decision #288 did not contain and this successor does not either.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the compass chain and accept the price** (the status quo ADR 0058 shipped). Rejected on measurement, not on taste: §3.2's table is +39 to +49 ticks a leg and up to +91% on rooms this colony works, and that number feeds `haulRoundTripTicks`, which feeds ADR 0049's hauler quota, which buys bodies. A tie-break that buys bodies is not a tie-break.
- **Reorder `adjacent`.** Rejected, and the research rejected it first: the order is the tie-break for *every* pair in the world, so turning one L-shaped target's chain the right way turns some other pair's the wrong way, with nothing anywhere saying which. It swaps a measurable systematic error for an unmeasurable one.
- **Choose one canonical chain per ordered pair and memoise that.** Rejected: there is no canonical price. The winner depends on the body's fatigue factor (a swamp corner and a plain corner rank differently loaded and empty), on the pricing (`Walk` against `TravelCost` against `Baseline`, ADR 0029, ADR 0030), and on where in its own room the creep stands. A memo holding one chain per pair would be a memo holding one body's opinion, and ADR 0049's own rule — a fleet is not sized off the creeps standing today — is the same objection at the quota's altitude.
- **Widen the memo key to `(pair, body, pricing, tile)` and memoise the chosen chain.** Rejected: that is the key `floodPriced` already carries, one level down, where it is paid for by a flood. Paying it again for a list of four room names buys nothing — the dear term is the far leg, and the far leg *is* already memoised per chain (`farFieldAlong`), colony-wide.
- **Price the round trip as two independent minima.** Rejected on measurement: 113 against 114, and 147 against 182 on an amplified L — 19% under the best realisable trip. It prices a journey no chain realises, and it does it silently and in the direction that under-buys haulers.
- **Search the chain in the shell over `Game.map.describeExits`.** Rejected again, exactly as ADR 0058 rejected it: the shell would own a derivation Core has to agree with tile for tile, and the two would be free to disagree about which chains exist. `seams` is the passability fact and it lives in Core.
- **Let `stepTowardRoom` take the priced chain too.** Deferred to #297, not rejected. It needs a decision about what a mover with no price may price, and that decision has consequences for the vision grace this ADR has no measurement for.

## Consequences

- **In steady state, on today's declarations, the mechanism costs nothing, and the measurement says so with its baseline named.** Five profile scenarios against `main` `73c57e1`, same machine and same session, with `docs/profiling.md`'s run-to-run spread at ~0.1 ms: stub 2.44 → 2.36, outpost 3.01 → 2.90, young 1.97 → 1.97, pair 4.62–4.71 (five runs) → 4.78, outpost `--raided` 4.92 → 4.89. Every delta is inside the spread, and an interleaved A/B/C on `pair` chasing an apparent +0.15 ms did not survive repetition. **This is structural and not luck: every pair this colony declares today answers exactly one chain.** W12S27 is one hop off W12S28, W13S29 and W14S28 one hop off W13S28, and W15S28 is two hops due west of W13S28 — a straight line on the name grid has one shortest chain. The mechanism costs nothing until an L is declared.
- **Two different "+0.37 ms" figures now exist and they are not the same number.** ADR 0058's Consequences record +0.37 ms as **the cost of declaring a two-hop outpost at all** — two more grids laid a tick, the chained flood per far field, and the outpost's own furniture and pool — measured against that branch with no such declaration. #288 measured +0.37 ms mean / +0.83 median on the **first tick** of a real two-hop L with both corners projected (k = 2), with overlapping ranges, which is **the cold-memo cost of enumerating and pricing a second chain for such a pair**; its steady state was nil there too, because the doubled cast-walk tables are built once per census. Two baselines, the same digits by coincidence. **Neither figure may be quoted without naming which.** An independent micro-benchmark put the marginal chain at about **+0.035 ms**, and ≲1 ms is the honest bound on a cold two-chain tick.
- **Only the enumeration is memoised, and the affordability argument has to be stated that way.** #288's ticket said the mechanism was affordable *because* the route is memoised per ordered pair. That is half right: `Atlas.routes` is memoised per ordered pair, but `joinedAlong` runs **per creep, per Task, per pricing**, so the pricing cost multiplies by k and no pair memo absorbs it. What makes it affordable is that **k = 1 for every pair worked today**. The multiplier, stated for the day it is not: **k far fields and k band joins per priced pair, k ≤ 3 at `Tuning.MaxHops = 3`** — the far fields memoised per chain colony-wide (`farFieldAlong`), the band scans and any `Resuming` relaxation paid per creep and memoised nowhere. `haulRoundTripTicks` is the one caller with no creep to key a memo on, so its far leg is `chainedInto` direct and a second chain is a second chained flood per leg — paid inside a census-keyed row (ADR 0017, ADR 0049), and nil today.
- **The declaration arithmetic in `multihop-outposts.md` §4 was taken under the old rule and should be re-read before anything is declared on it.** The table's prices are the *compass's* chain, which is what the code charged on the day it was written; today the same rooms would be priced at their cheapest chain. The two rooms the research declined on this ground — W15S27 and W15S29 for W13S28, at 146 and 141 against cheapest chains of 107 and 101 — are the two the correction moves most, and the recommendation that they wait for W15S28 was argued from numbers that are now 36% and 40% too high. Re-running `econ.mjs` against today's chooser before either is declared is a ticket, and it is the first real use this mechanism has.
- **`observe.mjs`'s refusal wording is behind the code and will misread.** `scripts/observe.mjs` still prints *"N declared outposts that do not border {home}"* and *"not a neighbour of {home}, so it is worked by nobody"*, where today's refusal may be "inside the budget and no chain reaches it". Both colonies read an empty refusal list, so nothing is being read wrongly yet. A small ticket, and it is the channel a human's declaration slip arrives on.
- **The profile harness has never executed a two-chain pair.** No scenario declares an L, so `joinedAlong`'s fold, the round trip's per-chain pricing and the second far field are paths `npm run profile` has never run — which is ADR 0056's finding restated one mechanism further on: a path no scenario executes is a path no profile has ever seen. A scenario with an L-shaped declaration, and its cold first tick recorded separately from its steady state, is a ticket of its own and is what the two "+0.37 ms" figures above would stop being confusable against.
- **A tie now falls to the exit tile rather than to the compass, and nothing observable moves.** Two chains that price equal are the same walk by the only measure anything downstream reads; the mover takes the lower exit, which is the rule inside one band, and the Verdict, the step and the quota are identical either way.
- **The glossary already carries this decision and needs no addition.** `CONTEXT.md`'s **Seam** entry was rewritten by #288 — *"since #288 which chain is the price's own answer and not the search's … A round trip keeps the cheapest chain over the whole trip and never per leg, or the number is one no creep walks"* — and **Transit room** and **Outpost** are unchanged by this ADR. What is owed is not a term but the correction above to `observe.mjs`'s prose.
- **`Outpost.routable` is unchanged, and deliberately.** It asks whether a chain exists, not which, so it takes the first and is right to: a declaration is refused when *no* chain reaches the room, and the cheapest of none is none. #243's loud refusal and `ColonyView.Refused` carry over untouched, and the test over the live `Colony.declared` is red on the same slip at the same altitude.
- **#297 is open and is the one place this ADR's rule does not hold.** It is named above rather than folded in, because the fix needs a decision about the vision grace that no measurement in this ADR reaches.
