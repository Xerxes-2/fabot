# The Anchor row casts into a vacant Post, and is charged one body a Post

ADR 0042 narrowed ADR 0021's Work ceiling "by changing its input and nothing else": the row's cap stopped being the held rate written as a constant and became `workCapOf` of a source's own rate, folded over the posted sources with `List.max`. Its Consequences promised the number would move — *"a lapsed reservation is now a visible economic event"* — and for this colony it never did. The colony's own room is in that fold and an owned room prices at the held rate, so `List.max` was `heldOutputPerTick` in every state a colony holding one posted home source can reach. W12S27's reservation could lapse, its rock could drop to five a tick, and the row went on casting `6W/1C/1M` at it: 700 energy and twelve a tick bought for a rock that gives five, for the whole of a 1,500-tick life, with `workforceTarget` amortising the same 700. The only place the number ever moved was a test that deleted the home room's Posts (`withoutHomePosts`).

#132 named the fix and deferred it, and ADR 0042's own #216 R4 banner refused it again, because narrowing the fold is a wider change than it looks. Two traps stood in front of it. **(i)** The unmanned Posts have to be paired to their sources *at arrival* (ADR 0026): judged by who is standing where, an ordinary home succession finds its own Post occupied by the incumbent it is replacing, falls through to whatever outpost Post is free, and buys the home room's replacement off a neutral rock — four energy a tick lost for a whole life, which is the error the fold existed to prevent. **(ii)** The amortization charges `anchorQuota × one ceiling`, which is the same mistake in the income base: a 700-energy home Anchor booked at 350 the tick an outpost's ceiling fell, and the number flickering over the ~50 ticks an outpost Anchor spends crossing.

We decided **the anchor row's ceiling is a fact about one Post, and a cast is bought for the Post it is filling**:

1. **One ceiling per Post** (`Decide.postWorkCapsOf`): a map from each Post's [[room position]] to `workCapOf` the rate of the rock that Post seats. The **ground** census (`Atlas.postsOf` — a Seat under a standing container, a Seat under a container site, a [[dual seat]]) and not the income one, because that is the census the row's own quota counts (`Atlas.postCount`), so the map holds one entry per Anchor the colony hires. A Post whose room the colony cannot price this tick keeps the **held** ceiling rather than leaving the map: the Post is there and the row hires for it, so the only question is which way to be wrong, and ADR 0042's asymmetry answers it — over-buying wastes 300 energy once in 1,500 ticks and still digs everything the rock has, where under-buying loses four a tick for a whole life.
2. **A cast is bought for the dearest vacancy** (`Decide.planSpawns`): the Posts with nobody standing on them who will still be there when a replacement could arrive, and the tick's casts are sized at the **richest** of those ceilings. This is what pairs a body to a rock without giving the cast a role. ADR 0021 refused to size an Anchor "by the Post it will man" because *"a spawn does not know which Post an Anchor will man — the creep chooses by matching after birth"*, and that refusal stands: the row is not sizing for a posting, it is sizing for the hole it is filling, and the finished body still lands wherever [[travel cost]] pins it. Which is exactly why the ceiling is the richest vacancy's and not each vacancy's own: a body bought for the neutral hole can land on the held one, so a tick that sized its second cast cheaper would be buying the very under-sized Anchor this rule exists to refuse. The richest is also what a gap outrunning the vacancies falls back to, and a bank that cannot pay for it yields the tick (ADR 0050) rather than buying below it.
3. **Judged at [[arrival]]** (ADR 0026), which is trap (i): an [[expiring]] incumbent is already outside the living count, so the Post it is still standing on reads vacant and its own successor is sized off its own rock. The home succession and the outpost succession are then two vacancies in one tick, and the row buys for the dearer of them — which is why a lapsed outpost's `3W` is bought on the tick its own Post is the only hole, the tick decision 5's lead opens it.
4. **The amortization is a sum over the Posts** (`Decide.surplusOverLifetime`): `Σ_Post bodyCost (anchorBodyFor cap capacity)` and no longer a quota times one ceiling. The map is the quota's own census read from the source end, so this is the row's charge one place at a time rather than a second count of it.
5. **A [[lead]] is priced at the body its incumbent's own Post will be cast** (`Decide.castBodyOf`, taking the tile the successor must stand on): ADR 0026 defines a lead as what the successor needs to stand *where this creep stands*, and where an Anchor stands is a Post. So an Anchor on the home room's Post is led by a six-Work successor while the Anchor on a lapsed outpost's Post is led by a three-Work one, in the same tick and off one rule. A creep on no Post — a body still walking to one — takes the richest ceiling, which is what `bodyFor` gives a caller holding no projection at all.

**`sourceOutputOf` is unchanged as a number and is now non-circular from both ends.** #208 capped a source's output at what the row's cast digs there and kept the ceiling itself on the rock's *rate*, because "capping that output by the body would size the body off a number the body decides". The cast it reads is now the one standing over the rock it is pricing (`workCapOf` of that rock's rate) rather than the colony's richest, and that moves nothing: the ceiling is `rate / 2 + 1`, so a body under it digs `rate + 2` wherever the bank affords the ceiling — over the cap either way — and where the bank affords less, the old colony-wide ceiling was larger still and clamped nothing. What it buys is that the charge and the credit come out of one rock's arithmetic.

## Considered Options

- **Leave the fold and accept the window** (ADR 0042's #216 R4 banner, and the status quo). Rejected now that the two traps have answers: the window is not small — it is every tick of every lapse, on the row that costs 700 a body — and ADR 0042's stated consequence was that a lapse becomes visible to the quotas that read a source one at a time. This is the last quota that read them all at once.
- **Derive the ceiling from `sourceOutputOf`** (the #158 spec's own wording). Refused as the authority refuses it: that output is the rate capped by what the row's cast digs, so a ceiling derived from it sizes the body off a number the body decides — at a 300 bank the cast is `2W`, the capped output four, the ceiling three, the next cast still `2W`, a circle that can only ratchet downwards. The ceiling reads `sourceRateOf`, which is ADR 0042's own #208 amendment and what the code already did.
- **Key the ceilings by source rather than by Post.** Rejected: the amortization and the vacancy walk both count *places*, and a source with two Posts hires two Anchors. A per-source map would have to be re-expanded by the Post census at both readers, which is the join this map already is.
- **Judge a vacancy by who is standing on the Post right now.** Rejected — it is trap (i), and it fails on the commonest event the row has: an ordinary succession.
- **Cast into the *cheapest* vacancy first.** Rejected on ADR 0042's twenty-to-one asymmetry: the Matcher pairs the finished body to a Post by travel cost and not by which vacancy it was bought for, so the cheapest-first order buys a three-Work body that can land on a held rock and lose four a tick for its life.
- **One seat per vacancy, each at its own vacancy's ceiling, richest first.** The first shape this ADR was written in, and not buildable on ADR 0050's cascade: a spawn takes the first seat its bank can pay for and *steps over* the seats it cannot, so seats of descending price are cheapest-first at every bank that falls between two of them — a colony with two held vacancies and a neutral one at a 1,300 bank bought `6W/1C/1M` and then, out of the 600 left, the neutral hole's `3W/1C/1M`, while both held Posts stood empty a few tiles from the spawn. Rejected for the reason directly above, which it reintroduces through the cascade rather than through the ordering. The Matcher is the whole of it: it does not pair the tick's k-th body to the k-th vacancy, so no ordering of differently-priced seats is safe.
- **Make the vacancy set a field of `RowSizing`.** Not buildable: `RowSizing` is what a [[lead]] is priced from, a vacancy is judged at arrival, and arrival is a judgement about leads. The vacancies are paired one step later, in `planSpawns`, where the expiring creeps are already counted out.

## Consequences

- **This supersedes ADR 0042's `anchorWorkCapOf` clause and the #216 R4 banner that re-deferred it.** ADR 0042 keeps its per-source *rate*; what it loses is the colony-wide `List.max` over it. ADR 0021 is untouched as a rule for the third time: saturation plus one spare, derived from a source's output rather than written as a literal.
- A colony whose Posts disagree now casts two different Anchor bodies on different ticks, and which one it buys this tick depends on which Post is vacant: the cheaper body is bought only once the cheaper Post is the dearest hole left. Two idle spawns in one tick therefore buy two bodies at one ceiling, or one and a yielded seat where the bank falls short of the second.
- The amortization stops flickering with an outpost's reservation across the whole row: a home Post is charged 700 whatever W12S27's controller says.
- The over-buy is still there and is now bounded rather than being the rule. Two shapes reach it: a colony whose vacancies disagree, where every cast of the tick is bought at the dearest of them, and a Post whose room the colony cannot price this tick, which keeps the held ceiling. A gap outrunning the vacancies is not a third — the gap is the quota less the living and the casting, and every Post a living Anchor mans is one the vacancy walk has already dropped, so the gap can never exceed the vacancy count — and the fallback in the code is there to make the expression total rather than to name a state.
- The `outpost` profile scenario reads the ceilings live — W12S27's Post prices at six Work reserved and three lapsed on the captured terrain — but its hiring loop cannot show the *cast* moving: it starts from an empty fleet, so the home room's two Posts are vacant and richest-first correctly buys them first, and it stations hired Anchors beside the Post tiles rather than on them, so the vacancy list never shrinks over the loop. The pairing is pinned in `DecideTests` instead, at `decide`.
- DecideTests: a vacancy is cast into at its own rock's ceiling, pairwise on the outpost's reservation; a home succession under a neutral outpost is still cast at six Work, and still is when the outpost's Post is the one standing empty; a bank that cannot pay for the dearest vacancy casts no Anchor rather than the cheapest one's body; a lead on an outpost's Post moves with that Post's reservation while the home Post beside it moves neither answer; the charge over five Posts at two rates is 2 × 700 + 3 × 400 and not 5 × 700.
