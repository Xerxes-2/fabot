# A crossing is only a crossing when the landing has ground beside it

> **Status:** accepted

ADR 0058 made the [[seam]] band one definition read by two holders of terrain, and said so in its own Consequences:

> `Seam.bandBy` / `Seam.joinedBy` now hold that geometry once, and the two callers differ only in where they keep their terrain — `Atlas.seams` over the ring grids it lays per tick, `World.linked` over the border maps themselves. One definition, so the scan set and the price cannot disagree about which rooms are joined.

One definition it was, and it was the wrong definition. A band is a fact about **two border rings**: this room's exit tile and the tile a creep stepping onto it lands on, both non-wall. Neither ring can see the row of the far room's own **ground** behind the landing tile — and the engine puts a body **on** the landing tile, from which it must step onto that ground or onto nothing. Where that row is gone the crossing is an **orphan**: real to the ring, real to `World.linked`, real to `Atlas.routes`, and a dead end to any body that takes it. The flood then prices `None`, because `joinedAcross` drops every such crossing on the far leg, and **nothing walks, silently** — the #243/#259 failure arriving through a join the scan set had asserted.

ADR 0060 decision 2 is what made this ordinary rather than freak. A [[keeper margin]] of six masks a room's ground *and* its ring, and a rock **seven** tiles from an exit row is **six** from the ground row behind it: the ring keeps its crossings and the ground behind them goes. Measured against `tests/Core.Tests/rooms/W15S26.room` at the shipped margin:

| W15S26 border | crossings the rings answer | of which orphaned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| north (landing `(x,0)`) | 20 | **7** — x = 37..43 |
| south (landing `(x,49)`) | 20 | 0 |
| east (landing `(49,y)`) | 7 | **7 — all of them** |
| west (landing `(0,y)`) | 8 | 0 |

Without any mask there is **not one** orphan landing on any of the four committed captures. This is the mask's artefact and not the terrain's, which is why it is decided here and not in ADR 0060: what the mask exposed is that the band was never asking the whole question.

#317 closed the one **exposed** reader — `Atlas.stepTowardRoom`, the vision grace's mover (#151), the one walker with no price — and deliberately left the model alone. **This is the model's answer**, and it names every clause of its parents it replaces.

We decided **a crossing is only a crossing when the far room's ground reaches the tile the crossing lands on**.

## The decision, in four parts

**1. `bandBy` and `joinedBy` take a third predicate, and it is the far room's *ground*.** Both now read `nearWalkable`, `farWalkable` and `farGround`, and a pair survives when this room's exit tile is on the near ring, the landing is on the far ring, **and one of the landing's eight neighbours is a tile of the far room's ground**. The eight are `Geometry.neighbours`, moved up from `Grid` so the Seam can see it: "beside" has to be the same eight tiles a flood steps through, or the band and the price would be free to disagree about a diagonal. `Seam.landsOnGround` is that one question, written once and asked by every reader.

The ring tests run first, so the ground question is paid for only by a pair both rings have already passed — on a walled border, none of the forty-eight.

**2. The ground is *raw* ground and never the walking grid.** `Atlas.seams` reads `groundOf` — the room's terrain with the keeper mask taken off it, the grid the walking one is copied from — and not `weightsOf`, which carries the tick's roads and obstacles besides. A band is **geometry**: if a road laid or a rampart raised this tick could change which rooms are joined, the scan set would move with the furniture, and `Atlas.routes`' per-pair memo would be keyed on a fact that changes under it. `World.groundWalkable` is the same reading off the world's own layer, terrain and mask and nothing else, so the two cannot disagree.

The residual is named rather than hidden: **a landing whose only ground neighbours are held by structures is still a crossing.** The price drops it on the far leg exactly as it always did — there is nothing for the flood to be reached at — and the one mover with no far leg keeps a filter of its own for it (part 3).

**3. `World.linked` reaches past the border maps into the terrain it already holds.** This is the cost this decision was taken knowing, and it turned out to cost no new plumbing: `World.ofGame` reads terrain for **every** declared and transit room whether or not there is vision (ADR 0031, ADR 0041, `Game.map.getRoomTerrain` needing none), so `RoomFacts.Layer.Terrain` has been in hand on every tick `RoomFacts.Border` was. What was true of the ring — *"what this can see is exactly what a walk could use"* — is true of the ground for the same reason and by the same code path. `linked` therefore asks the far side twice: its ring, for whether the engine lands a body there at all, and its ground, for whether the body can then step off.

The consequence a reader must carry: **a band is directed.** `linked A B` and `linked B A` are two questions about two different rooms' ground and are free to answer differently, where before they were one question read twice. Live, W15S26 ↔ the room across its east column is exactly that: **out** of the keeper room is still a crossing, **into** it is none.

**4. A declaration is admitted only where a chain runs *both* ways.** `Declaration.routable` — the one chokepoint every admission reader passes through, `Outpost.routable`, `Errand.routable`, `Declaration.refused` and `ColonyView.Refused` alike — asks `RoomName.routesBy` twice, out and home.

This is decision 3's directedness arriving where it bites, and it is the half the first draft of this ADR did not audit. `routesBy` expands away from `home` and evaluates `linked room next` in that direction alone, so every one of those readers was asking the outbound half of a question it meant symmetrically. A colony at W15S27 declaring anything in W14S26 was therefore **admitted** over the orphaned east border — reserver, miners and anchor hired and walked out — while `haulRoundTripTicks` asked `Atlas.routes container.Room sink.Room`, the direction that answers `[]`, and priced `None`. That is #243/#259's silent failure returning through the one direction the model had just learned to see, and it would have broken the invariant `Declaration.refused` states in its own doc: *"`pricedAcross` and `haulRoundTripTicks` answer `None` for every target in it"* — refused ⊇ unpriceable.

What a declaration buys is a **round trip**: the reserver walks out, the hauler comes back loaded, the errand brings its ore home. Nothing live crosses an orphaned border today, and "not live" is what #326 said about the east band before it was found.

Two searches and **not** a symmetric `linked`, which was the other shape available. `World.linked` is the world's reading of one band and `Atlas.seams` is the atlas's reading of the same one; ADR 0058's invariant is that those two cannot disagree, and folding the round trip into `linked` would have put the scan set's predicate one layer away from the price's — buying back the directedness this ADR spent three parts acquiring. The round trip is admission's question and is asked where admission is decided. Asking it as two searches also lets the two legs take **different** chains, which is what a directed relation permits and a symmetric predicate would have forbidden. The cost is one more bounded breadth-first search per declared room per tick, at `Tuning.MaxHops` = 3 over four neighbours, and it did not move any profile scenario.

What is deliberately **not** changed by it is `Atlas.routes` and every price below it: those are asked in the direction of travel and are right to be. `Atlas.haulRoundTripTicks` is the one that prices two directions off one chain, by the design #288 gave it, and it keeps that design — with its assumption now named in its own doc: it can be an under-estimate of a return walk whose bands are narrower, and it cannot be `None` against a walk that exists, because this decision is what guarantees the chain it asks for.

## What this replaces in its parents, clause by clause

ADR 0058, ADR 0059 and ADR 0060 are accepted and their bodies are not rewritten. What follows is the list a reader needs to read them correctly today, in the form ADR 0059 used for the same purpose.

**One banner is added, to ADR 0058 alone**, per this repo's convention that the banner sits at the top of the **amended** ADR and names the clause that no longer reads true (ADR 0004, 0006, 0011, 0013, 0029, 0051, and #317's banner on ADR 0004 for a decision recorded in the separate ADR 0060). ADR 0058's *"the two callers differ only in where they keep their terrain"* is a literal sentence this ADR makes false, and a reader of 0058 today would be misled with no signpost. **ADR 0060 takes none**: its own seam paragraph already carries #317's inline `**Insufficient, as #317 built it:**` correction, which names #326 and describes the orphan, so its reader is routed already. **ADR 0036 takes none**: it contains no such clause — see the last bullet below, which corrects an attribution this ADR's first draft got wrong.

- **ADR 0058's Consequences, the #259 paragraph** — *"`Seam.bandBy` / `Seam.joinedBy` now hold that geometry once, and the two callers differ only in where they keep their terrain … One definition, so the scan set and the price cannot disagree about which rooms are joined."* **Half replaced, and the better half kept.** One definition, still, and it is what made this change a three-line edit in one module rather than three edits that could have disagreed. What is replaced is *"differ only in where they keep their terrain"*: the two callers now keep **two** layers apiece, the ring and the ground, and the `World` half reaches into a map it did not read before. The clause's promise — that the scan set and the price cannot disagree — is what this ADR makes true, the disagreement having been between the band and the *flood*, which that sentence did not cover.
- **ADR 0058 decision 1, *"`seams` … decides which of a room's four grid neighbours a creep can actually step into"*, and ADR 0059 decision 1's restatement of it.** **Unchanged in what it decides and corrected in what it was answering.** "Can actually step into" is now literal: before this, a room whose every crossing was orphaned was one `routesBy` would extend a chain through. `Atlas.routes` and `RoomName.routesBy` are untouched; what moved is the relation they walk.
- **ADR 0058 decision 3, *"`joinedAcross` already took its far side as a bare `far: Pos -> int`; what changed is what is handed to it."*** **Untouched, and now redundant in one direction only.** The far leg still drops a crossing it cannot reach; what it no longer has to carry alone is the mask's orphan, the band having dropped it first. Every price pin standing before this change is green and **unedited** — the live chain's `castWalkTicks` is the 160 ticks `RoomSeamTests` has always asserted.
- **ADR 0059's stated exception, `Atlas.stepTowardRoom`, and #317's filter inside it.** **Narrowed, not retired.** The filter's stated reason — the mask's orphan, which is what #317 found — is now the band's and the mover inherits it. What the filter still carries is part 2's residual: the band reads *raw* ground, so a landing whose neighbours are built over is a crossing this mover must still refuse, and it has no far leg to refuse it with. It now asks `Seam.landsOnGround` over the walking grid, so the two readings cannot drift on what "beside" means. `AtlasSeamTests`' *"a landing whose ground is built over is still a crossing, and the grace mover still drops it"* is the pin; dropped, it is the only test that reds. ADR 0059's larger exception — that this mover walks the compass's chain — is **#297's** still and is untouched here.
- **ADR 0060 decision 2, the keeper mask.** **Untouched as a rule and answered as a consequence.** The margin is still six, still derived from the Reach, still declared by room name and terrain-blind, and still laid on the raw ground ahead of every other pass. What ADR 0060 wrote about its seam consequence — *"a mask near a room's border can take exit tiles out of a band, so a chain that exists over raw terrain may not exist over masked terrain"* — was right about the ring and silent about the row behind it. Its claim that the consequence is *"answered, loudly, by decision 1's third rule and by `Errand.routable` asking `linked` over the same masked layer"* did **not** reach the orphan, because `linked` and `bandBy` both read the ring and neither could see the ground. That claim does not stand unqualified in ADR 0060 and is not being corrected here for the first time: #317 already wrote the inline **Insufficient, as #317 built it** correction into that very paragraph, naming #326 and describing the orphan exactly, and a reader of ADR 0060 is routed by it — which is why that ADR takes no banner from this one. What this ADR adds is the answer rather than the diagnosis. It reaches the orphan now, through the same `linked`, at the same altitude, with no new channel: the loudness ADR 0060 promised is delivered by making the predicate right rather than by adding a second one. And the *round trip* is where that loudness had one more direction to reach — decision 4.
- **`RoomSeamTests`' own note under *"six costs the crossing nothing that five did not"*, which is a test comment and not a clause of ADR 0060** — *"measured over this capture the room's ground is cut in two at eight, and `routes` goes on answering with a chain — because it reads the rings … That is #326's silent failure."* **Half true, and the half that is false is the reason.** What this ADR closes is the *reason*: `routes` no longer reads the rings alone, and measured at a margin of eight the north border orphans **nothing** — the ring answers ten crossings, the band answers the same ten, and the predicate this ADR adds takes none of them. What it does **not** close is the failure the note names. At eight the room's own **interior** is severed between its two bands, so `Atlas.routes` still answers the full chain and the flood still prices `None`, exactly as the note says — and no landing-neighbour predicate can see a transit room cut in half. That is a **second** silent failure wearing #326's clothes; it is named under *What this ADR does not decide* below, and `RoomSeamTests` now pins its shape so that no later reading of this ADR can mistake it for closed. The number at eight stays #327's table's.
- **ADR 0058's own Consequences, *"a test world without a border ring models a world the shell cannot build"*.** (Not ADR 0036's, which contains no such clause — it is the rule ADR 0058 derived from ADR 0036's "real terrain is a counterexample generator" and stated in its own words.) **Widened by one layer**, and the fixtures moved with it exactly as ADR 0058's five `ViewTests` cases did: a test world with a ring and **no ground** models a world the shell cannot build either, because `World.ofGame` reads a room's terrain wherever it reads its ring. Seven fixtures gained the ground they had been leaving out. They got more faithful, not more permissive.

## Considered Options

- **Make `World.linked` symmetric and leave admission alone.** The one-line shape, and the first one proposed. Rejected: `World.linked` and `Atlas.seams` are two readings of **one** band, which is the whole of ADR 0058's invariant, and a `linked` that answered a two-band question would stop being the price's twin the day it was made the scan set's. It would also spend the directedness this ADR's first three parts exist to buy: every caller below would be back to a relation that cannot tell "out of" from "into". The round trip is admission's question and belongs where admission is decided — decision 4.
- **Leave admission asking one direction and let the price refuse the room.** Rejected on the failure mode, which is the one this whole line of tickets is about: the price refuses by answering `None`, and `None` is silent. The room would be projected, pooled and hired for, and `Declaration.refused`'s stated invariant — *"`pricedAcross` and `haulRoundTripTicks` answer `None` for every target in it"* — would be false in the direction nobody asked.
- **Leave the model alone and let each reader remember the far side.** The status quo #317 shipped. Rejected on the count: three readers already had to remember it — `joinedAcross` on the far leg, `carriedAcross` on the seeds, `stepTowardRoom` since #317 — and a fourth, `World.linked`, was never told it had to. A rule four readers must each re-derive is a rule one of them will forget, and one of them did.
- **Close it at `World.linked` alone, leaving `bandBy` as it is.** Rejected. It would make `linked` and `Atlas.seams` answer differently about the same pair of rooms, which is precisely the disagreement ADR 0058 wrote `Seam` to prevent, and it would leave `Atlas.routes` returning chains through orphaned bands for every reader below the scan set.
- **Read the far room's *walking grid* rather than its ground.** Rejected, and it is the tempting one: it would make the band agree with `joinedAcross` to the tile. It would also make which rooms are joined a function of this tick's structures and construction sites — so a rampart raised on the far side of a border would take a room out of the scan set, un-project it, and take with it the vision that saw the rampart. The band is geometry; occupancy is the price's business (ADR 0008, ADR 0029). The residual this leaves is stated in decision 2 and pinned by a test.
- **Have `World` carry a pre-computed "ground behind each exit" set per room.** Rejected as a second derivation of one answer (ADR 0030): it would be a fact the shell computes that Core has to agree with tile for tile, and the two would be free to disagree. The terrain map is already there and answers the question directly.
- **Drop `stepTowardRoom`'s filter as redundant.** Rejected on decision 2's residual. It is redundant against the *mask*, which is what it was written for, and not against a built-over landing. Dropped, no test reds today — which is why the test that makes it red was written rather than the filter deleted.
- **Give `Pos` a clamped `neighbours` while moving it.** Rejected: `tilesWithin` beside it is deliberately unclamped for the reason its own comment gives, and every caller drops off-grid coordinates through the walkability test it was applying anyway. Two neighbourhood functions with different edge behaviour is how a border case comes to be answered twice.

## Consequences

- **The live chain survives, and its price is unmoved to the digit.** W15S28 → W15S27 → W15S26 → W15S25 is still the one chain `Atlas.routes` answers, still three crossings, and `castWalkTicks` still prices the re-claimer's walk at **160 ticks** for a `[Claim; Move]` body from W15S28's Thorium seat — `RoomSeamTests`' cadence case is unedited and green, and so is the 434-tick cadence derived from it. What moved is a **direction**: the bands along that chain are 20 / 20 / 20 outbound and 20 / 20 / **13** on the return, the last leg losing x = 37..43 to the mask's orphans. Thirteen crossings is not a narrow chain; it is `AtlasSeamTests`' own "the band is narrowed rather than closed".
- **W15S26 and the room across its east column are no longer joined, and that is the point.** Every one of the seven crossings the rings leave open there is an orphan, so `World.linked` answered **true** for a join no body could use, `Outpost.routable` and `Errand.routable` would have called that room reachable, and the flood would have priced `None` in silence. It answers **false** now. Nothing live is declared across that border, so nothing the colony does today changes; what changes is that a human who declared it would be refused loudly instead of quietly. (The neighbour is **W14S26** and not W16S26 — `RoomName.offsetOf` — which is #336's correction and is the room the new tests name.)
- **`docs/research/third-colony.md`'s "20 / 20 / 20 全开" was stale and is corrected, dated, in its own §10.** That number was `seams.mjs`'s two-ring count and was exactly `bandBy`'s reading on the day it was written. The correction records the directional table above and the east border's collapse, and says what did not change: the chain, and its price.
- **The cost is at the edge of what this harness resolves, and the honest reading is "about +0.08 ms on a scenario that declares something".** All seven profile scenarios, before and after, against a clean build of `09b13970` on the same machine in one session, **five interleaved runs apiece** at `6 5` rather than one, means of means in ms/tick: default 2.96 → 2.87, stub 3.07 → 3.14, outpost 4.10 → 4.15, young 2.74 → 2.81, pair 5.78 → 5.94, reactor 4.46 → **5.00**, outpost `--raided` 5.97 → 6.27. The `reactor` figure is the only one outside `docs/profiling.md`'s ~0.1 ms spread and it is the six-tick run's warm-up rather than the change: re-measured at forty ticks, five interleaved runs apiece, it is 3.59 / 3.68 / 3.48 / 3.52 / 3.55 before against 3.53 / 3.52 / 3.58 / 3.69 / 3.72 after — 3.56 against 3.61, with two of the five running the other way. At forty ticks `pair` is 5.54 → 5.63, outpost `--raided` 5.54 → 5.63 and `outpost` 3.43 → 3.51.
  That residue is small, consistent and positive on every scenario that declares a room, and it is decision 4's second breadth-first search rather than the band's third predicate: the search is bounded by `Tuning.MaxHops` = 3 over four neighbours, `joinedBy` short-circuits on the first surviving pair, and it is asked once per declared room per reader of the scan set. **No perf issue is filed**, per the repo's standing rule: report the finding, file the issue when the cost actually hurts. Written down here so that the next run has a number to compare against rather than a claim of "nil".
- **And the reason it is not measurable is structural rather than lucky.** The third predicate is eight `Map.tryFind`s, paid only for a pair that has already passed both rings — so on a walled border it is never paid at all, and on an open one it is eight lookups against the forty-eight the band already walks. `Atlas.seams` reads it off the ground grid it already lays, at an array index apiece. `World.linked` short-circuits on the first surviving pair, as it always did, so the common answer costs one landing's neighbourhood and not forty-eight.
- **No decision line moved in any scenario.** Every line of all seven profile runs before the `ms/tick` line is byte-identical between the two builds: the same creeps hired, the same reservers, upgraders, errand and mine blocks. The change is invisible to every world the harness stands.
- **Seven test fixtures got more faithful, and two existing assertions' premises moved.** `RoomInvariantFixtures.acrossFrom` had said in its own words that *"a room with no ground at all still has its exits, which is the separation under test"*; that separation is what this ADR retires, so it now carries both captures' ground. `AtlasFixtures.bordered` lays a plain interior, `seamGround` gives each far room one ground tile diagonally behind each exit, `AtlasFixtures.keeperRoom` gives W15S28 and W16S26 the ground it already gave the other three, `AtlasSeamTests`' *"an exit tile is in nothing the projection offers to stand or build on"* gains a landing row for W12S27, `ViewTests`' hand-built world gains the inner frame behind its ring, and `ViewTests`' `keeperWorld` carries the captures' terrain beside their rings. The first assertion that moved is `AtlasSeamTests`' #317 case: its premise used to be *"the ring still carries the crossing at x = 37"* and is now *"the ring carries it and the band does not"*, which is the change stated as a test. The second is in the same file's *"no band, no ground and no path each answer with no walk at all"*, and it moved because `seamGround` can no longer produce what it asks for: its clause about **a room the projection carries no ground for** now stands its own projection, a ring with nothing behind it, and asserts that emptiness before asking the question — the shared fixture gives every ring room ground, and tuning that fixture to keep one tile groundless would have been a premise the fixture states and the assertion no longer tests.
- **One test world was genuinely a world the shell cannot build, and it was load-bearing.** `OutpostHaulTests`' Layout-invariance case stands the trunk fixture — ground from x 10..40, y 15..35 — as a colony with an outpost one room north, and the outpost's container Seat is picked on the walk out to the shared border. That home's ground stops fourteen tiles short of its own ring, so under this ADR nothing could cross **into** it and the outpost lost its container. The fixture now runs a corridor to its own border, on both sides of the comparison. It is worth reading as a finding rather than a fixture edit: a room whose ground does not reach its ring is a room nothing can enter, and that used to be invisible.
- **A band is directed, and every reader of one has to know it.** `Atlas.routes`' memo is already keyed per **ordered** pair (ADR 0059 decision 1), so nothing there needed to change. The readers, audited one by one rather than asserted:
  - **`Declaration.routable`** and everything above it — `Outpost.routable`, `Errand.routable`, `Declaration.refused`, `World.scanOf`'s two clauses, `ColonyView.Refused` — asked the outbound direction and meant the round trip. That is decision 4, and it is the one reader this ADR had to change.
  - **`Atlas.haulRoundTripTicks`** prices **both** legs off `routes atlas container.Room sink.Room` — one chain, two fatigue factors, by #288's design, *"the leg back being the same direction priced on the empty body"*. Harmless while bands were symmetric; now the return is walked over bands that may be narrower than the ones it was priced over, so the number can be an under-estimate. Kept as it is, with the assumption written into its own doc, because the alternative moves every hauler price and every fleet size for a defect nothing has measured — and because decision 4 is what stops it being worse than an under-estimate: an admitted room is one this direction has a chain for.
  - **`Atlas.carriedAcross`** is the one reader that consumes a band in the direction opposite to the one it asked for — always `seams hop.From hop.To`, folded either way. Traced and correct, and correct for a reason worth writing down rather than by luck: the ADR's predicate is the *far* room's ground beside the landing, and which room is far swaps with the direction the pair is read in, so on a backward fold the band's filter is exactly the ground that walk needs to reach its exit from, while `besideExit seedGround` supplies the other end. Its doc now says so.
  - **A test or a script that assumes `seams A B` and `seams B A` are the same list swapped.** `RoomSeamTests`' *"the band reads the same from the neighbour's side"* still holds on the four captures, and holds because none of them orphans anything, not because the symmetry is a rule — which that test now says in its own comment.
- **`Grid.neighbours` moved to `Geometry` and is `internal` there.** It is the same eight tiles in the same order, used by the same Atlas call sites; what it buys is that the Seam's "beside" and the flood's "beside" are one list. `Geometry` is `[<AutoOpen>]`, so the name is now visible across Core — the one thing a future reader should not do is shadow it locally.
- **What this ADR does not decide.**
  - **A transit room severed across its own middle.** The failure this ADR's own parent note describes is *not* closed by it, and saying otherwise would be the worst rot this repo could ship. Measured over `W15S26.room` at a margin of eight: the north border orphans **nothing** — ten crossings on the ring, ten in the band — `Atlas.routes` answers the full four-room chain, and `castWalkTicks` over that chain is `None`, because the room's ground is cut in two between its two bands and the walk stops inside it. A landing-neighbour predicate cannot see that by construction: both landings are fine, and what is missing is a path *between* them. Closing it means a band knowing something about the room behind it — a reachability fact, not a neighbourhood one — which is a different model change and is nobody's ticket yet. `RoomSeamTests`' *"a margin that severs the room's middle leaves the chain standing and the price gone"* pins the shape; the number at eight stays #327's table's.
  - Whether the *ring* should be masked at all where the ground behind it is not — the margin's question, #327's.
  - Whether `stepTowardRoom` should take the priced chain — #297's, untouched.
  - Whether an orphaned crossing should be visible anywhere an operator can see it: today it simply is not a crossing, and nothing names the fact that a border narrowed. That is a channel question, not a model one, and no measurement here reaches it.
  - Whether `haulRoundTripTicks` should price its return leg over the return chain — see the directedness bullet above, and the follow-up filed off it.

## Glossary additions

- **[[orphan]]** — a crossing whose landing has no ground of the far room's beside it: real to both border rings, and a tile a body arrives on and never leaves. Added to `CONTEXT.md` with the keeper margin's part in making one ordinary, its consequence for directedness, and what it is *not* (a landing whose ground is built over, which is still a crossing and the price's to drop).

No other term is new. **Band**, **crossing** and **landing** are ADR 0041's and ADR 0058's and are used here as they were defined there; what this ADR changes is which pairs the band holds, not what a band is called.
