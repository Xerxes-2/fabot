# Safe mode fires on sight of CLAIM; the downgrade timer is a hard deadline

Safe mode is the colony's only pre-tower defense, and the research survey (docs/research/seasonal-threats-safemode.md) found it sufficient against everything an early room actually meets — except the two ways it silently disarms: a hostile CLAIM tap on the controller blocks activation for 1,000 ticks, and a controller downgrade zeroes the banked stock outright. The colony that motivated this ADR died exactly that way: a claim wave locked the controller before any reflex existed, then repeat taps rode the downgrade timer to zero. We decided on two reflexes through the decide seam, both driven by new Snapshot facts. **First**, the Snapshot projects hostile creeps' bodies verbatim (`HostileInfo`, and `BodyPart` grows to the engine's full closed vocabulary), and any CLAIM-part hostile in a spawn room emits `ActivateSafeMode` the same tick — on sight, at the room edge, while firing is still possible. Fighters without CLAIM never spend the stock: one activation outlasts any invader raid 13×, so it keeps for when the room is actually being taken. **Second**, the controller's `TicksToDowngrade` becomes a deadline: within half the level's full timer (CONTROLLER_DOWNGRADE — 5,000 at RCL2, 20,000 at RCL4) Upgrade escalates from surplus rank to above the feeding tier. Half is not arbitrary: the engine itself refuses `activateSafeMode` once the timer sinks below half minus 5,000 (CONTROLLER_DOWNGRADE_SAFEMODE_THRESHOLD), so escalating at half keeps the first reflex fireable with the engine's whole 5,000-tick grace still banked — the two reflexes guard each other. The escalation lives inside `rank`, which becomes Snapshot-aware; Matcher and Resolver keep pricing from the same function, so urgent upgraders both win assignment and win contested tiles.

## Considered Options

- **Fire on any hostile** — rejected: spends the irreplaceable RCL2 activation on a passing invader raid that towers-later could shrug off; the research verdict is that only CLAIM threatens the controller, and only the controller matters.
- **Fire when the hostile reaches the controller** — rejected: a 2-CLAIM creep taps the tick it arrives, and the tap itself blocks activation; the only winning move is on sight.
- **Gate the reflex on `upgradeBlocked` / cooldown facts** — rejected for now: both merely make the Intent fail loudly in the Executor's log, which is the observability we want, at the cost of two Snapshot fields we otherwise never read.
- **A flat 5,000-tick deadline** — rejected: right at RCL2/3 by coincidence, but asleep at RCL4+, where the engine's refusal threshold (half minus 5,000) rises past it — the deadline would arrive after the safe-mode reflex was already unfireable.
- **Escalate Upgrade into the feeding tier (rank 0) instead of above it** — rejected: a deadline shared with Refill still loses the tie whenever the spawn is closer; "never let it downgrade" is an ordering, not a preference.
- **A persistent emergency mode** — rejected: both reflexes derive fresh from the tick's Snapshot; nothing about an emergency needs remembering, and per-tick rebuild is the architecture.

## Consequences

- `BodyPart` now carries the five combat parts our own spawns never cast. `bodyCost` and the Core's one part-name table (`partName`, whose reverse the Snapshot derives rather than restates) cover them totally, so a future pattern-table row (ADR 0006) can use any part without touching vocabulary again.
- The reflex can emit an Intent the engine refuses (cooldown, an already-landed tap): the Executor logs the failure code once per tick — accepted as the alarm bell, not silenced.
- An urgent Upgrade outranks Harvest for a loaded creep; a colony deep in deadline trouble deliberately starves its spawn before it surrenders the level.
- The deadline table (`fullDowngradeTimer`) restates engine constants in Core, like `directionCode` before it; a Screeps rebalance must be mirrored by hand.
- Hostile projection is body-only (`HostileInfo` has no position or id): threat *location* is a post-RCL3 concern (towers, ramparts — issue #19's non-goals), and the field list grows when a decision reads it.
