# The container plan defers to a target, not a tile: a moved pick keeps the container that stands, and the loss is recorded

ADR 0012 gave the [[layout]] its containers — one **source container** per source on the [[seat]] nearest that source's [[trunk]], one **controller container** in the Upgrade [[work area]] — and left the plan to work out which of them still had to be built. It worked that out by subtracting a set of *tiles* from its picks: the tiles holding a container, the tiles holding a container site, and the tiles still owed a road. That set answers two questions that look alike and are not. **Is this target already served?** is a question about a source and a controller. **Can a site go down on this tile today?** is a question about the tile, and it exists only because the engine takes one construction site per tile, so a container planned onto the trunk's first tile has to wait for the road to stand and then coexist with it.

While a pick never moves, the two answers coincide and the mixture is invisible. The pick moves when the trunk moves, and the trunk moves when the reservation changes — which is a commit, not a tick: the [[working ground]] exclusion (#62), the Storage's first pick (#63), the horizon at RCL5 (ADR 0039). On such a commit the container standing on the *old* pick no longer answers the *new* one, and a second container drops beside the first. Nothing in this colony demolishes anything, so both stand, and every rule that counts containers counts two.

The bill was measured on the `trunkColony` fixture, whose source at `(15,25)` sits west of its spawn. One container on the pick hires **one** hauler. The same source with a second container standing on a Seat on its far side hires **three** — the [[hauler unit]] quota sums one term per source container, each `ceil(round-trip ticks × source output ÷ carry capacity)`, so the duplicate is not priced as half a source but as a whole one, and the far Seat's longer round trip prices it dearer than the container the colony actually uses. Where the duplicate lands on the near side the row goes to two. Either way it is at least one hauler the colony did not need. Beside that the second container stands on a [[seat]], which makes it a second [[post]] — `posts` is the Seats under standing containers united with the [[dual seat]]s — so the [[anchor]] row hires one more body to garrison a source that one Anchor already drains, and ADR 0034 ramparts the tile and repairs it forever. And the container itself is 5,000 energy, spent at the surplus tier by workers who had somewhere better to put it: ADR 0038 refused two links at 5,000 each because their prize was one hauler body, and this defect spends half of that to *buy* itself an extra hauler.

So the two questions are separated, and each is asked of the thing it is about.

**The target clause.** A source is served when a container stands, or a container site is pending, within range 1 of it — the same geometry `isSourceContainerTile` already uses for the Planner's Refill filter and for the hauler quota. The controller is served when one stands or is pending in its Upgrade Work Area. A served target is not planned for, wherever the thing serving it happens to sit; an unserved one is planned onto its pick as before.

**The tile clause.** A pick whose tile is still owed a road waits, exactly as it does today, and for the reason it always did: one construction site per tile. This is not about targets and does not move with them.

The target clause counts a pending site; a [[post]] counts only a standing container. That asymmetry is deliberate and is the clearest statement of what the two are for. The plan asks *must another one be built?*, and a site that is already going up answers yes-it-is-handled. A Post asks *is there something on this tile catching a heavy body's overflow right now?*, and a site catches nothing.

**The orphan is kept, not demolished.** The colony has no vocabulary for destroying anything, and inventing one here would be a large decision bought with a small prize — the difference between a container on a good tile and a container on a slightly worse one, against a rule that has to say who orders a demolition, what happens to the energy inside the store, whether it is an Intent kind, and how a stateless Layout is supposed to know that a structure belongs to a superseded version of itself. What this ADR fixes is *two* containers, not *the wrong* container. One container on a worse tile is strictly better than two, and it is reached without a new verb. The residue rides the [[layout record]] instead (ADR 0035, as #106 and #107 extended it): the target, the pick the plan wanted, and the tile that actually serves it — because an orphan that is never recorded is a room that quietly hires an extra hauler with no line anywhere saying why.

One edge is named and left: a tile can satisfy both predicates at once — a [[dual seat]] is within range 1 of a source and inside the Upgrade Work Area, so a container there would serve the source and the controller together. The two targets are judged independently and nothing here merges them. It is not reachable in any room we hold: across the four captured rooms (ADR 0036) there is **no Dual Seat at all**, and the nearest any source stands to a controller is 5 tiles where 4 is the most that could produce one. The room that meets it can decide it, with the Planner's Refill filter in hand — that filter classifies any range-1-of-a-source tile as a source container, so a shared container would be an upgrade buffer nobody refills, and that, not the plan, is where the real question lives.

## Considered Options

- **Demolish the orphan and build the new pick** — rejected here and moved to its own ticket. It is the right end state and the wrong thing to attach to a two-line defect: it is the colony's first demolition, its energy recovery is not something we have verified, and it wants the trigger it does not yet have — an orphan actually standing in a live room.
- **Keep the standing container only when it prices no worse than the pick** — rejected. It sounds like the careful answer and is the worst of the three: when the standing container prices worse the rule builds the new one anyway, so the colony carries the bad tile *and* the duplicate, which is precisely today's defect with an extra condition in front of it.
- **Leave the tile set alone; the pick rarely moves** — rejected on the arithmetic above. It moves on a commit rather than on a tick, which makes it rare and also makes it *ours*: every layout change from here (starting with #105's doorstep exclusion) is a chance to fire it, and the failure is silent, permanent and paid in bodies.
- **Judge the target from standing containers only, ignoring sites** — rejected: it drops a second site beside a site, which is the same defect one tick earlier.

## Consequences

- **W12S28 changes nothing today.** Its three containers stand on their own picks (`7,39`, `10,43`, `16,39`), and ADR 0039 moved the horizon without moving a trunk, so no pick has ever moved in this room. The fix is a latch closing before the next layout change, not a repair to a live room.
- **#105 can land.** Its triage named this defect as the hazard its doorstep exclusion would trip — the exclusion moves the clustered picks, and through them the trunks and the container picks. With the target clause in place a moved pick costs a worse tile, not a second container.
- The [[layout record]] grows one entry kind beside the unserved footings (#106) and the unroutable trunks (#107), and `observe.mjs layout` reads it with them. Nothing here is a [[verdict]]: it is a colony fact about the plan, exactly the split ADR 0035 drew.
- The hauler quota, the Anchor quota, the Post rule, the [[refill]] tiering and the Repair pool are **unchanged**. They were never wrong; they were counting a room that had two containers in it.
- The **demolition ticket** inherits a condition rather than an open question: pick it up when an orphan actually stands, and the record above is what will say so.
- `DecideTests`' "built containers and pending container sites are never placed again" pins the coinciding case and should stay green — it stands its containers on their own picks. The moved-pick case is new coverage, and the one that would have caught this.
