# The fallback to a source's bare Seats is a bootstrap rule, and so belongs to the home room alone

> **Amended by #205** on what counts as a Post below — and, with it, on the sentence *"One switch — a container standing on a Seat — read once as income and once as ground"*, which is now two: ground is the container **or its site** (`Atlas.postsIn`) and income is the standing container alone (`Atlas.standingPostsIn`, `Decide.isPosted`). On nothing else: a [[container]] **construction site** standing on a [[seat]] is a [[post]], so an outpost source whose container is going up has a Work Area for a [[work-heavy body]] again — that one tile — and hires an [[anchor]] to stand on it. The rule this file states is untouched, because it is a rule about a source with *no* Post: an outpost source with nothing on any Seat still answers the empty set, and the bare-Seat fallback is still the [[home room]]'s alone. What moves is the premise underneath the argument. This ADR reasons that an outpost bootstraps through "a [[reserver]] and a light builder raising the container", and the live colony measured that: three containers demolished by an invader, a worker row of two crossing a [[seam]] at fifty energy a trip against 5,000 progress apiece, thousands of ticks of lost income each time. A heavy body standing on the site digs twelve a tick and spends it into the progress under its own feet, which is how those containers were raised before this ADR and ADR 0046 closed the door between them. The stranding this file guards against does not reach that body: the tile it is walked to is one it has work on, which is the same condition every other Post already met.

ADR 0020 narrowed Harvest's [[work area]] for a [[work-heavy body]] to the source's [[post]]s where the source has any, and closed by saying what happens where it has none: *"Before the first container is built and with no Dual Seat, a source has no Post and nothing changes — the fallback to the full Seat set is what carries RCL1–2."* Since #159 that sentence is true of `atlas.Home` and of no other room. `Atlas.narrowedArea` answers the **empty set** for a work-heavy body harvesting an unposted source the [[spatial projection]] places in any room other than home, which makes that Harvest inapplicable to such a body. We record the succession, because neither ADR 0041, which layered the projection by room name and so put the second room in it, nor ADR 0042, which decided what the colony does in that room and is #159's parent, says anything about a heavy body's Work Area at all, so an implementer following `docs/agents/domain.md` and reading the ADRs before the code opens ADR 0020 and comes away holding exactly the belief that caused #159's live failure.

**The rule.** The fallback to the bare [[seat]]s is a **bootstrap** rule, and it is the [[home room]]'s because bootstrapping is. At home it carries a colony whose first container is not built yet, and a heavy body that strands itself there is a few tiles from the spawn that can replace it and from haulers already working the room. An [[outpost]] bootstraps through neither of those: its first steps are a [[reserver]] and a light builder raising the [[container]] (ADR 0042, #157). A heavy body on an outpost Seat with no container under it digs twelve a tick onto the ground of a room whose haul quota does not exist yet, because a standing container is what switches that quota on and this source has none on any Seat — one anywhere on its Seats would be a Post, and the body would be narrowed to it rather than reaching this rule at all — and [[travel cost]], which reads only that the Seat is near, is exactly what walks it there. The live shape was an Anchor hired for one outpost's Post spent on another outpost's bare rock (#159). So **an unposted outpost source's Work Area for a work-heavy body is empty, and its Harvest is inapplicable to one**; ADR 0020's narrowing to the Posts, and its inseparable half that a creep may act only from inside its Work Area, are unchanged in every room.

This is the geometric dual of ADR 0042's *"an unposted outpost source is worth nothing to the workforce"*: worth nothing in the quotas, and standable on by nobody in the geometry. One switch — a container standing on a Seat — read once as income and once as ground. A source the projection places in **no** room keeps the fallback (ADR 0004): it has no room to be outside of, its Work Area is empty either way, and absence answers as it did before there was a second room rather than becoming a third answer.

## Considered Options

- **Drop the fallback everywhere.** Rejected: it is what carries RCL1–2, exactly as ADR 0020 says. A colony before its first container would leave its whole Anchor row with no Work Area anywhere, and #159 is not a reason to break the bootstrap the fallback was written for.
- **Keep the fallback everywhere and stop the pull with pricing** — a travel-cost penalty on an outpost Seat, or an Anchor quota counted per room. Rejected on ADR 0041's own argument against weighted exit tiles: a large cost says "prefer not to", and the Matcher with no better candidate still picks it and still loses the body. What is needed is a prohibition, and a prohibition is what a Work Area is.
- **Gate on ownership, or on the [[reservation]], rather than on the home room's name.** Rejected. Ownership names the same room today and names it worse: what carries the bootstrap is that a spawn stands there to replace a body the fallback strands, not who holds the controller. A reservation gate is worse still — a lapsed reservation would restore the fallback in precisely the room it is most expensive in, and it would move every tick. When a second **owned** room arrives it will hold a spawn too, and the premise to re-derive against then is written down here: the fallback belongs to a room that can replace the body it strands.
- **Cast no Anchor until a container stands in that room.** Rejected as a fix for a different bug: the quota already does this — an unposted outpost source is worth nothing to the workforce (ADR 0042) — and #159's body was not hired for a bare rock. It was hired for a real Post and then pulled onto a bare one, which is a Work Area question and not a quota question.
- **Pool Harvest per Post outside home.** Rejected for ADR 0020's original reason, unchanged by the second room: it moves Task identity and Task capacity to fix a standing-position problem.

## Consequences

- **ADR 0020's RCL1–2 sentence is read as the home room's RCL1–2** from #159 onward. Everything else in ADR 0020 stands in every room, including the unreachable-Post consequence: a Post that cannot be reached makes Harvest inapplicable to that heavy body, with no retry against the Seats.
- **Light bodies are untouched anywhere.** An unposted outpost source still offers every Seat to a body that is not work-heavy, and travel cost still decides whether one walks there. The narrowing is a fact about the body, as ADR 0020's was.
- **The theft that remains has the home room as the thief**, and it is a known consequence rather than an open bug: an Anchor hired for an *outpost's* Post can still be pulled onto the bare Seats of a **home** source whose container has just been demolished, because home keeps the fallback. That is the fallback working as designed — home is where a mis-seated heavy body is cheap, being a few tiles from a spawn and inside the haul the colony already runs — and it is recorded because a reader who has just absorbed "the fallback is home's alone" will otherwise meet it as a surprise. It was seen by #159's adversarial lens and deliberately left standing by that ticket's own terms.
- **A reader who arrives here from ADR 0020 gets the answer #159 needed.** The banner on ADR 0020 is the mechanism: the belief that an unposted source anywhere offers its Seats to an Anchor is exactly what the banner denies, at the sentence that used to say it.
- The glossary carries the narrowing in [[harvest]], [[work area]], [[post]] and [[home room]]; this ADR is where the decision behind those sentences lives.
