# The guard is sized to the exchange, and a guarded room's reserver seat waits for it

> **Accepted 2026-09-18** on #375; the decision is the user's ("两条都做" — both halves). Implemented by the change that accepted it.

One NPC invader — `2 TOUGH 5 MOVE 1 RANGED_ATTACK 1 WORK 1 ATTACK`, 1,000 hits, 40 damage a tick, no heal — stood in W15S25's Reactor ring from t547,947 and in W15S27 from t549,483, and the raid log records what W15S28 fed it: 25 re-claimers and 3 anchors in the first room, then 8 reservers in 491 ticks in the second, one every fifty ticks. No guard was ever cast. At t549,974 the colony had 6 living bodies of a target of 13, no hauler, no worker, no upgrader, its spawn at 6 of 300 and casting another reserver.

Three rules composed into it. ADR 0056's guard row prices the raid for its **count** (`guardBlocksBeat`: one block kills this invader, so one guard) and the bank for its **size** (`wholeBlockBodyFor` at capacity: three blocks, 2,250, at a 2,300 bank). ADR 0050's yield lets a row the bank cannot afford give the tick to the rows behind it, so the tick the bank held 650 the reserver row — whose gap was open because its bodies kept dying — bought a `[CLAIM; MOVE]`, and the bank never climbed past 650 toward 2,250. And #366 keeps a guarded-from-memory room's Tasks offered while `reserverClaimsOf` counts its claim regardless, so every death reopened the gap and the next 650 bought the next body into the same room. ADR 0050 had named this failure mode in its own Consequences and pointed at its root — "an outpost raider harvesting reservers one after another" — as something to fix "at its own seam". This is that seam.

## Decision

1. **The guard is sized to the exchange it must win.** `Quota.guardBlocksFor` is the smallest whole-block count `guardBlocksBeat` answers yes to for a room, up to the largest body the engine casts and that largest where none wins; `guardBlocksWanted` is the worst of the guarded rooms' answers, one where nothing is guarded, and every cast this tick carries it, as the reserver row's casts carry the largest outstanding claim. It rides `RowSizing` and `BodySizing` as `GuardBlocks`, and `sizedBodyFor` buys `min capacity (blocks × 750)` — the bank truncates the blocks and the blocks truncate the bank. A room the colony is blind in prices at one block: its raid is remembered, not seen, and a 750 body is what going to look is worth. The count (`guardsWanted`) is unchanged; where one block loses and the count is two, each of the two is now sized to win alone.

2. **A guarded room's reserver seat waits for its guard.** While the guard row has a gap — fewer guard bodies standing or in an oven than `guardQuota` — the rooms it is hired for are removed from `reserverClaimsOf`. **The row's gap, not the room's**: the guard row counts bodies and never which room each stands in (ADR 0042, ADR 0056), so with two raided rooms and one guard standing, both rooms' seats wait until the second guard is bought. A deliberate coarsening — the alternative is a per-room guard census the row does not keep — and a short one, the second guard being the next thing the cascade buys. The tick a guard stands or is cast, the seat returns. The Reserve Task stays offered (#366) and the errand's Reclaim is ADR 0064's; what stops is the *buying* of bodies into a room the colony knows is held. The workforce target moves by one per withheld room, keyed to the guard gap and not to the spending, so it flips once rather than every cast — the oscillation ADR 0050 refused for a bank-keyed quota does not arise.

## Consequences

- At W15S28's bank the guard row now asks 750 against this invader and stands first in the cascade the tick the bank holds it; nothing behind it is bought for W15S27 until it does. The colony's energy goes to the guard, then to the rows the guard protects.
- ADR 0050's yield is untouched: a row the bank cannot afford still gives the tick to the rows behind it. What changes is that the guard row is affordable when its fight is small, and that the row most likely to be bought behind it no longer buys bodies into the guarded room.
- ADR 0056's "the guard the row casts is the block the bank buys" is superseded by decision 1; its count, its cap of two, and its blindness rule (#366) stand. Where one block loses and the count is two, each guard is now sized to win alone — 2 × 1,500 against two healers where the old rule at a 2,300 bank bought 2 × 2,250 — and a raid no body of five blocks beats is priced at five, which is the bounded loss ADR 0056's two-cast cap already accepts while its stand-down is not yet open.
- Not decided here: why ADR 0064's withdrawal did not fire for the same invader in W15S25 (25 re-claimers lost). That is a separate read.
- Tests: `QuotaGuardTests` pins the size against a lone melee (one block at a bank that buys two), against two healers (two casts of two blocks), and against a remembered raid (one block), and the cascade order with the seat withheld and returned; `QuotaReserverTests` pins the seat withheld, and returned by a guard standing or in the oven.
