# The clustered horizon moves to RCL5: the trunks do not move, and a horizon left behind plans nothing the level unlocks

ADR 0011 set the Layout's horizon at RCL4 and said to revisit it when RCL4 was in sight. ADR 0022 revisited it there and refused to move it, for a reason it stated precisely: "ten more extensions land in the north band (rows 35–37) where the source→controller trunks already run and in the controller pocket, buying longer trunks for structures a level away." That was weighed in the same session that decided to exclude the [[working ground]] from the ordering, and it was weighed **against the old ordering** — the one that could still put an extension on an Upgrade standing tile or a [[seat]]. With the exclusion in force the controller pocket is not on offer to a clustered structure at all, so the half of the reason that came from the pocket is gone, and the half that came from the north band is an empirical claim we can now check instead of predict.

We re-derived it. W12S28's committed terrain fixture, planned from the live spawn tile `12,40`, at horizon 4 and at horizon 5: **the trunks do not move** — both plans pave the same 25 tiles, not a comparable length but the same set; **the footings do not move** — all four are served in both, on the same tiles, with no shortfall; and the ten further extensions land on `7,37 9,35 13,35 14,36 15,35 16,36 16,38 14,44 16,44 15,45`, with the second tower on `13,39`.

So we move the horizon to RCL5. The move is also not optional in the way a horizon usually is: the clustered kinds are *sized* at the horizon and only *filtered* at the current level, so a room standing at RCL5 under a horizon of 4 computes an extension gap of zero and asks for none of the ten the engine just unlocked — and none of the second tower. Left alone, the horizon is not a conservative choice at RCL5; it is a room that stops growing without saying why.

## Considered Options

- **Leave the horizon at 4 and raise it when RCL5 actually lands** — rejected: the horizon exists so today's trunks route around tomorrow's structures, and raising it the tick the level arrives is exactly the moment the reservation can no longer do that. The whole cost of a horizon is paid early or not at all.
- **A horizon of `current level + 1`, computed rather than declared** — rejected: it makes the plan a function of the level, and ADR 0027's invariant — `reserved ∪ standing sites` unchanged as the Layout is built out — is what makes the plan deterministic across ticks. A horizon that moves on its own breaks it once per level instead of once, deliberately, in a commit.
- **Horizon to RCL8, ending the revisits** — rejected for ADR 0011's original reason, undiminished: RCL6 adds ten more extensions and a second Storage-class structure, RCL7 a second spawn, and reserving for structures four levels out taxes today's trunks with detours for a colony that may never get there. One level of lookahead is the standing bargain.

## Consequences

- **Today's RCL4 plan changes once, on the commit that moves the constant.** The second tower's slot is reserved ahead of the extensions, so it takes `13,39` and the twentieth extension moves to `12,44`. For a living room this costs no demolition — a standing structure is a target, its tile is out of the ordering, and the tower's pick simply falls to the next tile — but ADR 0027's invariant does step once here, and it steps because a human moved a constant rather than because the Layout churned.
- **A room short of candidate tiles pays for the lookahead at its current level.** The second tower's reservation sits ahead of the extensions in the ordering, so where the ordering runs out, a tile that would have been an extension today is held for a tower a level away. The synthetic `openRoom 3` in DecideTests is exactly that room, and it is where the two failures below come from. W12S28 has the spare tiles and loses nothing, but #83's remote candidates are not W12S28, and a cramped room is where this will be felt.
- Two DecideTests carry the old horizon in their expected values, both on `openRoom 3`, and both are golden values of the horizon rather than independent assertions about the ordering. `placement.RCL2 on open terrain places 5 extensions checkerboard, nearest first` still places five, on a shifted set of tiles, so its expected list moves; the stale comment above it, naming the tower's pick under the RCL4 horizon, moves with it. `placement.the controller's tile is never chosen` is the one that drops from five extensions to four, because that room spends a tile on the controller and has none to spare for the second tower's reservation. `storage.RCL4 places one Storage on the ordering's first pick, the tower next` **passes unchanged** — placement still truncates the towers to the current level's allowance — and it is worth naming here because it is the test a reader would expect to break. The four-room invariant sweep in RoomInvariantTests passes unchanged.
- **The second tower joins the [[keep]] and takes a [[rampart]], by ADR 0034's existing rule and with no amendment.** That rule's set is the rule's own — "every standing Keep structure", as the [[rampart]]'s set is "a three-source room ramparts three Posts" — so a second tower is covered by construction. Naming it here because ADR 0034 was written when the room could hold one, and the glossary still said "the tower" in the singular; a rule that silently changes cardinality is worth one sentence even when it needs no code.
- The RCL6 revisit inherits a north band that is now spoken for, and a question this ADR does not answer: whether the *reason* to keep a horizon at one level of lookahead survives a room that has run out of cluster room. That is a decision for the level that meets it.
