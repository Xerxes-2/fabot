# The reactor's store never runs dry: a store-less miner over the mineral container, 999 a trip down a declared route, and a resident re-claimer

> **Amended by #306** on decision 3's rank and on nothing else it decided: **the mine's intake is ordered inside the [[storage]]'s tier, and a mineral container bleeding onto the floor is drawn before the Storage's own energy Withdraw** — `TwoRungsUp` on the container once it holds `Tuning.MineContactCliff` (1,000) or more, `OneRungUp` on the pile it has overflowed onto, the container above the pile for #242's reason (with the small copy on top the haulers chase it all day and never drain the store that is making it). Decision 3 put the draw at `StockDraw` so the season's ore never went ahead of the energy the spawn waits on, and what that rank *also* did was tie the bank the colony sits on, which shares this tier (ADR 0023) and is the nearer target in every room: live at t402,520, W12S28 had banked **3,600** Thorium with an empty Storage while W13S28 — the same code, 485,916 energy banked, a full [[refill cluster]] — had banked **none**, its container at the 2,000 cap with 838 bleeding on the floor beneath the [[miner]], and that starvation is the normal state of a healthy colony and worsens the healthier it gets. The lift fires at the **contact cliff and not at the cap** on the arithmetic of the two states: drawn at a thousand the container cycles 0..999 and the miner over it averages `1 + p ≈ 2.9` (≈3.0 counting the walk-in window), the three `Tuning.MineContactAgeing` is written for, where left to the cap it cycles 500..2,000 — one 1,500-carry load off the cap — with ~300 of ~450 ticks over the cliff, averaging **3.67** and buying the row a third more bodies for the same ore. **What it costs**: empty carriers fetch ore before topping up from the bank, bounded by the pool's own `ceil(stock / haulerLoad) + ceil(pile / haulerLoad)` — three bodies at W13S28's numbers, not one, and widest exactly when the mine has backed up — and it reaches the intake a waiting spawn needs one hop before decision 3's sentence looks, because an empty body is applicable to no Refill (#315 records that price and why no gate was taken). #311's `Planner.minePickups` and its rung comment argued the other way from this same boundary and are corrected in the commit that lands this: the boundary decision 3 drew is the **flow**, not the stock.

`docs/research/thorium-reactor.md` is a code-level read of `mod-season5` (`da59118`, tag 1.0.3) corroborated live at tick 202,041, and #251 is the ticket that spends it. Four of its findings decide this ADR between them, and every number below is one of theirs rather than one of ours.

**The score is a step function of continuity and nothing else.** `1 + floor(log10(1 + continuousWork))` per Thorium consumed, 1 T/tick consumed, and `launchTime` cleared on any tick the store is empty — so N Thorium is N reactor-ticks, past 10,000 ticks the marginal Thorium is worth 5 score, and **one dry tick costs ≈ 11,106**. Our reachable stock is 44,000 (W12S28's 22,000 and W13S28's; W12S27 is reserved-only and an extractor needs an **owned** RCL6 room). Unbroken that is 208,894; the arithmetic for one, two and three breaks is 197,788, 186,682 and 175,576, and today's whole-board leader stands at 74,768. The user's decision on #251 follows from that spread and is this ADR's premise: **the goal is never to let the store run dry, and not to chase one unbroken streak** — a break is a 5% tax, not a failure, and a programme that treats it as a failure buys perfection with the fragility that causes it.

**The contact penalty is a property of a *tile*, and it is the whole hazard model.** `thorium.js` sums `store.T` over every object on a tile, takes `p = floor(log10 total)`, and subtracts `p` from `ageTime`, `decayTime` and `nextDecayTime` of everything standing there — so a creep on such a tile burns `1 + p` ticks of life a tick. Dropped piles and the untouched deposit have no `store` and are free; containers, tombstones, the [[storage]] and the reactor all have one and all count. The Storage is the free warehouse because it is an obstacle nothing can stand on. **The decade cliffs at 10 / 100 / 1,000 are what every body sizing below is derived from.**

**The delivery is six room transitions and one trip per body.** 267 steps roaded from W12S28 (26,5) to the reactor at W15S25 (44,6), keeping five tiles off every keeper lair, source and mineral of the SK room in the middle; 999 in a 1,000-capacity body costs 825 TTL for the loaded leg against 1,100 for 1,000, so **one Thorium past the cliff costs 275 ticks of life**. And the reactor is indestructible, walkable, and stolen by any one `[CLAIM, MOVE]` creep for one tick with `launchTime` inherited — which cuts both ways, because we take it straight back on the same terms.

Against that, the colony. At the research's live tick W12S28 was RCL5 with 258k in the Storage, W13S28 owned and RCL4, and GCL 1,823,489 of the 5,278,032 a third room needs — so 44,000 is the stock and W12S27's 22,000 is out of reach, an extractor needing an **owned** RCL6 room and a reservation deriving `rcl = 0`. W12S28 reaches RCL6 on ADR 0055's move, which is the gate on all of this, and every live number here should be re-read before the first ticket lands rather than trusted off this paragraph: the colony outruns its own documents.

The load-bearing constraint on *us* rather than on the engine is ADR 0041. The [[seam]] model prices **one hop**: `walk = min over the band of (near leg + 1 + far leg)`, both legs single-room floods, and `Atlas.seams` matches only orthogonally adjacent room names. #243 named this a design boundary rather than a bug and filed multi-hop as its own feature. A six-hop delivery therefore cannot be a Task priced by a Seam join, and decision 4 is where that is paid.

**1. The extractor is a [[layout]] kind whose tile is its target's, and the mineral container defers to the mineral exactly as ADR 0040 has a source container defer to its source.** Two placements and two different rules, and the split is ADR 0040's own.

- **`Extractor` joins `StructureKind` and `BuiltKind`, filtered at `Tuning.ExtractorLevel` (6, beside `StorageLevel` and for its reason: it is the level the engine unlocks the kind at, `CONTROLLER_STRUCTURES.extractor`), and planned on the Thorium mineral's own tile.** It is **not** reserved at the horizon and needs no ordering at all, and the reason is stronger than convenience: the deposit sits on a **wall** tile at the mouth of a wall (`mineral.roomObject.js` places it so), which is off the clustered checkerboard, unbuildable for every other kind, and outside the ordering by construction. There is no window to hold open, so ADR 0022's argument for reserving the [[storage]] and the [[link footing]]s — a tile an extension takes never comes back — does not reach it. One per room ever, and it is a **choice**: the room's ordinary ore is never extracted, there being no market this season.
- **The container is planned on the mineral's [[seat]] nearest the Storage's [[trunk]]** — the source container's rule with the source swapped out, and the target clause of ADR 0040 as written: the mineral is *served* when a container stands or a site is pending within range 1 of it, wherever that container happens to sit, so a moved pick costs a worse tile and never a second container.
- **Both tiles join the [[working ground]] exclusion (ADR 0022).** The Seat is a garrison's, and an extension landing on the one accessible tile at a wall mouth would cost the room its whole deposit.
- **The extractor is planned before the room can hold it and filtered at the level**, which is ADR 0055's own rule for the clustered kinds applied to a kind that is not clustered: `CONTROLLER_STRUCTURES.extractor` is 1 at RCL6, 7 and 8 and 0 below, `createConstructionSite` refuses a second one, and a room that plans at its current level rather than above it asks for none of what the engine just unlocked. What it does not need is the horizon's *reservation*, per the paragraph above.
- The mineral is a **fact and not a declaration**. Unlike ADR 0041's [[outpost]]s, it stands in a room the colony owns and sees every tick, so `World` reads it off `FIND_MINERALS` filtered to `mineralType = "T"` and it joins the projection as `TargetKind.Mineral`. The day the mod deletes the exhausted object it leaves the projection, and decision 7 is what that retires.

**2. The `miner` row: a body with no store at all, standing on the [[post]] the container makes.** A row of its own beside worker, [[anchor]], [[hauler unit]], [[reserver]] and upgrader, and it is a row rather than a widened Anchor because ADR 0006's test is the sizing rule: the Anchor's ceiling is `workCapOf` a source's regeneration (ADR 0021) and a mineral has no regeneration to saturate, its quota is per-Post richest-first (ADR 0053) and there is one Post here, and its Task is a source's.

- **The block is `[Work; Work; Move]` and the sizing rule is one Move per `Tuning.MinerWorkPerMove` (5) Work, Work taking every slot the bank affords up to twenty.** It walks to a tile once and then never leaves it, so its Move is bought for a single commute and not for ADR 0003's parity, which is a rule about a body that keeps moving. **At an 1,800 bank it casts `[16 Work; 4 Move]`, 1,800 energy, 2.67 T/tick; at 2,300 it casts `[20 Work; 4 Move]`, 2,200 energy, 3.33 T/tick.** The ceiling of twenty is the bank's and never the part cap's — even at road parity, one Move per two Work, a twenty-Work miner is thirty parts of fifty — and it is where the arithmetic stops mattering: `WORK / 6` per tick against a reactor that eats 1 T/tick means **mining has never been the bottleneck**, and past twenty the deposit only ends sooner.
- **No Carry, and that is the decision rather than an economy.** A body that holds Thorium ages by `floor(log10 store.T)` and a twenty-Work miner passes 10 T in three ticks; a harvest with no room to put the yield drops it on the creep's own tile, and a drop onto a container tile lands **in** the container — the same engine rule the Anchor's overflow already rides on. So the miner is [[body class]] **Heavy** (Work over Move), ADR 0016 shuts its [[withdraw]] as it shuts every Heavy body's, and there is nothing left for a load-shaped rule to price it by.
- **Its quota is 1 while the mineral stands in the projection with a non-zero amount and the extractor is built**, and 0 otherwise. Not a site: an extractor under construction extracts nothing and a miner cast for it stands idle for a life.
- **Its Task is Harvest, widened from a source to a mineral.** Same act, same Intent, same applicability shape — ADR 0054's own test for refusing a new Task kind — so `Harvest of targetId` takes either and every exhaustive match over `Task` grows no arm. Its [[work area]] is ADR 0020's narrowing with one tile in it, the Post; its [[capacity]] is one Garrison, the Post count, exactly as ADR 0024 counts a source's.
- **The Emitter issues the harvest only on a tick the extractor's cooldown is zero.** `EXTRACTOR_COOLDOWN` is 5 and the intent pass runs before the object pass, so successive harvests land **six** ticks apart and the other five are refused by the engine. The cooldown joins the projection as an id-keyed `Cooldowns` map under ADR 0007's growth rule — a field arrives on the tick a decision reads it — and the gate is the same shape as ADR 0025's empty window read off a different clock: the body keeps its Task and stands, and the [[verdict]] does not claim it dug.
- The gate is in the **Emitter** and never in applicability, and the distinction is the one ADR 0013 and ADR 0025 spent two decisions on. A cooldown is five ticks long and a re-match is a flood; a Task that vanished and returned every sixth tick would churn the pool the way ADR 0054's ring of extensions did, for a body that has nowhere else to be and no way to get there. **The Task exists exactly while the deposit does; what the cooldown decides is whether this tick's act is issued.** Chat bubble ⛏️ on the ticks it digs and nothing on the ticks it waits.

**3. Thorium is a second id-keyed map beside `Stores`, and the mine-to-Storage leg is the [[hauler unit]]'s existing pair with a resource on it.** `SpatialInfo.Stores` is `Map<string, int>` of energy and every one of its readers is about the haul cycle.

- **`Thorium: Map<string, int>` joins `Hits` and `Stores`**, id-keyed, absent per entry (ADR 0004), carrying the mineral container's T, the Storage's, the reactor's and the deposit's own remaining amount. Rejected in its favour: `Map<string, Map<Resource, int>>`, which makes every existing energy reader ask a question it never asks and gives a bug somewhere to answer it wrongly. Two resources that share no Task, no tier, no sink and no quota are two facts, and the generalisation is one commit away on the day a third resource has a reader.
- **`Withdraw` and `Refill` gain a resource** — `Withdraw of storeId * Resource`, `Refill of structureId * Resource`, `Resource = Energy | Thorium` — and every existing construction is `Energy`. The act, the applicability shape and the Intent are the same and the engine's own `withdraw`/`transfer` have taken a resource argument all along; what changes is one argument we have been passing implicitly. The **where** half of #183's rename landed ahead of this ADR and on its own: the Intent is `WithdrawFromStore`, named for the store the Task already named, so what this decision adds is the resource argument and not a second rename.
- **The Withdraw Intent gains an amount**, `int option`, `None` meaning what it means today. The delivery's load is the one place in the colony a Withdraw names a number, and decision 4 is why.
- **Applicability on the Thorium arm is an *empty* store, not #232's half-empty one.** A body carries one resource at a time here: a mixed load pours energy into a reactor that refuses it and arrives at the cliff with the wrong count in its store. The worth-the-trip half of #232's sentence stays and is read down the Thorium column — half a load in the container is what makes it worth a visit.
- **The leg is worked by the existing hauler row and no new one**, priced by one more term in ADR 0049's colony sum: the mineral container's round trip to the Storage times the miner's own rate over one load. It is a row's quota that grows, not a row. The Thorium pair ranks at the [[storage]]'s tier (ADR 0023) and below every energy sink: a miner making 3.33 T/tick against a delivery cadence that consumes 1.25 has 2.6× of slack, so this is genuinely the work a body does when it has no better, and the container's own overflow penalty is 0.3 energy a tick of repair.

**4. The delivery walks a route a human declared, one hop at a time, and every flood stays in one room.** This is the ADR 0041 boundary being worked around rather than moved, and it is written so that moving it later deletes a constant and no rule.

- **`Tuning.ReactorRoute` is a list of room names from the colony's home to the reactor's room** — `W12S28; W12S27; W12S26; W12S25; W13S25; W14S25; W15S25` — chosen by a human in a commit, exactly as ADR 0041's outpost set and ADR 0055's horizon are. Beside each intermediate room a human also writes its **keeper centres**: the sources, minerals and lairs of an SK room, each masking the tiles within `Tuning.KeeperMargin` (5) of it. `keepers/pretick.js` pins every keeper to range 1 of its assigned rock and it never pursues, so five tiles is the whole of crossing W14S25 untouched, and W16S25's level-2 stronghold is avoided by the route not going there.
- **A route room enters the [[spatial projection]] as a bare terrain layer and nothing else.** ADR 0031's terrain memo answers for any room in the world with no vision and never goes stale, so the marginal cost is zero; targets, stores, creeps and hostiles stay absent, which is ADR 0004's per-entry absence and is the truth — the only route room we ever see is the one a body is standing in.
- **The mover walks the route hop by hop with ADR 0041's existing machinery, unamended.** The body's current room is looked up in the route, its successor is the next entry, and the mover aims it at the near side of the winning [[seam]] between exactly those two rooms — the crossing aim landed for the one-hop case, reused verbatim. In the last room the [[work area]] is the reactor's walkable range-1 ring and the walk is an ordinary single-room flood. **No flood ever leaves a room and no price is ever summed over more than one Seam**, so ADR 0041's boundary is where it was; what a human supplied is the room sequence a search would have produced.
- **The `courier` row's body is a constant: `[20 Carry; 10 Move]`, 1,500 energy.** Twenty Carry because 999 is the load and 1,000 is a cliff; ten Move because the road the human paved makes that fatigue parity loaded, and off it the body would want twenty and cost 2,000. A row whose sizing rule is "one size, or nothing": below 1,500 it does not cast, which is ADR 0050's yield rather than a smaller body, because a smaller body is a whole trip that carries less than a tenth as much for the same 267 steps.
- **Its quota is a cadence and not a count: one cast per `Tuning.DeliveryInterval` (800 ticks) while the conditions hold.** Read off the fleet's own clocks and never off remembered state — the row asks for one body on a tick when no courier of this colony has a `ticksToLive` above `CREEP_LIFE_TIME − DeliveryInterval` (700), and for none otherwise. 800 against a 999-tick buffer and a 275-tick walk means one body in flight at a time, one late arrival absorbed, and two never needed at once.
- **Its Task is `Deliver of reactorId`, and it is a new kind for the *route* and not for the resource.** The line ADR 0054 draws is the act and its pricing: a Thorium Refill into the Storage is a Refill because it is priced by [[travel cost]] inside one room, and this is not, because nothing in the pool can price it. Its [[capacity]] is one, its [[priority]] is the Feeding tier's — the season's whole score rides on it and it fires once per 800 ticks.
- **The Emitter issues a `Withdraw` of exactly `Tuning.ReactorLoad` (999) T from the Storage at home, and a `Transfer` of Thorium into the reactor at range 1.** On arrival it pours every tick until its store is empty: the reactor caps at 1,000 and burns one a tick, so a courier arriving into a part-full store leaves with a remainder and gives it up over the next few hundred ticks standing beside it. It stands on a ring tile and never on the reactor itself, whose own store would otherwise take `p = 3` off everything on it.
- **A courier in a route room is applicable to `Deliver` and to nothing else, and it is placeable.** This is #164's defect stated as a rule before it can happen: a body the projection cannot place prices at nothing, out-bids every body that can be priced, and never goes home. So a route room's layer carries **our own creeps' tiles beside its terrain** — the room a courier stands in is a room we see — and every *other* Task's [[work area]] is unreachable from it by ADR 0004's totality, there being no target in that layer at all. The one Task it holds is not priced by a flood, so nothing about it can be compared against a home-room candidate; it holds it from the tick it is cast to the tick it dies, which is exactly one life and one trip.
- **A reactor another player holds is not a sink.** `transfer` checks no ownership, so pouring into a rival's reactor feeds a rival's score. The rule is: pour while the reactor is ours **or unowned** — an unowned reactor consumes nothing, scores nothing for anyone, and *keeps what it holds*, which makes it a legitimate staging store — and where it is held by somebody else the courier stands on the ring and waits for decision 5 to take it back.

**5. The `reclaimer` row: `[Claim; Move]`, resident, and the colony's only eye on W15S25.** 650 energy, `CREEP_CLAIM_LIFE_TIME` 600, a 275-tick walk, so ~325 ticks of standing per body.

- **Its quota is the courier's cadence in the other clock: one cast per `Tuning.ReclaimerInterval` (300 ticks)**, read the same way — one when no reclaimer of ours has a `ticksToLive` above 300. Three hundred and not 325 so the relief arrives twenty-five ticks before the incumbent expires; the row is a **relay and never a garrison of two**.
- **Its Task is `Reclaim of reactorId`**, [[work area]] the reactor's range-1 ring, capacity one, applicable to a body with a `Claim` part. The **act fires only on a tick the reactor is not ours**: `claimReactor` has no cooldown, no ownership precondition and does not touch `launchTime`, so a theft is undone on the tick it is seen and **the streak survives the exchange**, continuity depending on the store and not on the owner. Every other tick the body stands there and says nothing, which is what resident means.
- Both new bodies are [[body class]] **Light** — no Work part between them, and the `Carrier` this ADR was written against was folded into `Light` by #233 — so ADR 0016's shut Withdraw does not reach them and ADR 0046's standing rule does not either. **Neither flees.** ADR 0033's Flee is applicable to any creep inside a [[reach]], and a Reach is derived from Threats a projection can see; a route room carries terrain and our own creeps and no hostiles at all, so there is no Reach out there to be inside of and no safe set to walk to. That is a deliberate hole and it is the cheap side of the trade: the alternative is vision of six rooms every tick for two bodies whose answer to a keeper is the five-tile margin they were routed around in the first place, and whose answer to a player is that they are 1,500 and 650 energy against a streak worth 200,000 score.
- It is not ADR 0047's [[claim]] and must not be folded into it: that Task spends a GCL level, needs a human's declaration and a takeable controller, and W15S25 has **no controller at all**. Nor is W15S25 an outpost — `Outpost` requires a controller by its own type, for ADR 0042's reason, and this room has nothing to reserve.
- **And it is the vision.** `continuousWork`, `store.T` and `owner` are readable only with vision of the room, so the leaf in decision 6 is true exactly while a reclaimer stands. That is the second reason the relay overlaps rather than gaps.

**6. One flat `reactor` leaf and `observe.mjs reactor`.** Under `Memory.fabot.observe.reactor` beside `cpu`, and **flat rather than colony-keyed**: there is one reactor in the sector and two colonies will eventually feed it, and a colony-keyed leaf would have them write two answers about one object — which is exactly the split ADR 0047 drew between a colony's own record and the bot's.

- The fields are `owner` (ours, a rival's id, or none), `storeT`, `continuousWork` (`time − launchTime`, zero when idle), `seen` (the tick those three were last read — they are stale the moment the relay gaps), `bankedT` (the home Storage's own T), `lastDelivery`, and **`dryTicks`**, the count of ticks we have seen the store at zero. `dryTicks` is the strategy's own scoreboard: the premise of this ADR is a number that should stay at zero and costs 11,106 each time it moves.
- **The season score is not readable in game and the ADR says so rather than leaving it to be discovered.** The mod exposes `FIND_REACTORS`, `LOOK_REACTORS` and a `Reactor` prototype with `store`, `owner`, `my` and `continuousWork`, and **nothing that carries the score**. `observe.mjs reactor` prints the leaf and then fetches `GET https://screeps.com/season/api/scoreboard/list` for our rank and score beside it, which is the one place in the observe surface that reads the server rather than Memory.

**7. Start and stop are conditions and never switches.** #251's last comment sets the start and this ADR only orders it. **The reclaimer row opens when the extractor stands, the road is paved to W15S25 and the home Storage holds `3 × Tuning.ReactorLoad` (2,997) T — three trips banked.** **The courier row opens one step later, when a reclaimer of ours is standing in W15S25**, which the colony knows because that body is the vision. The stop is not written at all: the mod deletes an exhausted deposit outright (`postProcessObject` on `mineralType == 'T' && !mineralAmount`), so the mineral leaves the projection, the miner row's quota reads 0 from the same fact that gave it 1, and the courier row runs on until the Storage's banked T falls below one load and then reads 0 too. **The extractor and the container stay standing** — nothing in this colony demolishes anything (ADR 0040) — and an emptied container decays at the ordinary owned rate with no penalty on it at all.

## Considered Options

- **A drop pile at the mineral instead of a container** — rejected on the engine's own arithmetic: a pile loses `ceil(amount / 1000)` per tick, which is 1/tick against a 3.33/tick dig, a 30% loss for the whole life of the deposit. The pile's freedom from the contact penalty is real and is not worth a third of the stock.
- **A miner with Carry parts** — rejected: a body holding Thorium ages by `floor(log10 store.T)`, a twenty-Work miner passes 10 T in three ticks, and every Carry part bought is a part not digging. The store-less body is the design and not a saving.
- **1,000 per load** — rejected on the decade cliff: `p` goes from 2 to 3, the loaded leg costs 1,100 TTL instead of 825, and the reactor caps at 1,000 anyway. One extra Thorium costs 275 ticks of life.
- **Terminal transfer** — rejected: `terminal-restriction.js` nulls any `send` to a terminal with a different `user`, and the reactor's room has no controller and so can never hold one of ours. It becomes interesting only if a room *beside* the centre is ever ours, which needs GCL 3 against our GCL 2, and it is then a range-3 send at ~96 energy per 1,000 T — worth reopening then and not now.
- **Claiming the reactor before the supply chain exists** — rejected: an unowned reactor consumes nothing, scores nothing and keeps what it holds, so there is no prize in early ownership; what a claim buys is a flag on a map telling four scoring rivals which sector centre is contested.
- **One unbroken streak as the goal** — rejected as the *goal*, and it is the user's decision on #251 rather than an inference. Three breaks still score 175,576 against a whole board's leader at 74,768, so the programme is robust by two and a half times and does not need to be perfect; designing for perfection buys the fragility that causes the break.
- **A multi-room flood over an allowed-room set for the route** (the community mainstream, and ADR 0041's rejected option) — rejected again on ADR 0041's own cost argument, now with #171's measurement behind it: one full-room flood per creep per tick is already 37% of the tick.
- **Making W15S25 an outpost** — rejected by the type: an `Outpost` carries a controller because an unreserved source is worth half, and this room has no controller, no sources we would work, and nothing to reserve.
- **A buffer container beside the reactor** — rejected: it costs 1.5 energy a tick of repair (a room with no controller decays containers on the 100-tick clock, tripled by the Thorium in it) and it buys slack the 1,000-capacity reactor already has at this cadence.
- **A dedicated Thorium hauler row for the mine-to-Storage leg** — rejected: the act is the existing pair with one argument on it, so the answer is a term in ADR 0049's sum and not a sixth row and a seventh quota.
- **`Stores` generalised to a map of maps** — rejected above: every energy reader would grow a question it never asks, and the two resources share no Task, tier, sink or quota.
- **Reserving the extractor and its Seat at the [[layout]] horizon** — rejected: the tiles are a wall and a wall's mouth, off the clustered checkerboard and unbuildable for every other kind, so there is no window ADR 0022's argument would be holding open.
- **Two couriers in flight, or a courier that makes two trips** — rejected on the TTL arithmetic rather than on taste: 1,500 ticks less ~40 to reach the Storage less 825 for the loaded leg leaves ~635, which is most of the way home and not a second load. Two in flight is the same energy for the same throughput with twice the exposure, and the 1,000-unit buffer plus a 800-tick cadence already absorbs one late arrival.
- **Recycling the courier at the far end, or walking it home** — rejected as a saving that costs more than it returns: it is ~267 steps home for a body whose remaining life is ~635 ticks and whose recycle refunds a fraction of 1,500, against a body that stands beside the reactor pouring out its remainder and giving us a second tick of vision for free.
- **Folding `Deliver` into `Refill` with a route field on it** — rejected on ADR 0054's own test read the other way: what makes a new kind here is not the resource and not the target but that **nothing in the pool can price the walk**, so every reader of Refill that asks [[travel cost]] a question would grow an arm answering "not this one". A Refill is a Task the Matcher ranks; this is a Task the Matcher only counts.
- **Deriving the keeper margin from vision instead of declaring it** — rejected for ADR 0041's reason at one more remove: vision of an SK room needs a creep in it, a creep needs a Task, and a Task needs the room in the projection. The lairs are static for the season and a human can read them off the API once.

## Consequences

- **Thorium costs about 5 energy apiece, and the guard is the largest single line.** The courier is 1,500 energy per 999 T, **1.50 e/T**. The miner is the number #251's comment got wrong and the research's own table has right: it stands **on** the container, so it ages at `1 + p` like anything else there, and a container the haul keeps under 1,000 T is `p = 2` — a 500-tick life, ~1,665 T per body, **1.32 e/T** and roughly twenty-six bodies across both deposits. The reclaimer is 650 energy per 300 ticks against a reactor burning one a tick, **2.17 e/T**. Together ≈ **220,000 energy for 44,000 Thorium**, about a quarter of what W12S28 grosses over the same 44,000 ticks. The programme is affordable and it is not cheap, and the line that dominates it is the one that produces nothing.
- **The score arithmetic, stated so the next reader argues with the number and not the strategy**: 0 breaks 208,894, 1 break 197,788, 2 breaks 186,682, 3 breaks 175,576 — exactly 11,106 apiece, because the next 9,999 ticks earn 38,889 where they would have earned 49,995. `dryTicks` in decision 6 is what that arithmetic will be re-read against.
- **Seven numbers join [[tuning]] and four join the [[engine constant]]s, and the split is the usual one**: changing `ReactorLoad`, `DeliveryInterval`, `ReclaimerInterval`, `MinerWorkPerMove`, `KeeperMargin`, `ReactorRoute` or the banked-trips floor would be a different colony, and changing `EXTRACTOR_COOLDOWN` (5), `HARVEST_MINERAL_POWER` (1), the reactor's 1,000 capacity or `CREEP_CLAIM_LIFE_TIME` (600) would be a lie about the server. `ExtractorLevel` goes to Tuning beside `StorageLevel` and for its stated reason. Each Tuning field arrives naming the [[stage]] and the bank it was derived at and with its own pairwise test, because a number without one is a bug that has not happened yet — and the two cadences are the fields most likely to be wrong, being derived from a 267-step walk measured once.
- **The scarcer budget is the spawn's time, not the bank, and the number is a quarter of it.** One spawn casts three ticks a part until RCL7: the miner is twenty-four parts every ~500 ticks, the courier thirty every 800, the re-claimer two every 300 — **about 27% of W12S28's only spawn**, on top of the worker, [[hauler unit]], [[anchor]], upgrader and [[reserver]] rows it already serves. The energy is affordable and the oven is what will be contended, so the cascade's ordering is where this programme will actually be felt, and the first thing to watch in `observe.mjs quotas` after the rows land is which existing row's lead starts slipping.
- **The two new rows yield rather than shrink** (ADR 0050). Both bodies are constants — 1,500 and 650 — and neither has a smaller version that does anything useful, so at a bank that cannot afford one the row asks for nothing and says so, rather than casting a courier that carries 400 and pays the same 267 steps. The supply floor is untouched: neither row is in the cascade's floor and neither may take the last of a bank the colony feeds itself out of.
- **The Seam model is worked around and not moved, and #243 inherits a cleaner ticket for it.** A proper multi-hop route model is a room-level graph over `Game.map.describeExits`, searched once per (home, goal) and memoised for the bundle's life, with ADR 0041's Seam join applied pairwise along the resulting list and the per-room flood untouched. Decision 4's declared route **is** that list, written by a human. So the test of whether this workaround was the right shape is precise: landing #243 should delete `Tuning.ReactorRoute` and change no rule. Until then the cost is the honest one — a route a human must re-derive if we ever own a different home room.
- **CPU is one more multi-room walker and the cadence is what bounds it.** At most one courier and one reclaimer stand outside the colony's worked rooms at any tick, by construction of the two cadences, so the route costs about two creeps' floods over rooms whose terrain memo is free (ADR 0031) — against #171's finding that a full-room flood per creep per tick is a third of the tick, that is a measurable and bounded addition rather than a budget. `npm run profile` gains a reactor scenario with a courier standing in a transit room, and it is part of this work rather than a follow-up, for ADR 0056's reason: a path no scenario executes is a path no profile has ever seen.
- **Theft is undone in one tick or not at all.** `claimReactor` inherits `launchTime`, so a rival's `[CLAIM, MOVE]` takes a 40,000-tick streak and scores 5/tick off our Thorium from the tick he lands — and we take it back on the same terms with the streak intact. What the exchange costs is the ticks between the theft and our next act: one, while the relay stands; 575 if it has gapped. **The reclaimer's cadence is the entire defence** — the reactor has no `hits`, cannot be attacked or dismantled, and cannot be ramparted in a room with no controller (ADR 0056's finding for an [[outpost]], one level harsher here).
- **The route crosses one Source Keeper room and the margin is declared, not derived.** Keepers are pinned to range 1 of their own rock and never pursue, so five tiles is sufficient and the failure mode of getting it wrong is a dead courier and one lost trip, not a lost streak — the reactor's 1,000-unit buffer absorbs a missed delivery, which is the second thing the 800-tick cadence buys.
- **A courier that dies loaded makes the tile it died on hot** until its tombstone decays: ~999 T is `p = 2`, so the next body over that tile ages 3 for the tick it stands there. Noise, and recorded here so the reader who sees it in a [[raid log]] episode or a `dryTicks` spike knows what it is.
- **The road under the mineral container is the only road in the colony that decays fast**, at 3× while the container holds 100–999 T — 0.3 hits/tick, inside the ordinary Repair pool's trigger and worth about 0.3 energy a tick together with the container itself. Every other road on the route is untouched, the Thorium being in a store on one tile and never in transit over them.
- **The Matcher gains a Task it can only count, and that is the first of its kind.** Every pooled Task so far is ranked by a [[priority]] and a [[travel cost]] the [[atlas]] can answer for; `Deliver` has a priority and no price, because its walk is a declared route and not a flood. The Matcher needs no new rule for it — a candidate with no price and a capacity of one binds the one body shaped for it — but the reader who arrives expecting every Task to be comparable should be told here rather than find out from a `Scoring` line with a blank in it. It is also the reason the courier's applicability is written narrowly: an unpriced Task that any body could hold is an unpriced Task every body would win.
- **ADR 0056's guard does not reach the route and is not meant to.** A Guard is cast on a Threat seen standing in a declared [[outpost]], and a route room is not an outpost and carries no hostiles in the projection at all. The two decisions meet only in what they say about a room with no controller: there is no rampart, no tower and no safe mode, and the only defence available is a creep — which for an outpost is a melee body and for the reactor is the re-claimer, because the thing being defended is an ownership flag and not a structure that can be killed.
- **Four ADRs take a banner on the tickets that land the clauses amending them**: ADR 0006 (a sixth, seventh and eighth row, and the [[body class]] fold reading a store-less Heavy body), ADR 0012 (the container family gains a mineral container placed by a rule of its own), ADR 0040 (the target clause reads a mineral beside a source and a controller) and ADR 0041 (the route, which is the first walk in this colony that is not priced by a Seam join and is deliberately not priced at all).
- **W13S28, the second [[colony]], inherits every rule and none of the constants.** Its deposit at (42,30) is the same row, the same Task and the same body the tick it reaches RCL6; what it does not inherit is decision 4's route, which starts from a different home room and is a second human sentence beside the first.
- **The fixtures grow a mineral, an extractor and a route.** `DecideTests` gains a mineral fixture in the home room — the miner row's quota pairwise (extractor standing and not, deposit non-empty and gone), the `bodyFor` table's two new banks, the cadence pairwise for both new rows (a 700-tick courier suppressing a cast and a 701-tick one not), and the start conditions pairwise. `AtlasTests` gains the mineral Post and the route's hop-by-hop aim. The six route rooms' terrain should be committed as fixtures the way ADR 0036's four rooms were, because a route over invented terrain proves nothing about the one we will walk.

## Glossary additions

Proposed for `CONTEXT.md`, not written here:

- **Reactor** — the sector centre's scoring structure, W15S25 (44,6): 1,000 capacity for Thorium, one consumed a tick, `1 + floor(log10(1 + continuousWork))` score apiece, the streak reset by one dry tick and by nothing else. Indestructible, walkable, and taken by any CLAIM body adjacent for one tick with the streak inherited. Not a [[post]], not an [[outpost]] and not a [[claim]]'s target: it stands in a room with no controller. _Avoid_: the score building, the sink.
- **Thorium** — the season's scoring resource, and the one resource beside energy this colony carries. A deposit that never regenerates, 22,000 apiece in W12S28 and W13S28; a store holding it penalises everything standing on its tile by `floor(log10 total)` ticks of life and decay per tick, which is what every body sizing in ADR 0057 is derived from. Free in a dropped pile, in an untouched deposit, and in the [[storage]], which nothing can stand on.
- **Miner** — the row and the body that digs the Thorium: work-heavy, **no Carry at all**, standing on the [[post]] the mineral container makes, harvesting only on the tick the extractor's cooldown is zero. Not an [[anchor]] and not sized like one — a mineral has no regeneration to saturate. _Avoid_: driller, extractor (which is the structure).
- **Courier** — the row that carries one 999-unit load of Thorium down the declared route to the [[reactor]] and dies there. One body, one trip, one size — `[20 Carry; 10 Move]` — and a cadence rather than a quota. Distinct from the [[hauler unit]], whose whole life is a [[trunk]] inside one colony's rooms. _Avoid_: hauler, runner.
- **Re-claimer** — the resident `[Claim; Move]` body beside the [[reactor]], re-cast every 300 ticks, which takes the reactor back on the tick it is not ours and gives the colony its only vision of W15S25. The whole of the defence, there being no rampart, no tower and no safe mode in a room with no controller.
- **Route** — a list of room names a human declared from a colony's home to a goal no [[seam]] join can price, walked one hop at a time with the one-hop machinery and no flood ever leaving a room. Beside each intermediate room, the keeper centres its tiles are masked around. A workaround with an expiry date: #243's multi-hop model should delete the list and change no rule.
