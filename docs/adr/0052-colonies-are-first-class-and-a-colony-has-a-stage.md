# Colonies are first-class, and a colony has a stage

Two days of live deploys of the second colony (W13S28, ADR 0047) broke one rule after another that had been written for the one RCL5 home the bot grew up in: pricing every row at the bank's capacity (#203), a posted source worth the room's rate rather than what the Anchor row digs at a 300 bank (#208), road and rampart sites at RCL1–2 in a room earning eight a tick (#209, #214), a trunk priced at a creep's swamp weight (#211), a standing body crossing a Seam for a pile (#206), the Post's Seat filled by the light crowd (#212), pioneers hired for the child and kept home by the tier they were pooled in (#213), and before all of these a second spawn casting into a colony-wide cascade and a Layout reading that spawn as home coordinates (#191). None was a defect in the rule's own terms; every one was a hidden constant of the single home — its level, its bank, its room — spelled as if it were a colony fact. The user's verdict (2026-09-06): "从一开始商量的时候我就不应该和你妥协，前提直接从多殖民地、多 remote 开始定".

We decided the premise is rewritten, not patched further. **The skeleton stands** — the layered Atlas and its flood tables (ADR 0041), a `decide` derived fresh every tick from facts, a pure Core under Expecto, the Verdicts and `observe.mjs` — and **the model under it is rewritten around two facts: a colony is the unit everything is computed for, and a colony has a stage.**

## Decision

**1. A colony is the unit.** The shell builds one `World` — every room seen this tick, once — and one `ColonyView` per living colony: that colony's home, the rooms it works, its own bank, controller, creeps and stock. Every function in Decide takes a `ColonyView` and nothing else. There is no `Snapshot.Controller` that is "the" controller, no `ColonyHomes` beside `Colony.declared`, no `richestCapacity` over rooms the colony does not own. What one colony may read of another is an explicit field of its view (a child's controller and sites, for the mother), never a narrowed layer.

**2. A position carries its room.** `RoomPos = { Room; X; Y }` at every boundary a tile crosses. Grids stay per room; the sets handed between functions do not drop the room. A Layout goal, a Work Area, a Seat, a Post, a trunk tile are room-joined by type, not by convention.

**3. A colony has a stage, and the rules read the stage.** `ColonyStage = Nursery | Bootstrapping | Independent`: claimed with no spawn of ours; a spawn standing and the controller under `bootstrapLevel`; at or past it. One derivation (`Colony.stageOf`), read wherever a rule today reads `isNurseryRoom`, `isBootstrapRoom`, `roadLevel`, `rampartLevel` or the pioneer addend. A rule that differs by stage says so in one place; a level number appears only inside `stageOf`.

**4. Every quota input is what this colony's row casts at this bank.** Never a nominal rate, never the bank's capacity, never a living body. `sourceOutputOf`, `castFromBank`, the hauler round trip, the upgrader drain, the reserver body, the worker floor: each priced off `bodyFor row view.Bank`, and each carrying a pairwise test at a 300 bank and an 1,800 one.

**5. Tunables are one record, parameterised by stage and bank.** `pioneerCount`, `rampartFloor`, `repairTrigger`, `pickupThreshold`, `outpostContainerBuilders`, `bootstrapLevel` and their kin move out of scattered module constants into `Tuning`, where each field states the stage and bank it was derived at and what it reads below them. A number without a pairwise test is not a tunable; it is a bug that has not happened yet.

**6. The pool carries its own priority and capacity; the Matcher is dumb.** `planTasks` emits each Task with its `Priority` and its `Capacity: BodyClass -> int`, computed by the colony that knows why. The Matcher matches, and recognises no Task kind. The exceptions that accreted on the old tier ladder — deadline rank, feeding sites, borrowed Upgrades, Post caps, light caps — become fields the planner sets.

**7. Cross-colony borrowing is an explicit, bounded exception on the view.** Today's one is the pioneers (ADR 0047 decision 4, #213). A mother's view names the child's Tasks it may take and the cap; nothing else of the child's reaches her pool.

**8. The shell boundary is testable.** `Snapshot.build` becomes a pure function of a captured, API-shaped input, run in tests on committed captures; Executor intents replay. The harness gains a `young` scenario (RCL1, 300 bank, two Posts, mini bodies) and a `pair` scenario (mother RCL5 with a bootstrapping child), with a CPU line per colony.

## Considered options

- **Keep patching by live incident.** Rejected by the user and by the count: eight tickets in two days, each a constant of the one home, each found by a creep standing in the wrong place at 3 a.m.
- **Rewrite everything, Atlas included.** Rejected: the Atlas, the per-tick derivation and the observability are what let the eight be found and fixed inside a day each; they are not where the debt is.
- **Stages as levels.** Keep reading `Level >= 3` in each rule. Rejected: four rules read the number today and a fifth would read it tomorrow; a stage is the fact, the level is how it is derived.

## Consequences

- Types.fs and the quota, pool and casting layers of Decide.fs are rewritten; Atlas.fs and Observe.fs change at their signatures (`RoomPos`, `ColonyView`) and not in their algorithms. Snapshot.fs and Main.fs are rewritten around `World`/`ColonyView`.
- Every ADR that reads "the spawn room", "the colony's controller" or "home" as a singular is re-read under decision 1; those already banner-amended (0011, 0020, 0024, 0034, 0040, 0042, 0046, 0047) keep their banners.
- The work is sequenced so the live colonies keep running: harness and captures first (8), then the view and stage (1, 3), then positions (2), then quotas and tunables (4, 5), then the pool and Matcher (6, 7). Each step ships green on the existing suite plus the pairwise tests it adds.
- CONTEXT.md gains `World`, `Colony view`, `Stage`, `Tuning`; `Nursery` and `Bootstrap window` become the first two stages.
- **Decision 3 landed** (#216 R1, 2026-09-06): `ColonyStage`, `Colony.stageOf` and `Snapshot.Stages`, with the road gate, the rampart gate and floor, the nursery, the bootstrap and the bootstrapping-room predicates and `Colony.bootstrapping` all reading it — ADR 0047's Consequences records reader by reader what moved and what did not. Behaviour is unchanged for every colony the live declaration can be in, and the two gates that read `Controller.Level` answer identically for a living colony, whose stage and controller come off the same `controller` under the same `my` guard. It is **not** unchanged in general, and the plan's "行为不变" overstated it: folding a level and an ownership bit into three cases reads wider below independence (a spawnless child at RCL3+ is raised again) and narrower where ownership goes (an unowned declared child is projected by nobody), both recorded in ADR 0047's Consequences with what they cost. One place the landing reads wider than the plan's own table: the mother's borrowing predicate answers for a child at `Bootstrapping` **or** `Independent`, because ADR 0047's Consequences make the pre-split window's closer the scan set and never a level read at the predicate.

- **Decision 1 landed in its structural half, and decision 8's first half with it** (#216 R2a, 2026-09-07; the second half, and #137 with it, is not landed — the engine read is still compiled by no test project). The shell builds one `World` (`World.ofGame`, the one reader of the game's objects) and Core cuts one `ColonyView` per living colony (`ColonyView.ofWorld`, pure and under test). `Snapshot` is `ColonyView`; `ColonyHomes` is `Declared`, cut from the declaration the view was built under; the per-room bank map and `richestCapacity`'s cross-room fold are one `Bank`, the home room's; `narrowToBootstrap` is `ColonyView.borrowed`, driven by `Borrowed.Rooms`; and what a colony does *not* hold in the rooms it works is `Foreign`. Behaviour is unchanged for every world the live declaration reaches — the 927 tests that pinned it are green with two edits, and the harness's four scenarios measure the same CPU (`young` 1.42 quiet / 4.49 census, `pair` 3.89 / 7.83, against R0's 1.45 / 4.50 and 3.93 / 7.96). Two things that measurement and that sentence do **not** cover, both found in R2a's review. The world is `Game.rooms` ∪ the declaration, so a room we can *see* and no colony works is swept in full where the old shell swept nothing — a [[stand-down]]'s withheld outpost with one of our creeps still in it is that room — and every harness scenario populates `Game.rooms` with exactly the rooms its colonies work, so the union equals the scan set there and the case is invisible to the numbers above. And the undeclared-world fallback (`Colony.living`) now takes the first owned spawn room in **room-name** order, where the shell handed it `Game.spawns` enumeration order: in the one state it fires in — nothing declared living, spawns in two owned rooms — it can name the other room. The world files rooms under their names and carries no engine order to hand it; the rule is stated in `World.spawnRooms` and pinned in `ViewTests` instead.
- **Decision 7 landed, in its arbitration half and one of its two borrowings** (#216 R2b, 2026-09-07). Movement is the one step of the pipeline that is not a colony's: `decide` returns its colony's Move Intents unarbitrated (`Decision.Movement`) and the shell arbitrates each room once over every creep of ours standing in it (`Decide.resolveRooms`), each moving on the intent its own colony registered — which is what #220's live deadlock asked for, a mother's pioneer standing fifteen minutes on a tile the child's Anchor held and her flood priced at nothing. The arbitration core is a weighted augmenting search (sy-harabi's algorithm, rewritten in F#: the library is unlicensed and issues the engine's moves itself, which ADR 0001 and ADR 0009 each refuse), and it buys an injective answer the claim-by-claim settle could not promise (`RoomInvariantTests`). Beside it a Move Intent's candidates became a head and a tail, which is #219's deadlock: a traveller whose step is held by a body that cannot move sidesteps around it. `ColonyView.Foreign` has both its readers now — the flood's occupancy surcharge (ADR 0008) and the single-colony pass, where such a tile is a wall. On the borrowing side, `BorrowedWork.Rooms` gained the **reclaim** (`Colony.reclaiming`, #221): a declared child that stops being ours, and that nobody else holds, is projected by its mother again so a Claim is pooled to take it back.
- **Decision 6 landed, and with it decision 7's last owing** (#216 R5, 2026-09-07). A pooled Task carries two numbers and one flag the Planner sets — `Priority`, `Capacity` and `Borrowed` — and the Matcher knows no Task kinds at all: it compares the priority and the [[travel cost]] and counts holders against the capacity. `tierOf`, `rankOfTier`, `rank`, `taskCapacities` and `postCapacities` are gone as Matcher readers; the tier ladder, the [[downgrade deadline]]'s lift, the Seat/Post/light split (ADR 0024, ADR 0051), the store-over-load divisors (#161, #167), the outpost builders' budget (#157), the [[pioneer]]s' ceiling (#213) and #205's two tile sets are all `planPool`'s, said once. Three things moved that had been spelled twice and could disagree: `applicable`'s Work-heavy Build gate now reads the Task's own priority instead of re-deriving the site's class, its standing-body Upgrade gate reads the `Borrowed` flag, and the crowding term of the matching key is maintained beside the fold rather than rescanned per pair (#95). Tiers stand **ten rungs apart** so a Task can be ordered inside its tier, which buys the one behaviour change asked for beside the refactor: a [[pickup]] outbids the [[withdraw]] standing on the same tile, because a pile decays and a container does not. The rung is carried by the **Pickup**, stepping up where a drawable store stands under it, and not by the Withdraw stepping down (moved by the R5 review): a priority is a scalar the whole tier is ordered by, so the demotion put that container behind every other store at any distance and sent the rest of the hauler row past two thousand energy three tiles from its feet — and the engine drops an overflow onto a container's tile only once the container is full, so it switched on exactly when the store most needed emptying. `weightOfRank` subtracts one step before dividing, so a tier and either of its stepped neighbours land on one push weight. Beside it a [[capacity]] can now answer per [[body class]], which is #196 — the [[buffer]] divides by the generalists' load for them and by the [[standing body]]'s fifty for the row that lives there, with no total over the two.

  And the **ferry** (#222): `ColonyView.ofWorld` carries exactly one store of a **bootstrapping** child's — the container in that child's controller's own Upgrade area and on none of its Seats — so `planTasks` pools its Refill, denies its Withdraw, and `haulerQuota` prices its round trip to the very tile the Refill is on. `Tuning.FerryLoads` ships at **one**: the two halves of the split feature land together, so a body hired for the lend has a Task to be matched to. Three things the review moved (#216 R5 review, 2026-09-07). The store is carried for a `Bootstrapping` child and for **no other stage**: `Borrowed.Rooms` is also the [[nursery]] she is raising and the child she has lost (#221), and a sink carried there is a sink nothing fills — a plain Feeding-tier Withdraw of the mother's, her haulers crossing the Seam to bring another colony's upgrade energy home, which is the drain ADR 0047 decision 1 refuses. Beside the narrowing, `planTasks` denies the Withdraw over the **borrowed rooms** rather than over the ferry's own sinks, so the denial does not ride on the lend. And ADR 0023's storage gate counts the ferry's Refill: it is a Refill target that is not the Storage, which is the whole of that ADR's condition, and left out it made the two conditions nearly exclusive — the lend's Refill sits on the buffer's deep tier, so it is the last sink to be reached, which is exactly the state that used to leave the stock unpoolable, and the body the quota hired for the round trip had a sink and no intake.

  **Measured, harness against harness** (criterion D, the four scenarios on `@-` and on `@`, 100 ticks each): the per-Task holder distribution is **identical in all four** — `stub` harvest 0.98 / refill 0.97 / upgrade 5.56 / withdraw 3.81 bodies a colony-tick, `outpost` claim 0.97 / harvest 2.87 / reserve 0.98 / upgrade 1.83 / withdraw 6.46, `young` harvest 1.96 / upgrade 4.60 / withdraw 4.77, `pair` build 1.89 / harvest 2.43 / reserve 0.49 / upgrade 2.27 / withdraw 4.22 — and the CPU is flat inside this machine's ±3% run-to-run spread (median ms/tick over three runs: `stub` 2.16–2.21 before, 2.13–2.26 after; `pair` 4.00–4.12 before, 4.13–4.14 after; `outpost` 2.67 → 2.71, `young` 1.51 → 1.51). So the number says the refactor is behaviour-neutral where it claims to be and says nothing about the two places it is not; those are pinned pairwise in the suite instead, which is the only seam that can hold them. The [[ferry]] is in no scenario either — `pair`'s child has no buffer container — so its CPU is unmeasured here.

  **Four instructions of R5's plan are not implemented, three of them because the authority disagrees.** #82's gate — pooling the Storage's Withdraw always and shutting it with a `Capacity` of zero — was left as a pooling rule: it swaps the `TaskGone` release the issue complains about for an `OverCapacity` one and cures nothing, and the fix #82 itself names (judge the condition per creep at [[arrival]], the move ADR 0025 made for Harvest) is a decision this step did not take. #164's rule — a creep standing in a [[stand-down]] room prices lowest and caps at zero for every Task — **cannot** be a field of a pooled Task: it is a fact about a creep, and a Priority that varied by candidate is not a priority. #197's — a `Heavy` capacity of zero at an unposted source — would rename an existing rejection (`Unreachable`, off ADR 0045's empty Work Area in an outpost) without giving the full [[anchor]] anywhere to go, which is the whole of that issue; and at home ADR 0045's bare-Seat fallback means the cap would have to be room-shaped as well. #94's — the arrival gate re-holding a creep for the first step of its own dispatch — is the ADR-level choice between three options its own issue lists and costs no ticks; it stays open. #198 needed nothing: a Withdraw's capacity has been its store's stock over one load since #161, and holders full is already what sends the next body to another store.

- **Decision 2 landed** (#216 R3, 2026-09-07). `RoomPos = { Room; X; Y }` is what a tile is once it leaves the grid it indexes, and `Pos` keeps the narrower meaning it always had in fact: a grid coordinate, a key of one room's terrain, obstacles, roads, flood arrays and Seat, Reach and Work-Area tables. Every Atlas answer that leaves the Atlas carries its room (`positionOf`, `creepTile`, `seatTilesOf`, `postsOf`, `standingPostsOf`, `workArea`, `workAreaFor`, `placedTowers`, `droppedEnergyIn`, `placedCreeps`, `firstStep`, `trunkPath`, and the areas those queries are handed back), and the implicit-home variants that stood beside the `…In atlas room` ones are gone — the room is in the argument or it is in the type, never in the callee's hand. The fields that carried a room name *beside* a tile carry it *in* the tile: `HostileInfo`, `MoveIntent` and its candidates, `Movement.Placed`, `ColonyView.Foreign`, `Outpost.Sources` and `Outpost.Controller`, the Layout's `UnservedFooting`, `ServedFooting` and `DeferredContainer`, `Intent.PlaceConstructionSite`, and the Raid log's closest approach (#204, which closes with it — the tile prints its room). `RoomPos.range` answers **None** across a border rather than a number, which is what the accidental "range 0" answers were.

- **Decisions 4 and 5 landed** (#216 R4, 2026-09-07). The numbers split in two by the one question that tells them apart — a number is an `Engine` constant when changing it would be a lie about the server, and a `Tuning` field when changing it would be a different colony. Sixteen module constants across Decide, Atlas and Observe became `Tuning`, carried on `ColonyView` and handed in to `ColonyView.ofWorld` and `Colony.stageOf` (the one reader of `BootstrapLevel`) rather than read off a constant, and sixteen more became `Engine`, each named for the server constant it spells.
