# Post capacity is body-aware: one garrison per Post, and only a garrison's overflow keeps Harvest

> **Joined by ADR 0051**: beside the Post cap over heavy bodies sits its complement, a light cap of `seats − posts` over light ones, and a light body's Work Area excludes the Posts. One garrison per Post, and the Post's Seat is the garrison's (#212).

> **Revised by ADR 0048** on where the full-store reprieve is asked, never on what it says: "a full store keeps Harvest only for a Work-heavy body on a container Seat" is a rule about a body that is *digging*, and a Work-heavy body that has not yet reached the tiles it may dig from is walking. So such a body keeps Harvest while `mayAct` is false and its source has a Post to walk to — it never empties (ADR 0016, ADR 0046, ADR 0048), so full is its ordinary condition and a store gate read as "done here" would strand it a tile off its own Post. On arrival this ADR's condition governs unchanged: the only tile that keeps a full body digging is still the container. The Post cap and the light body's sentence are untouched.

ADR 0012 widened Harvest's applicability so that a full creep standing on a built source container keeps digging — the engine drops the overflow into the container underfoot — and ADR 0020 narrowed a Work-heavy body's Harvest Work Area to that source's Posts. Both were written for the Anchor row, but each was expressed body-blind on one side, and the seasonal room showed what the gap costs: at t69340 source `…94aa` had exactly one Seat, its container standing on it, and a full `4W/4C/4M` worker had been sitting on that Post since t69135 — a full store on a container is never Inapplicable, so anti-thrash never released it, so it never hauled, never upgraded and never moved. The Anchor cast for that Post read `idle (none-free)` and settled instead on the other source, whose five Seats admitted it beside the Anchor already standing on that source's single Post. Two Posts, two Anchors, both in the wrong place.

We decided **Post count is a capacity unit of its own, and the garrison widening reads the body**: a source admits at most as many Work-heavy harvesters as it has Posts, beside the Seat cap every harvester shares; and a full store keeps Harvest only for a Work-heavy body on a container Seat — a light body's full store ends its dig wherever it stands. Both rules read part arithmetic, never a row name (ADR 0006), and both are the same judgement ADR 0020 already made: the tile a heavy body may work from is the Post, so the Post is what it competes for and what it may garrison past a full store.

## Considered Options

- **Fix only the widening (gate it on a Work-heavy body)** — rejected as insufficient: it frees the squatted Post's Seat, but the Anchor that mis-settled stays `kept` on the five-Seat source — never oversold against the Seat cap — until it dies. The over-admission is what left the second Post unmanned, and it needs the Post cap to release it.
- **Fix only the cap (Posts instead of Seats for a heavy body)** — rejected as insufficient in the other direction: the cap counts heavy bodies, so a light squatter still holds the Post tile itself for life, and the Anchor would be admitted only to stand next to a tile it can never claim.
- **Replace the Seat cap with the Post cap outright** — rejected: a Seat is still the concurrency unit for the light bodies that carry the colony before the first container, and a source with no Post must stay open to every Seat (ADR 0020's fallback). Both caps hold; a Post cap of zero derives nothing.
- **Reserve the Post tile in the spatial projection (make it impassable to light bodies)** — rejected: it would make a Task inapplicable on grounds of traffic, which ADR 0008 refused — traffic re-prices a route, it never blocks work — and the Resolver already evicts a light body from a tile a garrison claims. What was missing was never eviction but release.
- **Let the Matcher prefer the Anchor already standing on the Post when releasing** — rejected as unearned machinery: the anti-thrash release walks the remembered assignments in memory order, so which of two equivalent Anchors is released is arbitrary, and the released one rematches the same tick by travel cost. One tick of suboptimal placement is not worth a positional tie-break inside the release fold.

## Consequences

- A light body can still stand on a Post while digging — it is simply released the moment it fills, and the Resolver evicts it when a garrison claims the tile. The Post stops being a place a light body can retire to.
- `IdleReason.NoneFree` on an Anchor now means the Posts are manned, not that the Seats are full: the verdict became diagnostic instead of misleading.
- The workforce target is unchanged — it already counted one Anchor per Post and retired a posted source's other Seats (ADR 0012) — so the quota and the matching cap finally agree on what a Post is.
- Two Anchors alive against one Post (a recast racing a survivor, or a Post lost with a container) leaves one of them released and rematched each tick until a Post opens; with the cap in place it goes to another source's Post or to Upgrade rather than crowding a tile.
- The DecideTests surface: the "gate is body-blind" test is inverted — a full worker on the container now releases Harvest as Inapplicable — and a `post capacity` list pins the cap, the fresh-match half of it, the light body's untouched Seat cap, and the no-Post fallback.
