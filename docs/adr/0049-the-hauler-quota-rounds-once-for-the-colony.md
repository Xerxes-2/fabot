# The hauler quota rounds once, for the colony, because a hauler is not the property of one container

> **Amended by #216 R4** on what the sum is taken over, never on the rounding this ADR is about. Each source container's demand is now priced to **every sink the colony spends into** — the spawn/extension cluster, the controller's upgrade [[buffer]], the [[storage]] — with its flow spread evenly over the sinks it can price (ADR 0012's banner carries the argument), and the [[ferry]] a [[mother colony]] lends a bootstrapping child is added beside the sum in whole bodies rather than in tick-energy. The demands are still summed first and the ceiling still taken **once, for the colony**; the per-container average truncates by at most two units of tick-energy against a divisor of a hundred and up, which is under a thousandth of a body and is not the rounding this ADR forbids.

ADR 0012 gave the [[hauler unit]] its quota as "per source [[container]], round-trip ticks to the spawn × 10 e/tick ÷ carry capacity, rounded up", and ADR 0037 quoted that "rounded up" back as the precedent for the [[worker unit]]'s own rounding. Neither ADR ever decided the **granularity** of the rounding — how much demand one ceiling is taken over — and the implementation answered it by accident: one `ceilDiv` per container, so every container bought a whole body for its own fraction. On the live colony (t156,2xx, RCL5) that is three [[outpost]] containers wanting 1.3 haulers apiece and two home containers wanting 0.5, hiring 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = **eight** against a demand of **4.9**. Nine were standing, one of them visibly idle. Each surplus body is 1,800 energy over a 1,500-tick life — four to five energy a tick of hire the colony never asked for — plus the CPU every creep costs (#171). The fractions compound with distance, which is why the defect was invisible at home (two containers at 0.2 each) and expensive the tick the outposts switched on.

**The rule.** The hauler row's quota is `ceil(Σ over the source containers of round-trip ticks × that source's output ÷ the cast body's carry capacity)`: the demands are summed first and the ceiling is taken **once, for the colony**. Every container's own round trip is still priced at its own source's output (ADR 0042), and its cheapest spawn is still its own (ADR 0012's canonical sink); what changes is that the fraction a container leaves over is added to its neighbours' instead of being bought outright.

**Why the colony is the right grain now, and was not in 2012's shape.** ADR 0012 rejected the *flat* quota — one hauler per container regardless of distance — because it "systematically under-ships the far source", and the fear is exactly right: a shared row of haulers is only safe if the far container can pull bodies out of it. Since #161 it can. A `Withdraw`'s capacity is that store's own stock divided by one hauler load, so a container that fills faster than it is drained admits more drawers than one that does not, and the row's shared integer is spent where the energy actually stands rather than pinned per container at hiring time. **The cap is a capacity and not an order**, and this ADR does not claim otherwise: `tierOf` files every source container's `Withdraw` on the feeding tier alike and travel cost ranks inside that tier, which is the *near* container's advantage. What #161 buys the far container is room for the bodies the shared integer no longer spends at home — not priority. Whether the dispatch then fills that room fast enough is a live question and not this ADR's: it is a property of the ranking, it was there under the old rounding too, and it goes to the tracker rather than being asserted here. This ADR is the opposite of the option ADR 0012 rejected in the one respect it decides: distance still sets each container's share of the number, and only the rounding is shared.

**What does not move.** The direction of the rounding (up, ADR 0012 and ADR 0037's asymmetry argument, unchanged: the colony is hired for at least the whole flow). The per-source output rate (ADR 0042). The per-container cheapest-sink minimum. The worker and [[upgrader]] rows' own roundings, which are already colony-wide sums and were never at issue.

**One divisor, and it is the pipeline's own.** Summing before dividing means every term has to be a fraction of the *same* body, so the sum is divided once by the row's cast at `richestCapacity` — the same load `workforceTarget` charges this row's amortization at and the same one a `Withdraw`'s cap divides its store's stock by (#161). A per-spawn denominator would give the colony a third reading of "one hauler load" and let the quota, the charge and the cap disagree; on the one banked room the projection has ever carried the three are the same number anyway.

## Considered Options

- **Keep a ceiling per container.** Rejected: it is the defect. Its cost is not a rounding error but up to one body per container, it grows with the number of containers, and it grows again with distance — the outpost containers it overbuys are the dearest bodies the colony casts.
- **Round to nearest, or floor, for the colony's total.** Rejected on ADR 0037's own argument, which this ADR does not reopen: the ceiling's oversell is bounded by one body over the whole colony and is paid out of stock, while a floor's undersell leaks every tick into a container that overflows onto the ground. At the colony's grain the oversell is now *one* body total rather than one per container, which is the cheapest it has ever been.
- **A colony-wide flat number, or a per-room quota.** Rejected: both throw away the round trip, which is the only thing in the arithmetic that knows an outpost is fifty tiles away.
- **Leave the ceiling per container and shrink the fleet with a cap.** Rejected: a cap is a second number with no derivation, and it would hide the fact that the demand was being computed correctly and rounded wrongly.

## Consequences

- **The granularity is now written down** where it was previously only implied by an implementation. ADR 0012's "rounded up" reads as "the colony's demand is rounded up"; ADR 0037's citation of it is unaffected, because the worker row's own quota was always one division over one colony-wide surplus.
- **Every multi-container fixture's hauler count falls**, by design: W12S28's two home containers go from two bodies to one at the RCL5 bank and from four to three at the 300 bank; the [[outpost]] switch adds two bodies to the row where it used to add three. The tests carrying those numbers move with them (#194).
- **The [[workforce target]] moves twice over**, in opposite directions: the hauler row is a smaller addend, and its replacement `amortization` is a smaller deduction, so the worker row is hired against a slightly larger surplus. On the live shape the two nearly cancel and the worker count is unmoved.
- **The row is more sensitive to distance, not less.** Under a ceiling apiece a container could grow by half a body and hire nothing; one rounding spends every fraction, so lengthening one haul by seven steps is a hire.
- **ADR 0038's conclusion survives; the arithmetic it was derived from does not, and ADR 0038 carries a banner saying so.** Its answer is unchanged — this colony builds no links at RCL5, and W12S28 still sits on the hauler row's floor — but the floor it named is gone: "one term per source container, each rounded up, so the row's floor is the number of source containers" was the old granularity, and the floor is now **one body for the whole colony's haul**, which is where the room's two home containers land (`richIncomeFleet` in `DecideTests.fs` pins it). Its flip condition moves with it: a second body used to arrive when one container's round trip passed eighty ticks at the 1,300 bank, and now arrives when the colony's *summed* trips do, which for a pair of home containers is roughly half that apiece. The prize a link could win is still one body and the price is still two links at 5,000, so the trade ADR 0038 refused is refused harder. The RCL6 revisit it defers inherits this rule, not the per-container one.
- The [[hauler unit]] and [[workforce target]] glossary entries carry the summed reading; this ADR is where the decision behind those sentences lives.
