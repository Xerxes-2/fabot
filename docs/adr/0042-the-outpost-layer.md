# The outpost layer: a reservation, a container, and what an unposted outpost source is worth

> **Amended by #216 R4** on which rooms the [[reserver]] row hires for, and on the body its lead is priced at. **A room with an owner is dropped, whoever owns it** (#184): the engine answers ERR_INVALID_TARGET on any owned controller, and the Consequences' one-tick window — the tick between first seeing a declared outpost held by a rival and ADR 0043's gate withdrawing from it, which reads the previous tick's [[raid log]] — cast the bank's largest reserver body, 1,300 energy at today's bank, for a creep that would walk over and be refused. The [[reserve]] pool drops the same room by the same read, so the row still hires against exactly the controllers the pool offers. And the row's **lead** is priced at `reserverBodyWithin` under this tick's demand rather than at `bodyFor`'s bank-truncated largest (#158, ADR 0026's banner): in steady state the two differ by six cast ticks and a walk, and the Anchor row's lead moves with `anchorWorkCapOf` for the same reason. `anchorWorkCapOf` itself is **unchanged** and stays the largest posted rate: deriving it from a body the ceiling itself sizes is the circle this ADR's own #208 amendment refuses, and the per-Post pairing #158 asks for waits on the arrival-time pairing and the per-Post amortization that ADR would need.

> **Amended by #205** on the timing of two clauses in *"the tick it stands the source becomes a Post, gains an Anchor, and enters the income base"*, and on nothing else. The [[post]] and the [[anchor]] place arrive one step earlier, with the container's **construction site** on a [[seat]]: the body that garrisons a Post is the body that raises it, digging twelve a tick into the progress under its own feet, and an outpost hired no such body while its Work Area was empty (ADR 0045) and its [[build]] shut (ADR 0046) — so an invader demolishing three containers cost the colony thousands of ticks of worker commute instead of hundreds of ticks of digging. The third clause is unmoved and it is the one this ADR's argument rests on: the [[hauler unit]]'s term and the income base still wait for a container to *stand*, because a rock whose energy goes into 5,000 progress fills no store and feeds no mouth. So the census splits in two — `Atlas.postsIn` is ground, `Atlas.standingPostsIn` is income (`Decide.isPosted`) — and "until one stands, the outpost is invisible to every quota" holds of every quota but the Anchor row's.

> **Amended by #208** on what "the source's output" is in the two quotas that read a store, and on nothing else. **A posted source is worth what the [[anchor]] row's cast digs there, capped at the room's rate**: the rate is what the rock regenerates and so a ceiling, and the output the [[hauler unit]]'s term and the income base read is `min(rate, anchorWork × HARVEST_POWER)` for the body `anchorBodyFor` would cast at this colony's richest bank (`Decide.sourceOutputOf`). Read at the rate alone, an RCL1 child banking 300 counted 20 a tick off two Posts its `2W/1C/1M` Anchors dug 8 out of, hired ~18 [[worker unit]]s and 4 [[hauler unit]]s against it, and stood 12 of them idle beside a full spawn (t169,0xx). The row's *cast* and never the living Anchor's body, because a quota read off a living body oscillates on that body's death and the row's cast is the colony fact (ADR 0006) — and it is the same body this ADR already charges the row's amortization at, so credit and charge come out of one cast. The **ceiling** below is untouched and stays the room's rate: `anchorWorkCapOf` derives the row's Work cap from a source's output, so capping that output by the body would size the body off a number the body decides. Nothing here moves a colony whose bank buys six Work — twelve a tick, over any rate — which is every bank from 700 up.

ADR 0041 makes an [[outpost]] visible to the decision layer. This ADR decides what the colony *does* there, and like ADR 0012 — whose remote twin it is — it is written whole because its parts are each other's preconditions: without a [[reservation]] the source is worth half; without a [[container]] the source has no [[post]]; without a Post the source has no [[anchor]] and falls back on a rule that would send generalists on a fifty-tile commute; and the [[reserver]] that supplies the reservation is a fourth pattern row, which by ADR 0006 arrives with its quota or does not arrive.

The colony takes **two** outposts at once, W12S27 to the north and W13S28 to the west, three sources between them. The economic case is the whole reason the ticket exists: the home room is spending everything it earns, so the next energy has to come from somewhere else. W12S27 also holds this season's only scoring resource — Thorium, density 3, 22,000, at `24,16` — which is a reason to hold that room rather than a reason to design for it; extracting it needs an extractor at RCL6 and nothing here is built for that.

**Every outpost is reserved from the first version.** An unreserved source regenerates 1,500 over 300 ticks and yields 5 energy a tick; a reserved one yields 10. A `[2Claim;2Move]` body costs 1,300 — exactly the RCL4 bank — and amortises over `CREEP_CLAIM_LIFE_TIME` (600) at 2.17 energy a tick, so it pays for itself more than twice over on a single source. **Each outpost gets its own reserver.** The one roving reserver the community runs — a `[4Claim;4Move]` covering several rooms — costs 2,600 and is therefore an **RCL7** body; the bot that runs it is RCL7. Our two outposts are also diagonal to each other and share no exit, so a rover would spend its 600-tick life crossing the home room.

**The [[reserver]] row casts before the Anchor row.** `planSpawns` orders Anchor, then hauler, then worker; the reserver goes in front of all three. Every other row spends income; this one decides whether the income is five a tick or ten, and a lapsed reservation halves three sources at once. It is also the cheapest body on the table. Its quota and its sizing are one rule, TooAngel's: `claimParts = ceil((5000 − ticksToEnd) / 600)`, one reserver per posted outpost. Deficit-shaped sizing suits this architecture exactly — it holds no state, recomputes per tick, shrinks to the minimum in steady state and automatically sends a bigger body when the reservation has slipped. Sizing to the bank's capacity, as every other row does, happens to give the right body at RCL5 and the wrong one at RCL6, where 2,300 would buy a third CLAIM for a reservation that caps at 5,000 anyway.

**An unposted outpost source is worth nothing to the workforce.** `workforceTarget` splits sources into posted and unposted: posted ones enter the income base at the source's output, unposted ones **add their [[seat]] count to the target**. That rule's stated justification is that an unposted source's "output is spoken for by the seat crews that walk it" — which presumes the walk is cheap. In an outpost it is not. Taken literally across three outpost sources the rule adds six worker places (3 + 2 + 1) and sends generalists to harvest a swamp fifty tiles away, and five of those six seats *are* swamp. So outpost sources are excluded from `unpostedSeats` entirely. The consequence is the useful half: **a standing container is the switch that admits an outpost into the economy.** Until one stands, the outpost is invisible to every quota; the tick it stands the source becomes a Post, gains an Anchor, and enters the income base.

**`sourceOutputPerTick` stops being a constant.** It is read by the hauler quota and, through ADR 0021, by `anchorWorkCap`. Ten is the *reserved* rate. Because a reservation can lapse — a reserver dies, an invader core taps the controller — a source's output must be a per-source fact read from its actual reservation, not a colony-wide ten. Sizing miners and haulers for ten against a source yielding five overbuilds both rows by a factor of two, and it is a known trap: Overmind infers the rate from the colony's RCL and lands in it. The other two dishonesties in the same arithmetic are **named and not fixed here**: the income base counts *nominal* output rather than what is delivered, which for an outpost arrives fifty ticks late; and `amortization` deducts the Anchor and hauler rows' replacement cost but never the worker row's, a pre-existing home-room defect worth about 2.6 energy a tick today and about 8.4 after this change. Both leak toward *hiring one worker too many*, which ADR 0037's rounding already prefers and the stock's 130,000 absorbs. Neither is this ticket's debt to pay.

**The outpost gets a container and nothing else.** No roads, and no Layout. A container per outpost source, on the [[seat]] whose [[walk]] to the [[seam]] is shortest — one rule, not the Layout's trunk-anchored pick, because an outpost has no spawn to anchor on. Roads are worth about 3.6 energy a tick here: with them the hauler quota is one body per outpost container, without them two, and three bodies at 1,800 over a 1,500-tick life is the difference. That is a real prize and it is a separate ticket, because paving an outpost drags in three unrelated problems at once — the Layout's trunk routing is spawn-anchored and single-room (#105's counterexample is W12S27 itself), construction sites would need a cross-room allowance, and the Repair pool judges a whole line at once (ADR 0010), so a remote road enters as cross-room Repair Tasks.

## Considered Options

- **Mine first, reserve later** (the community's near-unanimous order, and 46 unreserved live outposts on this server). Rejected: the reason for that order is that a reserver is unaffordable early, and at RCL4 ours costs exactly the bank. Reserving from the start also doubles the outpost's output before the first hauler is sized, so the per-source output fact is exercised in its normal state rather than in a transitional one.
- **One outpost first, the second when the first is proven.** Rejected in favour of both at once, once the roving-reserver arithmetic showed that concurrency costs a second 1,300 body rather than nothing: two reservers at 4.33 energy a tick buy three sources their second five. The exposure argument that ordered them (W12S27 walks 40 of 47 tiles inside our own room; W13S28 walks 34–44 outside) survives as an ordering of *risk*, and ADR 0043 is where risk is answered.
- **Treat an outpost source as posted the moment it enters the projection.** Rejected: it hires against income that has no container to flow through yet — worse than the status quo it replaces, not better.
- **Put outposts into the Layout and pave them.** Deferred, not rejected: the 3.6 energy a tick is real and the ticket is worth opening. It is refused *here* because the container chain is what this decision needs and the road chain is what would make it three decisions.
- **Fix all three dishonesties in the income base.** Rejected: only the per-source output makes the bot take a *wrong action*. The other two make it hire slightly generously, which is the direction the design already leans.

## Consequences

- **This revises ADR 0012.** The income base gains a second kind of Post-bearing source and loses the unposted-seat fallback for outposts; the pattern table gains a fourth row.
- **This narrows ADR 0021.** `anchorWorkCap` is unchanged as a rule and changes as a number: its input is a per-source output rather than a constant ten.
- **This narrows ADR 0038, in a direction it did not anticipate.** ADR 0038 said its refusal flips "when the hauler row leaves its floor" and named #83 as the live candidate. Outpost containers do lift the row off its floor — round trips of 138 to 168 ticks unpaved put two haulers on each term. But **a link cannot cross rooms**: `links/transfer.js` resolves its target from the local room's objects. The pair of links ADR 0038 declined can never reach an outpost container, so its refusal stands, and its flip condition must be read as being about the **home room's** source containers alone. Without that sentence the next reader will reopen ADR 0038 with the outpost hauler count in hand.
- **The fleet roughly doubles**, from seven creeps to about sixteen, against a single spawn — the second is RCL7. Casting order now has four rows in it and one spawn casts one body a tick.
- **A lapsed reservation is now a visible economic event**, because output is read per source: the quotas shrink on their own rather than overbuilding into a halved room.
- The glossary gains [[reservation]] and [[reserver]]; [[container]], [[harvest]], [[workforce target]] and [[hauler unit]] each stop being home-room-only.
