# fabot — Domain Glossary

Screeps seasonal-server bot, written in F# and compiled to JS via Fable.

## Terms

### Snapshot
The immutable projection of the current tick's game state that the decision layer reads. Contains only the fields decisions need — never the full game object graph.

### Intent
A single described action the decision layer wants performed this tick (e.g. "creep X harvests source Y"). Intents are data; they do not touch the Screeps API.

### Executor
The thin imperative shell that turns Intents into Screeps API calls. The only layer allowed to act on the game (mutating API calls); Snapshot may call read-only game methods to build its projection.

### Task
A unit of work in the task pool (e.g. "deliver 300 energy to spawn"). Creeps are interchangeable executors that get matched to Tasks; a creep has no fixed role.

### Harvest
The Task of digging energy out of a source, in the spawn room or an [[outpost]] — one pool, one ranking, no second geometry to ask (ADR 0041). Pooled for every placed source, stocked or drained, and judged at [[arrival]] (ADR 0013, revised by ADR 0025): a drained source's Harvest is applicable to a creep only when its [[walk]] covers the [[restock]] wait — so a creep already beside a dry rock is released, exactly as ADR 0013 released it, while a distant one is dispatched the tick its walk equals the wait. One exemption, on ADR 0024's condition: a [[work-heavy body]] standing on a built source [[container]] keeps Harvest through the window — that tile is its job. The Emitter issues no dig while the source is drained. Feeding-tier intake beside [[withdraw]]; capped by the source's [[seat]] count, and for a [[work-heavy body]] by its [[post]] count as well (ADR 0024). Applicability: a Work part and free capacity — widened for a full **work-heavy** creep standing on that source's built [[container]], whose overflow the engine catches (ADR 0012, narrowed to the garrisoning body by ADR 0024: a light body's full store ends its dig wherever it stands, or it would hold the Post for life — never inapplicable, so never released). For a body with more Work than Move parts the [[work area]] is that source's [[post]]s when it has any (ADR 0020): a heavy body digs from the tile that catches its overflow or lets it upgrade in place, and travel cost walks it there — it does not dig, and so does not fill, from a Seat that would strand it.

### Refill
The Task of delivering energy to any energy-hungry structure — spawn, extension, tower, or the controller [[container]]; the Planner filters by free capacity. One generalized Task, but rank layers by target (ADR 0010): spawn-feeding Refill is feeding-tier work, tower Refill is surplus-tier — the colony feeds its own reproduction before its guns — and controller-container Refill sits one tier deeper still, below every surplus Task (ADR 0012): a full creep beside the buffer sinks its load into the controller rather than dumping it back into the container it just drew from, so the buffer is filled by bodies with no surplus work of their own — the hauler row's whole life. The [[storage]] sits one tier deeper still (ADR 0023): the place surplus goes when even the upgrade buffer is full.

### Withdraw
The Task of taking energy out of a stocked [[container]] (ADR 0012) or, one tier lower and only while some other sink is hungry, out of the [[storage]] (ADR 0023). Feeding-tier intake beside Harvest: an empty creep's choice between digging and collecting is made by [[travel cost]], not by rule — for bodies that are not Work-heavy. Applicability: a Carry part, free capacity, and no more Work than Move parts (ADR 0016) — a heavy-Work body's intake is digging, so a stocked container never outbids an unmanned [[post]] — plus, on the controller [[container]] alone, a Work part (ADR 0019): a body that can spend nothing at the controller only sends the buffer's energy back the way it came, and with every other sink full that same container is its only [[refill]] target, so the pair cycled a hauler in and out of one store tick after tick. The intake half of the haul cycle — a filled hauler loses Withdraw applicability and rematches to [[refill]], the same emergent alternation as every other Task pair.

### Build
The Task of spending carried energy into a construction site. Surplus work: same rank tier as Upgrade, below Harvest and spawn-feeding [[refill]] — the economy is fed before anything is constructed. Pooled for our sites in every room the colony has vision in, the spawn room's and an [[outpost]]'s alike — one pool, one ranking, like [[harvest]] (#150) — and unlike a declared source a site *needs* that vision, because no declaration carries one (ADR 0004). Only the outpost [[container]]'s rule places outside the home room, so a container's is the only site an outpost can hold (ADR 0042). Which creep builds a site is [[travel cost]]'s answer and nothing else: sharing Upgrade's tier, a site a [[seam]] and fifty tiles away is built by a creep already out there — one that walked into the outpost for its [[harvest]] and filled up — never by one standing beside its own controller.

### Repair
The Task of restoring a structure's hits (ADR 0010) — created when a repairable structure falls below its **whole line**, gone when it is whole. The line is the kind's (ADR 0034): half of max for the decaying kinds ([[trunk]] roads, [[container]]s), the [[rampart]]'s floor for a rampart, full hits for the [[keep]] — the Keep does not decay, so below max means damaged and nothing else. Surplus-tier like Build, same applicability (Work part, carried energy) and the same range. Only repairable kinds put hits in the [[spatial projection]].

### Reserve
The Task of holding an [[outpost]]'s controller under this colony's [[reservation]] (ADR 0042): the one Task that decides how fast a third of the colony's income flows rather than spending it. Pooled for every controller in the [[spatial projection]] that is not the colony's own — the engine refuses the act on a room we own, and ours carries the Upgrade Task instead — and pooled whatever the reservation has left on it: the ticks remaining size the [[reserver]]'s body and never the pool, because a Task that vanished at the 5,000 cap would release its holder there and rematch it the tick after. Read off the projection's kind census and never off the Outpost declaration, so a room the [[stand-down]] keeps out of the projection leaves this pool with it rather than through a second gate. Applicability: a CLAIM part, and no energy state at all — a reserver carries nothing and spends nothing. The gate cuts both ways, which is why the row and the Task have to arrive together (ADR 0006): every other Task asks for a Work part or a Carry part, so a `[Claim; Move]` body is applicable to this Task, to [[flee]], and to nothing else — a [[reserver]] cast before this Task existed would have stood where it was born for its whole 600-tick life. Range 1, and a controller's tile is an obstacle, so the [[work area]] is its walkable neighbours: two of them at W12S27's `37,43`, both swamp. Capped at one holder per controller — ADR 0042 buys one [[reserver]] per posted outpost, and a reservation is a single capped number one body's CLAIM parts are sized to hold, so a second body there buys nothing while the other outpost stays at five a tick; the cap is what sends it to the controller nobody holds, since travel cost alone would send every reserver to the nearest one. Feeding tier beside [[harvest]] — ADR 0042 and #116 fix only the *casting* order, so the matching order is a decision recorded where it is made: the row that decides whether the income is five a tick or ten ranks with the flow and above everything that merely spends it, and below Safety, so a reserver under fire runs. 🚩.

### Flee
The Task of getting out of a [[reach]] (ADR 0033): applicable to any creep standing inside one — no part, no capacity, no cap — except a [[work-heavy body]], which cannot outrun anything and stays at its work. Its [[work area]] is every walkable tile of the room the creep stands in outside that room's Reach — the room's safe set (#138) — so [[travel cost]] walks the creep to the nearest safe tile; it has no action. The one Task in the **Safety** tier, ranked above even the [[downgrade deadline]]'s Upgrade. It ends by its own rule: a creep outside its room's Reach no longer qualifies, is released, and rematches to ordinary work. 🏃.

### Planner
The pure step that reads a Snapshot and generates this tick's full Task pool. Runs every tick from scratch — Tasks are never persisted.

### Matcher
The pure step that assigns creeps to Tasks (greedy matching): Assignments in, Assignments out. Current assignments are the only thing remembered between ticks (anti-thrash).

### Emitter
The pure step that turns the tick's assigned Tasks into each assigned creep's action Intent and [[chat bubble]]. Judges actions from tick-start geometry — it consults the same [[atlas]] as the Matcher and Resolver, never resolved positions.

### Seat
A walkable tile adjacent to a source. The capacity unit of Harvest: a source supports at most as many concurrent harvesters as it has Seats — beside which a [[work-heavy body]] is capped by the [[post]] count, the standing room it actually has (ADR 0024).

### Dual Seat
A [[seat]] that also lies inside the controller's Upgrade [[work area]]. A creep standing on one can alternate Harvest and Upgrade without ever moving. One of the two kinds of [[post]] — the spawn room's kind: the colony upgrades one controller, its own, and reserves an [[outpost]]'s, so a Seat inside an outpost controller's area is ground nobody upgrades from and makes no Post (ADR 0042). Derived by the [[atlas]] each tick, never persisted.

### Post
A tile worth garrisoning with a heavy-WORK body (ADR 0012): a [[dual seat]], or the [[seat]] under a source [[container]]. The capacity unit of the [[anchor]] quota — one Anchor per Post, in every room the projection carries and not the spawn room's alone (ADR 0042): an [[outpost]]'s Post hires from the same row, sized by the same rule and pinned there by [[travel cost]], so remote mining needs no miner of its own. Counted room by room and summed, never unioned — a [[spatial projection]] tile carries no room, so two rooms' Posts sharing a coordinate are two garrison tiles a border apart (ADR 0041). And, since ADR 0024, of Harvest itself for a [[work-heavy body]]: a source admits as many heavy harvesters as it has Posts, so the [[seat]] count never piles two Anchors onto one tile. The one tile such a body may also garrison past a full store. Counted off standing containers alone — a site catches no overflow — which is the deliberate opposite of the container plan's own question, where a pending site does serve its target (ADR 0040): one asks what is here now, the other whether another must be built. Also the only footing a [[work-heavy body]] harvests that source from, when the source has one (ADR 0020) — the narrowing that makes [[travel cost]] pin an [[anchor]] to a Post instead of leaving it wherever it happened to land. Derived by the [[atlas]] each tick, never persisted.

### Container
A container structure as the [[layout]] places it (ADR 0012): one **source container** per source, on the [[seat]] nearest that source's [[trunk]]; one **controller container** on a buildable work-area tile adjacent to a trunk — the upgrade buffer that lets upgraders work standing still. An [[outpost]]'s source container is placed by a rule of its own, on the [[seat]] whose [[walk]] to the [[seam]] is shortest, because an outpost has no spawn for a trunk to anchor on (ADR 0042) — and it is the switch that admits the outpost into the economy: until one stands the outpost's source is in no quota at all, and the tick it stands the source becomes a [[post]]. A repairable kind: its hits enter the [[spatial projection]] and the Repair pool (ADR 0010). A stocked container is a [[withdraw]] target; the controller container is also a [[refill]] target, on the deepest tier but the [[storage]]'s (ADR 0023) — source containers never are. Planned per **target**, never per tile (ADR 0040): a source is served by any container within range 1 of it and the controller by any in its Upgrade [[work area]], standing or still a site, so a pick that moves keeps the container that already stands and the [[layout record]] carries the tile the plan wanted — the two of them would otherwise both be built, and every rule that counts containers would count both.

### Work Area
The set of tiles a creep may stand on while performing its current Task: ordinarily the passable tiles within the action's range of the target, narrowed for a [[work-heavy body]] harvesting a source that has [[post]]s to that source's Posts alone (ADR 0020), and taken less this tick's [[reach]] at the moment of judgement (ADR 0033) — a Task with nothing left is inapplicable to that creep, and a Task with tiles left is worked and walked to over those. Body-aware, not a pure fact about the Task — the same Harvest offers a light body every [[seat]] and a heavy one only the tile worth garrisoning. A creep acts only from inside it; an unreachable Work Area makes the Task inapplicable, narrowed or not. Derived fresh each tick, never persisted.

### Seam
A tile on a room's border, and the tile it lands you on in the neighbouring room. Never a place to stand: a creep that ends its tick on one is moved out of the room by the engine, which is why the [[spatial projection]]'s ground holds only tiles 1..48 (ADR 0036) and a Seam is an [[atlas]] query answered off the border layer instead — the exit rings the terrain memo fills beside that ground, keyed by room name because a Seam joins two rooms (ADR 0041). The third geometry beside the [[seat]] and the [[post]] — those are tiles a creep works from, this is a tile it can only pass through — and the joint a cross-room [[walk]] is summed at, and the tile the mover aims a crossing creep at, the near side of the crossing its price was taken at (#142). It is also the anchor an [[outpost]]'s source [[container]] is placed against, an outpost having no spawn for a [[trunk]] to anchor on: the pick is the [[seat]] whose walk *out* to the Seam is shortest (ADR 0042) — a walk to the border and not across it, charging every tile walked onto and the crossing itself, never the Seat the haul leaves from, and tied Seats falling to the lowest x then y like every other tie.

### Move Intent
A creep's movement desire for one tick: candidate standing tiles plus a priority. Input to the [[resolver]] — not an Intent; the Resolver's output (a single-step move) is what becomes an Intent. Standing tiles but one: a creep matched across a border is handed the near side of the winning [[seam]], and the last of those steps is the exit tile itself (ADR 0041, #142) — a destination the engine empties by the end of the tick, never a tile the [[atlas]] offers as somewhere to stand. Every other candidate is this room's ground, so what the Resolver arbitrates is unchanged.

### Resolver
The pure step that arbitrates a room's Move Intents into actual single-step moves (priority first, most-constrained first, swap when contested). Fourth pure step beside Planner, Matcher, and [[emitter]]; movement is never issued outside it. A [[grounded]] creep sits arbitration out: its tile is blocked for the tick and no move Intent is issued to it (ADR 0008).

### Grounded
A creep still paying off fatigue this tick: the engine would answer any move with ERR_TIRED, so the [[resolver]] neither asks it to move nor lets anyone claim or displace through its tile (ADR 0008). Recomputed each tick from the Snapshot's fatigue — a stationary creep drains 2 fatigue a tick, so grounding is always transient.

### Travel cost
The cheapest-path cost from a creep to a Task's Work Area over the [[spatial projection]], for that creep's body: terrain weights (road 1, plain 2, swamp 10 — the engine's own fatigue costs; impassable excluded) scaled by the body's fatigue factor (ADR 0002, revised by ADRs 0006 and 0010), plus the [[occupancy surcharge]] on tiles under standing creeps (ADR 0008). Priced from the load carried right now: carried energy loads Carry parts 50 apiece, and an empty Carry generates no fatigue — the engine's own rule. Breaks rank ties in the Matcher; a Work Area with no travel cost — unreachable or empty — makes the Task inapplicable to that creep: never matched fresh, and a remembered assignment to it is released. Priced in half-ticks (ADR 0010) — and a ranking price and nothing else since ADR 0029: halving it yields no clock, and every time-aware judgement reads the [[walk]] instead.

### Walk
A traffic-blind path priced in whole ticks, no step below one tick (ADR 0029): the [[atlas]]'s clock, beside [[travel cost]]'s ranking price. Same weights, the same body-aware [[work area]] and the same totality — a creep or target the [[spatial projection]] cannot place prices 0, an unreachable Work Area has no walk at all and its readers count from now — with two deliberate differences. Every step costs `max(1, ceil(units / 2))` on travel cost's own unit price: no body crosses a tile faster than a tick however much Move it carries, and the nested rounding is exact, so this is the step's physical time rather than an approximation of it. And today's standing creeps price at nothing: a bystander who moves on next tick is not part of the path, so no [[occupancy surcharge]] can dispatch a creep this tick and recall it the next. A creep's walk to a Task's Work Area is its [[arrival]], priced off the same per-tick flood memo travel cost reads, keyed by pricing so no two of its prices can drift apart. Across rooms it is a sum, never a wider flood (ADR 0041): a walk into an [[outpost]] is the smallest `walk to a [[seam]] + the exit tile's own step price + walk from its far side to the target` over the whole Seam band. ADR 0041 writes that middle term as `+ 1`, which is what a plain exit costs a body at [[fatigue parity]] and not what a swamp one costs, so it is priced by this entry's own `max(1, ceil(units / 2))` rule like every other step. Each leg floods inside one room on the same memo, the far leg flooding *into* the target's [[work area]] with each of its tiles seeded at that tile's own step price — so one entry serves every creep, and the join charges every tile the creep steps onto exactly once: the exit tile and the far room's first tile included, the landing tile not at all, because the engine puts the creep on it at the end of the tick for nothing. [[Travel cost]] crosses at the same Seam under its own pricing — a third, the traffic-blind ranking price the reroute attribution compares against, joined them there (ADR 0030). One rule for every clock in the colony: a successor's walk out of the spawner is the half of a [[lead]] paid after the cast, and the [[hauler unit]] quota's round trip is two walks — one loaded, one empty — summed. Each floods from its own origins and memoises accordingly, but the price of a step is the walk's everywhere, so nothing in the colony turns units into ticks a second way.

### Occupancy surcharge
The extra cost (10, one swamp step — ADR 0008, re-expressed by ADR 0010) the flood prices onto a step landing on a tile some creep occupies this tick. Sends travellers around standing traffic when a lane is cheaper, but the tile stays passable — traffic re-prices a route, it never makes a Task inapplicable, unlike an obstacle.

### Workforce target
The number of creeps the colony maintains, derived fresh each tick from the Snapshot, never persisted. Four addends, each a pattern row's own quota rule (ADR 0012, ADR 0042): [[reserver]]s — one per posted [[outpost]]; Anchors — one per [[post]]; haulers — the throughput arithmetic per source [[container]]; workers — unallocated income divided by one worker body's Work drain, rounded up as the hauler row's is, so the mouths always cover the surplus rather than fall a body short of it (ADR 0037). Floored at 2. A source whose Post is provided for retires its other [[seat]]s — the seat count stopped being the base the moment one heavy body could drain a source alone. An **unposted** source still contributes its seat count, but only in the spawn room: an outpost's unposted source contributes nothing at all (ADR 0042), because "its output is spoken for by the seat crews that walk it" presumes a walk that an outpost does not have. Income is counted per source at that source's own output, not at a colony-wide rate — a source in a room this colony neither **owns nor reserves** is worth half a *held* one (ADR 0042, narrowing ADR 0021; the ADR's own "unreserved is half" would price the spawn room, which is owned and which nothing reserves, at half and halve the home income base and the hauler quota together). A posted source in a room the colony has no vision in this tick is worth nothing at all rather than half, per-entry as every absence is (ADR 0004). Spawning fills the gap between the target and the creeps that will still be alive when a replacement could arrive — an [[expiring]] creep is already outside the count (ADR 0026). That whole-fleet gap gates the **generalist** row alone: every other row hires against its own gap, the row's own quota less the bodies of that row still living, so a target that fell under the living count in one tick — a [[container]] demolished, an RCL step, a lost tick of vision unposting an [[outpost]] source — still fills an empty [[post]] and still ships a standing container, and only the generalist row stops. The whole-fleet gap *stands in for* the generalist row's own gap rather than being it: the two agree exactly while every specialist row is at or under its quota, and a row standing **over** its quota holds the generalist row down by the surplus instead — the half of the whole-fleet gate that was kept.

### Room energy
One room's shared spawn-energy account (spawn + extensions) — a colony fact, not spawn state. Spawn planning allocates bodies from it in spawn order, debiting as it goes, so the same energy is never committed twice; a spawn whose room banks nothing waits.

### Spatial projection
The Snapshot's map-shaped view of the rooms the colony works — the spawn room and its [[outpost]]s. Still **one** projection (ADR 0005), layered by room name since ADR 0041: the tile-keyed layers sit under a room name, the id-keyed ones (target kinds, hits, stores) do not, because an object id is already unique across the world. Per room it carries the room's name, three-state terrain (plain / swamp / wall), entity positions, what kind of thing each target is (source, controller, a structure or a site of some built kind), which tiles hold a built road (ADR 0010), and current/max hits on repairable kinds only — fields nobody decides on stay out. Raw data, always present (possibly empty); decisions consult it only through the [[atlas]]. A tile absent from the projection is impassable — absence is per-entry, never per-projection (ADR 0004), which is what carries the changing half of an outpost the colony can only sometimes see — the reservation remaining, hits, stores, creeps, hostiles. The declared half does not wait for vision: each source's and the controller's id and tile enter the projection because a human wrote them down, laid under whatever vision answered (ADR 0041) — absence read onto the declaration as well is what left ADR 0042's chain with no first step, since no Task could name an outpost and so nothing ever walked there to get the vision. Its ground covers tiles 1..48 and never the border rows (ADR 0036): a border tile is a [[seam]], not ground. The border rings ride beside that ground as a layer of their own, under their room's name, so the [[seam]] query has the engine's own exit terrain while no weight table, walkable or buildable set, [[seat]] or [[work area]] can ever reach one. Assembled whole every tick, with one field recalled rather than rebuilt: the terrain is memoised per room name in the App's heap (ADR 0031) — the engine's whole 50×50, read once on the first tick that projects the room and reused after, split into the ground and the ring the projection asks for, because room terrain never changes for the life of the server. That means the key can never go stale and there is nothing to invalidate; it does not mean anything about terrain is persisted — the memo dies with a global reset and the next tick rebuilds it — nor that any other field outlives the tick.

### Atlas
The per-tick query interface over the [[spatial projection]]: Seats, Work Areas (body-aware for a [[work-heavy body]] harvesting — ADR 0020), [[seam]]s, travel costs, first steps, action permission, standing candidates, placed creeps — and the placement queries the [[layout]] derives from — home room name, target positions, buildable tiles, [[working ground]], structure and road censuses, raw-terrain [[trunk]] paths (ADR 0005, ADR 0011, ADR 0022). Task-aware where a Task's own geometry answers, tile-aware where it does not: the price, the first step and the action gate take the tiles the decision layer judged a creep may stand on this tick, so the [[reach]]'s subtraction reaches every one of them and the projection still never learns what a [[threat]] is (ADR 0033). The price and the first step take the Task as well, for the one answer no tile set can carry: a target in another room leaves those tiles empty — they would be the neighbour's, and a `Pos` cannot say so — so both cross at the [[seam]] instead, the price as a minimum over the band and the step as the near side of the crossing that minimum was taken at (ADR 0041). One minimisation answers both, because two would agree on every number and split on every tie, walking a creep to one crossing while ranking it at another. The action gate does not cross: a creep may act only from the room its target is in. Total (ADR 0004): geometry the projection cannot place gets one documented answer per query — it never counts against a [[task]] and never blocks an action. Built fresh each tick, with one table recalled rather than refilled: the spawn-origin [[walk]]s a [[lead]] is priced on ride the [[census signature]] (ADR 0032) and survive from tick to tick while the census holds. Matcher and Resolver consult the same one.

### Anchor
The heavy-WORK [[body pattern]] cast for a [[post]]: as many Work as saturate one source plus one spare — six — beside one Carry and minimal Move (ADR 0021: past saturation a further Work only drains the source sooner and idles until it regenerates; the spare absorbs an unmanned Post's gap). Its slowness is the point — body-aware [[travel cost]] pins it to a [[post]] — Harvest's [[work area]] for its heavy body is nothing else (ADR 0020), so it neither works nor fills anywhere but the tile worth garrisoning — where it works in place: alternating Harvest and Upgrade on a [[dual seat]], draining the source into the container under it on a source-[[container]] Post (ADR 0006, generalized by ADR 0012). The one Carry is kept on both footings so one sizing rule serves the whole row. Travel cost alone doesn't walk it home: [[withdraw]] is inapplicable to it as a [[work-heavy body]] (ADR 0016), so its only feeding-tier candidate is Harvest and an unmanned Post wins it regardless of distance. Dispatched by [[arrival]] (ADR 0025): a fresh Anchor leaves the spawn while its source is still drained, timed to reach the Post as it [[restock]]s, and a container-Post Anchor holds its tile through every empty window. Replaced by [[arrival]] too (ADR 0026): once [[expiring]], its successor is cast and walks while it still digs — the spawn room's half of the row, because a [[lead]] is priced on the home flood alone and an Anchor standing on an [[outpost]]'s Post is therefore never expiring, so that half is replaced only after it dies (a recorded gap, not a rule). Exempt from [[fatigue parity]], which governs only the [[worker unit]] pattern. One Anchor per Post, inside the [[workforce target]].

### Hauler unit
The repeating [Carry; Carry; Move] block hauler bodies are built from (ADR 0012): 150 energy, full speed loaded *on roads* — the row carries its own parity declaration (road parity, not the plain parity of [[fatigue parity]]), because a hauler's whole life is the [[trunk]]. No Work part, so Harvest, Build, Upgrade and Repair are inapplicable by body; it lives in the [[withdraw]]→[[refill]] cycle. Quota: per source [[container]] — an [[outpost]]'s as much as the spawn room's — round-trip ticks to the spawn (the canonical sink the trunks radiate from) times that source's own output over carry capacity, rounded up. The round trip is two [[walk]]s summed, the leg out loaded and the leg back empty (ADR 0029) — nothing is halved on the total, because each leg is already whole ticks. An outpost's container crosses a border to reach the spawn, so each of those two legs is the [[walk]]'s own minimum over the [[seam]] band and not a second cross-room arithmetic (ADR 0030): two joins and not one, because the loaded body and the empty one pay a swamp exit differently and can be right to cross at different exits. Unpaved that trip runs 138–168 ticks (ADR 0042), which hires two haulers where a home container hires one — the number is large because the haul is long.

### Work-heavy body
A living body with strictly more Work parts than Move — the [[anchor]] row's shape, and readable off the parts alone: [[fatigue parity]] forbids a worker body from reaching it (ADR 0003) and only the Anchor row's floor of two Work over one Move clears it. A predicate over any body, not a [[body pattern]]: what a creep is is decided from what it is made of, never from the row name in its name (ADR 0006). Four gates read it — [[withdraw]] is inapplicable to one (ADR 0016), Harvest's [[work area]] narrows to a [[post]] for one (ADR 0020), Harvest's Post cap and its full-store garrison reprieve are its alone (ADR 0024), and [[flee]] is inapplicable to one (ADR 0033: at four to seven ticks a step it neither escapes nor digs, and its Post's [[rampart]] is its defence) — so its intake is digging, from the tile worth digging on, and that tile is what it competes for.

### Body pattern
The repeating part block a body is generated from; capacity buys as many whole repeats as it can. The [[worker unit]] is one pattern. Which pattern a spawn casts is a colony decision; a pattern shapes what a creep is good at, never what it is assigned — creeps stay interchangeable and matching stays Task-based.

### Worker unit
The repeating [Work; Carry; Move] block worker bodies are built from — the generalist [[body pattern]]: 200 energy, full speed empty, half speed loaded. Capacity buys as many whole units as it can; what's left is remainder.

### Reserver
The [Claim; Move] row, one per posted [[outpost]], that holds that room's [[reservation]] through the [[reserve]] Task (ADR 0042). Cast **before** every other row: the others spend income, this one decides whether the income is five a tick or ten, and it is the cheapest body on the table. Quota and sizing are one rule rather than two — `ceil((5000 − reservation remaining) / 600)` CLAIM parts — so it shrinks to the minimum in steady state and arrives bigger when the reservation has slipped, with no state kept between ticks. Not sized to the bank like every other row, which would buy a third CLAIM at RCL6 for a reservation that caps anyway; and never one body roving two rooms, which costs 2,600 and is therefore an RCL7 creep.

### Fatigue parity
The body-generation invariant (ADR 0003): a worker body padded beyond whole [[worker unit]]s never moves slower than the pure-unit body, empty or loaded. The remainder buys as much Carry as parity allows, then Move — never Work.

### Layout
The deterministic full structure plan (ADR 0011): every clustered structure (tower first, then extensions) picked by one ordering rule — buildable tiles on the spawn's checkerboard colour, nearest-to-spawn first, [[working ground]] excluded and the [[link footing]]s held out (ADR 0022) — plus the [[storage]] (the first clustered pick, ADR 0022), the [[link footing]]s, the [[container]]s, the [[trunk]] roads and the [[rampart]]s (ADR 0034), computed whole from the [[atlas]], never persisted to Memory. The clustered horizon is RCL5 (30 extensions + two towers, ADR 0039) — one level of lookahead, so today's trunks are routed around tomorrow's structures; Storage and the Link footings are reserved regardless of level because their tiles, once taken by an extension, never come back, and the reserved window carries one spare tile per footing so the picks a footing pushes outward are reserved before the trunks are routed too (ADR 0027). Recomputed only when its [[census signature]] changes (ADR 0017) — same census, same plan. Placement filters the Layout to what the current RCL unlocks and what is missing; the tick a level lands, its sites drop.

### Trunk
A paved line in the [[layout]]: each source to the controller, each source to the spawn, plus the swamp tiles inside the controller's Upgrade [[work area]]. Priced on raw terrain only and routed around the Layout's reserved tiles. The rule is general — short trunks still pave; room-specific exemptions don't get encoded (ADR 0011). A goal no tile of which is reachable from the source once the reservation is marked impassable routes nothing at all, and that trunk is **recorded** rather than dropped (#107): the empty path unions into the road plan contributing nothing, so without the [[layout record]] a source paved to nothing looks exactly like a trunk nobody asked for.

### Working ground
The tiles the colony works from: every source's [[seat]]s and the controller's Upgrade [[work area]]. Excluded from the [[layout]]'s clustered ordering (ADR 0022) — a clustered structure there eats a standing tile the [[anchor]]s and upgraders need, and in this room the cluster's nearest-to-spawn ring reaches the controller pocket by RCL5. A [[link footing]] is the one structure *footprint* allowed on it; a [[rampart]] is no footprint — walkable, blocking nothing, taking no tile from the [[post]] it covers (ADR 0034). Derived from the [[atlas]] each tick, never persisted.

### Storage
The colony's stock: the one storage structure the [[layout]] places on the cluster's first pick (ADR 0022), built the tick RCL4 lands. Two roles, both ordered so it never outbids the flow (ADR 0023): the deepest [[refill]] target — below even the controller [[container]], so energy reaches it only when every other sink is full — and a [[withdraw]] source one tier below the source containers, pooled only while some sink other than itself has free capacity, so a hauler beside it never cycles energy in and out of the same store. A [[keep]] structure: ramparted, repaired to full, and a reason to spend safe mode (ADR 0034). Never the [[trunk]] hub — the spawn stays the canonical sink, and the Storage sits beside it by construction.

### Keep
The structures worth defending: the spawn, every tower and the [[storage]] (ADR 0034). One list, three rules: each is covered by a [[rampart]], each is repaired to full hits rather than to half, and any one of them below full hits while a [[hostile]] stands in the room fires the [[safe-mode reflex]]. The [[post]]s are ramparted with the Keep but are not of it: a container's hits never spend the stock.
_Avoid_: base, core

### Rampart
The walkable defensive structure the [[layout]] places over every [[keep]] structure and every [[post]] a [[container]] stands on, the tick the thing it covers stands (ADR 0034) — standing is the built census, so a [[storage]] that is still a site waits, and a Post that is a bare [[dual seat]] has no structure to cover. A creep on its own rampart cannot be attacked, so the tile is in no [[reach]] — the [[anchor]]'s whole defence, since it cannot [[flee]]. Kept above a **floor** of hits by [[repair]]: hungry below it, whole at it, the number a tunable derived from the ticks to hold times the damage per tick to hold against, never a term of the decision. Placed from RCL2, the level the engine allows a rampart at, and needing no level of its own past it; the set is the rule's, so a three-source room ramparts three Posts.

### Link footing
A tile the [[layout]] reserves for a link (ADR 0022) while the level that unlocks links is still ahead: one beside each **planned** source [[container]], one beside the controller container, one beside the [[storage]] — planned, not built, because a [[post]] needs a standing container and a Post-anchored rule would reserve nothing at level 0, when the reservation is worth the most. The count is the rule's, never a constant: a room with three sources holds five footings. Each footing is the buildable tile within range 1 of its target that is off every [[trunk]], off the footings' own targets and off the other footings, nearest the spawn, ties by x then y — read back from a standing link too, so the tile does not move the tick its link goes up. The clustered reservation is the one thing it does not dodge, because it outranks it: the tower and the extensions yield by taking their picks with the footings held out, and the reservation is widened by the footing count so the picks they push outward are still tiles the trunks were routed around (ADR 0027). The only structure footing allowed on [[working ground]]: a link there buys the [[anchor]] and the upgraders a transfer without leaving their tile. A link is a built kind and nothing else — no placement [[intent]] ever names one (ADR 0027) — and at RCL5 this colony fills none of them (ADR 0038): the only thing a link could save here is a hauler body, and the [[workforce target]]'s quota is already at its floor of one per source container, which no wire goes under. The floor being spoken of is the **home room's** source containers alone (ADR 0042, narrowing ADR 0038): an [[outpost]]'s container hires two or three haulers for its round trip across the [[seam]] and does lift the row off that floor, but a link resolves its transfer target out of its own room's objects and can never reach one, so no haul a link could shorten is in that number — a reader arriving here with the outpost hauler total in hand is holding the wrong one. The footings stay reserved regardless, because their tiles never come back and reserving them costs nothing; what fills them is a decision for the room whose haul has left that floor, not this one. A target the fold can find no tile for — every neighbour gone to a [[trunk]], to another target, to another footing or to a standing structure — reserves nothing, and that shortfall is **recorded** rather than dropped (ADR 0035): it rides the [[layout record]], because a guarantee that can degrade in silence is not one. W12S28's `10,43` source container is already in that state, its last candidate taken by an extension built before the reservation existed (#77).

### Outpost
A neighbouring room this colony mines but does not own. Declared, not discovered (ADR 0041): the constant is a human's, moved in a commit as the [[layout]]'s horizon is (ADR 0039) — so the bot has no scouting, and cannot take a third one by itself. One declaration is the room's name, each of its sources, and its controller, positions and all under the ids the **engine** gives them, because a live projection keys every target by the server's own id and a declaration in any other vocabulary would match nothing, in silence (ADR 0004). Only what cannot wait for vision is declared; the reservation remaining, hits and stores are read off the projection where there is vision and absent entry by entry where there is none. The controller is not optional: an unreserved source is worth half (ADR 0042), so a room with no controller to reserve — a sector centre, a Source Keeper room — is not a candidate Outpost at all. Its rooms enter the [[spatial projection]] under their own names and its Tasks enter the one pool. The community calls these *remotes*.
_Avoid_: remote, franchise, territory

### Reservation
A neutral controller held by this colony's CLAIM parts. It doubles every source in that room — 5 energy a tick becomes 10 — decays by 1 a tick, and caps at 5,000. The economic precondition of an [[outpost]] (ADR 0042), and the reason a source's output is a per-source fact rather than a constant: a lapsed Reservation halves a room, and quotas sized for the *held* rate would overbuild it twofold. **Held** is the colony's word for either half of what the engine pays the full rate for — a room it owns or a room this Reservation covers — because the spawn room is owned and unreserved and is worth ten all the same. A Reservation another player holds is not held: the engine pays ten in that room too, and the colony still prices it at five, because a room somebody else holds is one it is withdrawing from. It does not keep an invader core out — the engine's expansion tests only for an *owner* — which is what the [[stand-down]] answers.

### Arrival
The tick a creep can first act on a Task: its [[walk]] to that Task's [[work area]] from where it stands now (ADR 0029) — whole ticks over a path blind to today's traffic, never [[travel cost]] halved. The horizon at which time-aware judgements are made, not the current tick (ADR 0025, ADR 0026): a drained source's Harvest is applicable when the walk covers the [[restock]] wait; a creep is [[expiring]] when its successor's [[lead]] outlasts its life; a Task's capacity counts a holder only when its stay and the candidate's overlap — the holder is still alive when the candidate arrives, and has arrived itself before the candidate dies. Derived each tick from the Snapshot and the [[atlas]], never persisted.

### Restock
The moment a drained source holds energy again — the engine's regeneration, projected as ticks remaining (zero for a source holding energy now). The one time fact the Snapshot carries about a source (ADR 0013, widened by ADR 0025): stocked is a restock of zero, not a field of its own.

### Expiring
A living creep whose remaining life is at or under its [[lead]] — it will be dead before a replacement cast now could stand where it stands (ADR 0026). Excluded from the [[workforce target]]'s living count and its row's gap, so the successor is cast while it still works; never released for it — anti-thrash keeps it on its Task to the last tick. Derived each tick, never persisted.

### Lead
The time a creep's replacement needs to stand on its tile: the successor body's cast time (3 ticks a part) plus that body's [[walk]] out of the spawn, priced over [[travel cost]]'s own weights for the successor's fatigue factor, not the incumbent's (ADR 0026) — whole ticks with no tile below one, and blind to today's traffic as the hauler quota's round trip is (ADR 0029): the path does not start until the body is cast, and the tile it ends on is the one the creep being replaced is standing on. It begins *beside* the spawner, where the engine places a finished creep, never on the spawner's own tile — a step the replacement never walks would buy a lead it cannot use. One rule for every [[body pattern]] — a slow Anchor earns a long lead, a hauler on a [[trunk]] a short one — never a per-row constant. Several spawns resolve as the quota resolves them, at the cheapest; geometry the [[atlas]] cannot price leads nobody. The walk out of the spawn reads nothing but the census — weights and the successor's body alike — so it is recalled while the [[census signature]] holds and recomputed when it moves (ADR 0032), never per tick.

### Verdict
The reasoned outcome a decision step returns beside its decision — data, never a log line (ADR 0009). The [[matcher]]'s Verdict on a creep says which [[task]] won it and why (rank, [[travel cost]], load, tie-break), that a remembered assignment was kept (anti-thrash, distinct from a fresh match), why an assignment was released, or why an unassigned creep got nothing — including a Task whose time has not come (too early: the body and energy fit, only the [[arrival]] doesn't, ADR 0025); the [[resolver]]'s says what became of its movement ([[grounded]], yielded, reroute). The too-early reason is not a bare word: on a release and on a rejection alike it carries the two numbers the gate compared, the [[walk]] and the [[restock]] wait, because since ADR 0029 no reader recovers the walk by halving a [[travel cost]]. A Verdict that falls out of work already done is always-on; one whose evidence must be manufactured — full candidate scoring, reroute attribution (ADR 0018) — is computed only for creeps on the [[verbose list]]. The Planner and spawn decisions return no Verdicts.

### Census signature
The fingerprint of everything a census-derived plan reads: the kind and position of every standing structure, **each named with the room the projection places it in**; the kind and position of every pending site of the home room; the home room's controller level and its name; and the rate every projected room's sources are priced at — who **owns or reserves** it, or the third answer for a room the colony has no vision in this tick (ADR 0004). Named with the room because two rooms hold the same coordinates, so `Container@16,44` in either would otherwise be one census entry. The standing half spans every projected room and the pending half does not, and that asymmetry is the rule rather than an oversight: the memo's one cross-room reader is the hauler quota, which folds every projected room's source [[container]]s and prices each at its own room's rate (ADR 0042), while the [[outpost]]'s own container plan — the only thing that places a site outside the home room — is derived fresh every tick and rides no memo entry at all. The rate is signed as the rate and never as a reservation's ticks left, which decays every tick and would throw the memo away on each of them. While it is unchanged the [[layout]] and the hauler quota are both provably identical and neither is recomputed (ADR 0017) — the signature signs the *union* of what the two read, ADR 0017's "the quota's input set is a subset of the Layout's" having stopped holding the tick the quota reached a second room, and the spawn-origin [[walk]]s behind every [[lead]] are recalled rather than flooded again (ADR 0032). Held in heap only — a global reset discards it and the next tick recomputes.

### Verbose list
The creep names owed full candidate scoring, stored beside the [[transition log]] under `Memory.fabot.observe` and read fresh each tick — flipped from the terminal through the Memory HTTP API, so an investigation needs no redeploy. Empty (or absent, or malformed) means off; each listed creep's Scoring [[verdict]] carries one Candidate per pooled [[task]]: scored on the full matching key, or rejected at the first gate it failed (threatened, inapplicable, capacity-full, unreachable, too-early — the last carrying the [[walk]] and the wait it was judged against, since a rejected row has no cost to read one off). The scored row is not widened to match: only a rejection raises the question of how long the creep still has to wait. A listed creep also gets reroute attribution (ADR 0018) — the manufactured-evidence Verdicts ride this list together. The attribution's traffic-blind route comes off the [[atlas]]'s shared flood memo since ADR 0030, so the flood ADR 0018 called unmemoisable no longer is; the list still gates it, because that decision was about log noise and not about the flood.

### Transition log
The per-creep ring of recent changes — task handovers and movement events, each with its [[verdict]] and tick — written only on the tick something changed. The colony's answer to "why did this creep flip", capped per creep; a quiet creep writes nothing. It keeps only creeps still alive, which is why a raid needs a channel of its own (ADR 0028).

### Raid log
The colony's episodic record of [[hostile]] presence in the spawn rooms (ADR 0028), stored beside the [[transition log]] under `Memory.fabot.observe` and read from the terminal with `observe.mjs raids`. A channel of its own for a structural reason: the Transition log is keyed by creep and prunes a creep's whole timeline the tick it dies, so it cannot record the one event a raid record exists for. An **episode** opens the first tick a spawn room holds any hostile, stays open while hostiles keep appearing, and closes after fifty quiet ticks — the **quiet gap**, long enough to outlast a poke-and-heal cycle, so a squad stepping in and out across 220 ticks is one episode and not forty. Each records its window (opened, and the last tick a hostile actually stood there), the **roster** — one row per distinct hostile id with its owner and part counts, from its first sighting, so a squad reads as five rows — the **closest approach** — the smallest range over the whole episode between any hostile and anything of ours **standing in that hostile's own room** (ADR 0041), an owned creep or an owned structure, with the tile and the tick, the number that separates a probe at the room edge from a loss; a `Pos` carries no room, so one of ours on the same coordinate of another room is not at range 0, and a hostile in a room the projection places nothing of ours in has no approach at all rather than a zero (ADR 0004) — and our **losses** inside the window, by name and tick: a creep gone while a hostile stood there, stamped at the tick it was last seen alive, because a name is missing the tick after its creep died. Never a creep whose own clock ran out — the record answers what the raid cost, and the Snapshot's ticks-to-live, the same fact [[expiring]] is judged from, tells old age from a kill before it happens. A ring capped at twenty episodes, oldest trimmed first, disposable by construction like its sibling. Since ADR 0034 each episode also carries the **damage**: the hits lost across the [[keep]] and the [[rampart]]s inside its window, decreases summed and repairs ignored. Since ADR 0043 the channel carries a second family beside spawn-room raids: an [[outpost]]'s threat episode, which adds one field those never needed — the tick the [[stand-down]] it drives expires. That is the single narrowing of "a record to be read, never a signal sent": the stand-down gate is the one reader that acts on this channel, and every other reaction (ADR 0033, 0034) still reads the Snapshot, not the log.

### Layout record
The colony-level channel carrying what the [[layout]] could not deliver (ADR 0035), stored beside the [[transition log]] and the [[raid log]] under `Memory.fabot.observe` and read from the terminal with `observe.mjs layout`. It holds three lists, all losses of the current plan: the [[link footing]] targets the fold found no tile for, each by its target tile and its kind — source container, controller container, storage — the [[trunk]]s the router found no path for (#107), each naming the source that lost one and the goal it lost it to, the Upgrade [[work area]] or a spawn by id, and the [[container]] picks the plan deferred because a container already serves their target (ADR 0040), each naming the target — a source by id, or the controller — the tile the plan picked and the tile actually serving it. Per goal and not per source, because the goals are collected per source: W12S27 planned from `6,18` drops the source→spawn trunk and paves the source→controller one. A channel of its own for the [[raid log]]'s structural reason: every [[verdict]] names a creep and none of a footing, a trunk or a container has one, the [[transition log]] is keyed by creep, and the [[planner]] speaks no Verdicts at all. Not a history — no fold, no ring, no baseline: the record is the current plan's, so under a stable [[census signature]] it is the same list every tick and rides the plan memo beside the site [[intent]]s rather than being rederived (ADR 0017). Written every tick, the empty lists included, which is what keeps a list's three answers distinct: no leaf at all is a bundle that predates the channel, an empty list is the guarantee holding — ADR 0022 and ADR 0027's one footing per target, ADR 0011's one trunk per (source, goal), ADR 0040's container standing where the plan wanted it — and a row is a reservation, a haul road or a container tile the colony no longer has. Each list is guarded in its own right on the way out, so a leaf carrying one and not another fails rather than letting the lists that are there vouch for the one that is not. A record to be read, never a signal sent — nothing reacts to it, and what to do about a loss is a decision of its own (#100).

### CPU line
The colony's per-tick record of what a tick cost, stored beside the [[transition log]], the [[raid log]] and the [[layout record]] under `Memory.fabot.observe` and read from the terminal with `observe.mjs cpu` (ADR 0041). One row per tick the loop finished — the tick number and the milliseconds the bot had spent by the time it stopped looking — oldest first, a ring capped at a hundred, disposable by construction like its siblings. Written last of all, after the Executor's [[intent]]s, because the intents are most of what a tick costs; what it therefore excludes is the write itself and the engine's own serialization of Memory, both outside anything the bot can move. The tick number rides each row rather than being implied by its place in the ring, so a tick that threw before the write is a **gap in the numbers** rather than a cheap tick that never happened. It exists because ADR 0041 states the condition to revisit the layered [[spatial projection]] as two numbers — a mean tick above 50 ms, or any single tick above 80 — and both are read off this window; the same judgement is printed by `npm run profile`, off the same thresholds in `scripts/cpu-trigger.mjs`. **Measured, never budgeted**: nothing in the bot reads this back, and no threshold lives in Core. A budget exists to size a territory and this colony's territory is a constant (ADR 0006's row arriving without its quota), while skipping ticks on a low bucket collides with the [[safe-mode reflex]], which has to be able to fire on the very tick a guard would skip (ADR 0007, ADR 0015).

### Chat bubble
The glyph an assigned creep says over its head each tick, one fixed glyph per [[task]] (⛏ Harvest · 📥 Withdraw · 🔋 Refill · 🔨 Build · 🔧 Repair · ⚡ Upgrade · 🏃 Flee). Observability only, private to our own viewer; unassigned creeps show nothing.

### Safe-mode reflex
The colony reflex (ADR 0007, revised by ADR 0015) that emits `ActivateSafeMode` the tick a CLAIM-part [[hostile]] stands within range 3 of the controller — the claim tap is a range-1 act, so holding until then is free and gives the [[fire reflex]] its window to kill the claimer en route; an unplaced controller falls back to firing on sight. A second arm since ADR 0034: any [[keep]] structure below full hits while any hostile — a [[threat]] or not — stands in the spawn room; the same shape as the first, hold until the harm is certain, and read statelessly off one tick's hits, which is why the Keep is repaired to full. Gated only on stock remaining and safe mode not already running; a hostile that neither claims nor damages never spends the stock.

### Pickup reflex
The colony reflex that emits a pickup Intent for every creep with free carry capacity standing within range 1 of a dropped energy pile — beside its assigned [[task]]'s action, since the engine's pickup conflicts with no other action. A reflex, not a Task: no movement, no matching, no threshold — it only recaptures what is already in reach (death drops, harvest overflow). Energy only; tombstones are not covered. Piles are projected as position and kind alone — no amount, since no decision reads one. Like the [[safe-mode reflex]], it speaks no [[verdict]] and shows no [[chat bubble]].

### Fire reflex
The colony reflex (ADR 0014) that has every tower shoot the [[hostile]] nearest to itself, each tick one stands in the room. Attack only — towers never repair (creep Repair is far cheaper per hit) or heal — and per-tower: no focus fire, no anti-drain gate, no energy floor (a dry tower's shot fails harmlessly; unlike the [[safe-mode reflex]] there is no stock to protect). Like its siblings, it speaks no [[verdict]] and shows no [[chat bubble]].

### Hostile
A hostile creep as the Snapshot projects it: its id, its owner, the room it stands in, its position, and its body parts, verbatim — the fields its readers need (ADR 0014, widened by ADR 0028 and ADR 0041), and the projection grows one only the tick a reader for it exists (ADR 0007's rule that the field list grows when a decision reads it, widened to any reader by ADR 0028). The room name is the field ADR 0028 explicitly left out as having no reader, and the reader ADR 0041 gave it is the [[raid log]]'s closest approach: a `Pos` carries no room, so a hostile is measured against the tiles of its own room and one of ours on the same coordinate of another is not at range 0. What a hostile can do is decided from what it is made of — CLAIM is the only part that threatens the controller, and the controller is the only thing safe mode is spent on (ADR 0007); where it stands is what the [[fire reflex]] aims at; whose it is, is the [[raid log]]'s roster, the reader the owner grew for and the only one that reads it. Hostiles stay out of the [[spatial projection]]: they block no tiles and price no paths. Since ADR 0033 they do gate Tasks — through a [[threat]]'s [[reach]], a colony-level fact the pipeline reads, never a change to the map.

### Threat
A [[hostile]] carrying an ATTACK or RANGED_ATTACK part (ADR 0033) — read off the parts, never the owner: an invader and a raider hurt a creep the same per part. A healer, a scout or a claimer is a hostile the [[fire reflex]] shoots and the [[raid log]] records, but only a Threat has a [[reach]].
_Avoid_: enemy, attacker

### Reach
The tiles a [[threat]] can hurt: its weapon range (1 for ATTACK, 3 for RANGED_ATTACK) plus a margin of two — one for its next step, one for our tick of lag; the margin a tunable — less every tile under one of our standing [[rampart]]s in that room (ADR 0033). Filed by the room the Threat stands in, as the [[spatial projection]] it is derived from is layered (ADR 0041, #138): a room with no entry has an empty Reach — ADR 0004's absence, which blocks nothing — and a room's **safe set** is that room's walkable tiles less that room's Reach, which is [[flee]]'s [[work area]] for a creep standing there. A colony-level fact derived once each tick, read in three places and nowhere in the [[atlas]], each reader taking the share of one room — a Task's target's, a creep's own, the spawn's: it subtracts from every Task's [[work area]] at applicability, so a Task with nothing left is inapplicable to everyone and its holders are released `Threatened`, while a Task with tiles left is worked from them; it is what [[flee]] is applicable inside of; and the spawn emits nothing while a tile beside it lies in one. No hysteresis: a Reach appears and vanishes with its Threat, and the blink is measured before it is designed against.
_Avoid_: danger zone, threat radius

### Downgrade deadline
The hard floor on the controller's downgrade timer: half the level's full timer (ADR 0007). Inside it, Upgrade stops being surplus work and outranks even the feeding tier — a downgrade costs a level and zeroes the safe-mode stock, and the engine refuses safe-mode activation below half minus 5,000, so escalating at half keeps the [[safe-mode reflex]] fireable with the engine's whole grace intact.

### Stand-down
An [[outpost]] withdrawn from, and the tick it may be re-entered (ADR 0043). Withdrawal is a gate and not a manoeuvre: a stood-down room does not enter the [[spatial projection]], so no Task pools there, no quota counts it and nothing walks toward it — which is all "retreat" can be in an architecture that keeps no state. The tick is **read off the threat**, not chosen: an invader core's collapse timer, or the end of the reservation it has taken, sampled while there is still vision, with 2,500 ticks as the fallback where none can be read. Re-entry is a clock running out and never a second look — a gate that reopened on *not seeing* would send creeps back into the same core forever. Recorded as an episode in the [[raid log]]. One gate per outpost. The other, clockless withdrawal is a room another player has owned or reserved: that one is not a threat, it is a room that stopped being ours to work.

### Disaster fallback
The zero-creep spawning rule: an empty colony spawns bare [[worker unit]]s from whatever [[room energy]] is banked right now — as many as the bank affords, up to the workforce deficit — rather than waiting for a full capacity it can never refill. The one body-generation path that ignores the remainder — time-to-first-creep outranks spending the bank.

## Avoided terms

- **Role** — creeps are not born with roles; work is Task-based. Don't reintroduce role-based vocabulary.
