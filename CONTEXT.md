# fabot — Domain Glossary

Screeps seasonal-server bot, written in F# and compiled to JS via Fable.

## Terms

### World
Everything this tick was seen to hold, once: every room a standing [[colony]]'s declaration names and every room the engine answers `Game.rooms` with, each under its own name, and every creep we own beside them (ADR 0052 decision 1). Built by the shell (`World.ofGame`) and the only place the bot reads the game's objects, so a room two colonies both work is read once and they cannot disagree about what stands in it. It holds facts and no conclusions: which rooms a colony works, which bodies are its own, what its bank is and which neighbours are children it is raising are all the [[colony view]]'s, derived from these facts plus the declaration and the [[stand-down]] gate. `decide` never sees one — a rule written against the world could reach a room its colony does not work.

### Colony view
One colony's whole reading of this tick, cut from the [[world]] by a pure function (`ColonyView.ofWorld`): the rooms it works as one [[spatial projection]], the bodies it holds, its own bank and controller, the [[stage]] of every colony of ours, where the bodies it does *not* hold stand in those rooms ([[foreign bodies]]), and the explicit little it may take of a neighbour's ([[borrowed work]]). The only argument `decide` has, and one per **living** colony every tick (ADR 0047 decision 1) — so what a rule can reach is what its colony works. Contains only the fields decisions need, never the full game object graph. Named **Snapshot** until ADR 0052: it had been one colony's share of the state since #191 while the name went on saying "the game state".

### Intent
A single described action the decision layer wants performed this tick (e.g. "creep X harvests source Y"). Intents are data; they do not touch the Screeps API.

### Executor
The thin imperative shell that turns Intents into Screeps API calls. The only layer allowed to act on the game (mutating API calls); the [[world]]'s builder may call read-only game methods to build its projection.

### Task
A unit of work in the task pool (e.g. "deliver 300 energy to spawn"). Creeps are interchangeable executors that get matched to Tasks; a creep has no fixed role.

### Harvest
The Task of digging energy out of a source, in the [[home room]] or an [[outpost]] — one pool, one ranking, no second geometry to ask (ADR 0041). Pooled for every placed source, stocked or drained, and judged at [[arrival]] (ADR 0013, revised by ADR 0025): a drained source's Harvest is applicable to a creep only when its [[walk]] covers the [[restock]] wait — so a creep already beside a dry rock is released, exactly as ADR 0013 released it, while a distant one is dispatched the tick its walk equals the wait. One exemption, ADR 0024's condition as ADR 0048 widened it: a [[work-heavy body]] within the engine's digging range of the source — range 1, container or no container — keeps Harvest through the window, because that is what being in position to dig means and a hauler drawing the container swaps it onto the Seat beside. A [[dual seat]] is subtracted from that widening and not from ADR 0024's own condition: there the body has somewhere else worth being and it is the tile it is on, so it upgrades through the window as ADR 0025 has it. And the dispatch is a **light** body's rule (ADR 0048, narrowing ADR 0025): for a [[work-heavy body]] a drained source is early whatever its [[walk]], outside that exemption — "the walk covers the wait" was written for a body paying a tick a tile, and one paying four to seven covers every wait there is, which walked an Anchor across a border onto a Post another was standing on while a full rock behind it went undug. The Emitter issues no dig while the source is drained. Feeding-tier intake beside [[withdraw]]; capped by the source's [[seat]] count, and for a [[work-heavy body]] by its [[post]] count as well (ADR 0024). Applicability: a Work part and free capacity — widened for a full **work-heavy** creep standing on that source's built [[container]], whose overflow the engine catches (ADR 0012, narrowed to the garrisoning body by ADR 0024: a light body's full store ends its dig wherever it stands, or it would hold the Post for life — never inapplicable, so never released), and widened again for a full **work-heavy** creep that has not yet reached the tiles it may dig from, where the source has a [[post]] to walk to (ADR 0048). That second widening is a walk and never a dig: ADR 0024 asks whether such a body may keep digging where it stands, and a body still walking is not digging, so the Emitter's own `mayAct` decides — false while the walk is ahead of it, true the tick it arrives, where ADR 0024's container condition governs unchanged. It is load-bearing because a heavy body never empties (ADR 0016, ADR 0046, ADR 0048 between them close every way it could spend a store): full is its ordinary condition, so a store gate read as "done here" would take the Post away from the body whose job it is. For a body with more Work than Move parts the [[work area]] is that source's [[post]]s when it has any (ADR 0020): a heavy body digs from the tile that catches its overflow or lets it upgrade in place, and travel cost walks it there — it does not dig, and so does not fill, from a Seat that would strand it. When it has none the fallback to the bare Seats is the [[home room]]'s alone, because it is a *bootstrap* rule and an [[outpost]] bootstraps through a [[reserver]] and a light builder instead (ADR 0042, recorded as ADR 0045): a heavy body on an outpost Seat with no [[container]] under it digs twelve a tick onto the ground of a room whose haul quota the container has not switched on yet, and travel cost — which reads only that the Seat is near — is what put it there. So an unposted outpost source's Work Area for such a body is empty and its Harvest is inapplicable to it, the geometric dual of that source being worth nothing in the quotas.

### Refill
The Task of delivering energy to any energy-hungry structure — spawn, extension, tower, or the controller [[container]]; the Planner filters by free capacity. One generalized Task, but rank layers by target (ADR 0010): spawn-feeding Refill is feeding-tier work, tower Refill is surplus-tier — the colony feeds its own reproduction before its guns — and controller-container Refill sits one tier deeper still, below every surplus Task (ADR 0012): a full creep beside the buffer sinks its load into the controller rather than dumping it back into the container it just drew from, so the buffer is filled by bodies with no surplus work of their own — the hauler row's whole life. The [[storage]] sits one tier deeper still (ADR 0023): the place surplus goes when even the upgrade buffer is full.

### Withdraw
The Task of taking energy out of a stocked [[container]] (ADR 0012) or, one tier lower and only while some other sink is hungry, out of the [[storage]] (ADR 0023). Feeding-tier intake beside Harvest: an empty creep's choice between digging and collecting is made by [[travel cost]], not by rule — for bodies that are not Work-heavy. Applicability: a Carry part, free capacity, and no more Work than Move parts (ADR 0016) — a heavy-Work body's intake is digging, so a stocked container never outbids an unmanned [[post]] — plus, on the controller [[container]] alone, a Work part (ADR 0019): a body that can spend nothing at the controller only sends the buffer's energy back the way it came, and with every other sink full that same container is its only [[refill]] target, so the pair cycled a hauler in and out of one store tick after tick. The intake half of the haul cycle — a filled hauler loses Withdraw applicability and rematches to [[refill]], the same emergent alternation as every other Task pair. Capped by its store's **stock**: as many concurrent drawers as the energy standing in it can fill, `ceil(stored / one whole cast body's carry)`, counted at [[arrival]] like every other capacity (ADR 0026). Nothing else in the pipeline says it — the matching key ranks by [[travel cost]] before crowding, so every empty hauler picks the *nearest* stocked container whatever is in it, and a container holding one body's worth draws the whole row while a full one stands unvisited. The load is the row's cast body at the richest bank and never the candidate's own carry, because a capacity is a fact about the Task: one store must not answer two numbers depending on which creep asked. *Which* row is a fact about the store, so the store picks the divisor: the buffer's drawers are [[worker unit]] bodies and every other store's are [[hauler unit]] bodies — and the buffer's stays the worker row's even now that the [[upgrader]]'s quota has landed and two rows drink there (ADR 0046, amended by #187): divided by the standing row's fifty-energy Carry the same 900 admits eighteen drawers, which is no cap at all and re-opens for the generalists the pile-on the cap exists to stop, so which body a **two-row** store divides by is a question deferred rather than settled in passing. **Stock and not flow** (ADR 0023): what an [[anchor]] drops in while the hauler walks is counted the tick it lands. The [[storage]] takes the same rule and no exemption — a real stock divides into hundreds of trips, so the cap is there and never the thing that binds — and so does the controller [[container]], capped like every other store and divided by the worker load its own drawers carry (ADR 0019). **No new gate for the [[standing body]]** (ADR 0046): the row that stands beside the controller's container is the row that drinks from it, and the two gates above already say so — it is at `Work = Move`, inside ADR 0016's, and it carries the Work part ADR 0019's asks for. What ADR 0046 shuts such a body out of is the *delivery* half of the cycle ([[build]], [[repair]], [[refill]]), so its Withdraw ends in an Upgrade in place rather than in a walk. **A tombstone and a ruin are stores too** (#167) and are drawn by this same Task: the engine's `withdraw` takes either object, so the pool, the tier and the stock cap read them exactly as they read a container — 408 energy stood in one tombstone in the home room at t140,810 while the colony dug. What is different about them is only that they end, and a store that decays away mid-walk releases its holder through the ordinary task-gone path rather than through a rule of its own.

### Pickup
The Task of walking to a dropped energy pile and taking it (#167) — the [[pickup reflex]]'s counterpart for the energy no reflex will ever reach: a death drop in the open, an [[anchor]]'s overflow on a [[container]] whose hauler is not due. Feeding-tier intake beside [[withdraw]], and the choice between a pile and a container is [[travel cost]]'s and never a rule's. Pooled on the pile's amount alone, from a threshold of a hundred — the reflex already covers range 1 for free, so what this number prices is a walk made for the pile alone, and two CARRY parts' worth is the smallest load that pays for one. Applicability is the Withdraw's without its buffer clause: a Carry part, free capacity, and no more Work than Move parts (ADR 0016) — a heavy body's intake is digging, and a pile is not a dig — while ADR 0019's Work gate has no counterpart, because a pile is nobody's upgrade buffer. Capped by its own amount like every other store, `ceil(amount / one [[hauler unit]]'s load)` (#161), counted at [[arrival]] (ADR 0026). The amount is the field the piles grew for this Task; the reflex still reads none. Judged creep-blind like every Task: whether the pile is already at somebody's feet is a fact about a creep, so this Task pools a pile a hauler is standing on exactly as it pools one across the room — but the *act* is deduplicated where the two meet, because an arriving picker satisfies the [[pickup reflex]]'s own condition and one creep asking one engine for one pickup twice is a miscount in the [[cpu line]]'s accepted-[[intent]] column and nothing else. Two adjacent creeps both reaching for one pile stay two asks; one creep's two spellings of the same ask become one. A pile that is picked up, decays away or is taken by somebody else leaves the pool, releasing its holder through the ordinary task-gone path — and so, third way, does **a pile that falls back under the threshold**: the number is judged every tick against a pool rebuilt from scratch, so it gates persistence as well as entry, and a pile decaying at one a tick crosses back under within `amount - 99` ticks while the first of two hired haulers can take it under the line by arriving. The holders still walking are released with the walk spent for nothing. That loss is accepted and not overlooked: the alternative is pooling a pile some creep already holds, which is the Planner reading its own assignments back, and a walk is cheaper than that.

### Build
The Task of spending carried energy into a construction site. Surplus work, with one exception: same rank tier as Upgrade, below Harvest and spawn-feeding [[refill]] — the economy is fed before anything is constructed. Pooled for our sites in every room the colony has vision in, the [[home room]]'s and an [[outpost]]'s alike — one pool, one ranking, like [[harvest]] (#150) — and unlike a declared source a site *needs* that vision, because no declaration carries one (ADR 0004). Only the outpost [[container]]'s rule places outside the home room, so a container's is the only site *this colony* ever puts in an [[outpost]] (ADR 0042) — every other site standing out there is one a human placed, which is exactly what a [[nursery]] is built on. The exception is that site: an **outpost** container's Build is **feeding**-tier work, because a standing container is the switch that admits a room into the economy (ADR 0042), so building it decides whether a third of the colony's income exists at all rather than spending income the colony already has — the same argument that ranks [[reserve]] with the flow, one question deeper. Read as surplus it was never built: the site and its own controller shared a tier, only [[travel cost]] separated them, and every loaded worker was a few tiles from the controller and a [[seam]] plus fifty from the site (#157). A [[nursery]]'s sites are the same exception one question deeper (ADR 0047): in a room this colony has claimed and not yet stood a spawn in, **every** site is feeding-tier, because the site it is all waiting on is a spawn a human places by hand and no rule of this colony's ever places. The rank layers by the site's **room** as well as its kind — a container site standing outside the home room, plus every site in a nursery, so the colony's own container sites stay surplus and so would any other kind an ordinary outpost ever held — and a tile carries no room, so the room comes from the [[spatial projection]]'s own id-to-room join (ADR 0041). Inapplicable to a [[work-heavy body]] wherever it is feeding-tier — the nursery's sites included, the gate following the tier rather than the container — the same shape of gate ADR 0016 puts on [[withdraw]] and for the same reason: on the feeding tier no travel cost is left to pin an Anchor at its [[post]], and a heavy body's cross-room work is a Post and never a fifty-tile delivery (ADR 0020). Unless the site *is* that Post: a [[container]] site standing on the creep's own Seat, under its own feet, is applicable to any body at all (#205) — the one exception to this gate and to the [[standing body]]'s alike, because both of them refuse a walk and there is none here. That is how an [[outpost]]'s container is raised at all: the [[anchor]] hired for the Post digs twelve a tick and spends it into the progress it is standing on, where the worker row commuting a [[seam]] delivers fifty a trip against 5,000 progress. **Two** concurrent builders across all the outpost container sites together — not two apiece, because the outpost container rule places one site per unserved source on a single tick — spread one apiece while several stand, or the tier would walk the whole worker row over the Seam at once. What that number prices is a commute, so the body standing on the site is outside it (#205): counted inside, a worker still crossing holds the slot for the whole fifty ticks and the [[anchor]] stands full on the progress with nothing left to do. The budget does not reach a [[nursery]]'s sites: what two rations is how much home surplus a third of the income is worth, and a claimed room is no longer a room the colony merely mines. Excluded from the entries and not from the divisor, so a sibling outpost's site keeps the budget it had. Nothing then bounds the crowd that crosses for a nursery's site — every loaded Work-part body may take it and the home room's surplus work goes unheld while it stands, which is the price ADR 0047 was chosen at; the [[pioneer]]s are what the mother hires for the job, never a ceiling on who takes it. What shares this tier is a spawn- or extension-feeding [[refill]], and only [[travel cost]] separates the two: the home room feeds itself first through the creeps standing in it, not by any rule. A tower [[refill]] is surplus (ADR 0010) and [[repair]] is surplus (ADR 0034), so both now rank below this one site outright — ADR 0010's own "reproduction before its guns", with this Build counted as reproduction.

### Repair
The Task of restoring a structure's hits (ADR 0010) — created when a repairable structure falls below its **whole line**, gone when it is whole. The line is the kind's (ADR 0034): half of max for the decaying kinds ([[trunk]] roads, [[container]]s), the [[rampart]]'s floor for a rampart, full hits for the [[keep]] — the Keep does not decay, so below max means damaged and nothing else. Surplus-tier like Build's every other site — the outpost [[container]]'s is the one exception, and it now ranks above Repair — with the same applicability (Work part, carried energy) and the same range. Only repairable kinds put hits in the [[spatial projection]].

### Reserve
The Task of holding an [[outpost]]'s controller under this colony's [[reservation]] (ADR 0042): the one Task that decides how fast a third of the colony's income flows rather than spending it. Pooled for every controller in the [[spatial projection]] that is not the colony's own and not a [[candidate colony]]'s — the engine refuses the act on a room we own, ours carries the Upgrade Task instead, and a room we mean to own carries the [[claim]] (ADR 0047), because a controller carries exactly one of the three Tasks that act on one — and pooled whatever the reservation has left on it: the ticks remaining size the [[reserver]]'s body and never the pool, because a Task that vanished at the 5,000 cap would release its holder there and rematch it the tick after. Read off the projection's kind census and never off the Outpost declaration, so a room the [[stand-down]] keeps out of the projection leaves this pool with it rather than through a second gate. Applicability: a CLAIM part, and no energy state at all — a reserver carries nothing and spends nothing. The gate cuts both ways, which is why the row and the Task have to arrive together (ADR 0006): every other Task asks for a Work part or a Carry part, so a `[Claim; Move]` body is applicable to this Task, to [[flee]], and to nothing else — a [[reserver]] cast before this Task existed would have stood where it was born for its whole 600-tick life. Range 1, and a controller's tile is an obstacle, so the [[work area]] is its walkable neighbours: two of them at W12S27's `37,43`, both swamp. Capped at one holder per controller — ADR 0042 buys one [[reserver]] per posted outpost, and a reservation is a single capped number one body's CLAIM parts are sized to hold, so a second body there buys nothing while the other outpost stays at five a tick; the cap is what sends it to the controller nobody holds, since travel cost alone would send every reserver to the nearest one. Feeding tier beside [[harvest]] — ADR 0042 and #116 fix only the *casting* order, so the matching order is a decision recorded where it is made: the row that decides whether the income is five a tick or ten ranks with the flow and above everything that merely spends it, and below Safety, so a reserver under fire runs. 🚩.

### Claim
The Task of taking a [[candidate colony]]'s controller for our own with CLAIM parts (ADR 0047): the act that turns a declared home room into an owned one, and so the first tick of a second [[colony]]. Pooled one per candidate colony — a declared home this colony does not own yet — and never for a plain [[outpost]], whose controller is [[reserve]]d instead: claiming spends a GCL level and asks the colony to run the room, which is a human's sentence in the declaration and not something the projection can infer. Three conditions, and each is a different kind of fact: the human's declaration, the controller already standing in the [[spatial projection]] — which it does because the candidate room is *also* one of the mother colony's outposts until it stands on its own — and `RoomControl` saying the room is takeable, unowned and reserved by nobody but us. A rival's hold is not takeable (the engine answers ERR_INVALID_TARGET) and is the [[stand-down]]'s business, not this pool's; a room with no control entry is one nothing is looking into, and an unseen room is not one to claim. Applicability, [[work area]] and cap are [[reserve]]'s exactly — a CLAIM part and no energy state, range 1 beside an obstacle, one holder per controller — which is why one row casts for both and `patternOf` reads a claimer back as a [[reserver]]; what differs is the demand, one `[Claim; Move]` block whatever the reservation has done, because a claim is one act by one CLAIM part rather than a hold that decays — the *cast* is still the row's largest outstanding demand, so a claimer hired beside a slipping reservation is over-bought at that reservation's body rather than under-bought at one block it could be matched to the wrong controller with. Feeding tier beside Reserve: a reservation decides whether a room's income is five a tick or ten, and this decides whether there is a second colony at all, so nothing that merely spends energy outranks it and Safety still does. Finished the tick it succeeds — the room is ours, the Task leaves the pool because the room is no longer a candidate, and the reservation the colony was holding there stops being refreshed. 🏴.

### Flee
The Task of getting out of a [[reach]] (ADR 0033): applicable to any creep standing inside one — no part, no capacity, no cap — except a [[work-heavy body]], which cannot outrun anything and stays at its work. Its [[work area]] is every walkable tile of the room the creep stands in outside that room's Reach — the room's safe set (#138) — so [[travel cost]] walks the creep to the nearest safe tile; it has no action. The one Task in the **Safety** tier, ranked above even the [[downgrade deadline]]'s Upgrade. It ends by its own rule: a creep outside its room's Reach no longer qualifies, is released, and rematches to ordinary work. 🏃.

### Planner
The pure step that reads a [[colony view]] and generates this tick's full Task pool. Runs every tick from scratch — Tasks are never persisted.

### Matcher
The pure step that assigns creeps to Tasks (greedy matching): Assignments in, Assignments out. Current assignments are the only thing remembered between ticks (anti-thrash).

### Emitter
The pure step that turns the tick's assigned Tasks into each assigned creep's action Intent and [[chat bubble]]. Judges actions from tick-start geometry — it consults the same [[atlas]] as the Matcher and Resolver, never resolved positions.

### Seat
A walkable tile adjacent to a source. The capacity unit of Harvest: a source supports at most as many concurrent harvesters as it has Seats — beside which a [[work-heavy body]] is capped by the [[post]] count, the standing room it actually has (ADR 0024). A Seat under a [[post]] is the garrison's alone (ADR 0051).

### Dual Seat
A [[seat]] that also lies inside the controller's Upgrade [[work area]]. A creep standing on one can alternate Harvest and Upgrade without ever moving — which is why it is the one footing a [[work-heavy body]] gets no empty-window reprieve on (ADR 0025, kept by ADR 0048): it has work in place, so it upgrades through the window instead of standing over a rock the Emitter will not let it dig. One of the two kinds of [[post]] — the [[home room]]'s kind: the colony upgrades one controller, its own, and reserves an [[outpost]]'s, so a Seat inside an outpost controller's area is ground nobody upgrades from and makes no Post (ADR 0042). Derived by the [[atlas]] each tick, never persisted.

### Post
A tile worth garrisoning with a heavy-WORK body (ADR 0012): a [[dual seat]], or the [[seat]] under a source [[container]]. The capacity unit of the [[anchor]] quota — one Anchor per Post, in every room the projection carries and not the [[home room]]'s alone (ADR 0042): an [[outpost]]'s Post hires from the same row, sized by the same rule and pinned there by [[travel cost]], so remote mining needs no miner of its own. Counted room by room and summed, never unioned — a [[spatial projection]] tile carries no room, so two rooms' Posts sharing a coordinate are two garrison tiles a border apart (ADR 0041). And, since ADR 0024, of Harvest itself for a [[work-heavy body]]: a source admits as many heavy harvesters as it has Posts, so the [[seat]] count never piles two Anchors onto one tile. The one tile such a body may also garrison past a full store — and that half is the *standing* container's alone, a site catching no overflow, which is why on a site the cap is held by the body **standing** on the Post rather than by the one holding its Harvest (#205): the pair alternates there, dig until full and build until empty, and a slot read off the assignments alone would read as free on every build tick. Counted off a Seat's standing container **or its construction site** (#205): the body that will garrison a container is the one that raises it, digging twelve a tick and spending it into the progress under its own feet, so the Seat is worth garrisoning a few hundred ticks before the structure exists. Which splits the census in two, because a site is a garrison place and not yet an economy: the [[hauler unit]]'s term and the [[workforce target]]'s income share still wait for the container to stand (ADR 0042), and only the Anchor place, the Harvest cap and the [[work area]] arrive early. Also the only footing a [[work-heavy body]] harvests that source from, when the source has one (ADR 0020) — the narrowing that makes [[travel cost]] pin an [[anchor]] to a Post instead of leaving it wherever it happened to land. Derived by the [[atlas]] each tick, never persisted. Since ADR 0051 the Post's Seat is the garrison's: a light body's Harvest Work Area is the source's Seats less its Posts, and Harvest admits light bodies only into the Seats beyond the Posts.

### Container
A container structure as the [[layout]] places it (ADR 0012): one **source container** per source, on the [[seat]] nearest that source's [[trunk]]; one **controller container** on a buildable work-area tile adjacent to a trunk — the upgrade buffer that lets upgraders work standing still. An [[outpost]]'s source container is placed by a rule of its own, on the [[seat]] whose [[walk]] to the [[seam]] is shortest, because an outpost has no spawn for a trunk to anchor on (ADR 0042) — and it is the switch that admits the outpost into the economy: until one stands the outpost's source is in no [[hauler unit]] or income quota at all, and the tick it stands it enters both. Its [[post]] arrives one step earlier, with the *site* (#205): the Seat a container is going up on is worth garrisoning, because the [[anchor]] standing there is what raises it. A repairable kind: its hits enter the [[spatial projection]] and the Repair pool (ADR 0010). A stocked container is a [[withdraw]] target; the controller container is also a [[refill]] target, on the deepest tier but the [[storage]]'s (ADR 0023) — source containers never are. Planned per **target**, never per tile (ADR 0040): a source is served by any container within range 1 of it and the controller by any in its Upgrade [[work area]], standing or still a site, so a pick that moves keeps the container that already stands and the [[layout record]] carries the tile the plan wanted — the two of them would otherwise both be built, and every rule that counts containers would count both.

### Work Area
The set of tiles a creep may stand on while performing its current Task: ordinarily the passable tiles within the action's range of the target, narrowed for a [[work-heavy body]] harvesting a source that has [[post]]s to that source's Posts alone (ADR 0020) — and, for one whose source has none, to every [[seat]] in the [[home room]] and to nothing at all in an [[outpost]] (ADR 0045) — and taken less this tick's [[reach]] at the moment of judgement (ADR 0033) — a Task with nothing left is inapplicable to that creep, and a Task with tiles left is worked and walked to over those. Body-aware, not a pure fact about the Task — the same Harvest offers a light body every [[seat]] and a heavy one only the tile worth garrisoning. A creep acts only from inside it; an unreachable Work Area makes the Task inapplicable, narrowed or not. Derived fresh each tick, never persisted.

### Seam
A tile on a room's border, and the tile it lands you on in the neighbouring room. Never a place to stand: a creep that ends its tick on one is moved out of the room by the engine, which is why the [[spatial projection]]'s ground holds only tiles 1..48 (ADR 0036) and a Seam is an [[atlas]] query answered off the border layer instead — the exit rings the terrain memo fills beside that ground, keyed by room name because a Seam joins two rooms (ADR 0041). The third geometry beside the [[seat]] and the [[post]] — those are tiles a creep works from, this is a tile it can only pass through — and the joint a cross-room [[walk]] is summed at, and the tile the mover aims a crossing creep at, the near side of the crossing its price was taken at (#142). It is also the anchor an [[outpost]]'s source [[container]] is placed against, an outpost having no spawn for a [[trunk]] to anchor on: the pick is the [[seat]] whose walk *out* to the Seam is shortest (ADR 0042) — a walk to the border and not across it, charging every tile walked onto and the crossing itself, never the Seat the haul leaves from, and tied Seats falling to the lowest x then y like every other tie.

### Move Intent
A creep's movement desire for one tick: candidate standing tiles in preference order, plus a priority. Input to the [[resolver]] — not an Intent; the Resolver's output (a single-step move) is what becomes an Intent. The list is a **head and a tail** and the two are read differently: the head is what the creep asked for and what the arbitration pays its whole priority for, the tail is a detour worth the least positive thing there is and worth it only to the creep whose turn it is. A traveller heads its next path step and tails the ground beside both itself and that step — a way *around* the tile it wanted, never a way back down the lane it came up — except on the border ring, where every tile beside it is forward and it keeps them all (#145); a creep already inside its [[work area]] heads its own tile, then the area's neighbours, then the ground outside it. Before #219 a traveller's list was the one step and nothing else, and a body standing on that tile that could not move — fatigued, or another colony's — stopped it for as long as it stood there. Standing tiles but one: a creep matched across a border is handed the near side of the winning [[seam]], and the last of those steps is the exit tile itself (ADR 0041, #142) — a destination the engine empties by the end of the tick, never a tile the [[atlas]] offers as somewhere to stand. Every other candidate is this room's ground, so what the Resolver arbitrates is unchanged.

### Resolver
The pure step that arbitrates a room's Move Intents into actual single-step moves. Fourth pure step beside Planner, Matcher, and [[emitter]]; movement is never issued outside it. **One pass per room, over every creep of ours standing in it** (#216 R2b) — and therefore not one [[colony]]'s step, alone among the four: a room a [[mother colony]] and her child both work is one room, and each of them arbitrating its own half against the other half read as empty is how a [[pioneer]] came to claim the child's [[anchor]]'s tile every tick for a body the engine would never let it into (#220). So `decide` hands its colony's Move Intents out unarbitrated and the shell folds every colony's together; each creep still moves on the intent its own colony registered. What it does is a **weighted matching**: the room starts as the identity — each creep on the tile it stands on — and each intent in turn is searched for an augmenting chain of displacements ending on a free tile, taken only when the chain's net priority is positive (a creep landing on the tile it asked for is worth its rank's weight, one pushed off such a tile costs its own, and one merely shuffled aside costs nothing, which is how a creep with slack in its [[work area]] yields to a creep without). A chain that dead-ends is dropped whole, so a swap needs no special case and the answer is always a matching: one creep to a tile, one tile to a creep. Rank stays a lexicographic order over whose turn it is and is a weight only inside the arithmetic. A [[grounded]] creep sits arbitration out: its tile is a wall for the tick and no move Intent is issued to it (ADR 0008), and so is the tile of a [[foreign body]] no intent in the fold can move.

### Grounded
A creep still paying off fatigue this tick: the engine would answer any move with ERR_TIRED, so the [[resolver]] neither asks it to move nor lets anyone claim or displace through its tile (ADR 0008). Recomputed each tick from the view's fatigue — a stationary creep drains 2 fatigue a tick, so grounding is always transient. A traveller stopped by one waits where there is nothing beside it to step to and sidesteps where there is (#219): the transience is what ADR 0008 built the wait on, and the ten minutes eight creeps spent in W13S28's north corridor is what it does not cover — with a queue behind each of two bodies tired every other tick, the tick on which both are rested and could swap never comes.

### Travel cost
The cheapest-path cost from a creep to a Task's Work Area over the [[spatial projection]], for that creep's body: terrain weights (road 1, plain 2, swamp 10 — the engine's own fatigue costs; impassable excluded) scaled by the body's fatigue factor (ADR 0002, revised by ADRs 0006 and 0010), plus the [[occupancy surcharge]] on tiles under standing creeps (ADR 0008). Priced from the load carried right now: carried energy loads Carry parts 50 apiece, and an empty Carry generates no fatigue — the engine's own rule. Breaks rank ties in the Matcher; a Work Area with no travel cost — unreachable or empty — makes the Task inapplicable to that creep: never matched fresh, and a remembered assignment to it is released. Priced in half-ticks (ADR 0010) — and a ranking price and nothing else since ADR 0029: halving it yields no clock, and every time-aware judgement reads the [[walk]] instead.

### Walk
A traffic-blind path priced in whole ticks, no step below one tick (ADR 0029): the [[atlas]]'s clock, beside [[travel cost]]'s ranking price. Same weights, the same body-aware [[work area]] and the same totality — a creep or target the [[spatial projection]] cannot place prices 0, an unreachable Work Area has no walk at all and its readers count from now — with two deliberate differences. Every step costs `max(1, ceil(units / 2))` on travel cost's own unit price: no body crosses a tile faster than a tick however much Move it carries, and the nested rounding is exact, so this is the step's physical time rather than an approximation of it. And today's standing creeps price at nothing: a bystander who moves on next tick is not part of the path, so no [[occupancy surcharge]] can dispatch a creep this tick and recall it the next. A creep's walk to a Task's Work Area is its [[arrival]], priced off the same per-tick flood memo travel cost reads, keyed by pricing so no two of its prices can drift apart. Across rooms it is a sum, never a wider flood (ADR 0041): a walk into an [[outpost]] is the smallest `walk to a [[seam]] + the exit tile's own step price + walk from its far side to the target` over the whole Seam band. ADR 0041 writes that middle term as `+ 1`, which is what a plain exit costs a body at [[fatigue parity]] and not what a swamp one costs, so it is priced by this entry's own `max(1, ceil(units / 2))` rule like every other step. Each leg floods inside one room on the same memo, the far leg flooding *into* the target's [[work area]] with each of its tiles seeded at that tile's own step price — so one entry serves every creep, and the join charges every tile the creep steps onto exactly once: the exit tile and the far room's first tile included, the landing tile not at all, because the engine puts the creep on it at the end of the tick for nothing. [[Travel cost]] crosses at the same Seam under its own pricing — a third, the traffic-blind ranking price the reroute attribution compares against, joined them there (ADR 0030). One rule for every clock in the colony: a successor's walk out of the spawner is the half of a [[lead]] paid after the cast, and the [[hauler unit]] quota's round trip is two walks — one loaded, one empty — summed. Each floods from its own origins and memoises accordingly, but the price of a step is the walk's everywhere, so nothing in the colony turns units into ticks a second way.

### Occupancy surcharge
The extra cost (10, one swamp step — ADR 0008, re-expressed by ADR 0010) the flood prices onto a step landing on a tile some creep occupies this tick. Sends travellers around standing traffic when a lane is cheaper, but the tile stays passable — traffic re-prices a route, it never makes a Task inapplicable, unlike an obstacle.

### Workforce target
The number of creeps the colony maintains, derived fresh each tick from the [[colony view]], never persisted. Five addends, each a pattern row's own quota rule (ADR 0012, ADR 0042, ADR 0046): [[reserver]]s — one per **declared** [[outpost]], the one row the [[container]] switch does not gate, because it is the creep whose vision lets the container be placed at all; Anchors — one per [[post]]; haulers — the throughput arithmetic over every source [[container]] at once, one rounding for the colony (ADR 0049); [[upgrader]]s — the surplus divided by one standing body's upgrade drain, and zero unless a built controller [[container]] stands in the room *and* the row's own cast at this bank is a [[standing body]], the shape the row's living count is read by; workers — what the upgrader row has *not* eaten, divided by one worker body's Work drain, plus the [[pioneer]]s a [[nursery]] adds (ADR 0047): three more generalists on top of the whole worker row and outside its floor, while a room this colony has claimed still has no spawn in it. The two rows are hired out of one surplus and only one of them may round up, because ADR 0037's oversell is bounded by *one body's* lifetime drain and two roundings sell that bound twice: the **upgrader** row rounds **down** — the whole bodies the surplus pays for — and the remainder passes to the worker row, whose division rounds up as the hauler row's does (ADR 0037, unchanged; the split between the two rows is ADR 0046, amended by #195), so the bound is the nine-Work body ADR 0037 argued about rather than the eleven-Work one it never saw. The standing row's own replacement is charged against that remainder before the worker row divides it, so the remainder can still come to nothing, and where it does the worker row is at its **floor**: two while anything stands in the [[build]] or [[repair]] pool, one otherwise (ADR 0046). The floor sits on the worker row's whole share, seat crews included, since both hire the same body from the same row. Floored at 2 overall. A source whose Post is provided for retires its other [[seat]]s — the seat count stopped being the base the moment one heavy body could drain a source alone. An **unposted** source still contributes its seat count, but only in the [[home room]]: an outpost's unposted source contributes nothing at all (ADR 0042), because "its output is spoken for by the seat crews that walk it" presumes a walk that an outpost does not have. Income is counted per source at that source's own output, not at a colony-wide rate — a source in a room this colony neither **owns nor reserves** is worth half a *held* one (ADR 0042, narrowing ADR 0021; the ADR's own "unreserved is half" would price the [[home room]], which is owned and which nothing reserves, at half and halve the home income base and the hauler quota together). A posted source in a room the colony has no vision in this tick is worth nothing at all rather than half, per-entry as every absence is (ADR 0004). And a source's **output** is what the [[anchor]] row's own cast at this colony's richest bank digs there, capped at that rate (ADR 0042 as #208 amends it): the rate is what the rock regenerates and so a ceiling on it, and a colony banking 300 casts `2W/1C/1M` and earns four a tick from a Post rather than the ten the room would pay a body big enough to take it — the row's cast and never the living Anchor's body, which would make the quota oscillate on that body's death. Above a 700 bank the cast digs twelve and the cap binds on no rate at all. Spawning fills the gap between the target and the creeps that will still be alive when a replacement could arrive — an [[expiring]] creep is already outside the count (ADR 0026). The rows are **asked** in that order and none of them holds a veto over the ones behind it: a spawn casts the first body its bank can pay for, and a row the bank is short for yields the tick to the rows below it rather than stopping the tick (ADR 0050). Nothing is skipped for longer than a tick — the rows are rebuilt from the quotas every tick, so the [[reserver]] the extensions could not pay for is the head again the tick they can. In front of all five sits the [[supply floor]], one row that is a **floor** and not a quota: a colony holding no body that can put energy into an extension casts one hauler, sized from what is banked *right now* rather than from the bank's capacity (ADR 0050). The gate is [[refill]]'s body clauses beside [[withdraw]]'s — a Carry part, no [[standing body]] ratio, no more Work than Move — and never "has a Carry part": the [[anchor]]'s `6W/1C/1M` has one and can still refill nothing, which is how a colony of two Anchors stood 1,235 ticks without casting a body while 246,818 energy sat in the [[storage]] beside it. It is ADR 0006's disaster fallback carried to a colony that is not empty, and that fallback — a minimal [[worker unit]] from whatever is banked, for a colony with no creep at all — is unchanged in front of it. That whole-fleet gap gates the **generalist** row alone: every other row hires against its own gap, the row's own quota less the bodies of that row still living, so a target that fell under the living count in one tick — a [[container]] demolished, an RCL step, a lost tick of vision unposting an [[outpost]] source — still fills an empty [[post]] and still ships a standing container, and only the generalist row stops. The whole-fleet gap *stands in for* the generalist row's own gap rather than being it: the two agree exactly while every specialist row is at or under its quota, and a row standing **over** its quota holds the generalist row down by the surplus instead — the half of the whole-fleet gate that was kept.

### Room energy
One room's shared spawn-energy account (spawn + extensions) — a colony fact, not spawn state. Spawn planning allocates bodies from it in spawn order, debiting as it goes, so the same energy is never committed twice; a spawn whose room banks nothing waits.

### Home room
The one room this colony owns, spawns from and plans for: the room the [[spatial projection]] is anchored in (`SpatialInfo.RoomName`), the room the [[layout]] is derived for, the name the [[census signature]] carries out front, and the room the [[trunk]]s radiate from. *Home*, and not "the spawn room": holding the spawn is a **fact** about this room and never its definition (ADR 0041) — the shell read the name off the first spawn while there was one colony to read it off, and since ADR 0047 it is the declaration's — `ColonyView.ofWorld` is handed the [[colony]] whose home it is, and a spawn standing here is what makes that colony *living* rather than what makes this room home. Which room is home decides which layer of the projection the Layout plans in and which room's grid a [[lead]]'s near [[walk]] floods (ADR 0032); an [[outpost]] is a room *beside* it, carried under its own name in the same one projection (ADR 0041), so home is a room name the [[atlas]] answers with and never a synonym for the colony. Two rules are the home room's **alone**, and both for one reason — they are *bootstrap* rules, and an outpost bootstraps through a [[reserver]] and a light builder raising its [[container]] instead (ADR 0042, #157): a source with no [[post]] falls back to its bare [[seat]]s for a [[work-heavy body]] here and offers nothing at all in an outpost (ADR 0020, revised by ADR 0045), and an unposted source contributes its Seat count to the [[workforce target]] here and nothing at all there. A third is home's by geometry rather than by bootstrap: the hauler floor a [[link footing]] is weighed against is read off this room's source containers alone (ADR 0038, narrowed by ADR 0042), because a link resolves its transfer target inside its own room and can never reach an outpost's haul. Not the same set as the plural **spawn rooms** — every room holding one of our spawns. Since ADR 0047 what one [[colony view]] carries of that plural is this colony's share of it: [[room energy]], the refillable list and the spawns themselves are the *home room's*, because a spawn in another colony's home is that colony's to cast from and a bank it does not hold is one it cannot spend. A second spawn raised in this room joins them without changing what home is; one raised in another declared home ends that colony's [[nursery]] and starts its own `decide`, which the mother reads off the projection rather than off her spawn list. The [[hostile]] sweep was one of that plural's readers until #201 and is not any more: it covers every room the colony works and can see, so an outpost's raider reaches the [[reach]] and [[flee]] rules that ADR 0033 wrote for a creep being shot. The two rules that must *not* follow a raider across a border narrow back to **home** and not to that plural — the [[safe-mode reflex]] is spent on a controller of ours and an outpost has none, and a tower shoots only inside its own room (ADR 0007, ADR 0014).
_Avoid_: spawn room (as the definition), main room, home base

### Room position
A tile of a **named** room (`RoomPos`), and the only shape a tile takes once it leaves the grid it indexes (ADR 0052 decision 2). Beside it `Pos` keeps the narrower meaning it always had in practice: a **grid coordinate**, a key of a room's terrain, obstacle, road, flood, [[seat]], [[reach]] and [[work area]] tables, meaningful only beside the name of the room whose grid it indexes. Two rooms hold the same fifty-by-fifty coordinates, so a bare `Pos` handed between functions is joined to its room by convention — and the convention is what produced a phantom [[post]] (#191), a [[trunk]] drawn to a coordinate of another room's spawn, a hostile in an [[outpost]] measured at range 0 from [[home room]] (#204) and a [[reach]] dug out of the wrong room's ground (#138). ADR 0041 patched each site by carrying a room name *beside* the tile — `HostileInfo.RoomName`, `MoveIntent.Room`, `Outpost.RoomName`, the `…In atlas room` query pairs — and every one of those was two facts that could disagree, kept in step by whoever remembered. Since #216 R3 the join is the type: every [[atlas]] answer that leaves the Atlas carries its room, the [[layout]]'s footings and placements carry theirs, a [[move intent]] and its candidates are tiles of the room the creep stands in, and `RoomPos.range` answers **None** across a border rather than a distance two coordinate systems cannot have between them. `RoomPos.at` joins a grid tile to a room and `RoomPos.pos` drops back to the grid at the moment a reader has decided which room's table it is indexing; `RoomPos.inRoom` takes one room's share of a mixed set, a filter and never a cast, which is what makes narrowing safe at a flood's edge. Grids stay per room and stay keyed by `Pos` — the room is chosen once, and inside a grid nothing carries it (ADR 0041's own reason, and the [[atlas]]'s cost). **Tile** is the word for either, and deliberately: the code binds `tile` to both and the type is what says which, so prose keeps the qualifier — *grid tile* or *this room's tile* for a `Pos`, *room position* for a `RoomPos` — rather than reserving the noun for one of them.
_Avoid_: position (unqualified), coordinate (for the room-joined thing), room position (for a bare `Pos`)

### Spatial projection
A [[colony view]]'s map-shaped view of the rooms the colony works — the [[home room]] and its [[outpost]]s. Still **one** projection (ADR 0005), layered by room name since ADR 0041: the tile-keyed layers sit under a room name, the id-keyed ones (target kinds, hits, stores) do not, because an object id is already unique across the world. Per room it carries the room's name, three-state terrain (plain / swamp / wall), entity positions, what kind of thing each target is (source, controller, a structure or a site of some built kind), which tiles hold a built road (ADR 0010), and current/max hits on repairable kinds only — fields nobody decides on stay out. Raw data, always present (possibly empty); decisions consult it only through the [[atlas]]. A tile absent from the projection is impassable — absence is per-entry, never per-projection (ADR 0004), which is what carries the changing half of an outpost the colony can only sometimes see — the reservation remaining, hits, stores, creeps, hostiles. The declared half does not wait for vision: each source's and the controller's id and tile enter the projection because a human wrote them down, laid under whatever vision answered (ADR 0041) — absence read onto the declaration as well is what left ADR 0042's chain with no first step, since no Task could name an outpost and so nothing ever walked there to get the vision. Its ground covers tiles 1..48 and never the border rows (ADR 0036): a border tile is a [[seam]], not ground. The border rings ride beside that ground as a layer of their own, under their room's name, so the [[seam]] query has the engine's own exit terrain while no walking weight grid, walkable or buildable set, [[seat]] or [[work area]] can ever reach one — the [[atlas]] gives the ring a weight table of its own instead, answering impassable for every interior tile, and the Seam query and the crossing's price are its only readers. Assembled whole every tick, with one field recalled rather than rebuilt: the terrain is memoised per room name in the App's heap (ADR 0031) — the engine's whole 50×50, read once on the first tick that projects the room and reused after, split into the ground and the ring the projection asks for, because room terrain never changes for the life of the server. That means the key can never go stale and there is nothing to invalidate; it does not mean anything about terrain is persisted — the memo dies with a global reset and the next tick rebuilds it — nor that any other field outlives the tick.

### Atlas
The per-tick query interface over the [[spatial projection]]: Seats, Work Areas (body-aware for a [[work-heavy body]] harvesting — ADR 0020), [[seam]]s, travel costs, first steps, action permission, standing candidates, placed creeps — and the placement queries the [[layout]] derives from — [[home room]] name, target positions, buildable tiles, [[working ground]], structure and road censuses, raw-terrain [[trunk]] paths (ADR 0005, ADR 0011, ADR 0022). Task-aware where a Task's own geometry answers, tile-aware where it does not: the price, the first step and the action gate take the tiles the decision layer judged a creep may stand on this tick, so the [[reach]]'s subtraction reaches every one of them and the projection still never learns what a [[threat]] is (ADR 0033). The price and the first step take the Task as well, for the one answer no tile set can carry: a target in another room leaves those tiles empty — standing and acting do not cross a border, so the tiles a creep is handed are its own room's — so both cross at the [[seam]] instead, the price as a minimum over the band and the step as the near side of the crossing that minimum was taken at (ADR 0041). One minimisation answers both, because two would agree on every number and split on every tie, walking a creep to one crossing while ranking it at another. The action gate does not cross: a creep may act only from the room its target is in. Total (ADR 0004): geometry the projection cannot place gets one documented answer per query — it never counts against a [[task]] and never blocks an action. Built fresh each tick, with one table recalled rather than refilled: the spawn-origin [[walk]]s a [[lead]] is priced on ride the [[census signature]] (ADR 0032) and survive from tick to tick while the census holds. Matcher and Resolver consult the same one.

### Anchor
The heavy-WORK [[body pattern]] cast for a [[post]]: as many Work as saturate one source plus one spare — six on a held source, three on a source nobody holds — beside one Carry and minimal Move (ADR 0021: past saturation a further Work only drains the source sooner and idles until it regenerates; the spare absorbs an unmanned Post's gap). The ceiling is no longer a constant (ADR 0042, narrowing ADR 0021: unchanged as a rule, changed as a number): it is read over the **set** of posted sources and never off one Post, at the richest saturation in that set. A cast is a body and not a posting — ADR 0021 already refused to size an Anchor by the Post it will man, because [[travel cost]] pins it on the nearest Post only once it is alive — so the row takes the direction it is cheap to be wrong in, an Anchor over-sized for a neutral source wasting 300 energy once in a life where an under-sized one on a held source loses four energy a tick for the whole of it. A posted source the colony cannot price this tick raises no ceiling, and a set with nothing priceable in it keeps the held one (ADR 0004). Which leaves the number at the held six for as long as the colony's own owned room has a Post in that set, so the lapsed-reservation window ADR 0042 opens is closed for the quotas that read a source one at a time — the hauler's and the income base's — and still open for this row's body: an outpost whose reservation has lapsed is garrisoned by six Work against a rock giving five until the ceiling is narrowed to the Posts a cast is filling, which is its own decision (#132's deferred half). Its slowness is the point — body-aware [[travel cost]] pins it to a [[post]] — Harvest's [[work area]] for its heavy body is nothing else (ADR 0020), so it neither works nor fills anywhere but the tile worth garrisoning — where it works in place: alternating Harvest and Upgrade on a [[dual seat]], draining the source into the container under it on a source-[[container]] Post (ADR 0006, generalized by ADR 0012), and on that Post's construction site digging a shovelful and building a shovelful until the container it will drain into stands (#205). The one Carry is kept on both footings so one sizing rule serves the whole row. Travel cost alone doesn't walk it home: [[withdraw]] is inapplicable to it as a [[work-heavy body]] (ADR 0016), so its only feeding-tier candidate is Harvest and an unmanned Post wins it regardless of distance. Dispatched by [[arrival]] (ADR 0025, narrowed by ADR 0048): it is **not** sent to a drained source at all, however well its walk would cover the wait — a fresh one waits beside the spawn instead, at most a [[restock]]'s fifty ticks once in a life — and an Anchor already within digging range of its source holds its Post through every empty window, whether or not it is standing on the container. Off that Post ADR 0016's accepted one-time commute to the controller is closed (ADR 0048), because it was one commute *per release*: rather than spend fifty energy at the far end of a walk it pays for twice, it keeps Harvest — full or empty, a heavy body walking is not digging — and takes the step back onto its Post, where its overflow falls into the container again. Replaced by [[arrival]] too (ADR 0026): once [[expiring]], its successor is cast and walks while it still digs — both halves of the row alike, because a [[lead]] is priced wherever the creep stands (#153), so an Anchor on an [[outpost]]'s Post is led by its successor's cast time plus the crossing over the [[seam]] band and is replaced before it dies exactly as the [[home room]]'s is. Exempt from [[fatigue parity]], which governs only the [[worker unit]] pattern. One Anchor per Post, inside the [[workforce target]].

### Hauler unit
The repeating [Carry; Carry; Move] block hauler bodies are built from (ADR 0012): 150 energy, full speed loaded *on roads* — the row carries its own parity declaration (road parity, not the plain parity of [[fatigue parity]]), because a hauler's whole life is the [[trunk]]. No Work part, so Harvest, Build, Upgrade and Repair are inapplicable by body; it lives in the [[withdraw]]→[[refill]] cycle. Quota: the colony's whole haul, summed over its source [[container]]s — an [[outpost]]'s as much as the [[home room]]'s — each of them round-trip ticks to the spawn (the canonical sink the trunks radiate from) times that source's own output — what its garrison digs, capped at the rock's rate (#208) — over carry capacity, and the sum rounded up **once** (ADR 0049, on the granularity alone: a ceiling per container bought a body for each container's leftover fraction, hiring eight against a demand of 4.9). Distance still sets each container's share of the number; what is shared is only the rounding, and what makes the shared integer safe is [[withdraw]]'s capacity, which divides each store's own stock by a hauler load, so a container filling faster than it is drained admits more drawers than one that is not. A **capacity and not an order**: the feeding tier ranks its Withdraws by [[travel cost]], which is the near container's advantage, so what the cap buys the far one is room for the bodies the shared integer no longer spends at home and never priority over them. The round trip is two [[walk]]s summed, the leg out loaded and the leg back empty (ADR 0029) — nothing is halved on the total, because each leg is already whole ticks. An outpost's container crosses a border to reach the spawn, so each of those two legs is the [[walk]]'s own minimum over the [[seam]] band and not a second cross-room arithmetic (ADR 0030): two joins and not one, because the loaded body and the empty one pay a swamp exit differently and can be right to cross at different exits. Unpaved that trip runs 138–168 ticks (ADR 0042), so such a container puts one to one and a half bodies of demand into the sum where a home container puts a fifth of one — the number is large because the haul is long.

### Work-heavy body
A living body with strictly more Work parts than Move — the [[anchor]] row's shape, and readable off the parts alone: [[fatigue parity]] forbids a worker body from reaching it (ADR 0003) and only the Anchor row's floor of two Work over one Move clears it. A predicate over any body, not a [[body pattern]]: what a creep is is decided from what it is made of, never from the row name in its name (ADR 0006). Five gates read it — [[withdraw]] is inapplicable to one (ADR 0016), Harvest's [[work area]] narrows to a [[post]] for one (ADR 0020), Harvest's Post cap and its full-store garrison reprieve are its alone (ADR 0024), [[flee]] is inapplicable to one (ADR 0033: at four to seven ticks a step it neither escapes nor digs, and its Post's [[rampart]] is its defence), and it is pinned to its source (ADR 0048: a drained one is early for it whatever its walk unless it is already in digging range, and Upgrade is applicable to it only where it can already act on it) — so its intake is digging, from the tile worth digging on, that tile is what it competes for, and it walks to nothing else.

### Standing body
A living body carrying fewer than one Carry part per four Work — `Carry × 4 < Work` (ADR 0046). A predicate over any body and never a row, exactly as [[work-heavy body]] is (ADR 0006): the [[upgrader]] row's `11W/1C/11M` is one and so is the [[anchor]]'s `6W/1C/1M`, and the ratio and not the name is what any gate reads. Three Tasks are inapplicable to one — [[build]], [[repair]] and [[refill]] — because all three are deliveries, and a delivery of fifty energy by a body holding eleven Work is a commute: one tick spent delivering for every tick of the walk out and the walk back, with the Work idle at both ends. One exception, and it is the walk that makes it one (#205): a [[container]] construction site standing on the creep's own [[post]], under its own feet, is applicable to it — there is no commute to a site it is already on, and the fifty it spends is what it dug from the source beside it. Every other Task is left exactly as its own gates already had it — Upgrade, [[withdraw]] and [[harvest]], the working life the [[upgrader]] row was shaped for, and [[pickup]] too, which stays open because a pile is an intake and not a delivery. Untouched is not the same as applicable: ADR 0016 still shuts [[withdraw]] to a [[work-heavy body]], so of the two standing bodies only the upgrader draws. A prohibition and not a price, for the reason [[travel cost]] could not carry it: the delivery such a body must not take is usually the nearest one to it. The [[hauler unit]] falls outside by arithmetic rather than by exception (`Carry × 4 < 0` is false at every size), and the [[worker unit]] by its own [[fatigue parity]], which buys one Carry per Work where this line is one per four. For the Anchor the rule only writes down what a rank-0 Harvest at its [[post]] was already doing — wherever a Harvest is in its pool at all. Where one is not, a full Anchor off a container Post holds no Task at all — ADR 0046 accepted the other half of that sentence too, a pooled Upgrade taken at any distance in place of the nearer [[refill]] it used to take, and ADR 0048 closed it: a [[work-heavy body]] takes Upgrade only where it can already act on it. Which is why the same ADR keeps Harvest applicable to it while its walk to a [[post]] is still ahead of it: closing every walk to a body that can never empty would leave it holding nothing and going nowhere for the rest of its life. It fetches from the [[buffer]] alone: no Pickup and no Storage Withdraw (#206).

### Body pattern
The repeating part block a body is generated from; capacity buys as many whole repeats as it can — for the rows that repeat a block. For the rest the block is the row's *minimal cast* and the row's own sizing rule spends the bank (ADR 0006): the [[anchor]]'s Work up to its ceiling beside a fixed Carry/Move pair, the [[upgrader]]'s Work/Move pairs beside one Carry. So a row is a name and a sizing rule before it is a block, and two rows may share a block and cast nothing alike — the upgrader's is the [[worker unit]]'s three parts. Which pattern a spawn casts is a colony decision; a pattern shapes what a creep is good at, never what it is assigned — creeps stay interchangeable and matching stays Task-based. A row arrives with the colony fact that says when it is cast, or it is not a row yet — the one exception being the [[supply floor]], which is armed by the *absence* of a capability rather than by a quota (ADR 0050) — the [[upgrader]] being the one row ever to land ahead of its own quota, deliberately and across two tickets (ADR 0046), so that until the second lands the table holds every body the colony casts and not the converse.

### Worker unit
The repeating [Work; Carry; Move] block worker bodies are built from — the generalist [[body pattern]]: 200 energy, full speed empty, half speed loaded. Capacity buys as many whole units as it can; what's left is remainder. **The commuting row**, and it stays one (ADR 0046): it crosses a [[seam]] to build an [[outpost]]'s [[container]] and repair the road it walks, so its Carry is the point of it and making it dense would have it deliver fifty energy per fifty-tile trip. The row that does not commute is the [[upgrader]], which is why there are two. Beside a standing buffer the [[upgrader]] row is hired out of the surplus first and this row out of what is left — the remainder of a division that rounds down, less the standing bodies' own replacement (#195) — and where that comes to nothing what keeps generalists in the colony is the floor on this row's share of the [[workforce target]]: two while anything stands in the [[build]] or [[repair]] pool, one otherwise, because a colony of standing bodies and haulers can neither build nor repair anything at all.

### Upgrader
The row that stands beside the controller's [[container]] — the upgrade buffer — and spends the colony's surplus into the controller (ADR 0046): one Carry and Work/Move pairs for the rest — `W = M = floor((capacity − 50) / 150)`, never below one pair — so RCL5's 1,800 bank casts `11W/1C/11M` where the [[worker unit]] buys nine Work, 22% more upgrade out of the same energy. Every part is paid for by work done in place: it draws from the [[container]] at its feet ([[withdraw]], ADR 0019) and upgrades from where it stands, and it is a [[standing body]], so it takes no [[build]], [[repair]] or [[refill]] anywhere. Paired Work with Move rather than sized heavier, which keeps it at `Work = Move` and so inside ADR 0016's gate — the alternative, an exemption buying `14W/1C/7M`, is ADR 0046's deferred option and ADR 0016's own "revisit if a Dual-Seat room shows the loss". Pinned by the buffer's Upgrade [[work area]] — [[working ground]], never a [[post]] (ADR 0046: what ADR 0012's Post generalized was the source seat, and it does not reach this tile). Its quota is the surplus divided by one body's upgrade drain, rounded **down** so that the oversell ADR 0037 licenses is spent on the smaller body of the two rows that share the surplus (#195) — the remainder goes to the [[worker unit]] row, which rounds it up — and zero while no built controller container stands in the room — the buffer is a precondition of the hire and never its cause, so a colony with no surplus hires none however long the container has stood. Cast fourth, after the [[reserver]], the [[anchor]] and the hauler rows that produce the surplus and before the [[worker unit]] that would spend it at nine Work to this row's eleven. Hired only where the row can count what it hired: under an 800 bank its own cast is no [[standing body]] and is read back to the generalist, so the quota is zero there rather than hiring a body whose gap nothing could close — the ratio saying that three Work against a fifty-energy load is not yet a commute, and the band losing nothing, since the generalist row buys the same body at the same price out of the same surplus. Competes with the worker row for Upgrade on [[travel cost]] like any two bodies, and the one standing beside the buffer wins.

### Reserver
The [Claim; Move] row, one per declared [[outpost]], that holds that room's [[reservation]] through the [[reserve]] Task (ADR 0042) — and one per [[candidate colony]] beside them, which takes that room instead through the [[claim]] (ADR 0047): the same body applies to both Tasks and to nothing else, so a second row would be this block under a second name (ADR 0006) and every count that reads a body back — the casting order, the row's gap, the amortization — reads a claimer as a reserver. A candidate colony's room hires **one** body, not two: its controller carries a Claim and no Reserve, so a reserver hired for it would arrive at a controller with no Task on it. Sizing tells the two demands apart — a claim asks for one block whatever the reservation has done, because it is one act by one CLAIM part — though every body the row casts this tick is still cast at the largest demand in the list, the claimer included, since the Matcher and not the list decides which controller each finished body holds. Cast **before** every other row: the others spend income, this one decides whether the income is five a tick or ten, and it is the cheapest body on the table. Quota and sizing are one rule rather than two — `ceil((5000 − reservation remaining) / 600)` CLAIM parts — so it shrinks to the minimum in steady state and arrives bigger when the reservation has slipped, with no state kept between ticks. Not sized to the bank like every other row, which would buy a third CLAIM at RCL6 for a reservation that caps anyway; and never one body roving two rooms, which costs 2,600 and is therefore an RCL7 creep.

### Fatigue parity
The body-generation invariant (ADR 0003): a worker body padded beyond whole [[worker unit]]s never moves slower than the pure-unit body, empty or loaded. The remainder buys as much Carry as parity allows, then Move — never Work.

### Layout
The deterministic full structure plan (ADR 0011): every clustered structure (tower first, then extensions) picked by one ordering rule — buildable tiles on the spawn's checkerboard colour, nearest-to-spawn first, [[working ground]] excluded and the [[link footing]]s held out (ADR 0022) — plus the [[storage]] (the first clustered pick, ADR 0022), the [[link footing]]s, the [[container]]s, the [[trunk]] roads and the [[rampart]]s (ADR 0034), computed whole from the [[atlas]], never persisted to Memory. The clustered horizon is RCL5 (30 extensions + two towers, ADR 0039) — one level of lookahead, so today's trunks are routed around tomorrow's structures; Storage and the Link footings are reserved regardless of level because their tiles, once taken by an extension, never come back, and the reserved window carries one spare tile per footing so the picks a footing pushes outward are reserved before the trunks are routed too (ADR 0027). Recomputed only when its [[census signature]] changes (ADR 0017) — same census, same plan. Placement filters the Layout to what the current RCL unlocks and what is missing; the tick a level lands, its sites drop. One kind is filtered by a level the engine does not impose: **road sites are placed from RCL3 up and never below it** (`roadLevel`, read off `Colony.bootstrapLevel`, #209 amending ADR 0011) — a level gate and not pacing, which stays rejected. The plan is still whole at every level, so the [[trunk]]s go on routing around tomorrow's structures; what waits is the placing. A colony under RCL3 is buying the economy the roads would serve: W13S28 at RCL1 planned 64 road sites, some 19,000 energy, against 8 a tick of income and an [[upgrade]] sitting in the same surplus tier but further from every worker, around 2,400 ticks of the colony's whole income laid out ahead of the 200 progress that doubles the body. A road is worth what ADR 0010 prices it at to that body too — paved, its loaded commute is twice bare speed — and the gate narrows ADR 0010 rather than denying it: half a tick a loaded step does not buy back 2,400 ticks of income, and the [[work area]]'s swamps wait with the [[trunk]]s because a swamp road costs five times a plain one. The [[container]]s are not held back with them — the tile clause that defers a container to a road (ADR 0040) reads the road *sites*, and below the gate there are none to collide with; the placement subtracts the pending container sites in turn, so the tile a container took below the gate is not asked for a road while that container is still a site.

### Trunk
A paved line in the [[layout]]: each source to the controller, each source to the spawn, plus the swamp tiles inside the controller's Upgrade [[work area]]. Priced on raw terrain only — paved length, plain 2 and swamp 3 for its construction cost, never the walk's swamp 10 (#211) — and routed around the Layout's reserved tiles. The rule is general — short trunks still pave; room-specific exemptions don't get encoded (ADR 0011). A goal no tile of which is reachable from the source once the reservation is marked impassable routes nothing at all, and that trunk is **recorded** rather than dropped (#107): the empty path unions into the road plan contributing nothing, so without the [[layout record]] a source paved to nothing looks exactly like a trunk nobody asked for.

### Working ground
The tiles the colony works from: every source's [[seat]]s and the controller's Upgrade [[work area]]. Excluded from the [[layout]]'s clustered ordering (ADR 0022) — a clustered structure there eats a standing tile the [[anchor]]s and upgraders need, and in this room the cluster's nearest-to-spawn ring reaches the controller pocket by RCL5. A [[link footing]] is the one structure *footprint* allowed on it; a [[rampart]] is no footprint — walkable, blocking nothing, taking no tile from the [[post]] it covers (ADR 0034). Derived from the [[atlas]] each tick, never persisted.

### Storage
The colony's stock: the one storage structure the [[layout]] places on the cluster's first pick (ADR 0022), built the tick RCL4 lands. Two roles, both ordered so it never outbids the flow (ADR 0023): the deepest [[refill]] target — below even the controller [[container]], so energy reaches it only when every other sink is full — and a [[withdraw]] source one tier below the source containers, pooled only while some sink other than itself has free capacity, so a hauler beside it never cycles energy in and out of the same store. A [[keep]] structure: ramparted, repaired to full, and a reason to spend safe mode (ADR 0034). Never the [[trunk]] hub — the spawn stays the canonical sink, and the Storage sits beside it by construction.

### Keep
The structures worth defending: the spawn, every tower and the [[storage]] (ADR 0034). One list, three rules: each is covered by a [[rampart]], each is repaired to full hits rather than to half, and any one of them below full hits while a [[hostile]] stands in the room fires the [[safe-mode reflex]]. The [[post]]s are ramparted with the Keep but are not of it: a container's hits never spend the stock. Both the cover and the floor apply from `bootstrapLevel` (#214): a colony under RCL3 places no rampart and repairs none.
_Avoid_: base, core
### Rampart
The walkable defensive structure the [[layout]] places over every [[keep]] structure and every [[post]] a [[container]] stands on, the tick the thing it covers stands (ADR 0034) — standing is the built census, so a [[storage]] that is still a site waits, and a Post that is a bare [[dual seat]] has no structure to cover. A creep on its own rampart cannot be attacked, so the tile is in no [[reach]] — the [[anchor]]'s whole defence, since it cannot [[flee]]. Kept above a **floor** of hits by [[repair]]: hungry below it, whole at it, the number a tunable derived from the ticks to hold times the damage per tick to hold against, never a term of the decision. Placed from RCL2, the level the engine allows a rampart at, and needing no level of its own past it; the set is the rule's, so a three-source room ramparts three Posts.

### Link footing
A tile the [[layout]] reserves for a link (ADR 0022) while the level that unlocks links is still ahead: one beside each **planned** source [[container]], one beside the controller container, one beside the [[storage]] — planned, not built, because a [[post]] needs a standing container and a Post-anchored rule would reserve nothing at level 0, when the reservation is worth the most. The count is the rule's, never a constant: a room with three sources holds five footings. Each footing is the buildable tile within range 1 of its target that is off every [[trunk]], off the footings' own targets and off the other footings, nearest the spawn, ties by x then y — read back from a standing link too, so the tile does not move the tick its link goes up. The clustered reservation is the one thing it does not dodge, because it outranks it: the tower and the extensions yield by taking their picks with the footings held out, and the reservation is widened by the footing count so the picks they push outward are still tiles the trunks were routed around (ADR 0027). The only structure footing allowed on [[working ground]]: a link there buys the [[anchor]] and the upgraders a transfer without leaving their tile. A link is a built kind and nothing else — no placement [[intent]] ever names one (ADR 0027) — and at RCL5 this colony fills none of them (ADR 0038): the only thing a link could save here is a hauler body, and the [[workforce target]]'s quota is already at its floor of **one body for the colony's whole haul** — the sum of the home containers' round trips rounds up once (ADR 0049, revising the per-container floor ADR 0038 derived its refusal from), and no wire goes under one. The haul being spoken of is the **home room's** source containers alone (ADR 0042, narrowing ADR 0038): an [[outpost]]'s container adds one to one and a half bodies of demand to that sum for its round trip across the [[seam]] and does lift the row off the floor, but a link resolves its transfer target out of its own room's objects and can never reach one, so no haul a link could shorten is in that number — a reader arriving here with the outpost hauler total in hand is holding the wrong one. The footings stay reserved regardless, because their tiles never come back and reserving them costs nothing; what fills them is a decision for the room whose haul has left that floor, not this one. A target the fold can find no tile for — every neighbour gone to a [[trunk]], to another target, to another footing or to a standing structure — reserves nothing, and that shortfall is **recorded** rather than dropped (ADR 0035): it rides the [[layout record]], because a guarantee that can degrade in silence is not one. W12S28's `10,43` source container is already in that state, its last candidate taken by an extension built before the reservation existed (#77).

### Colony
A [[home room]] and the [[outpost]]s worked from it (ADR 0047): the unit the whole decision layer is written in — one [[spatial projection]], one [[atlas]], one [[layout]], one set of quotas, one Task pool, one plan memo — and therefore the unit the declaration is written in. Declared, never discovered, exactly as an outpost is: a human writes `{ Home; Outposts; Mother }` in a commit, and the bot never edits it, because owning a second room is a decision a person makes and independence is an event a person can see. The home is a room *name* and not a spawn — a colony outlives every spawn standing in it — and it is the key the whole record is filed under: `ColonyView.ofWorld` is handed the entry rather than reading a home off the first spawn, and a home nobody declared works no outposts at all, which is the behaviour the empty declaration shipped with. Declaring a colony is not a claim that one exists: a home nobody owns yet is a [[candidate colony]], and one with no spawn of its own is a [[nursery]] and not yet independent. A **living** colony is one that is neither — its home is ours *and* holds one of our spawns — and living is exactly the set `decide` is run over, once each, every tick: a colony with no spawn has nothing to cast from, no bank to cast out of and no anchor for its [[layout]], and it is its mother who works that room until one stands. When the declaration names no living colony at all, the room the first spawn stands in is one anyway, with no outposts — a slip in a constant a human moves costs a colony its outposts and never its whole tick, which is the same answer a home nobody declared already gets for its outposts. What each run is handed is its own: its own rooms, its own spawns, its own bank, its own creeps. A creep belongs to the colony whose spawn cast it — read off the creep's own name — with one exception, **adoption**: a creep standing in a room only some *other* colony projects is that colony's for the tick, priced, matched and moved by it, and its caster's again the tick it walks home. Nothing is kept and nothing is shared: a room its own colony projects too is still its own colony's business, and a room nobody projects — a [[stand-down]]'s withheld [[outpost]] — adopts nobody, so the creep standing there still has no tile. A child that has left its mother's outpost list carries her name in a `Mother` field of its own, and she goes on raising it through its [[bootstrap window]]. Today two colonies are declared and both run.
_Avoid_: base, empire, room group

### Stage
Where a [[colony]] stands in its life, derived fresh every tick and read wherever a rule differs by age (ADR 0052 decision 3). Three answers: **[[nursery]]**, claimed with no spawn of ours standing in it; **bootstrapping**, its own spawn standing and its controller still under RCL3 — the [[bootstrap window]]; and **independent**, at that level or past it, which is the colony every rule this bot grew up with was written for. One derivation (`Colony.stageOf`) off three facts of the world — the room is ours, a spawn of ours stands in it, the controller's level — and the only place the bootstrap level is read, so the rules that each compared a level of their own now compare one answer: the [[layout]]'s road gate and its rampart gate, the [[repair]] pool's rampart floor, the tier a site in a young room is built on, and which rooms a [[mother colony]] is still raising. A room that is no colony of ours has no stage at all — never claimed, lost, or one nothing can see — and every reader answers that the way it answers a room it knows nothing about, which is what closes the raising half: a room we do not own is one no [[mother colony]] is bootstrapping. The taking-back half is read off ownership instead of off a stage, so it survives the absence — a declared child that stops being ours, and that nobody else holds, is projected by its mother again for a [[claim]] (#221, `Colony.reclaiming`). A stage says what a room *is* and never whose business it is: it is derived for every declared home, and for the home of every living colony so that a spawn room no declaration names has one too, then handed to every colony alike — whether this colony is the one raising that child is its own [[spatial projection]]'s answer.
_Avoid_: phase, generation, tier (which is the Matcher's ranking)

### Candidate colony
A declared [[colony]] whose home room this colony does not own yet (ADR 0047) — the shape of "I mean to take that room". Both halves are needed and they come from different places: the declaration is a human's constant, carried into the [[colony view]] whole because no projected fact distinguishes a room we mean to own from a neighbour we merely mine, and the ownership is read off `RoomControl` in Core, so the tick the [[claim]] lands the room stops being a candidate with no state kept and nothing to reset. Until it is claimed it is projected and worked as an [[outpost]] of the mother colony — that is what puts its controller in the projection for the Claim to name, and it stays in the mother's list until the day it stands on its own — so one room being two colonies' business at once is the ordinary arrangement here and not a conflict. Its sources are priced as an outpost's while it is one and at the held ten a tick once it is owned, so the economics do not move on the tick it changes hands. What it becomes on that tick is a [[nursery]]: claimed, projected by its mother still, and not independent until a spawn stands in it.

### Mother colony
The declared [[colony]] that raises another (ADR 0047): the colony that projects, mines and reserves the child's room until it stands on its own, and so the colony whose [[spatial projection]] holds the controller the [[claim]] names. It is the only place two colonies' business meets. The relation is written twice over, once for each half of the child's life, and never in two places at once. While the child is a [[candidate colony]] or a [[nursery]] it is one entry of the declaration reading another's home — a room is a candidate because a human declared it a home, and claimable because that same human left it in a mother's outpost list — and nothing else is needed. The day the human takes the room out of that list, the child names the mother instead, in a `Mother` field of its own, and it is that field the [[bootstrap window]] is read off: the outpost entry is gone by then and the borrowing rule still has two levels to run. The field is a human's like the rest of the declaration, and the room a mother still declares as her outpost is worked as one, never as a bootstrap layer — the outpost reading names it first. The same field is what carries a child home again after it is lost: a declared room of hers that stops being ours, and that nobody else holds, is [[borrowed work]] of hers for a [[claim]] and nothing else (#221), so a colony can be taken back without a human editing the constant that declared it.
_Avoid_: parent colony, host colony, parent (as the field)

### Nursery
The first [[stage]] of a [[colony]]'s life: claimed by us, with no spawn of its own standing yet (ADR 0047). The state between a [[candidate colony]] and an independent one, and the only state in which two colonies' *work* — and not merely their declarations — overlaps. It is not independent, so its [[mother colony]] goes on projecting it as an [[outpost]] and running the one `decide` that covers both. The three facts the stage is derived from come from three places: the human's declaration, the ownership the world reads back the tick the [[claim]] lands, and the absence of any of our spawns in the room — so a nursery begins the tick a claim succeeds, with nothing carried over from the pool that emptied itself, and ends the tick a spawn stands, before and not because of the human's edit splitting the constant in two. A rule that acts on one adds the projection to the stage: the stage says the room is a nursery, and this colony projecting it says it is *hers*. The [[home room]] of the colony doing the reading is never one: `decide` runs only for a colony whose home holds a spawn. Three rules read it and nothing else does — the tier, the builders' budget and the worker row (`workforceTarget`, see [[pioneer]]) — and only the first two are spelled through one predicate, so a change to what a nursery is has to be followed to all three. **Every** construction site in the room is feeding-tier [[build]] — not the [[container]]'s alone, as an ordinary outpost's is (#157) — because what the room is waiting on is the spawn a *human* places by hand, and a rule that picked sites out by kind would be a list of the kinds a human is allowed to want built; and the concurrent-builder budget those outpost sites share does not reach here, by room and not by kind, because what two rations is how much home surplus a third of the income is worth and a claimed room is past that question — which leaves the crowd crossing for a nursery's site bounded by nothing but the site being finished. Its own Upgrade is nobody's business yet — an RCL1 controller has 20,000 ticks before it downgrades, which is longer than the nursery — and its [[work-heavy body]] gate is the outpost site's, unmoved and for the same reason: a heavy body's cross-room work is a [[post]] and never a fifty-tile delivery (ADR 0020). It has Posts of its own — it is still its mother's [[outpost]], so the container rule places a container on its source and the tick that container stands the room has a Post and an [[anchor]] on it — and the gate is what keeps that Anchor there. What follows it is the [[bootstrap window]]: the tick a spawn stands the nursery ends, the child begins running its own `decide`, and the mother goes on raising it under a rule that reads the same three facts with the spawn clause inverted.
_Avoid_: child colony (as the state), colony under construction

### Bootstrap window
The second [[stage]] of a child [[colony]]'s life, in which its [[mother colony]] is still raising it though it runs its own `decide` (ADR 0047 decision 4): from the tick a spawn stands in it — which ends the [[nursery]] and makes it living — until its controller reaches RCL3. The one cross-colony borrowing rule there is, written down as a bootstrap exception and closed at that level, because a colony at RCL3 has a tower and a bank it can cast a real body out of. Inside the window the mother projects the child's room beside her own and pools two Tasks in it, the child's [[upgrade]] and every [[build]] standing there, and her worker row keeps hiring [[pioneer]]s; the child pools that same Upgrade in its own tick, so one target sits in two pools and nothing but [[travel cost]] says which colony's body takes it — her room feeds itself through the bodies standing in it and the child's through the ones that crossed the [[seam]]. What she carries of that room is only what she works there: the controller, the sites, and the spawn that says the room is a child and no longer a nursery. Its rocks, containers, stock and stores stay the child's, because a mother pooling them would hire a second [[anchor]] for a [[post]] the child already garrisons, count that room's output into her own quotas twice and haul its energy home — the single-projection arrangement ADR 0047 rejected, arriving through the back door. The stage is derived off the world once a tick and decides the **scan set**: the tick the child is independent the room simply leaves her projection, and the Upgrade, the Build and the pioneers' addend go with it in one subtraction rather than through three gates that could disagree. What closes her window is that subtraction and never the stage on its own — while a human still declares the child's room one of her outposts the room is in her scan set through the outpost reading, which asks no stage, and she goes on raising it at any RCL until the commit takes that entry out. The same level is the child's [[layout]]'s road gate (#209): inside the window the child plans its [[trunk]]s and places none of them, so the crowd the mother hired builds the room's [[container]]s and its own Upgrade — and, from RCL2, the extensions the level unlocks — instead of 19,000 energy of pavement, and the road sites arrive on the tick the borrowing ends. While she projects it, adoption is inert there — a room two colonies project names no single adopter — so the pioneers she hired stay hers to match; the tick the window closes they are the child's, like every creep standing in a room only it projects.
A site in a bootstrapping room is feeding-tier in both pools — the bank before the controller — and the borrowed Upgrade waits while one stands.
_Avoid_: bootstrap phase, adolescence, borrowing period

### Borrowed work
What one [[colony]] may take of another's, named on its [[colony view]] and bounded (ADR 0052 decision 7): the home rooms of the children it is raising, whose [[upgrade]] and [[build]] its bodies may cross for (ADR 0047 decision 4), and the home rooms of the children it has **lost** — declared colonies of ours that stopped being ours and that nobody else holds, whose controller is a [[claim]] to make (#221). The two are disjoint by construction, one being a room we own and the other a room we do not, and they narrow to the same three kinds of target, because a Claim asks for exactly what an Upgrade does: a controller placed on a tile a body can stand beside. The lost half exists because a [[stage]] is nothing at all for a room we do not own, so the subtraction that stopped a mother raising a room nobody ever claimed also stopped her taking back one she had raised: the room left every projection there was, no Claim was pooled anywhere, and a human's edit was the only way home. The list is what narrows those rooms in the view — the controller, the sites and the spawn tile reach the mother, and the rocks, containers, stock and hits stay the child's — so the one arrangement in which two colonies' work overlaps is a field a reader can enumerate rather than a layer the shell quietly cut. The **cap** is not on it yet: how many bodies cross is the worker row's `pioneerCount` ([[pioneer]]), a quota rather than a fact about the child's room, and it moves here with the rest of the tunables.
_Avoid_: shared work, cross-colony pool

### Foreign bodies
Where the creeps a [[colony]] does **not** hold stand in the rooms it works, kept per room on its [[colony view]] and cut from the same layer its own fleet is cut from (`ColonyView.Foreign`, ADR 0052 decision 1). Its own bodies are on the layer as occupants the [[resolver]] arbitrates; another colony's are here instead, because a body two colonies both moved would be moved twice. It exists so that the one place two colonies' work overlaps is a fact a rule can read rather than an invisible occupant: a [[mother colony]]'s [[pioneer]] standing on the child's [[anchor]] tile is on this list of the child's view. Two rules read it (#220). The walking flood charges the [[occupancy surcharge]] on these tiles like any other occupied tile, so a traveller is *priced* around a garrison it can never displace — the half no arbitration can do. And a colony arbitrating alone treats them as walls: tiles nobody may claim and nobody may push through, exactly as a [[grounded]] creep's tile is. In the shell's own pass they are walls no longer, because the colony that holds each of them is in the same fold and registered an intent for it.
_Avoid_: other creeps, outsiders, hostiles (which are another player's)

### Pioneer
One of the `pioneerCount` extra [[worker unit]]s a [[mother colony]] hires while a [[nursery]] of hers stands or a child of hers is inside its [[bootstrap window]] (ADR 0047): the addend on the worker row's share of the [[workforce target]], and the whole of what a nursery costs the mother in bodies. Not a row and not a [[body pattern]] — a pioneer is a generalist, cast from the generalist row and read back as one by `patternOf`, and what makes it a pioneer is the job across the [[seam]], never anything in the body. The worker row and no other because the job is a [[build]]: the hauler row carries no Work part, an [[anchor]]'s cross-room work is a [[post]] and never a delivery, and a [[standing body]] takes no delivery at all. **Three**, a tunable: a spawn is 15,000 progress against fifty energy a trip, so the crowd decides whether the child stands this cycle or the next, and the user chose it over letting a 300-energy bank bootstrap itself; small, because each of them is a body the mother's own surplus work does without for the length of the walk. A flat addend and not one per nursery — a declaration is written one candidate at a time. Hired off the room's state and not off the pool, so the crowd is already across the border when the human's site appears rather than starting the fifty-tile walk on that tick. One addend across both states and not two: the crowd that raises the spawn is the crowd that builds the child's first [[layout]], so standing it down on the tick of independence would take three bodies out of the room on the tick the work doubled. Since #213 the job reaches them by rank: a child's Upgrade is feeding-tier in the mother's pool for exactly `pioneerCount` holders, so the three cross and the fourth stays home.

### Outpost
A room neighbouring the [[home room]] that this colony mines but does not own. Declared, not discovered (ADR 0041), and declared *inside* the [[colony]] that works it (ADR 0047) — the outposts are one home room's, and a room's name in that list is what makes it this colony's business: the constant is a human's, moved in a commit as the [[layout]]'s horizon is (ADR 0039) — so the bot has no scouting, and cannot take a third one by itself. One declaration is the room's name, each of its sources, and its controller, positions and all under the ids the **engine** gives them, because a live projection keys every target by the server's own id and a declaration in any other vocabulary would match nothing, in silence (ADR 0004). Only what cannot wait for vision is declared; the reservation remaining, hits and stores are read off the projection where there is vision and absent entry by entry where there is none. The controller is not optional: an unreserved source is worth half (ADR 0042), so a room with no controller to reserve — a sector centre, a Source Keeper room — is not a candidate Outpost at all. Its rooms enter the [[spatial projection]] under their own names and its Tasks enter the one pool. The community calls these *remotes*.
_Avoid_: remote, franchise, territory

### Reservation
A neutral controller held by this colony's CLAIM parts. It doubles every source in that room — 5 energy a tick becomes 10 — decays by 1 a tick, and caps at 5,000. The economic precondition of an [[outpost]] (ADR 0042), and the reason a source's output is a per-source fact rather than a constant: a lapsed Reservation halves a room, and quotas sized for the *held* rate would overbuild it twofold. **Held** is the colony's word for either half of what the engine pays the full rate for — a room it owns or a room this Reservation covers — because the [[home room]] is owned and unreserved and is worth ten all the same. A Reservation another player holds is not held: the engine pays ten in that room too, and the colony still prices it at five, because a room somebody else holds is one it is withdrawing from. It does not keep an invader core out — the engine's expansion tests only for an *owner* — which is what the [[stand-down]] answers. A core that lands takes the Reservation for itself, so the holder is one of three and never a flag: ours, the NPC Invader's, or another player's. The last two price the same and withdraw on opposite rules (ADR 0043) — the NPC's ticks left are the clock a level-0 core's stand-down runs to, because such a core carries no collapse timer, while a player's hold is the clockless exit — so a projection that answered both with "not ours" would leave the gate no correct answer. Whose it is, is decided in the shell against two names; the view carries the answer and never a username.

### Arrival
The tick a creep can first act on a Task: its [[walk]] to that Task's [[work area]] from where it stands now (ADR 0029) — whole ticks over a path blind to today's traffic, never [[travel cost]] halved. The horizon at which time-aware judgements are made, not the current tick (ADR 0025, ADR 0026): a drained source's Harvest is applicable when the walk covers the [[restock]] wait — for a light body, the only kind ADR 0048 leaves that dispatch open to; a creep is [[expiring]] when its successor's [[lead]] outlasts its life; a Task's capacity counts a holder only when its stay and the candidate's overlap — the holder is still alive when the candidate arrives, and has arrived itself before the candidate dies. Derived each tick from the [[colony view]] and the [[atlas]], never persisted.

### Restock
The moment a drained source holds energy again — the engine's regeneration, projected as ticks remaining (zero for a source holding energy now). The one time fact a [[colony view]] carries about a source (ADR 0013, widened by ADR 0025): stocked is a restock of zero, not a field of its own.

### Expiring
A living creep whose remaining life is at or under its [[lead]] — it will be dead before a replacement cast now could stand where it stands (ADR 0026). Excluded from the [[workforce target]]'s living count and its row's gap, so the successor is cast while it still works; never released for it — anti-thrash keeps it on its Task to the last tick. Derived each tick, never persisted.

### Lead
The time a creep's replacement needs to stand on its tile: the successor body's cast time (3 ticks a part) plus that body's [[walk]] out of the spawn, priced over [[travel cost]]'s own weights for the successor's fatigue factor, not the incumbent's (ADR 0026) — whole ticks with no tile below one, and blind to today's traffic as the hauler quota's round trip is (ADR 0029): the path does not start until the body is cast, and the tile it ends on is the one the creep being replaced is standing on. It begins *beside* the spawner, where the engine places a finished creep, never on the spawner's own tile — a step the replacement never walks would buy a lead it cannot use. One rule for every [[body pattern]] — a slow Anchor earns a long lead, a hauler on a [[trunk]] a short one — never a per-row constant. Several spawns resolve as the quota resolves them, at the cheapest; geometry the [[atlas]] cannot price leads nobody. Priced wherever the creep stands, home or an [[outpost]]: the spawner is at home, so a creep across a border is led by the same minimum over the [[seam]] band every other cross-room price is read off (ADR 0030) — one row, one rule, both sides of a border, since an outpost's [[post]] hires its [[anchor]] off the home row and a [[reserver]]'s whole working life is the far side of a Seam. Two rooms with no crossing lead nobody, as an unreachable tile does. The walk out of the spawn reads nothing but the census — weights and the successor's body alike — so it is recalled while the [[census signature]] holds and recomputed when it moves (ADR 0032), never per tick; that recall covers **both legs**, keyed by the spawner's tile, the successor's fatigue factor and the room the goal stands in. The room in the key is what keeps two rooms' coordinates apart, so a home walk and an outpost's answer at the same coordinate are two entries rather than one, and the far leg qualifies for the recall on the same terms the near leg does: an outpost's ground needs no vision (ADR 0031), the roads, obstacles and obstacle-kind sites vision does pay for are signed per projected room as the home room's are, the [[seam]] band is border terrain and never moves, and which rooms are projected at all is signed too — so a room the stand-down withdraws (ADR 0043) drops the table rather than leaving its answer behind. One flood per census for a whole outpost, where it was once one per ask.

### Verdict
The reasoned outcome a decision step returns beside its decision — data, never a log line (ADR 0009). The [[matcher]]'s Verdict on a creep says which [[task]] won it and why (rank, [[travel cost]], load, tie-break), that a remembered assignment was kept (anti-thrash, distinct from a fresh match), why an assignment was released, or why an unassigned creep got nothing — including a Task whose time has not come (too early: the body and energy fit, only the [[arrival]] doesn't, ADR 0025); the [[resolver]]'s says what became of its movement ([[grounded]], yielded, reroute, or stalled — rested, settled off the tile it asked for, with nobody its room's pass can name holding it, which is the silence a kept traveller that simply failed to move used to leave in the timeline, #219; whether it stood still or sidestepped onto its tail is the Intent's business and not the Verdict's). The too-early reason is not a bare word: on a release and on a rejection alike it carries the two numbers the gate compared, the [[walk]] and the [[restock]] wait, because since ADR 0029 no reader recovers the walk by halving a [[travel cost]]. A Verdict that falls out of work already done is always-on; one whose evidence must be manufactured — full candidate scoring, reroute attribution (ADR 0018) — is computed only for creeps on the [[verbose list]]. The Planner and spawn decisions return no Verdicts.

### Census signature
The fingerprint of everything a census-derived plan reads: the kind and position of every standing structure, **each named with the room the projection places it in**; the kind and position of every pending site, named the same way; the [[home room]]'s controller level and its name; and the rate every projected room's sources are priced at — who **owns or reserves** it, or the third answer for a room the colony has no vision in this tick (ADR 0004). Named with the room because two rooms hold the same coordinates, so `Container@16,44` in either would otherwise be one census entry. Both halves span every projected room, because the memo has two cross-room readers: the hauler quota, which folds every projected room's source [[container]]s and prices each at its own room's rate (ADR 0042), and the spawn [[walk]] table's far leg, which floods the goal room's own step-weight grid. The second is why the pending half widened with the standing one — an obstacle-kind site closes its tile in whatever room it stands in, so a site outside home moves a grid the walk table holds an answer off, and a census stopping at the home layer would be a signature gap out there. The rate is signed as the rate and never as a reservation's ticks left, which decays every tick and would throw the memo away on each of them. While it is unchanged the [[layout]] and the hauler quota are both provably identical and neither is recomputed (ADR 0017) — the signature signs the *union* of what the two read, ADR 0017's "the quota's input set is a subset of the Layout's" having stopped holding the tick the quota reached a second room (ADR 0044), and the spawn-origin [[walk]]s behind every [[lead]] are recalled rather than flooded again (ADR 0032). Held in heap only — a global reset discards it and the next tick recomputes.

### Verbose list
The creep names owed full candidate scoring, stored beside the [[transition log]] under `Memory.fabot.observe` and read fresh each tick — flipped from the terminal through the Memory HTTP API, so an investigation needs no redeploy. Empty (or absent, or malformed) means off; each listed creep's Scoring [[verdict]] carries one Candidate per pooled [[task]]: scored on the full matching key, or rejected at the first gate it failed (threatened, inapplicable, capacity-full, unreachable, too-early — the last carrying the [[walk]] and the wait it was judged against, since a rejected row has no cost to read one off). The scored row is not widened to match: only a rejection raises the question of how long the creep still has to wait. A listed creep also gets reroute attribution (ADR 0018) — the manufactured-evidence Verdicts ride this list together. The attribution's traffic-blind route comes off the [[atlas]]'s shared flood memo since ADR 0030, so the flood ADR 0018 called unmemoisable no longer is; the list still gates it, because that decision was about log noise and not about the flood.

### Transition log
The per-creep ring of recent changes — task handovers and movement events, each with its [[verdict]] and tick — written only on the tick something changed. The colony's answer to "why did this creep flip", capped per creep; a quiet creep writes nothing. It keeps only creeps still alive, which is why a raid needs a channel of its own (ADR 0028).

### Raid log
The colony's episodic record of [[hostile]] presence in the rooms the colony works (ADR 0028; the spawn rooms alone until #201), stored under `Memory.fabot.observe.colonies.<home>.raids` — one log per [[colony]] since ADR 0047, because it answers for the rooms one colony works and one shared ring would let one colony's raids evict another's [[stand-down]] — and read from the terminal with `observe.mjs raids [--colony <home>]`, its outpost family with `observe.mjs outposts [--colony <home>]`. A channel of its own for a structural reason: the Transition log is keyed by creep and prunes a creep's whole timeline the tick it dies, so it cannot record the one event a raid record exists for. An **episode** opens the first tick any of those rooms holds any hostile, stays open while hostiles keep appearing, and closes after fifty quiet ticks — the **quiet gap**, long enough to outlast a poke-and-heal cycle, so a squad stepping in and out across 220 ticks is one episode and not forty. Each records its window (opened, and the last tick a hostile actually stood there), the **roster** — one row per distinct hostile id with its owner and part counts, from its first sighting, so a squad reads as five rows — the **closest approach** — the smallest range over the whole episode between any hostile and anything of ours **standing in that hostile's own room** (ADR 0041), an owned creep or an owned structure, with the tile — a [[room position]], so it names the room it was measured in (#204) — and the tick, the number that separates a probe at the room edge from a loss; range is a measure inside one room, so one of ours on the same coordinate of another room is not at range 0, and a hostile in a room the projection places nothing of ours in has no approach at all rather than a zero (ADR 0004) — and our **losses** inside the window, by name and tick: a creep gone while a hostile stood there, stamped at the tick it was last seen alive, because a name is missing the tick after its creep died. Never a creep whose own clock ran out — the record answers what the raid cost, and the view's ticks-to-live, the same fact [[expiring]] is judged from, tells old age from a kill before it happens. A ring capped at twenty episodes, oldest trimmed first, disposable by construction like its sibling. Since ADR 0034 each episode also carries the **damage**: the hits lost across the [[keep]] and the [[rampart]]s inside its window, decreases summed and repairs ignored — and since #201 it is the one field of an episode that is one room's, summed over the ticks a hostile stood in the [[home room]] alone, because that is where the Keep and its ramparts stand and an episode held open from an [[outpost]] would otherwise record this room's decay as what a raid a border away cost. The episode itself names no room, and that is the decision ADR 0028 made: a raid that crosses a border is one episode. What used to make it a gap as well — the closest approach recording a tile with no room, so an operator reading `observe.mjs raids` could not tell an outpost raid from a home one — closed with #204: the approach is a [[room position]] and prints its room. Since ADR 0043 the channel carries a second family beside the raid episodes: an [[outpost]]'s threat episode, which adds one field those never needed — the tick the [[stand-down]] it drives expires, beside the room it shuts and which of the three deadlines that tick was read off. Its own ring beside the raids' and never rows mixed into them, because a shared ring is a shared depth and twenty raids at home would evict the episode holding an outpost shut — and this ring, unlike its sibling, never trims over a row whose clock is still running, so it may stand one row per shut room past its cap and pays the overrun back out of the spent stand-downs behind it the moment there are any. It is opened by an invader core rather than a hostile, records none of the roster, approach, losses or damage — a core is a structure the projection carries a room and a deadline for and nothing else — and it is **exempt from the quiet gap**: the gap asks whether a squad has left, and a core never leaves while the stand-down withdraws the very creeps whose vision would see it, so silence closes nothing here and only the clock does. A tick with vision re-reads that clock but only ever moves it outward — the later of the recorded deadline and this tick's is the one kept, because a re-read that landed on a worse deadline would cut a stand-down short, and ADR 0043 lets this record be wrong only in the direction that costs an outpost's income rather than a creep a cycle. Beside the two rings the leaf carries one flat map, which is neither: the rooms the colony last saw in another player's hands, each against the tick that look was taken on, the memory ADR 0043's clockless withdrawal is held in — no window, no expiry and no basis, because a room somebody else works is not a threat with a deadline. The tick is not a clock and nothing is measured from it; it is there so the closing of that gate can be dated, which is the whole of what this channel owes an operator reading an income drop months later. That is the single narrowing of "a record to be read, never a signal sent": the stand-down gate is the one reader that acts on this channel, and every other reaction (ADR 0033, 0034) still reads the view, not the log.

### Layout record
The colony-level channel carrying what the [[layout]] could not deliver (ADR 0035), stored beside the [[raid log]] under `Memory.fabot.observe.colonies.<home>.layout` — one record per [[colony]] since ADR 0047, because there is one Layout per colony — and read from the terminal with `observe.mjs layout [--colony <home>]`. It holds three lists, all losses of the current plan: the [[link footing]] targets the fold found no tile for, each by its target tile and its kind — source container, controller container, storage — the [[trunk]]s the router found no path for (#107), each naming the source that lost one and the goal it lost it to, the Upgrade [[work area]] or a spawn by id, and the [[container]] picks the plan deferred because a container already serves their target (ADR 0040), each naming the target — a source by id, or the controller — the tile the plan picked and the tile actually serving it. Per goal and not per source, because the goals are collected per source: W12S27 planned from `6,18` drops the source→spawn trunk and paves the source→controller one. A channel of its own for the [[raid log]]'s structural reason: every [[verdict]] names a creep and none of a footing, a trunk or a container has one, the [[transition log]] is keyed by creep, and the [[planner]] speaks no Verdicts at all. Not a history — no fold, no ring, no baseline: the record is the current plan's, so under a stable [[census signature]] it is the same list every tick and rides the plan memo beside the site [[intent]]s rather than being rederived (ADR 0017). Written every tick, the empty lists included, which is what keeps a list's three answers distinct: no leaf at all is a bundle that predates the channel, an empty list is the guarantee holding — ADR 0022 and ADR 0027's one footing per target, ADR 0011's one trunk per (source, goal), ADR 0040's container standing where the plan wanted it — and a row is a reservation, a haul road or a container tile the colony no longer has. Each list is guarded in its own right on the way out, so a leaf carrying one and not another fails rather than letting the lists that are there vouch for the one that is not. A record to be read, never a signal sent — nothing reacts to it, and what to do about a loss is a decision of its own (#100).

### CPU line
The colony's per-tick record of what a tick cost, stored beside the [[transition log]] and the verbose list under `Memory.fabot.observe` and read from the terminal with `observe.mjs cpu` (ADR 0041). Flat and keyed by tick, where two colonies cannot collide, so it stayed where it was when the [[raid log]] and the [[layout record]] moved a level down under `observe.colonies.<home>` (ADR 0047) — which is the sibling list "disposable by construction like its siblings" below now means. One row per tick the loop finished — the tick number, the milliseconds the bot had spent by the time it stopped looking, and where they went — oldest first, a ring capped at a hundred, disposable by construction like its siblings. The **phase split** is the second half of a row (#170): what `Game.cpu.getUsed()` read at each of the loop's boundaries, differenced, plus the count of [[intent]]s the engine accepted — the engine's prelude, the `entry` column, already spent before `loop` is entered and carried undifferenced, then the projection, the decision, the Memory writes and the Executor. Each column is the ground between two boundaries and not a noun's price, which is what keeps the attribution honest: the Memory parse rides in the `snapshot` column, because `Memory` deserializes on the loop's first touch of it and that touch is the [[raid log]]'s read inside that phase, and `save` spans the observe folds as well as the writes they feed. It exists because a single total cannot attribute the gap between the local ruler and the live line: `npm run profile` measures the outpost scenario at 10.45 ms/tick against a 49.4 ms mean on the server, and the ruler has no engine in front of it — no prelude, no 0.2 CPU per accepted intent. A row from a bundle older than the split carries **no phases at all** rather than zeros, and the reader prints that absence rather than inferring a cheap tick from it. Written last of all, after the Executor's [[intent]]s, because the intents are most of what a tick costs; what it therefore excludes is the read and the write of this leaf and the engine's own serialization of Memory, all outside anything the bot can move. The tick number rides each row rather than being implied by its place in the ring, so a tick that threw before the write is a **gap in the numbers** rather than a cheap tick that never happened. The line exists because ADR 0041 states the condition to revisit the layered [[spatial projection]] as two numbers — a mean tick above 50 ms, or any single tick above 80 — and both are read off this window; the same judgement is printed by `npm run profile`, off the same thresholds in `scripts/cpu-trigger.mjs`. **Measured, never budgeted**: nothing in the bot reads this back, and no threshold lives in Core. A budget exists to size a territory and this colony's territory is a constant (ADR 0006's row arriving without its quota), while skipping ticks on a low bucket collides with the [[safe-mode reflex]], which has to be able to fire on the very tick a guard would skip (ADR 0007, ADR 0015).

### Chat bubble
The glyph an assigned creep says over its head each tick, one fixed glyph per [[task]] (⛏ Harvest · 📥 Withdraw · 🧲 Pickup · 🔋 Refill · 🔨 Build · 🔧 Repair · ⚡ Upgrade · 🚩 Reserve · 🏃 Flee). Observability only, private to our own viewer; unassigned creeps show nothing.

### Safe-mode reflex
The colony reflex (ADR 0007, revised by ADR 0015) that emits `ActivateSafeMode` the tick a CLAIM-part [[hostile]] stands within range 3 of the controller — the claim tap is a range-1 act, so holding until then is free and gives the [[fire reflex]] its window to kill the claimer en route; an unplaced controller falls back to firing on sight. A second arm since ADR 0034: any [[keep]] structure below full hits while any hostile — a [[threat]] or not — stands in the [[home room]]; the same shape as the first, hold until the harm is certain, and read statelessly off one tick's hits, which is why the Keep is repaired to full. Both arms read the home room's hostiles alone: the sweep behind the list covers every room the colony works (#201), and a claimer tapping an [[outpost]]'s controller is tapping one safe mode does not cover. Gated only on stock remaining and safe mode not already running; a hostile that neither claims nor damages never spends the stock. A colony with no tower standing fires on the first armed hostile instead (#217).

### Pickup reflex
The colony reflex that emits a pickup Intent for every creep with free carry capacity standing within range 1 of a dropped energy pile — beside its assigned [[task]]'s action, since the engine's pickup conflicts with no other action. A reflex, not a Task: no movement, no matching, no threshold — it only recaptures what is already in reach (death drops, harvest overflow). Energy only; tombstones are not covered — a tombstone is a store and its verb is [[withdraw]]. The energy it cannot reach is [[pickup]]'s, the Task beside it (#167): a pile big enough to be worth a walk is matched and walked to, and this reflex stays what it was, the thing that costs nothing because nobody moved for it. Its rule is unchanged and so is its output, with one subtraction: a creep that arrives at a pile *for* the Pickup Task meets this reflex's condition too, so the pair would spell one creep's one pickup twice, and the duplicate is dropped where the two lists meet rather than by narrowing either rule. Paired one room at a time and never across two (ADR 0041): each projected room's piles against the creeps standing in that room, because the pile and the creep are both bare positions and one coordinate of two rooms would otherwise read as in reach. It fires in an [[outpost]] by exactly the rule it fires at home, which is what the long round trip makes it earn there — an [[anchor]] on a full [[container]] spills onto the tile its [[hauler unit]] comes back to stand on, and a full container never takes that pile back. Piles are projected as position and kind alone — no amount, since no decision reads one. Like the [[safe-mode reflex]], it speaks no [[verdict]] and shows no [[chat bubble]].

### Fire reflex
The colony reflex (ADR 0014) that has every tower shoot the [[hostile]] nearest to itself, each tick one stands in the [[home room]]. Both halves of that pairing are home's: a tower stands only in a room we own, and the hostiles are narrowed to match, because the sweep behind the list covers every room the colony works (#201) and a room-blind range would otherwise pick a raider a border away as the nearest target. Attack only — towers never repair (creep Repair is far cheaper per hit) or heal — and per-tower: no focus fire, no anti-drain gate, no energy floor (a dry tower's shot fails harmlessly; unlike the [[safe-mode reflex]] there is no stock to protect). Like its siblings, it speaks no [[verdict]] and shows no [[chat bubble]].

### Hostile
A hostile creep as the [[world]] projects it: its id, its owner, where it stands as a [[room position]], and its body parts, verbatim — the fields its readers need (ADR 0014, widened by ADR 0028 and ADR 0041), and the projection grows one only the tick a reader for it exists (ADR 0007's rule that the field list grows when a decision reads it, widened to any reader by ADR 0028). The room is the field ADR 0028 explicitly left out as having no reader, and the reader ADR 0041 gave it is the [[raid log]]'s closest approach: a hostile is measured against the tiles of its own room, and one of ours on the same coordinate of another is not at range 0. It rode as a `RoomName` field beside a bare tile until #216 R3 and is the tile's own type now (ADR 0052 decision 2), so the two halves of that join cannot drift apart. Since #201 it is load-bearing for every reader there is — a [[threat]]'s [[reach]] is filed under it, and the two colony reflexes read the [[home room]] out of the list by it. What a hostile can do is decided from what it is made of — CLAIM is the only part that threatens the controller, and the controller is the only thing safe mode is spent on (ADR 0007); where it stands is what the [[fire reflex]] aims at; whose it is, is the [[raid log]]'s roster, the reader the owner grew for and the only one that reads it. Hostiles stay out of the [[spatial projection]]: they block no tiles and price no paths. Since ADR 0033 they do gate Tasks — through a [[threat]]'s [[reach]], a colony-level fact the pipeline reads, never a change to the map. An NPC **invader core** is not one of these and never joins the list: it is a *structure*, so the hostile-creep sweep behind the list cannot answer with one, and the two facts the colony reads off it — the room it stands in and the absolute tick its collapse timer runs out at — ride a field of their own (ADR 0043). That field is projected only where there is vision and the [[stand-down]] gate is its reader (ADR 0043); the two lists are disjoint by construction and a stand-down reads none of this one. The sweep behind *these* Hostiles is every room the colony works and can see (#201) and no longer the spawn rooms alone — which is what puts an [[outpost]]'s raider in front of ADR 0033's rules at all, after a live raid found a creep standing on its Seat in W13S28 with no Reach to gate it, no [[flee]] to run it and no episode to record it.

### Threat
A [[hostile]] carrying an ATTACK or RANGED_ATTACK part (ADR 0033) — read off the parts, never the owner: an invader and a raider hurt a creep the same per part. A healer, a scout or a claimer is a hostile the [[fire reflex]] shoots and the [[raid log]] records, but only a Threat has a [[reach]]. Under safe mode in a room we own, a hostile standing there is no Threat at all, whichever colony is looking (#218).
_Avoid_: enemy, attacker

### Reach
The tiles a [[threat]] can hurt: its weapon range (1 for ATTACK, 3 for RANGED_ATTACK) plus a margin of two — one for its next step, one for our tick of lag; the margin a tunable — less every tile under one of our standing [[rampart]]s in that room (ADR 0033). Filed by the room the Threat stands in, as the [[spatial projection]] it is derived from is layered (ADR 0041, #138): a room with no entry has an empty Reach — ADR 0004's absence, which blocks nothing — and a room's **safe set** is that room's walkable tiles less that room's Reach, which is [[flee]]'s [[work area]] for a creep standing there. A colony-level fact derived once each tick, read in three places and nowhere in the [[atlas]], each reader taking the share of one room — a Task's target's, a creep's own, the spawn's: it subtracts from every Task's [[work area]] at applicability, so a Task with nothing left is inapplicable to everyone and its holders are released `Threatened`, while a Task with tiles left is worked from them; it is what [[flee]] is applicable inside of; and the spawn emits nothing while a tile beside it lies in one. No hysteresis: a Reach appears and vanishes with its Threat, and the blink is measured before it is designed against.
_Avoid_: danger zone, threat radius

### Downgrade deadline
The hard floor on the controller's downgrade timer: half the level's full timer (ADR 0007). Inside it, Upgrade stops being surplus work and outranks even the feeding tier — a downgrade costs a level and zeroes the safe-mode stock, and the engine refuses safe-mode activation below half minus 5,000, so escalating at half keeps the [[safe-mode reflex]] fireable with the engine's whole grace intact.

### Stand-down
An [[outpost]] withdrawn from, and the tick it may be re-entered (ADR 0043). Withdrawal is a gate and not a manoeuvre: a stood-down room does not enter the [[spatial projection]], so no Task pools there, no quota counts it and nothing walks toward it — which is all "retreat" can be in an architecture that keeps no state. The tick is **read off the threat**, not chosen: an invader core's collapse timer, or the end of the reservation it has taken, sampled while there is still vision, with 2,500 ticks as the fallback where none can be read — and the reservation may never answer *earlier* than that fallback, because a core outlives the hold it takes and re-takes it, so a hold with a handful of ticks left is not a deadline but a note of what the core did last tick (ADR 0043, amended by #136). Re-entry is a clock running out and never a second look — a gate that reopened on *not seeing* would send creeps back into the same core forever. Recorded as an episode in the [[raid log]] and read from the terminal with `observe.mjs outposts`, which answers per room off the log's own rule — shut while the current tick is short of the expiry — and names the deadline that tick was read off, because "shut until 172,783" and "shut until 172,783 because nothing was readable" are different answers to an operator. A row it cannot decode fails the command rather than printing the room open. One gate per outpost. The other, clockless withdrawal is a room another player has owned or reserved: that one is not a threat, it is a room that stopped being ours to work. It is judged on the ticks with vision and **remembered** between them — the rooms taken ride the [[raid log]]'s leaf beside the episodes — because this gate's own effect is to withdraw the creeps whose vision judged it, and a conclusion re-read from nothing would reopen the room the tick after it shut. Only a tick with vision takes a room back out of that map, and nothing the colony does can produce one — the room is not scanned, so the look that would clear it never happens, and re-declaring it is a no-op, because the gate subtracts by name after the declaration is read. Clearing the room from the [[raid log]]'s leaf by hand, or moving the declaration to a room somebody else is not working, is the whole of the way back in, and `observe.mjs outposts` says so under every room it prints.

### Disaster fallback
The zero-creep spawning rule: an empty colony spawns bare [[worker unit]]s from whatever [[room energy]] is banked right now — as many as the bank affords, up to the workforce deficit — rather than waiting for a full capacity it can never refill. The one body-generation path that ignores the remainder — time-to-first-creep outranks spending the bank.

### Supply floor
The one spawning row that is a **floor** and not a quota (ADR 0050): a colony holding no living body that can put energy into an extension casts one hauler in front of every row, sized from the [[room energy]] banked *right now* rather than from the bank's capacity. Armed by the **absence** of a capability and disarmed by one living body, so it is not a minimum on the hauler row and hires nothing into a colony that already carries. Its gate is [[refill]]'s body clauses beside [[withdraw]]'s — a Carry part, no [[standing body]] ratio, no more Work than Move — and never "has a Carry part": the [[anchor]]'s `6W/1C/1M` answers that reading and can still put nothing into an extension, which is how a colony of two Anchors stood 1,235 ticks casting nothing while 246,818 energy sat in the [[storage]] beside it (#203). The [[disaster fallback]]'s own argument carried to a colony that is not empty — every other row prices its body at the bank's capacity, and a colony that cannot refill an extension never reaches that price again — with that fallback unchanged in front of it. What it casts wears the hauler row's name, because the floor casts from that row rather than from one of its own.

## Avoided terms

- **Role** — creeps are not born with roles; work is Task-based. Don't reintroduce role-based vocabulary.
